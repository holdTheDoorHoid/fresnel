Walton Harbour Lighthouse stands on a cliff above the harbour at Walton, on the south shore of the Minas Basin in the Municipality of East Hants, Nova Scotia. It is a square wooden tower, lit in 1872 or 1873, that guided ships into the harbour until shipping stopped there in 1978. The municipality bought it in 1991 and restored it as a historical museum, and in 2024 the municipality moved it about 45 m (148 ft) back from an eroding bluff.[^1][^3][^2]

## The place

When Acadians lived in the area, the settlement was known as Petite Rivière, after the small stream that empties into the Minas Basin there. After the Expulsion of the Acadians, New England Planters and Loyalists moved in, and in 1836 the settlement was renamed Walton in honour of James Walton, whose daughter and son-in-law owned extensive property nearby.[^1]

Francis Parker, whom Lighthouse Friends credits with starting much of the local industry, opened a gypsum mine in 1820 and shipped the rock from the harbour. He built a sawmill on the Walton River in 1837 and opened a shipyard around 1850. Lighthouse Friends says more than twenty-five vessels were built in Walton over the following thirty years.[^1]

In 1855 John Mosher and others petitioned the government of Nova Scotia for a light at the harbour entrance. A committee of the House of Assembly did not recommend it then. Two years later a Walton light was on a list of works to be built if money allowed, but it was not built, and residents raised the matter again in 1860.[^1]

## Building the light

The Marine Department's annual report for 1874, as Lighthouse Friends quotes it, says the light "was put in operation on 1st November, 1872."[^1] The tower was a square wooden building painted white, 6 m (20 ft) high, with a wooden lantern on top. The keeper lived in his own house about 1.2 km (three-quarters of a mile) from the light.[^1]

Timothy Parker, who became the first keeper, also built the tower. The same report gives his contract as $520 and the cost of the equipped lighthouse as $755.50.[^1] The NSLPS gives the cost as $620.00.[^2] Parker was appointed keeper in July 1873.[^1] Lighthouse Friends' keeper list starts in 1872, and the Rowlett directory dates the station to 1872, so the first year is not settled.[^1][^3]

In 1875 several trees on the point were cut down to give the light a larger range.[^1] Lighthouse Friends says a fourth-order Fresnel lens was installed in the lantern in 1915, and the light changed from fixed red to an occulting white at that time.[^1] The NSLPS dates the fourth-order dioptric lens and the occulting white to 1922.[^2]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends lists ten keepers, from Timothy Parker to Arthur Sanford, who was keeper until 1970.[^1] After Parker, the keepers were Archibald McNeil Parker, Burton Wheadon, Charles W. O'Brien, Henry A. Clark, Lewis E. Burgess, Stephen Brown, Burgess again, Harry Lake and Sanford.[^1] Harry Lake, the longest-serving of them, retired in 1962 after twenty-five years.[^1]

A Marine Department list of keepers in the report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1905 places Lewis E. Burgess at Walton Harbour, which agrees with the dates Lighthouse Friends gives him.[^4] The keeper table in the report for the fiscal year ended 31 March 1911 also lists Lewis E. Burgess on the Walton Harbour line, with an appointment date in 1903.[^5]

## After the keepers

The barite mine that had grown up near the village opened in 1941. A blast in a fault zone flooded it in 1970, and production ended in February 1978, which is also the year commercial shipping stopped at the harbour.[^1] The light was automated in 1970 and put out in 1986, Lighthouse Friends says.[^1] The NSLPS says it was "automated by 1986."[^2] Rowlett lists the light as inactive since 1978.[^3]

The Municipality of East Hants bought the lighthouse in 1991, when the Department of Transport had declared it surplus, and the NSLPS says the municipality bought it for its heritage value.[^2][^3] The lighthouse was designated as heritage property on 12 August 1992.[^2] Lighthouse Friends says Ted Burgess and the local historian Reg Clark gave thousands of hours to repairs to the tower and grounds after it closed.[^1] The NSLPS records a Craig Harding Award for lighthouse preservation in 2002.[^2]

In November 2024 the municipality moved the tower roughly 45 m back from the edge of the eroding bluff on which it stands.[^1][^3]

## Today

The lighthouse has been developed into an interpretation centre, with a landscaped picnic area and a short nature trail.[^1] The NSLPS says the municipality owns it and the East Hants tourism association maintains it.[^2] Rowlett gives the site and tower as open daily from early May to mid-October, with a donation requested.[^3] Lighthouse Friends gives the season as mid-May to mid-October.[^1] To reach it, follow the signs off Route 215 in Walton onto the gravel road to the lighthouse.[^1]
