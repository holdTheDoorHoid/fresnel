The upper Warren Landing range is a pair of lights on the west side of the Nelson River entrance, where the lake empties into the river at the north end of Lake Winnipeg in Manitoba.[^9] The Coast Guard list gives the front light a white square tower with an orange stripe, and Rowlett says red; the rear light stands 240 m (260 yd) north of it.[^10][^9]

## The place

The water at the entrance was the difficulty. In its report for 1903 the Department of Marine and Fisheries listed the small harbours at "Warren's Landing (entrance to Nelson river)," and said that "the narrow, crooked and none too deep entrances" had been "marked out by range beacons."[^1] Lighthouse Friends says mariners approached the settlement on the lower pair's bearing, then followed the upper pair in on a second bearing.[^8] The settlement takes its name, Lighthouse Friends says, from John Warren, a Hudson's Bay Company man who died there of a wound received in the Red River Rebellion.[^8]

## Building the light

The Department's report for 1908 describes the upper pair as "two inclosed wooden towers, square in plan, with sloping sides."[^2] The front tower stood on the west side of the mouth of the Nelson River, 9.4 m (31 ft) high to the ventilator on its lantern, with its light "elevated 33 feet above level of lake," about 10.1 m above the lake. The back tower was 12.2 m (40 ft) high, with its light at 12.8 m (42 ft).[^2] The same report says the towers were "erected by day's labour, under the supervision of" M. J. Egan, and gives one cost for them, $1,725.51.[^2]

The 1909 report lists the upper front light's equipment as a "Constant level lamp with 24-inch silvered copper reflector."[^3]

The back light was moved later. Lighthouse Friends says William Dewar of Warren Landing was paid $275 in 1910 to move the back light of the upper range to a new site.[^8] The 1911 report of the Chief Engineer lists "Moving back light to a new site" against Warren's Landing, with "Wm. Dewar, Warren's Landing, Man" as the contractor or foreman and $275.00 as the expenditure.[^4]

## The keepers

The upper pair had the same keeper as the lower. The Department's report for 1911 gives "Warren Landing Ranges Hugh McDonald . . Aug. 14, 1907," and its report for 1914 gives "H. McDonald Sept 14,1907."[^5][^6] The two reports differ on the month of his appointment. The Department's 1908 statement of lightkeepers gives 25 August 1905 for "Hugh McDonald" at Warren's Landing, and none of the reports explains the difference.[^11] The report for 1913 lists "Warren Landing ranges" as one station.[^7]

Lighthouse Friends says McDonald served until 1924, when J. Robertson took charge. It says a keeper it calls "Roberts" was responsible for the lights "through at least 1937."[^8]

## After the keepers

Lighthouse Friends says that at some point skeletal towers replaced the enclosed back towers on both pairs, while the original front towers stayed in use. It gives no date for the change.[^8] Rowlett describes the upper rear light as a "14 m (46 ft) square pyramidal steel skeletal tower," and the Coast Guard list gives its height as 14.0 m.[^9][^10]

In 2007 Canada Post issued five lighthouse stamps in its flag series, one of them showing the upper front range, Lighthouse Friends says.[^8]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns and manages the site, Rowlett says.[^9] Rowlett lists the front light as active, "Accessible only by boat," with the site open and the tower closed.[^9] The Coast Guard list marks the front light "Seasonal," with a focal height of 9.0 m.[^10] Lighthouse Friends says the range is visible from Warren Landing.[^8]
