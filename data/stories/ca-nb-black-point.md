Black Point Light stands at the northern end of Lamèque Island, New Brunswick, where it marks the western entrance to Miscou Harbour.[^1][^2] It is a Canadian Coast Guard light, and both LighthouseFriends and Rowlett date it to 1967.[^1][^2]

LighthouseFriends describes the tower as a skeleton tower with an enclosed central portion, 17.7 m (58 ft) tall, and says it is the only one of its kind in New Brunswick.[^1] The Coast Guard list gives the same height and describes a skeleton tower with a red top and a red and white rectangular daymark.[^3] Rowlett disagrees on the height, giving the tower as 16.5 m (54 ft).[^2]

The sources disagree about the light itself. The Coast Guard list gives a white light with a flash of two seconds and an eclipse of four, and a nominal range of 12 nautical miles.[^3] LighthouseFriends and Rowlett both describe a yellow light that shows four seconds on, two off, two on and two off.[^1][^2] Rowlett gives the focal plane as 18 m (58 ft).[^2] This story does not choose between them.

Rowlett describes a fourth-order drum-style Fresnel lens in use, in a tower painted with broad red and white horizontal bands.[^2] LighthouseFriends says the light is still equipped with a fourth-order drum lens, which it calls dioptric.[^1] Neither source mentions an earlier light on the point.

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the light.[^1][^2] LighthouseFriends says the grounds are open and the tower is closed, and gives directions by Route 313 and Light Road.[^1] Rowlett says the site is open and the tower closed, and that the entrance from NB 313 is not marked.[^2]
