Big Tignish Light marked the entrance to Tignish Harbour on the northwest coast of Prince Edward Island. It began in 1877 as a pair of masted beacons, and its tower first showed a light in 1881. The tower was moved in 2008 and now stands in a park at Tignish Shore.[^1]

## The place

Tignish Harbour is the mouth of the Big Tignish River, where the river meets the Gulf of St. Lawrence.[^1] In 1868 the government of Prince Edward Island began building piers on each side of the river mouth, narrowing the channel to 12.2 m (40 ft).[^1] Before that, "gales from the east would stir up sand from the surrounding beaches that would obstruct the entrance to the river."[^1] The narrowed channel gave enough current at ebb tide to keep the mouth open.[^1]

## Building the light

In 1877 two fixed beacons on masts were set up to guide vessels into the harbour. The front light stood at the outer end of the breakwater, and the back light about 320 metres shoreward from it.[^1] Theodore Bernard erected the lights for $130.[^1] Isidore Chasson became the first keeper, at a salary of $100 a year.[^1]

A gale on 29 October 1879 destroyed the beacons, about a year after they were put up.[^1]

Temporary range lights were shown the next season while Henry Williams built a formal lighthouse under a $545 contract.[^1] The tower, 10 m (33 ft) tall and set on the beach at the inner end of the north breakwater, first showed its light when navigation opened in 1881. It was a fixed white catoptric light, with a focal plane of 10.7 m (35 ft).[^1]

A gale carried away the front light in the fall of 1887, and in 1888 a new mast and shed replaced it.[^1] In 1895 Captain J. Gallant replaced the wooden foundation under the tower, which had rotted, with a stone one.[^1]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends lists the keepers as Isidore Chasson (1877 to 1881), Isidore Gaudet (1881 to 1894), J. S. Richard (1894 to 1895), Fidele J. Gaudet (1895 to 1897), Agate Gaudet (1897 to 1912), D. Handrahan (from 1912), Jerome J. Perry (1923 to 1956) and Alphonse Gaudet (1956 to 1962).[^1]

The Department of Marine's keeper table for the fiscal year ended 31 March 1910 gives the station as "Tignish Run," with Agape Gaudet appointed 30 August 1897.[^2] The Department's statement for fiscal 1908 gives the same name, keeper and date.[^3] Lighthouse Friends prints the name as Agate Gaudet. The two are probably the same man, but the sources do not confirm it, so both spellings appear here.[^1][^2][^3]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

Two gales took the early works. The one on 29 October 1879 destroyed the beacons, and the one in the fall of 1887 carried away the front light.[^1] No wreck or rescue connected with the light appears in these sources.

## After the keepers

The light ran on oil until the tower was electrified in 1962.[^1] Lighthouse Friends names the last keeper as Alphonse Gaude (its keeper list gives Alphonse Gaudet), and says he was replaced by a caretaker, John Pitre.[^1]

The tower served as a rear range light until 1989, when it became a coastal light. The light was decommissioned in 1997.[^1] In 2008 the tower was moved from the harbour entrance to Fisherman's Haven Park at Tignish Shore.[^1]

The Department's tables name the light differently over the years. "Tignish Run" appears in the 1908 statement and the 1910 keeper table,[^2][^3] and "Big Tignish range" in the 1912 agency list.[^4]

## Today

Lighthouse Friends says the tower still stands, relocated, in Fisherman's Haven Park at Tignish Shore.[^1] The Tignish Shore Community Council owns the lighthouse.[^1] The grounds are open, and the tower is closed to visitors.[^1] The light has been decommissioned since 1997.[^1]
