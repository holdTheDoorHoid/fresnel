Belyea's Point Light is a small white tower on the west bank of the Saint John River, at the lower end of Long Reach about 3 km (2 mi) north of Westfield, New Brunswick.[^1][^5] It began operation on 1 June 1882.[^1] The tower standing today dates from the 1930s, when floods forced a rebuild on a slightly different spot.[^1][^5]

## The place

The light stands on a point at the southern end of Long Reach, which LighthouseFriends describes as a wide, straight stretch of the Saint John River running along the west side of the Kingston Peninsula.[^1][^5] LighthouseFriends quotes the Department's report for 1882 as saying the light guided vessels clear of Purdy's Shoal on the opposite side of the river.[^1]

LighthouseFriends says that along this river the first keepers of many lights were the landowners who lived nearest the site, and that this happened at Belyea's Point.[^1]

## Building the light

In 1881 Parliament appropriated $800 for a light here, and a $395 contract for the tower went to Saunders Clark of Carleton.[^1] The light began operation on 1 June 1882, with Spafford Barker Belyea as its first keeper at a salary of $80 a year.[^1]

The 1882 report, as LighthouseFriends quotes it, describes the first tower as a square wooden building, painted white and 11 m (36 ft) high to the vane, standing on a small timber crib.[^1] Its fixed white light came from a 6th-order dioptric apparatus, 12.2 m (40 ft) above water, and was visible for 11 miles.[^1] About a decade later, LighthouseFriends reports, the Department's annual report found the tower painted inside and out, in good order, and the interior clean and well kept.[^1]

Severe floods in the 1930s damaged the original tower.[^1] It was rebuilt and moved from its position lower on the shore onto higher ground, on pylons.[^1][^5] LighthouseFriends says the spring freshet has damaged the new tower several times since.[^1]

## The keepers

Spafford Barker Belyea kept the light from 1882 to 1899.[^1]

The keepers' table in the Department's annual report, printed in 1906, lists "Day, W. A" at Belyea Point, appointed 20 September 1899.[^2] LighthouseFriends names him Westfield A. Day and gives his service as 1899 to 1906.[^1]

After him the keeper was Mrs. W. A. Day, whom LighthouseFriends calls Mrs. Westfield A. Day.[^1] It gives her service as 1906 to at least 1923.[^1] The Department's keepers' table in the annual report printed in 1908 lists "Day, Mrs. W. A" at Belyea Point, appointed 21 November 1906.[^3][^1] The 1923 date rests on LighthouseFriends alone.

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the light and manages the site.[^1][^5] The Coast Guard's list gives a green light flashing every five seconds, with a focal height of 12.7 m and a nominal range of 4 nautical miles. The tower is a white square tower with a red upper portion, 11.9 m high.[^4] The list marks the light as seasonal.[^4] LighthouseFriends and Rowlett give higher focal heights (13.6 m and 14 m) and LighthouseFriends gives a range of 5 miles. This story uses the Coast Guard's figures.[^1][^4][^5]

The site is open and the tower is closed.[^5] Rowlett says the entrance from Route 102 is unmarked.[^5] New houses now block the old view from the cul-de-sac, and LighthouseFriends says the best view is from the river.[^1]
