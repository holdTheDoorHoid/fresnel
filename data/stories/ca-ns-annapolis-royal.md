Annapolis Royal Lighthouse is a small white tower with a red lantern on the south bank of the Annapolis River, at the foot of St. George Street in the town of Annapolis Royal, Annapolis County, Nova Scotia.[^1][^2] It was built in 1889 and marks the end of navigable water for ships on the river.[^1]

## The place

Annapolis Royal is an old town. The Lighthouse Directory says it was founded in the 1630s, was the capital of French Acadia until British troops took it in 1710, and stayed the capital of British Nova Scotia until Halifax was founded in 1749.[^2] Lighthouse Friends says ships once sailed farther up the Annapolis River, but the tidal power station completed in 1984 has ended that.[^1]

The tower stands on the site of an earlier building. The Nova Scotia Lighthouse Preservation Society says the tower was put up where the old Government House had stood; that house, home to the garrison engineer, burned in 1833.[^3] Lighthouse Friends gives the same site and the same fire year.[^1]

## Building the light

The tower was built in 1889, and the light was first shown that year.[^1][^2][^3] None of the sources names a builder, an architect or a cost.

The tower is a square, tapering wooden structure about 8.5 m (28 ft) high.[^2][^4] The Canadian Coast Guard's list of lights gives the light a fixed red characteristic, a focal height of 9.1 m above the water and a range of 8 nautical miles.[^4] The Preservation Society, which gives the light's elevation as 30 feet (9.1 m), dates a 6th-order dioptric lens showing a fixed red light to 1922–1941.[^3]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends gives the keepers in order. Michael Riordan, the first keeper, served from 1889 to 1892. Simon W. Riley followed and stayed until 1908, the longest stint on the list.[^1] The Department of Marine and Fisheries' keepers' list for 1906 places Riley at Annapolis, which fits that stint.[^5] James McMillan kept the light from 1908 to 1913. Thomas Milledge is listed for 1912 alone, a year that falls inside McMillan's stint; no source explains the overlap. T.M. Gavaza served from 1914 to 1922, and F.B. Arnaud from 1922 to at least 1923.[^1]

## After the keepers

The sources do not say when the light stopped needing a resident keeper. The Preservation Society also records an alteration in 1948, of unstated form, which it says may have moved the light.[^3]

The Historical Association of Annapolis Royal bought the lighthouse in 2004.[^2] In 2015 the lighthouse won $40,000 in the National Trust's Lighthouse Matters competition, and the money supported a restoration in 2016.[^1][^2]

## Today

The Historical Association of Annapolis Royal owns the lighthouse and uses it as the headquarters for its National Historic District and Acadian Heritage tours.[^1][^3] The Coast Guard's list calls the light a private aid to navigation.[^4] Lighthouse Friends says the Coast Guard owns the lighthouse, but the same page says the association has owned it since 2004.[^1] The grounds are open and the tower is closed to visitors.[^1][^2]
