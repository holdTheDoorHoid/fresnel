Pictou Island South is a wooden light on the government wharf near the west end of Pictou Island, in the Northumberland Strait off Nova Scotia.[^4] The Department of Marine and Fisheries put it into operation in 1907.[^1] A separate light on the island's west end had been put into operation two years earlier, in 1905.[^6]

## The place

Pictou Island lies off the entrance to Pictou Harbour, roughly eight kilometres from the Nova Scotia shore.[^3][^4] The Department's annual report for 1912–13 lists three Pictou Island lights in its statement of light stations and keepers: "Pictou island S.E", "Pictou island, W.E" and "Pictou island wharf".[^8]

## Building the light

The Department's annual report for 1907–08 says the light was "established at the government wharf on the south side," near the west end of the island, and that it was "put in operation on the opening of navigation in 1907."[^1] The tower "stands on the top of the bank near the wharf." It is an enclosed square wooden building with sloping sides, 7.9 m (26 ft) high to the top of its ventilator. The light is a fixed white dioptric light of the sixth order, 9.8 m (32 ft) above high water, and the report says it is visible "10 miles from all points of approach by water."[^1]

Two dwellings appear in the next reports. The 1907–08 report says a new dwelling "for the keeper of the west pier lighthouse" was under construction.[^1] The 1908–09 report says a new dwelling "was erected for the lightkeeper at this station." It was framed, 8.0 m by 6.4 m (26 ft 4 in by 20 ft 10 in), and cost $782.39.[^2] Both entries sit under the same "Pictou island" heading, so the reports do not say which of the island's lights the second dwelling was for.

The light on the west end is a different light. The 1905–06 report says that the light put into operation there on the opening of navigation in 1905 was, "temporarily, a revolving light, instead of a group flashing light as previously described."[^6]

## The keepers

The keepers' tables are the main record. The 1906–07 table gives Charles D. Patterson's station as "West End of Pictou Island," with an appointment date of 29 March 1905 and a salary of $400.[^5] The 1908–09 table lists him against "Pictou Island Pier, west end," with the same appointment date and salary. The line beneath gives Hugh McLean, appointed 24 June 1905 at $100, against the same station. The table gives no role for McLean.[^2] The 1910–11 table prints the same station, names and dates.[^7] The 1912–13 table lists Patterson against "Pictou island, W.E" and a J. McLean, appointed 22 December 1911, against "Pictou island wharf."[^8]

The sources read here do not settle whether "Pictou Island Pier, west end" in the 1908–11 tables names the wharf light or the west-end light, or whether Patterson kept one light or both. The 1912–13 table is the one read here that names a keeper for the wharf light.

LighthouseFriends, which writes about the west-end light, names Patterson as its first keeper, at $400 a year, and says he retired in 1933.[^4] That account is secondary and is used here for those facts only.

## Today

The Lighthouse Directory lists the wharf light as active. It describes an 8 m (27 ft) square pyramidal wooden tower, painted white with a red lantern, and says the lighthouse was granted heritage status in 2015.[^3] The same entry says the light served as the rear light of a range from 1951 to 1974.[^3] The site is open and the tower is closed. Pictou Island is reached by passenger ferry from Caribou, on a varying schedule from May through November.[^3]
