The Cocagne range front light is a white tower with a red stripe, now at the Cocagne Marina on the New Brunswick coast.[^2] It was the front light of a pair of range lights set up at the mouth of the Cocagne River in 1907. It was taken off its range in 2018 and moved to the marina in 2019.[^1][^2]

## The place

The Cocagne River meets the sea on the east coast of New Brunswick, which Rowlett's directory describes as facing "the Gulf of St. Lawrence and Northumberland Strait."[^2] Nicolas Denys, a French explorer and colonizer in the seventeenth century, wrote of the harbour.[^1] LighthouseFriends quotes him: his people were "so surfeited with game and fish" that they wanted no more, and bad weather kept him there for eight days.[^1] He named the place the River of Cocagne for that good cheer.[^1]

## Building the light

The Department of Marine's report for the year ended 30 June 1906 lists "Cocagne, range lights to be established" among works under way.[^3] The report for the following year gives the plan.[^4] The front light was to be shown from an enclosed tower on the south side of the mouth of the Cocagne River, 21 m (70 ft) east of the Cocagne bridge.[^4] The tower was a square wooden building with sloping sides and a square lantern, painted white.[^4] It stood 8.5 m (28 ft) from its base to the vane.[^4] Its fixed red light was 7.9 m (26 ft) above high water and visible 6 miles, as the report prints it.[^4] The back light stood on the shore of the river, 264 m (865 ft) from the front.[^4] It was shown from an anchor-lens lantern on a pole.[^4]

The Department's report for the year ended 30 June 1908 says the range lights, "described in last year's annual report, and stated as under construction, were completed." The work was done by day labour, at a cost of $699.17.[^5] LighthouseFriends dates the completion to 1907.[^1] The report covers the year from July 1907 to June 1908, so it fits a completion in 1907 but does not give the month. The same report lists the front light as a 240° sixth-order French lens, with a duplex lamp and a red light.[^5]

## The keepers

The Department's keepers' list for the year ended 30 June 1915 gives "D. Gognen" an appointment date of 14 October 1907, at a salary of $220 a year.[^6] LighthouseFriends names this keeper as Dominique Goguen and gives his service as 1907 to 1926.[^1] In the Department's table the name and date sit in separate columns from the station names, so the link to Cocagne is made by position, and the printed spelling is "Gognen".[^6]

LighthouseFriends lists seven more keepers after him, the last from 1963. The roster below gives their names and years as that page gives them.[^1]

## After the keepers

LighthouseFriends says that in 2012 the original 1907 tower showed a fixed yellow light, as did the modern skeletal rear light, with a white daymark and a red vertical stripe, which was then the rear range light.[^1] Rowlett describes the front light as "Inactive since 2018" and says it was deactivated that year.[^2] LighthouseFriends says the range lights were discontinued in 2018, and that the front light was relocated to the marina on 26 July 2019.[^1]

## Today

The front light stands at the Cocagne Marina, across the river entrance from its first site at the south end of the bridge on NB 134. Rowlett says it was restored there.[^2] The tower is painted white with red trim and a broad red stripe on the range line, with a red lantern.[^2]

LighthouseFriends says the Canadian Coast Guard owns the light. The grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^1] Rowlett gives the Cocagne Marina as owner and site manager of the rear light, which stands at the marina.[^2]
