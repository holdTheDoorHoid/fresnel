Two small wooden towers on the shore at Northport, Prince Edward Island, line up to guide vessels through the channel toward Alberton Harbour and Cascumpec Bay. The pair has been shifted and raised more than once over a century and a quarter.[^1][^2]

The channel they mark is a shifting one. In 1901 the Department of Marine and Fisheries described how last winter's storms had changed the channel over the bar at Cascumpec, and its sailing directions told masters to hold the Sandy Island range in line until "the red lights at Northport are in one", taking care not to overrun the alignment because the two lines crossed close to the shoal off Kildare Point.[^3] By those directions the Northport pair was the second stage of the approach, after the Sandy Island range.

The provincial heritage record says the lighthouse was built in 1885 by the builder James Keefe, to a Department of Marine and Fisheries design, as one of a pair of open-frame range lights, and that it first served as the front light.[^1] The same record names John Mountain as keeper from 1885 to 1897.[^1] The Department's keepers' statement for the year ended 30 June 1904 lists Wm. Champion at "Northport Range", appointed 25 October 1897, at a salary of $100.[^5] The two sources fit together: Champion's appointment falls in the year the heritage record gives for the end of Mountain's service.

The record of the work done is clearer after that. The heritage record says 3.3 metres were added to the original construction in 1887, and that the open frame was enclosed in 1903.[^1] The Department's report for the year ended 30 June 1903 agrees on the second point: under its Prince Edward Island works it says that at Northport "the towers from which the range lights are shown have been enclosed and painted white."[^4]

The keepers named in the heritage record are John Mountain (1885 to 1897), B. Mountain (1912 to 1914), W. A. Smith (1914 to 1923, and possibly longer), Joseph Perry (1926 to 1948), Lemuel Arsenault (1948) and James Andrew Gallant (1948 to 1961).[^1] The 1904 statement adds Wm. Champion, and the heritage record names no one else between 1897 and 1912.[^1][^5]

In 1962 a new lighthouse was built. The old front tower was moved to become the rear light, and 1.8 metres were added to it in 1970. The statement of significance says the rear tower has been moved four times and raised twice to suit the changing route through the channel.[^1][^2] The original rear tower was sold, moved nearby and made into a private house.[^1]

The rear tower was designated a heritage lighthouse in 2015 under the federal Heritage Lighthouses Protection Act, and the community of Northport now owns it, on a grassy lot about 20 metres from the shoreline in a residential area.[^1][^2] The Coast Guard's current list shows the rear light as an isophase green light, an 11.8 m white pyramid tower with a red vertical stripe, 277.3 m behind the front light, which is a square skeleton tower on the railway wharf; both are marked seasonal.[^6] The register describes the rear tower as highly visible from the water and its setting as accessible to the public; it is an operating aid to navigation, and the front light stands on a wharf.[^2][^6] The sources do not say whether the tower can be entered, and nothing here should be read as an invitation to climb it.
