Cow Head Light is an iron tower of 1909 on a headland near the town of Cow Head, on the west coast of Newfoundland's Great Northern Peninsula. Jacques Cartier named the headland Cap de Pointe on 16 June 1534, and French fishermen who had fishing rights along the coast until 1904 called it "Tete de Vache".[^1] The light stopped working in 1979 or 1988, depending on the source, and the town now keeps it as a municipal heritage building.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

## Building the light

Lighthouse Friends says the light was erected in 1908 in the hollow of a promontory on the western side of the peninsula, and activated in 1909.[^1] The register gives 1909 as the build date.[^3] Rowlett gives 1905, the only source that does.[^2] This story follows the 1909 date.

Lighthouse Friends calls the tower an iron tower, 5.5 m (18 ft) tall.[^1] The Heritage NL designation gives 5 m from the base to the balcony and 8 m to the vane.[^4] Lighthouse Friends says the fixed white light was displayed 43 m (141 ft) above high water and could be seen from fifteen miles; the source gives no unit for that distance.[^1] The Heritage NL note gives a range of 22.5 km and describes ten one-second flashes a minute.[^4] The two accounts do not agree on the light's character.

## The keepers

Jesse Payne was the first keeper.[^3] He lived in a small house beside the light, lit the kerosene lamp at dusk and put it out at dawn every day from spring until late December, while navigation was open.[^3][^1] After Jesse's death his son John L. Payne, known as "Lighthouse Jack", kept the light. Lighthouse Friends lists him as keeper from 1949 to at least 1953.[^1][^3]

In 1952 an acetylene light with an automatic timer was installed, so the keeper needed to visit the tower only now and then to clean the lens. Lighthouse Friends says his wages were cut, and he moved his family to Tuckers Cove, where he could work as a fisherman while still keeping an eye on the light.[^1]

The register names a grandson, Jesse, as a later keeper. Lighthouse Friends calls the final keeper "Jesse Payne, son of Lighthouse Jack". This story follows the register on the family line.[^3][^1]

The grounds were a place for Sunday visits. Lighthouse Friends and the register both say people walked up the Lighthouse Path with packed lunches, and after church in summer they picnicked there.[^1][^3]

## After the keepers

Lighthouse Friends says the light became battery operated in 1978 and was discontinued in 1988 by Transport Canada.[^1] The register says the light guided ships from 1909 to 1979, when battery-powered harbour buoys replaced it.[^3] Rowlett gives 1988 as the year it went inactive, and says the abandoned tower was endangered by neglect and rust.[^2]

In late 2001 the Central Development Association received $492,780 under the Canada-Newfoundland Comprehensive Economic Development Agreement, for tourism infrastructure in the Cow Head area. The lighthouse was restored in 2002.[^1][^2] The Town of Cow Head designated it a municipal heritage building on 17 February 2003, by a motion of council.[^3][^4] It was repainted in 2019.[^2]

## Today

The Town of Cow Head owns and manages the lighthouse. Rowlett and Lighthouse Friends both say the site and tower are open to visitors.[^1][^2] The register places the lighthouse inside Gros Morne National Park, a National Historic Site and UNESCO World Heritage Site, and its designation covers the building and the land around it.[^3] To get there, follow the road west from Route 430 into the town and then drive across the isthmus to the headland.[^1] The Lighthouse Trail is an easy 1.5 km loop that takes about 45 minutes.[^1]
