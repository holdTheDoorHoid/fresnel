The Anderson Hollow light was first shown in 1889 from a wooden tower at the government breakwater at Waterside, on the Chignecto Channel in Albert County, New Brunswick.[^1][^3] The tower that carries the name today is not in Waterside.[^11] The shore tower of 1903 that replaced the breakwater light has been moved, by one local count, four times, and it now stands at Harvey Bank, in Shipyard Park on Mary's Point Road, where the site is open and the tower is closed.[^11]

## The place

The light was there to guide vessels into a harbour of refuge behind the breakwater.[^1] The 1889 report, as LighthouseFriends quotes it, said vessels could run for the harbour an hour before high water, with a berth of 15 m (50 ft) to pass the light on the port hand.[^1] The first tower was a square wooden building, painted white with a red roof, 9.1 m (30 ft) from the wharf to the vane.[^1]

## Building the light

In 1898 a new enclosed tower went up on the outer end of the repaired public wharf, with a square wooden lantern and a seventh-order lens showing a fixed red light. LighthouseFriends gives the contract to H. O. Barbour of Waterside, for $182.[^2]

The tower that stands today was built after the breakwater light was lost.[^3] The Department of Marine's report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1903 places it "on the shore north of the breakwater," directly opposite the breakwater's outer end.[^3] It is a wooden tower, square in plan, with sloping sides and a square wooden lantern, painted white, 9.4 m (31 ft) from base to vane.[^3] The light was first shown at the opening of navigation in 1903: a fixed white light 27.7 m (91 ft) above high water, dioptric of the seventh order, visible six miles.[^3] LighthouseFriends names the builder as W. C. Anderson of Waterside, at $362.[^2] The Lighthouse Directory gives 1907 as the tower's date. That is the year the breakwater mast light went up, while the 1903 report and LighthouseFriends date the shore tower to 1903.[^10][^7][^3][^2]

## The keepers

The first keeper was the Reverend S. C. Moore.[^2] The Department's list of keepers for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1900 gives his appointment at Anderson's Hollow as 14 May 1889.[^5] LighthouseFriends has him there until 1901.[^2] In 1900 he was given a hand foghorn, so that he could answer the fog signals of vessels in the neighbourhood.[^2]

After Moore, LighthouseFriends names Ed. J. Copp (1901 to 1903), Aaron B. Copp (1903 to 1911) and J. E. Moore (1911 to at least 1923).[^2] The Department's 1903 keeper list pairs "Copp, A. B" with Anderson Hollow.[^6] The official lists read here do not pair Ed. J. Copp or J. E. Moore with this station reliably, so their service rests on LighthouseFriends alone.[^2] The sources give names and dates for these keepers, and almost nothing about their work or their families at Waterside.

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

LighthouseFriends says that a heavy storm damaged the first tower in 1891, and that in 1892 it was torn from its foundation and survived, to be put back on the wharf. It says a gale on 21 November 1895 carried away the end of the pier and the lighthouse.[^2]

The Department's report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1902 is the official account of the loss of the breakwater light. It says that "This lighthouse was destroyed by storm," and that a temporary light was then established to replace it.[^4] The storm date is 12 January 1902.[^3] The sources read do not record a wreck or a rescue at Anderson Hollow.

## After the keepers

In 1907 a light was established on the outer end of the government breakwater, shown from an anchor lens lantern hoisted on a 6.7 m (22 ft) pole.[^7] A year later a crib 2.7 m (9 ft) square was built around the mast and filled with stone for ballast.[^8] The Department's annual report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1909 lists the light formerly shown from the shore tower as permanently discontinued.[^9] LighthouseFriends says the mast on the breakwater then took over that work.[^2]

The sources do not agree on what the shore tower did after 1909. LighthouseFriends says it remained on its bank "until at least 1949", and that the List of Lights for that year named it as the standby light if the breakwater light could not be shown.[^2] The Lighthouse Directory says the tower has been inactive since the 1950s, and describes it with no lantern, its light shone through a square window.[^10] The 1903 report had given it a lantern.[^3]

The tower then moved.[^2] LighthouseFriends says it was moved, at some point, to the nearby Waterside residence of Herbert Armstrong, who had kept the Martin Head light in the early twentieth century. In the late 1980s it went to Riverside-Albert, beside the Old Bank Museum, where it was used for a few years as a craft outlet. On 31 October 2001 it was moved to the Harvey Bank Heritage Shipyard Park.[^2]

## Today

The tower stands in Shipyard Park on Mary's Point, near the mouth of the Shepody River.[^11][^2] The Lighthouse Directory says it is on display at the Shepody River Dam instead.[^10] The grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^11] It is a static display, so visitors can look at it but not go inside.[^11]

Ownership has changed on paper. On 4 June 2024 the Chipoudy Communities Revitalization Committee announced that it had acquired the lighthouse and the Old Bank of New Brunswick, with the land they stand on.[^12] The Lighthouse Directory and LighthouseFriends name the Albert County Heritage Trust as owner, and the sources read do not date those listings.[^10][^2] In an update dated 28 December 2025 the committee said the tower would move to a lot at the 915/114 corner, beside Crooked Creek Convenience and the Old Bank, once the Harvey Dam works were finished, which it expected in the fall of 2026. Repairs and paint were to follow the move.[^11] The sources read do not say whether the move has happened.
