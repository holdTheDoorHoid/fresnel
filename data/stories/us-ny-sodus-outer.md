Sodus Outer Light stands at the end of the west pier that marks the western side of the entrance to Sodus Bay, at Sodus Point in Wayne County, New York.[^1][^6][^7] The square pyramidal tower on the pier dates from 1938.[^7] The Coast Guard owns the light. The pier is open while the tower is closed.[^7]

## The place

In 1828 a project drew up plans to narrow the bay's entrance to 470 feet (143 m) with a pair of piers, and the piers were built between 1829 and 1834.[^7] Congress approved a lighthouse for Sodus Bay on 26 May 1824, with building costs put at $4,500.[^6] The first keeper, Ishmael D. Hill, had petitioned for the post. In 1829 he was disqualified, and Bennett C. Fitzhugh took over.[^7][^1]

Coal later became the port's main cargo.[^7] In 1940 more than one million tons of coal were shipped from Sodus Point Harbor.[^7]

## Building the light

The first pier light was a stone tower, built on the west pier in 1837 as "a mere guide for entering the harbor."[^7] Its lantern held four lamps and reflectors for a fixed white light.[^7] A gale destroyed the pier beacon in 1857. The following year a pair of range lights on masts took its place, and in 1872 frame towers replaced the masts.[^7] That year a 1,150-foot (350 m) elevated walk was built along the pier, so the keepers could reach the outer light in foul weather.[^7]

In 1892 a circular oil house was built 128 feet (39 m) south of the lighthouse and lined with brick, to hold the kerosene that had become the lamp's fuel.[^7] In 1902 the interval between the outer light's flashes was cut from two minutes to thirty seconds.[^7]

The Board's report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1892 records a mishap on the pier. A vessel of Windsor, Ontario, "while being towed outward, sheered into and broke down some 10 feet of the elevated walk."[^2]

The shore lighthouse on the hill was the other half of the station. Congress appropriated $14,000 for a new house and tower in 1870,[^6][^7] and the new tower was finished on 30 June 1871, when the old one of 1825 was pulled down.[^6] The new house followed the plan used at Stony Point, New York.[^7]

In July 1895 the inner beacon on the west pier was moved about 25 feet (8 m) to a new tower.[^3] The Board's 1896 report records that, in the year before, the keeper's dwelling had been extensively repaired and a new beacon with a lantern built to replace an unserviceable one.[^4]

On 10 June 1901 the Board changed the outer light from sixth to fourth order.[^5] The shore lighthouse was discontinued that year, and its fourth-order lens went onto the pier tower.[^6][^7] Kerosene gave way to electricity when the present tower went up in 1938.[^7] An air fog signal had been established at the pier's end in 1926.[^7]

## The keepers

The archive's roster for the Sodus Point station runs from 1826 to 1973 and names more than fifty keepers, assistants and Coast Guard crew. It lists them under one station and does not separate the shore light from the pier lights.[^1] After the 1901 change, the keeper's charge was the pier lights alone, and he continued to live in the shore house.[^7]

Daniel Phillips was principal keeper for 36 years, from 1881 to 1917, longer than any other keeper on the roster.[^1][^7] LighthouseFriends describes him as a Civil War veteran who fought at Gettysburg.[^7]

Wilfred A. Lewis was first assistant from 1905 to 1917 and then principal keeper until 1939.[^1] Edwin G. Ward, principal keeper from 1939 to 1952, is called the final civilian keeper. LighthouseFriends says he was born into a lighthouse family.[^1][^7] Harold A. Ayers, a boatswain's mate, took charge after him.[^1][^7]

Charles A. Plummer was an assistant for at least 1919 to 1943.[^1] Coast Guard names begin to appear on the roster in 1948, alongside the civilian keepers, and they make up most of the later entries.[^1]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

In 1935 Plummer was recognised for pulling a boy and a girl from the water after their canoe overturned near the lighthouse.[^7]

On 31 August 1964 a man fell off the breakwall into the channel.[^7] Thomas M. Muchow, on watch, dropped a life ring, but the man would not let go of a fishing stringer.[^7] Muchow went into the water and brought him to shore.[^7] LighthouseFriends says an attending doctor credited Muchow with saving the man's life.[^7]

## After the keepers

A marker radiobeacon was installed on the pier in 1948.[^7] The Coast Guard closed its station at Sodus Point in 1973, but a Coast Guard commander kept living in the lighthouse.[^7] The shore house was listed on the National Register of Historic Places. LighthouseFriends dates the listing to 1976, and the Society to 1977.[^7][^6]

After the Coast Guard left the house, it suffered vandalism and neglect. The Town of Sodus received it in 1984.[^6][^7] The Sodus Bay Historical Society opened it as a museum on 4 July 1985, and the Fresnel lens returned to the tower in 1988.[^6]

At the 1985 dedication, the family of the last civilian keeper was present, and a daughter recalled how cold the spiral stairs were in winter.[^7]

## Today

The Coast Guard owns the outer light, and the pier is open while the tower is closed.[^7] The museum in the old shore house is run by the Sodus Bay Historical Society, which lists its open hours on its website as Friday to Sunday, noon to 5 p.m.[^6]
