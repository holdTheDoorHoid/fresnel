Selkirk Light stands at the mouth of the Salmon River on the south shore of Lake Ontario, near Pulaski in Oswego County, New York.[^1][^2] It was first shown in August 1838 from a stone dwelling with an octagonal tower.[^1] It was deactivated in 1859, sold in 1895, and has been in private hands since, first as a home, then as a hotel and now as a rental.[^1][^2]

## The place

Lieutenant Charles T. Platt, who stopped by soon after the light was first shown, reported that the bay into which the Salmon River empties had "not a single harbor for vessels drawing over five feet" (about 1.5 m).[^1] Platt added that "a better harbor can be constructed here than at any other point in the bay."[^1] Off the river mouth, he wrote, lay "a bar having a depth of water, in different seasons, of from five to nine feet" (1.5 to 2.7 m).[^1] Shortly after the lighthouse was completed, two piers of more than 1,000 feet (about 305 m) were built at the mouth of the river to improve the harbor.[^1] The lighthouse was to stand "at the junction of the Salmon River with said Lake in the village of Port Ontario," according to the bid notice as LighthouseFriends quotes it.[^1]

## Building the light

Congress appropriated $3,000 on 3 March 1837, and the government bought about 5,760 square feet (535 m²) of land from Sylvester and Daniel Brown on 1 September 1837.[^1] As LighthouseFriends describes the bid notice, it called for "The dwelling house to be of stone, 25 feet wide by thirty feet long" (7.6 by 9.1 m), under an octagonal tower that would rise 16 feet (4.9 m) above the walls.[^1] Local contractors Joseph Gibbs and Abner French took the contract, but a local stonemason, Jabez Meacham, did most of the work, using stone from a nearby quarry.[^1] "John Box, a local blacksmith, formed the wrought iron railings" around the lantern.[^1] LighthouseFriends reports the total cost as $2,999.94.[^1]

Lewis Conant exhibited the light for the first time in August 1838.[^1] Platt's report, as quoted by LighthouseFriends, says: "This is a new light, just established, and is fixed on the keeper's dwelling."[^1]

## The keepers

The US Lighthouse Society's record lists four keepers: Lewis Conant (1838–1849), Lucius B. Cole (1849–1854), Charles M. Lewis (1854–1857) and Albert H. Weed (1857–1859).[^2] LighthouseFriends gives the same names and says Cole succeeded Conant "in July 1849."[^1] LighthouseFriends also says that during Lewis's service the light source was upgraded to a "270° sixth-order Fresnel lens powered by a single Hains Mineral Oil fountain lamp."[^1]

## After the keepers

The light was deactivated in 1859.[^1][^2] LighthouseFriends says that afterward "the lighthouse was reportedly used by the Lifesaving Service," and that former keeper Lucius B. Cole "lived in" it until his death in 1890.[^1] On 16 October 1895 a German émigré, Leopold Joh, bought the lighthouse at auction from the government for $155.[^1] Joh first used it as a private home and began a hotel complex around it in 1899.[^1] Joh died in 1907, and his family ran the hotel until 1916, when it was sold to the Heckle family, who soon doubled its size.[^1]

In 1987 the property was bought from its owner by a corporation that a group of individuals had formed, and in 1995 the lighthouse opened to overnight stays.[^1] In 1989 a "Coast-Guard-approved solar light" was installed in the lantern, and the lighthouse was reactivated as a Class II aid named Port Ontario Light.[^1] LighthouseFriends says new owners took over the property in 2014.[^1]

## Today

The lighthouse is privately owned. LighthouseFriends says its grounds are open and its dwelling and tower are open to overnight guests.[^1] Salmon River Lighthouse, which has three bedrooms and can accommodate up to eight people, is available for overnight stays.[^1] The US Lighthouse Society's listing places the lighthouse at the Salmon River marina, 5 miles due west of Pulaski, and describes three ways to rent it: the first floor, the second floor, or both.[^3]
