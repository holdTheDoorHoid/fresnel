Bluff Point Light, also called the Valcour Island Light, stands on the western shore of Valcour Island in Lake Champlain, in Clinton County, New York. It was first lit in the spring of 1874 and was replaced by a steel tower in 1930. The light returned to the old stone lighthouse in 2004, and the station can be reached only by boat.[^1][^2][^7]

## The place

The lighthouse overlooks the site of the Battle of Valcour Island, which began on the morning of October 11, 1776, when an American fleet under Benedict Arnold lay in wait on the western side of the island for a British fleet heading south along the lake.[^2][^6] The Clinton County Historical Association calls the battle "the most important naval battle of the American Revolution."[^6] The light was there to show ships the way through the water between the island and the New York shore.[^2]

LighthouseFriends says the Light-House Board first proposed a light here in 1868.[^2] An undated extract from the Board's reports, kept in the station's clippings file, says a light was proposed at this point and that an estimate of the requisite appropriation was submitted.[^3] Congress approved $15,000 for the work on July 15, 1870.[^3][^2]

## Building the light

The site took until 1873 to clear.[^2][^3] The clippings extract describes "many vexatious delays in the search of title" to the site. It says the owner of the island refused to convey the deed unless he was guaranteed the right to build a wharf near the site and a right of way across the light-house grounds. The government assented to these conditions, and the use of the wharf was also secured to the United States. The purchase "was concluded in April."[^3] LighthouseFriends dates that April to 1873.[^2] The cellar was opened in solid rock, and half of it served as a cistern for water collected from the roof.[^2][^3]

The tower is of blue limestone under a red-shingled Mansard roof, with an octagonal tower about 35 feet (11 m) high.[^2] LighthouseFriends says it was built like Barber's Point light, which had been finished the year before farther south on the lake.[^2] A fifth-order Fresnel lens was placed 95 feet (29 m) above the water.[^7][^2] The clippings extract calls the station "one of the best on Lake Champlain." It also says supplies had to be landed in a small bay and carried up to the station, and that a roadway was badly needed.[^3]

The light was first shown when the station opened in the spring of 1874, under William C. Wait.[^1][^2] LighthouseFriends calls it the last manned light station completed on Lake Champlain.[^2] The Board's report for 1893 records only a survey of the reservation that year.[^4]

## The keepers

Wait kept the light until 1876. LighthouseFriends says he was removed that year, and William Herwerth was appointed in his place.[^2][^1]

LighthouseFriends calls Herwerth "Major William Herwerth." He had been shot in 1865 after nearly four years in the 91st New York Volunteer Infantry. The bullet stayed in his body, and he was unable to ride a horse or do heavy manual labor.[^2] A Burlington newspaper of 1902 later attributed his death to "diseases contracted" in the war.[^9] His illness worsened, and his wife Mary took on much of the work of tending the light. Herwerth died at the lighthouse on February 17, 1881.[^2] The Plattsburgh Republican, as LighthouseFriends quotes it, reported that the widow "has recently been appointed to the same office."[^2] The archive lists Mary Jane Herwerth as principal keeper from 1881 to 1902.[^1]

In November 1902 the Burlington Weekly Free Press reported that Mrs. Herwerth, "keeper of Valcour light house," had been seriously ill and was staying with her daughter.[^9]

The archive's roster then lists a short run of principal keepers. Minnie D. Herwerth held the post in 1903. William A. Conn kept it until 1905, and Mary C. Conn held it for 1905 alone. Andrew Nelson kept the light from 1905 to 1910, James B. Hawkins from 1910 to 1919, Fred Lemieux from 1919 to 1921, and Thomas Pickup from 1921 to 1929. Lester B. Preece is the last name on the roster, for 1929.[^1] The sources do not say how the Herwerths and the Conns were related, if at all.

The Lighthouse Service Bulletin's list of efficiency flags, district by district, for the year 1925 names Thomas Pickup as keeper of the Bluff Point station in the third district.[^5]

## After the keepers

In 1930 a steel skeletal tower with an automatic light "was erected to replace the manned lighthouse," according to LighthouseFriends.[^2] Rowlett's directory describes the replacement as an approximately 80-foot (24 m) square skeletal tower, painted black.[^7] The station's thirteen acres were then sold to a private owner.[^2]

A Massachusetts dentist, Dr. Raboff, bought the light station and its acres in 1954 as a summer residence.[^6][^2] In the 1980s he offered it to the State of New York, which already held the rest of Valcour Island within the Adirondack Park.[^2] The Clinton County Historical Association says "the state would purchase the land for $40,000 and own the lighthouse," while the association maintained the structure.[^6] LighthouseFriends says the state paid $40,000 for a parcel of about one and a half acres.[^2]

The National Park Service's NPGallery record for the station gives a publication date of 26 August 1993.[^8] Rowlett records a state grant for interior repairs in 2001, and the light returned to the lantern room on November 16, 2004.[^7] LighthouseFriends says a public ceremony celebrating the relighting was held on June 11, 2005.[^2] A restoration followed. LighthouseFriends says a two-year restoration began in August 2014, funded in part by $170,000 from the State of New York.[^2] Rowlett dates the work from September 2014 and says it was completed in the summer of 2016.[^7]

Ownership is described differently in each source. The Clinton County Historical Association says the island "is owned by New York State."[^6] LighthouseFriends says the lighthouse is owned by New York State Parks and managed by the association.[^2] Rowlett names the Adirondack Park Agency as owner, with the association as site manager.[^7]

## Today

The lighthouse is reached only by boat.[^7] The Clinton County Historical Association runs guided tours on Sundays in July and August, from 1 to 3 p.m. It also offers transport to the island on the first Sunday of July and August, for a fee.[^6] LighthouseFriends notes that visitors on a tour still need their own transport to the island.[^2] Rowlett lists the tower as open on Sunday afternoons from July 1 to Labor Day.[^7] Valcour Island is state land inside the Adirondack Park, and the association asks visitors to "Pack it in, pack it out."[^6]
