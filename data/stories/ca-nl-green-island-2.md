Green Island Light sits on an island off the southwest tip of the Burin Peninsula and marks the eastern way into Fortune Bay.[^1][^2] The island lies roughly halfway to Île Miquelon.[^1]

Work on a light and fog alarm station began in 1907.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the fog alarm began operating in 1908, giving a three-second blast every ninety seconds, and that the light itself was not activated until 1909.[^1] The first buildings included an engine house, a storehouse, a keeper's dwelling, and a circular iron lighthouse on the island's high point.[^1]

LighthouseFriends says a square, pyramidal tower with a red lantern began serving as the light in 1955, and that a square skeleton tower with a red and white daymark was erected in 1993.[^1] The Coast Guard's list gives the present light as an isophase white light with a ten-second period, a focal height of 45.4 m and a nominal range of 15 nautical miles, from a 6.7 m square skeleton tower.[^2]

LighthouseFriends names one keeper, Robert F. Forsey, who is listed on the station from 1908 to at least 1912.[^1]

In 2010 keepers pulled a local mariner and his mother from the water after their small boat was caught in sudden bad weather on a trip from Fortune. LighthouseFriends says the pair would likely have drowned without the keepers, and that the keepers hosted them for five days until the weather cleared.[^1]

A ferry captain told the senators that a call to the keepers showed the winds at the island had risen to forty-five knots, and he turned his ferry back.[^1]

The Coast Guard had announced in 2009 that it would de-staff lighthouses gradually through attrition. A Senate committee toured several of Newfoundland's lighthouses in the first week of November 2010, and LighthouseFriends says the minister announced the cancellation of the plan in early March 2011.[^1]

The lighthouse is owned by the Canadian Coast Guard. Its grounds are open, and the tower is closed.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the island is best seen from a boat or from the air, and that a distant view is available from a pullout just north of Port May.[^1]
