Pointe de la Prairie Lighthouse, known locally as "le Block", stands on a shoal off the west end of Île aux Coudres, in the St. Lawrence opposite Baie-Saint-Paul in Charlevoix, Quebec.[^1][^2][^4] It is a concrete pillar light, and the site is closed to visitors.[^2][^6]

## The place

The light marks La Grande Batture, a shoal west of the point, and shows ships the channel to take.[^5][^1] The Quebec heritage record says the navigable channel narrows further upstream of the island, and that the most dangerous obstacles can be seen only at the last minute, when a ship is already well into the river.[^1] (Translated from the French by us.)

## Building the light

The first light on the shoal was built in 1931.[^2][^3] Rochette's guide text says work began in June 1930 and was finished in July 1931. It showed a white light every five seconds and a compressed-air fog horn that sounded a 2.5-second blast every thirty seconds.[^4] In April 1934 the horn was changed to a 3-second blast followed by 12 seconds of silence.[^4] LighthouseFriends describes a white light on an eight-sided concrete base at the north edge of Prairie Shoal, with a fog alarm on the lowest floor, keepers' quarters on the next and a lantern room at the top.[^2]

The present pillar is dated 1971 by the Quebec record, which describes an inverted-funnel-shaped concrete pillar carrying a circular platform and a four-level tower of three concrete sections, with a helicopter pad above the lantern. The engineer Gérard Laroche designed it, and Walsh-Canadian Construction Co. Ltd. built it.[^1] LighthouseFriends and the 2012 Le Charlevoisien article give 1972 for the rebuilding, and Rochette's text says the old light was demolished in 1972.[^2][^3][^4] The sources disagree on the year.

## The keepers

The 1931 light had keepers. LighthouseFriends names the first as J. Pedneault, who served from 1931 to at least 1937.[^2] Rochette's text says keepers lived at the old light until 1971, running its fog horn and lights.[^4] The Quebec record says the present tower was designed to hold keepers' flats, but that these were probably never built as planned. It was automated from the start, in 1971.[^1] No keeper was ever assigned to the present tower.[^2]

## Today

The Coast Guard list gives the present light as a white flash every 2.5 seconds from a red and white cylindrical tower. Its focal height is 15.6 m (51 ft), its range 16 nautical miles, and it operates year round.[^5] LighthouseFriends says the base, which was beginning to crumble, was reinforced with shotcrete in 2004.[^2] Le Charlevoisien reports a renovation in 2003.[^3]

The site is not open to the public. A tourism listing says the light can be seen from the ferry between Saint-Joseph-de-la-Rive and the island, and that the best closer view is from Chemin des Prairies.[^6]

In 2012 the MRC de Charlevoix said it wanted to see the light conserved, if Parks Canada, which the article says owns the lights, chose to give it up.[^3] LighthouseFriends says the Canadian Coast Guard owns the light.[^2] The sources disagree on who owns it.
