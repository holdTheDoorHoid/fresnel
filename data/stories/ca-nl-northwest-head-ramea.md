Northwest Head Light stands on the southernmost point of Northwest Island, in the Ramea Islands off Newfoundland's southwest coast, roughly 20 km from Burgeo.[^1] It was built in 1902 to serve the local fishermen.[^1] It is still a staffed light.[^5][^6] Visitors can walk the grounds, but the tower is closed.[^1]

## The place

The Ramea group has two large islands, Northwest and Great, with a scatter of islets, rocks and shoals lying to the south.[^1] Northwest Island is the only one with a settlement today.[^1]

The first recorded settlers reached the islands in 1822. By 1836 Ramea had twenty-nine residents, and most of them made their living from fishing.[^1]

In 1871 George Penny and his brother John set up a fishing business at Ramea. George Penny also ran a small shipyard, where boats were built for the fleet.[^1]

The Coast Guard list shows other lights around Ramea as well, among them the Ramea Light on Browns Rock, east of Great Island, and the Ship Cove Wharf Light in Ramea harbour.[^2]

## Building the light

The 1902 station had a cylindrical iron tower, painted in red and white spirals. A covered way connected the tower to a dwelling that was white with a flat roof. Fifteen metres south of the dwelling was a white outbuilding with a black roof.[^1]

The light was an occulting white light at a height of 38 m.[^1] Lighthouse Friends describes its rhythm as alternating periods of light and darkness, each about a second and a half long.[^1] Supplies were landed at the foot of the rocky headland, where a derrick unloaded them, and a tramway ran from the landing to the station.[^1]

In the 1950s Air Marine Services put a radio beacon near the light.[^1] A diaphone foghorn followed in 1952. A keeper and an assistant were employed to look after the fog alarm, the radio equipment and the light.[^1] The lighthouse was converted to commercial electricity in 1970.[^1]

## The keepers

John Chaffey, originally from England, had lived at Great Jervois before he followed the Pennys to Ramea. He was taken on as the first keeper.[^1][^4] The heritage record lists him at Ramea as a shopkeeper and fisherman.[^4]

On 20 August 1902 a letter told George Rossiter of Ramea that he had been appointed assistant keeper, at $150 a year.[^1]

John's son Charles succeeded him. The heritage record says Charles took over when his father retired, sometime before 1920. The Town says it was about 1920, and the first date Lighthouse Friends gives for Charles is 1921. Lighthouse Friends lists him at the light until at least 1938.[^4][^5][^1]

The house built for Charles Chaffey is a Second Empire house in Ramea, which the heritage record describes as a good example of that style.[^4] Charles died in 1955, at the age of 78.[^4]

Lighthouse Friends' keeper list then names Louis Kendall, at least from 1945, and Vincent Moores, at least from 1949. It gives no further detail for either.[^1]

## Today

The Coast Guard list, dated 24 September 2026, gives the light as a long flash of white every six seconds, with a focal height of 38.1 m and a nominal range of 14 nautical miles. Its fog horn sounds for three seconds in every thirty.[^2]

Rowlett and the Town both describe a white flash every three seconds.[^3][^5] The sources disagree on the rhythm. The Coast Guard list is the most recent official entry, so the figures above are its.[^2]

The Coast Guard gives the tower as 7.3 m high. Rowlett gives 9 m for the cast-iron tower, without a date for the measurement.[^2][^3]

The Fisheries and Oceans brochure lists Northwest Head (Ramea) among the staffed lightstations.[^6] The Town says that resident keepers carry on that tradition today.[^5]

The Coast Guard owns the station.[^1] It is a working, staffed lightstation, and the grounds are open while the tower is closed.[^1][^3][^5] Lighthouse Friends says a distant view of the light is possible from the ferry from Burgeo, and that the crossing takes roughly an hour and twenty minutes.[^1] The Town gives the boardwalk trail that runs round the perimeter of Northwest Island as the access to the grounds.[^5] Do not land on the station, and do not enter or climb the tower.
