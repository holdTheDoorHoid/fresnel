Deer Point Light stands at the southern tip of Deer Island, New Brunswick, in Deer Island Point Park, just south of the ferry landing.[^1][^2] Deer Island lies in the mouth of Passamaquoddy Bay, close to the United States border.[^2] The light is a small round white tower about 6 m (20 ft) tall. LighthouseFriends and the Canadian Coast Guard list give a two-second red flash every twelve seconds,[^1][^3] but Rowlett's directory gives two seconds on and eight off, a ten-second cycle.[^2] The sources do not reconcile the two.

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the light. LighthouseFriends calls it a modern light, and says it does not qualify for the official list of lighthouses, because it never had a keeper or an enclosed lantern room.[^1]

Just offshore is the Old Sow, which LighthouseFriends calls the largest tidal whirlpool in the western hemisphere.[^1] Rowlett's directory says visitors come to see the whirlpool, which forms on rising tides just off the light.[^2] In his 1876 book, J. G. Lorimer wrote approvingly of the sound the Old Sow makes. He called it a "fearful noise", but also "a friend to boatmen on dark nights", because it gave a "timely warning" that the whirlpool was near.[^4]

No source here dates the light or says why it was built. The Lighthouse Directory gives its date as unknown.[^2]

The grounds are open, and the tower is closed to visitors.[^1][^2] The Lighthouse Directory notes that the site is reached by road, with parking.[^2]
