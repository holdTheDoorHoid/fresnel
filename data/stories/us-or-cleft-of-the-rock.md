Cleft of the Rock Light is a private lighthouse on the north spur of Cape Perpetua, on the Oregon coast about two miles (3.2 km) south of Yachats. Its tower is 34 feet (10 m) high according to CoastView and LighthouseFriends; the Lighthouse Directory gives 35 feet (11 m).[^1][^2][^3]

Captain James Cook named the cape in March 1778. LighthouseFriends says he sighted land on 6 March and first sighted the cape on 7 March, St. Perpetua's Day.[^2]

Jim Gibbs built the tower in 1976 as a replica of Fiddle Reef Light, which operated in Oak Bay near Victoria, British Columbia, from 1898 to 1958.[^1][^2] The tower has redwood siding, and its optic had been used by the Canadian Coast Guard at Solander Island.[^2] The stair railing came from the keepers' dwelling at Yaquina Head.[^2] Gibbs had been a Coast Guardsman and was one of the keepers at Tillamook Rock Light in the 1940s.[^5]

The light was not originally considered for a lighthouse designation, LighthouseFriends says, because ships on this stretch of coast pass far out to sea. It serves vessels running the coast between Coos Bay and Yaquina Bay.[^2] LighthouseFriends says it was made an official navigational aid in 1979. The Lighthouse Directory describes it as a privately maintained aid that the Coast Guard accepted, and says the headland "never had an official lighthouse."[^3]

Gibbs lived in the house the light is part of, and a private museum is in the same home.[^1] He died at home in Yachats on 30 April 2010, aged 88.[^5] The Lighthouse Directory and LighthouseFriends both give May 2010 for his death.[^3][^2]

The US Lighthouse Society's archive lists Gibbs, deceased, as the owner, and it lists the light as not open to the public.[^4] The Lighthouse Directory says his family owned the house and kept the light in service for several years after his death. The sources also disagree about the sale. The Directory says the property was listed for sale in 2016, sold in June 2017 for $489,900, and was back on the market in 2023 at $2.25 million.[^3] LighthouseFriends says it stayed in the Gibbs family for several years and was placed on the market for the first time in 2023, at $2.25 million.[^2] None of these sources names the buyer.

Three sources agree that the light is no longer an active aid to navigation. The Lighthouse Directory lists it as inactive since 2016,[^3] LighthouseFriends says it is no longer active,[^2] and the US Lighthouse Society record marks it as not an active aid.[^4]

Today the grounds and tower are closed to the public.[^1] The light can be seen from a pullout on U.S. 101 at milepost 166.[^2] LighthouseFriends advises photographing it only from the highway shoulder, with care, because the property is private.[^2]
