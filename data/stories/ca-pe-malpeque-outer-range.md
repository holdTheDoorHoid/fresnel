The Malpeque Outer Range Lights are a pair of range lights at Darnley Point, Prince Edward Island, that from 1889 marked the way into Malpeque Harbour with fixed red lights.[^1][^2][^3] The register's list of the front tower's heritage features includes a square, tapered tower, cedar-shingle cladding and white paint with red trim.[^3] Sources disagree on whether the red lights still show: one directory says the station has been inactive since 2018, while Lighthouse Friends says the red lights are still displayed.[^1][^4]

## The place

The entrance to Malpeque Harbour, which Lighthouse Friends calls Richmond Bay, lies between Fish (Billhook) Island and Cape Aylesbury.[^1] Mariners first steer by the Darnley Point range until its line crosses the Fish Island range, which then guides them into the harbour.[^1] In 1874 nine men interested in the harbour asked the Minister of Marine and Fisheries for a second light on Fish Island and two lights on Darnley Point, set so as to guide vessels in over the bar.[^1][^3]

## Building the light

The Darnley Point lights were not built until 1889.[^1] Each was a lantern on a 9.1 m (30 ft) mast with a shed at its foot, and the rear light stood 440 m (1,444 ft) south-west of the front.[^1][^3] In 1892 slatwork that had been used on the Brighton Beach light in Charlottetown was fixed to the masts to make the lights more visible in daylight.[^3]

Lighthouse Friends says the masts were unsteady, and in 1897 open-frame towers topped by enclosed lanterns replaced them, erected under the supervision of Milton Walsh, foreman of works for the Department's Prince Edward Island division.[^1][^2] The register gives the same year and supervisor.[^3] Lighthouse Friends says the open frames were later enclosed to become the present towers.[^1] Rowlett's directory dates the present towers to 1922, with a question mark.[^4]

The Chief Engineer's report for the year ended 30 June 1900 lists a $10 item for "moving back tower" at Darnley point, and gives no more detail.[^5] In the fiscal year 1916-17 the Chief Engineer's table lists "Moving range lights to new sites" at Darnley point, done by contract.[^7]

## The keepers

John D. Morrison was the first keeper, appointed to the range in 1889. Lighthouse Friends says Cornelius Morrison took over on John's death in 1890 and kept the lights until 1896.[^1][^3]

G. W. J. Wiggins kept the range from 1896 to 1913.[^1][^3] The register spells the name "Wiggans", but the Department's lightkeepers' list for the year ended 30 June 1905 prints "Wiggins, G. W. J" against the Darnley Point range.[^3][^6]

Cornelius Morrison kept the range again from 1913 until his death in 1935, according to Lighthouse Friends. The register gives only the 1913 start, for an unknown period.[^1][^3] Daniel Champion became keeper in 1935 on Cornelius Morrison's death and served until the lights were electrified in 1962. Lighthouse Friends says he then agreed to serve as their caretaker.[^1]

## After the keepers

The Prince Edward Island Historic Places Protection Act registered the front light on 12 July 2012.[^1][^2] The Canadian Coast Guard now uses the name Malpeque Outer Range Lights in its official list of lights.[^1]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the lights.[^1] The site is at the end of Lighthouse Road, which is private, and the site and towers are closed.[^1][^2] The register says the front light is not open to visitors.[^3] Rowlett's directory says the lighthouse can be viewed from the beach, and that a group of private individuals has applied for ownership of the lighthouse and that meanwhile it is endangered by poor maintenance.[^4]
