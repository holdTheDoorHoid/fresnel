Sheboygan Breakwater Light stands at the outer end of the harbour breakwater at Sheboygan, Wisconsin, on Lake Michigan.[^10] A red lantern was first shown from the breakwater on 24 October 1900,[^1] and the steel tower standing there now was moved to the site from the old north pier in 1915.[^2][^5]

## The place

The Sheboygan River reaches the lake past a sandbar at its mouth. LighthouseFriends says the bar kept river commerce small until harbour improvements began in 1852, and that the federal government took over the work in 1866.[^10] LighthouseFriends reports that a survey made in 1898 found the northeast seas between the piers so heavy that they broke vessels from their moorings, and it concluded that "there seems to be but one satisfactory method of ameliorating this condition."[^10]

The works that followed removed the north pier and built a new north breakwater.[^2] The Commissioner of Lighthouses later explained that the breakwater was built "to form a stilling basin at the harbor entrance," and that the lights marking the entrance therefore had to be rearranged.[^2]

## Building the light

The first light on the breakwater was a beacon, not a tower. On 24 October 1900 a fixed red lens lantern was set on a wooden post at the south-east end of the breakwater, and a V-shaped timber protection was built on its exposed side.[^1]

LighthouseFriends says that in 1902 an enclosed tower went up, which "resembled an inverted funnel topped by a lantern room," and that a steel tower was raised at the outer end of the north pier in 1904.[^10] The Bulletin's list of changes for 1912 names the breakwater light among the lights changed to acetylene.[^3]

In 1915 the north pier was removed for the new works. The Lighthouse Service Bulletin describes "the removal of the entire cylindrical steel tower from the old north pier and its replacement on a concrete foundation on the new breakwater." A "scow 35 by 120 feet" was lashed alongside the pier, the tower was jacked up on timber ways, and it was towed out to the new site, a job that took three days.[^5] LighthouseFriends gives 19 August 1915 as the day it was set down.[^10]

The Commissioner's description of 1917 gives the details. The light stands on a timber crib built by the United States Engineers, capped with a concrete block. The tower is "of riveted steel plates and angles, circular in plan, conical in shape," and it carries a fourth-order helical-bar lantern.[^2] The lens is a fifth-order lens "showing a flashing red light of 160 candles every 4 seconds." Its focal plane is about 55 feet (17 m) above the lake, and the light was visible ten miles in clear weather. The burner used compressed acetylene gas.[^2]

"The entire system was in commission November 24, 1916," the Commissioner's report says, after the north pier and the new breakwater had been completed and the lights rearranged.[^2] The 1916 Bulletin described a planned fog signal: an electrically driven compressor in a small power house on the shore end of the breakwater, with the air piped out to a siren in the light tower.[^5] By 1917 the siren was a standard six-inch automatic type, giving "a blast of 3 seconds duration followed by a silent interval of 27 seconds."[^2][^6] In 1918 the Bulletin lists the light among those changed from acetylene to electric incandescent.[^7]

## The keepers

The names below come from LighthouseFriends' keeper lists and from the Bulletin's index, and the sources say where they differ.[^10][^8]

In 1911 a brick dwelling was built "for the keepers of the Sheboygan lights," which LighthouseFriends says had quarters for a keeper and two assistants.[^10]

The sources disagree about Amos Carpenter's years here. LighthouseFriends says that he "was in charge of the pierhead lighthouse from 1866 to 1903," but its list of breakwater keepers gives him the years 1886 to 1903. The same list starts in 1873, before the breakwater light was first shown in 1900.[^10] LighthouseFriends also recounts a night in 1892 in the pierhead tower. As it quotes the *Milwaukee Journal*, Carpenter "spent New Year's night in the lighthouse as is his custom during storms," and driven snow and sleet froze the door shut, so that he was not released until after twenty-four hours.[^10]

Gottfried M. S. Hansen, who is "G. M. S. Hansen" in the Bulletin, kept the light from 1903 to 1914, by LighthouseFriends' list.[^10] The Bulletin's index for 1912 to 1917 lists a rescue by "Hansen, G.M. S., keeper Sheboygan Breakwater."[^8] The Bulletin's notes for August 1914 record that on 17 August "G. M. S. Hansen, keeper of the" breakwater and pierhead lights rescued two men from a capsized boat.[^4] In the scanned text the two columns run together, but the page image reads clearly, and the index entry gives the same keeper and rescue.[^4][^8]

Ingvald Olsen followed, keeping the light from 1914 to 1930.[^10] In 1919, as LighthouseFriends reports from the local newspaper, local miscreants were warned against inscribing obscene words on the lighthouse with their jackknives. Keeper Ingvald Olsen reported that the practice had become common and warned that the "vicious practice must cease."[^10] In February 1924 Olsen and his helper and son-in-law, Arderll E. Pierce, were walking the shore after servicing the light when they heard boys calling for help on the thin ice. They tied a rope to a sled, pushed it across the ice, and pulled the soaked boy back to shore.[^10]

LighthouseFriends' list continues with Robert E. Paul as first assistant from 1907 to 1930, Jerome M. Robinson as head keeper from 1930 to 1941, Charles A. Linsmeier as first assistant from 1934 to 1941, and Domenic Carollo as head keeper from 1946 to 1947.[^10] The Bulletin's index also names a Sheboygan pierhead keeper, Robert H. Paul, and an assistant, Paul G. Pitscher, in its 1912 to 1917 entries. They are not linked to the LighthouseFriends names here, because the initials and spellings differ.[^8]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

On the morning of 9 April 1894 the lumber schooner *Lottie Cooper* was wrecked near the Life-Saving Service station at Sheboygan. The Service's report says she "reached the vicinity of Sheboygan shortly after midnight," with "a crew of five men." The tug did not answer her signals, and her master, judging the sea too heavy to enter port unassisted, anchored. At daylight she dragged toward the breakers and capsized. "The crew fortunately got upon a portion of the deckload of lumber," but "one of her crew, named Edward Olsen," tried to reach the shore on a raft of lumber and drowned.[^9] The station's lookout had seen her "off the piers" a little after one in the morning, and had reported "a vessel was outside showing a torch for a tug."[^9] The wreck was off the piers, before the breakwater light was first shown in 1900.[^9][^10]

LighthouseFriends counts seven crew, six of whom clung to the rigging and were rescued, and it spells the drowned man's name Olson.[^10]

LighthouseFriends says divers found the wreck in 1990, and that "the remains of the ship were salvaged in September 1992."[^10]

The keepers' rescues are described above: the 1914 rescue by Hansen, and the 1924 rescue on the ice by Olsen and Pierce.[^4][^10]

## After the keepers

In 1942 a Class D marker radiobeacon was established at Sheboygan, as an additional aid for mariners, LighthouseFriends says.[^10] At some point the lantern room was removed from the breakwater tower. LighthouseFriends reports that the light now shows "a flashing white light every four seconds," not the red light the Commissioner described in 1917, and that the fog signal still gives a three-second blast every thirty seconds when needed. "The top of the tower is now also home to a NOAA weather station."[^10]

In 2025 the light was declared excess to Coast Guard needs. A General Services Administration notice, as the *Sheboygan Sun* printed it, says: "The Sheboygan Breakwater Light (Property) has been determined to be excess to the needs of the United States Department of Homeland Security, United States Coast Guard (USCG)."[^12] Under the National Historic Lighthouse Preservation Act, it was offered at no cost to eligible entities, with letters of interest due "within 60 days from the date of this notice: September 1, 2025."[^12] If no public body or non-profit took it, the notice said, "the Property will be sold if it is not transferred to a public body or non-profit organization."[^12]

The City of Sheboygan moved to apply. A council resolution dated 23 February 2026 is titled "A RESOLUTION authorizing the appropriate City officials to submit an application to purchase the Sheboygan Breakwater Lighthouse." The resolution describes the property, which "occupies approximately 2,300 square feet," under GSA control number 1-U-WI-630.[^11]

The light is not on the National Register. "Although not yet listed, the lighthouse is eligible to be added to the National Register of Historic Places," a 2025 radio report says.[^13]

## Today

At the time of the 2025 notice the Coast Guard still owned the light and had declared it excess to its needs.[^12] LighthouseFriends says the grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^10] The pier is not part of the offer. "The pier it sits upon is not part of the deal," the radio report says, and the notice requires an eligible applicant to obtain a lease from the Army Corps of Engineers district office to use the site.[^13][^12] No transfer appears in the sources cited for this story.
