Summerside Back Range Light is a shingled, square-tapered tower on Glover's Shore Road, on the east side of Summerside, Prince Edward Island. It is 20.1 m (66 ft) tall, and it is the rear half of a pair of range lights: a ship lines the two up to find the channel into the harbour.[^1][^2][^3]

## The place

Summerside began as a settlement on Bedeque Bay. The Lighthouse Friends history says the name came from an inn, and that "the town was incorporated under that name in 1877."[^1] It grew into "a prominent shipbuilding center," as the same history puts it.[^1]

Large ferry steamers ran between Summerside and the mainland. Lighthouse Friends says the route was to New Brunswick, and the PEI Lighthouse Society says Nova Scotia.[^1][^2] Lighthouse Friends says the ferries "were finding it hard to locate the entrance to the harbour at night."[^1] The Register names the mail and passenger steamer *Northumberland* among the many vessels that used the harbour.[^3] Before the rear range there was a light on the railway wharf. Patrick Power kept a light on Queen's Wharf from 1854 until 1859, and a tower of about 14 m (45 ft) on Ellis Point was recommended in 1876 to range with the wharf light.[^1] The Indian Head Light, established in 1881, guided ships to the harbour entrance.[^1]

## Building the light

The rear range light was finished in 1895. Lighthouse Friends says it "was built at a cost of $225 by Milton Walsh," and that it was lit for the first time on 5 September 1895.[^1][^2] The tower was an open frame, boarded on its seaward face and painted white as a day mark, and it "stood 17 metres (56 feet) high."[^1][^2] The PEI Lighthouse Society says it stood on the farm of John and George Stavert, about a mile southeast of the railway wharf light.[^2]

The Department's annual report for 1903 gives a keeper's appointment date for the "Summerside Harbour Range Back Light" as "Sept, 8, 1895," three days after the lighting date the other sources give.[^5]

The tower has changed several times. It was enclosed in 1904, and the Canadian Register of Historic Places says it has "survived in the enclosed form adopted for it in 1904."[^3] The Lighthouse Friends history says the site was bought and the tower relocated in 1914, for $587.37. The PEI Lighthouse Society says the range was moved 15 m (50 ft) south in 1913.[^1][^2] The height of the light was raised in 1916, when the Department spent $212.63 on an addition to the tower.[^1][^7]

## The keepers

The Lighthouse Friends list for the rear light runs: "George Stevart (1895 – 1909)," then "George W. Bell (1909 – 1912)," then "Fred Muttart (1912 – 1936)," then "Harry H. Heckbert (1936 – 1952)."[^1] The Department's keeper list for 1904 includes a "Stavart, Geo."[^6] The spelling of the farm family's name varies across sources (Staver, Stavert and Stavart all appear).[^1][^2][^6]

Muttart's name appears in the Department's table for 1913 to 1914, against the "Summerside (back)" light.[^8] Lighthouse Friends says he kept the light until 1936, when he "was dismissed for inattention to duty."[^1] Heckbert then looked after the light "until it was automated in 1952," according to the same history.[^1]

The two sources disagree on the later keepers. The PEI Lighthouse Society's data sheet lists "1935-1961 - Albert E. Ferguson" as keeper of the back light.[^2] Lighthouse Friends has Ferguson at the front light, which it says he kept until 1961, when "the services of Albert Ferguson, its last keeper, were discontinued."[^1]

## After the keepers

The Lighthouse Friends history dates automation to 1952. The PEI Lighthouse Society's data sheet says the light was "De-staffed in 1961."[^1][^2] The two dates are both given here.

The front light changed too. The Register says the front range "was replaced by a metal tower with a light in 1961."[^3]

The City of Summerside recognized the back range light as a place of historic significance on 4 December 2007.[^3] Lighthouse Friends says the city "later petitioned for ownership of the structure" under the Parks Canada heritage lighthouse programme.[^1]

## Today

The light belongs to the federal government. Lighthouse Friends says it is "owned by the Canadian Coast Guard,"[^1] and the PEI Lighthouse Society says it is "owned by the Government of Canada."[^2] The Coast Guard list of lights gives a fixed green light with a focal height of 24.8 m and a range of 16 nautical miles. The tower is "White pyramid tower with red upper portion and red vertical stripe," 20.1 m tall.[^4]

The grounds and tower are closed to visitors, according to Lighthouse Friends.[^1] The PEI Lighthouse Society's directions say that after turning onto Glover's Shore Road "you will see it a short distance away on your right."[^2] Visitors should not go onto the grounds or try to climb the tower.
