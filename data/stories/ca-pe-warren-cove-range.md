The Warren Cove Range Lights are a pair of white wooden towers on the west side of Charlottetown Harbour, Prince Edward Island, built in 1907 to guide ships to the Charlottetown wharves.[^2][^3][^4]

## The place

Ships bound for the Charlottetown wharves followed the Brighton Beach range until they could line up the Warren Farm pair on their port beam, then kept that back range astern to reach the wharves.[^2] Historic Places says the Warren Farm lights were there "to assist mariners navigate through Blockhouse Point and Sea Trout Point."[^3]

## Building the light

The Department of Marine and Fisheries' report for the nine months ending 31 March 1907 says that range lights "have been established" on Warren farm, on the western side of Charlottetown harbour.[^1] Each tower was 9.1 m (30 ft) from its base to the top of the lantern ventilator. The towers were enclosed and wooden, square in plan with sloping sides, and painted white. Each showed a fixed red light from a seventh-order dioptric lens, visible 2 miles in the line of range.[^1] The front light stood 12 m (39 ft) above high water. The back tower stood 348 m (1,143 ft) from it, with its light 17 m (57 ft) above high water.[^1] The work was done by day labour under the Charlottetown agency.[^1]

Canada's Historic Places record says the front light was built in 1907 by M. Walsh to a "Department of Marine and Transport" design.[^3] The Lighthouse Society says the Department of Marine and Fisheries designed both towers, and that the front light was framed at the Government Wharf warehouse in Charlottetown.[^5][^4]

The reports disagree about how far the work had got. The Department's report for the fiscal year ending 31 March 1908 says the range lights that the previous year's report had described as "under construction" were completed in that year, and gives the cost complete as $326.64.[^6] The report for the nine months to 31 March 1907 had said they were already established.[^1]

The 1908 list gives both lights a red light from a seventh-order lens.[^7] Later sources describe yellow lights, and none of the sources read dates the change.[^4][^5][^8]

The heights disagree too. The 1907 report gives 9.1 m (30 ft) for each tower. The Lighthouse Society gives 11.2 m (37 ft) for the front and 7.7 m (25 ft 2 in) for the back. Rowlett gives 7.5 m (25 ft) for both.[^1][^4][^5][^8] Focal heights differ as well: the Society gives 17.3 m and 21.4 m, and Rowlett gives 17 m (56 ft) and 23 m (76 ft).[^4][^5][^8]

## The keepers

Alexander S. McNeil was the first keeper.[^2] A Canadian keepers' statement for the year ending 31 March 1910 lists him at the Block House light from 25 March 1901, and at the Warren Farm range from 16 May 1907, at a salary of $100.[^9] Lighthouse Friends says he took the range on as an added duty to the Blockhouse light he had kept since 1901.[^2]

C. E. H. Newson followed, from 1912 to 1921 in the accounts of Lighthouse Friends and the Lighthouse Society.[^2][^4] A keepers' statement for the year ending 31 March 1914 lists "Warren farm range" against his name.[^10]

Lighthouse Friends alone gives end dates for the later keepers: J. L. Doiron (1921 to 1923), James H. Feehan (1923 to 1931), Herbert Grovette (1931 to 1936), Maurice F. White (1936 to 1956) and Harold MacKinnon (1956 to 1957).[^2] Historic Places says only that Doiron began in 1921, with no end date given.[^3]

## After the keepers

Historic Places says the front light was renamed Warren Cove Front Range Light in the early 1960s, and that the light has been moved several times.[^3] Lighthouse Friends gives the change as "around the 1960s".[^2] In 2008 a building investigation found severe rot in the shingles and walls of the front light. It was covered with plywood and then Nailite siding, and its flared cornice was made angled.[^4][^3] The rear light was also repaired and sided with Nailite in 2008, but kept its original colours.[^5]

## Today

Rowlett lists both towers as active.[^8] The Canadian Coast Guard's list of lights carries both.[^12] Ownership is given two ways. Lighthouse Friends says the Canadian Coast Guard owns the lights.[^2][^11] The Lighthouse Society says the Government of Canada owns both lights, and that a group has formed to seek ownership through the Parks Canada Heritage Lighthouse Program.[^4][^5]

The front light stands on the grounds of the Fort Amherst–Port La Joye National Historic Site, at the edge of a bluff.[^2][^4] The grounds are open and the towers are closed.[^2][^5] Visitors park at the national historic site's interpretive centre. From there the front light is a walk past the fort to the bluff, and the rear light can be seen from the road in.[^4][^5]
