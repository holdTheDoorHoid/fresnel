Windmill Point Light is an octagonal tower of blue limestone on a point on the east shore of northern Lake Champlain, in Alburgh, Grand Isle County, Vermont, about two miles south of the Canadian border.[^1][^2] Congress paid for it, and it was built in 1858.[^1] Its light moved to a steel tower in 1931 and came back to the stone in 2002.[^1][^3] The tower now stands on private land and is closed to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]

## The place

The name goes back to a stone windmill that French settlers put up there. LighthouseFriends dates that settlement to the 1740s.[^1]

The point was a navigation problem before it was a light. In 1838 Lieutenant Charles T. Platt of the U.S. Navy asked for lights at Windmill Point and Crown Point. He wrote that navigation at the points "is often exposed to serious disasters from hidden rocks and the serpentine course of the channel."[^1] Until then, he wrote, temporary lights had been kept at the points for eight years, and the steamboat masters paid for them.[^1] Platt also described a strong wind "accompanied with thick hazy atmosphere," at a time when, he wrote, the temporary lights were often insufficient.[^1]

The point had other business too. In 1860 a ferry from Windmill Point to Champlain, New York, was among the matters listed for the Vermont legislature.[^4] In 1861 the *Burlington Free Press* reported that the appointment of the deputy collector at Windmill Point had been renewed.[^5] In 1911 a customs officer was transferred from Burlington to be in charge there.[^6]

## Building the light

LighthouseFriends says a private light was established at the point in 1830.[^1] The USLHS archive gives 1830 as the year the station was established.[^2] No action followed Platt's request until 1853, when William D. Fraser of the Corps of Engineers asked pilots and others interested in navigation on the lake about the need for more lights.[^1] Congress provided $8,000 for a lighthouse at Windmill Point on 3 August 1854.[^1]

After title to the land was cleared in May 1857, the contractors Ellis and O'Neil of Malone and Champlain, New York, built an octagonal tower of rough-hewn blue limestone from the Ira Hill quarry on Isle La Motte.[^1] LighthouseFriends gives its height as 44 feet (13.4 m). The USLHS archive gives 40 feet (12.2 m), from the base to the centre of the lantern.[^1][^2]

The keeper's house is joined to the tower by a passageway. The archive and Rowlett's directory call the house granite, while LighthouseFriends says it is rubblestone.[^1][^2][^3]

The tower held a sixth-order Fresnel lens, which the archive dates to 1858.[^2] The archive gives the light's height above mean high water as 52 feet (15.8 m), and LighthouseFriends gives the focal plane as fifty-two feet.[^1][^2] LighthouseFriends says the tower was built in 1858 and lit with the opening of navigation in 1859.[^1]

The station was kept up after it opened. The Light-House Board's report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1891 records that a summer kitchen was built at Windmill Point, the old barn was removed, and a new barn and a line fence were put up.[^7] In the fiscal year ended 30 June 1893 the board surveyed the reservation and made minor repairs.[^8] In the 1903 season the government lighthouse boat *Nettle* worked the lake, and a fence was built at Windmill Point and repairs were made to the buildings.[^9]

## The keepers

Clarinda Mott was the first keeper, from 1859 to 1862.[^2] LighthouseFriends says she was born in Alburgh, the seventh of nine children, and was fifty-five when she took charge. It calls her the only female lighthouse keeper to have served in Vermont.[^1] Henry Brayton followed from 1862 to 1867, and Herbert C. Phelps kept the light for twenty-two years, from 1867 to 1889.[^2] The sources give only names and years for those two.

John D. Bowman served from 1889 to 1907.[^2] Edward W. Hill was keeper from 1908 to 1924.[^2] LighthouseFriends says Hill was born at the Isle La Motte light, where his father Wilbur Hill was keeper.[^1] When Wilbur S. Hill died at Isle La Motte in March 1919, the *Burlington Weekly Free Press* said that for more than 47 years he had been the United States lighthouse keeper there, and that he had been placed on the federal retirement list on January 31. Among his children it named Edward W. Hill, light-housekeeper of Windmill Point.[^10]

Samuel L. Fuller held the post in 1924, and Leonard C. Fuller served from 1924 to 1931.[^2] LighthouseFriends calls Leonard Fuller the last keeper. He was transferred to New London Ledge Light when the Windmill Point light was automated.[^1]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

During the Revolutionary War the *radeau* (raft) *Thunderer* sank off the point in 1777 while carrying wounded British soldiers from the defeat at Saratoga, according to LighthouseFriends.[^1]

## After the keepers

In August 1931 the light was replaced by an acetylene light displayed from a black steel skeletal tower that had been brought from Crown Point, New York, and the stone tower went dark.[^1] Noah J. Belair was hired as a lamplighter for the newly automated lights at Windmill Point and Rouses Point.[^1] The property, minus a half-acre around the steel tower, was transferred to the U.S. Customs Service for use against rum-runners on Lake Champlain during Prohibition.[^1]

LighthouseFriends says the property was later sold to private owners, and dates two private purchases to 1949 and 1963.[^1] The Coast Guard kept the steel tower when it sold the light.[^1]

In 2001 the idea of putting the light back in the stone tower came up, LighthouseFriends says. Moving the light would cost a few thousand dollars, against tens of thousands to refurbish or replace the steel tower.[^1] On 7 August 2002, National Lighthouse Day, the light returned to the stone tower after more than seventy years.[^1][^3] LighthouseFriends calls it the first relighting on Lake Champlain.[^1][^3]

On 15 November 2013 a Coast Guard contractor arrived to remove the steel tower. LighthouseFriends gives its height as 66 feet (20.1 m). Part of it was salvaged and rebuilt on the property at a shorter height.[^1] On 16 October 2018 the Coast Guard installed an LED beacon in the lantern room.[^1]

## Today

The stone tower is a private residence at the end of Windmill Point Road, off US 2, opposite Rouses Point, New York.[^3] Rowlett's directory says the site and tower are closed.[^3] The USLHS archive lists the owner as private and the station as not open to the public.[^2] LighthouseFriends says the grounds, dwelling and tower are closed, and that visitors were discouraged from driving to the light while the right-of-way was contested in court.[^1] The USLHS archive lists the light as an active aid to navigation.[^2]
