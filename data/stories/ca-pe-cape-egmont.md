Cape Egmont Lighthouse is a square wooden tower on the high cliffs of the southwest shore of Prince Edward Island, facing Northumberland Strait.[^2][^3] The heritage record for the lighthouse calls it the only light along a 46-mile (74 km) stretch of coast.[^1]

## The place

Between the West Point and Seacow Head lighthouses lay a 74 km (46 mile) stretch of coast, and the heritage record says Cape Egmont was the only light along it.[^1] The Canadian Register of Historic Places adds that the tower still serves regional commercial shipping and the local lobster fishery, and that it is a landmark because of its siting on high cliffs.[^2]

## Building the light

Money for the lighthouse was appropriated in 1881, but building was held up until the land was secured. The contract went to Laurent Perry for $1,695, and construction began in 1883 to a Department of Marine and Fisheries design shared with the lighthouses at Cape Bear and Wood Islands.[^1] The light went into operation in September 1884, with Bruno Perry as first keeper.[^1] Lighthouse Friends, a modern site, reports an annual salary of $200 and a fixed red light.[^3]

The result is a three-storey square tapered wooden tower under an octagonal lantern, clad in shingles, with a pedimented doorway.[^2] The Department of Marine's report for 1906-07 records a change of apparatus: a single flashing fourth-order light replaced the fixed red catoptric light.[^4] The heritage record and Lighthouse Friends date the change to 1906.[^1][^3] A concrete foundation replaced the original rubble stone one in 1946.[^1]

## The keepers

The provincial heritage record names Bruno Perry (from 1884), F. C. Arsenault (1900 to 1902), Joseph Gallant (1902 to 1912), E. J. Arsenault (1912 to 1922) and Jean Wilfred "Jack Ferdinand" Gallant (1922 to 1950).[^1] The Department of Marine's keepers' table for 1910 shows Jos. J. D. Gallant at Cape Egmont, appointed 21 October 1902, at a salary of $285.[^5] Lighthouse Friends adds that Jean Wilfred Gallant and his wife, Marie Leonie (Richard), were in charge of the light from 1922 to 1950, and that Edward J. Arsenault was the last keeper, from 1950 to 1958.[^3] It spells the 1900 keeper Arseneault and gives Emmanuel as the first name of the 1912 keeper.[^3]

## After the keepers

The keeper's dwelling and the 1883 oil shed were removed in 1958.[^1] Lighthouse Friends says the light was electrified that year and the keeper became a caretaker on a much smaller salary.[^3] Because of severe erosion of the cliff, the lighthouse was moved inland in April 2000.[^1]

## Today

The Federal Heritage Buildings Review Office has a statement on the tower in the Canadian Register,[^2] and Lighthouse Friends reports its recognition as a heritage place under the provincial Heritage Places Protection Act on 3 October 2012.[^3] Lighthouse Friends says it is owned by the Canadian Coast Guard, with the grounds open and the tower closed.[^3]
