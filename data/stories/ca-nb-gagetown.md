Gagetown Lighthouse stands on the west bank of the Saint John River, just above the point where the main river makes an almost right-angled turn past the village of Gagetown, New Brunswick.[^1][^5] The first light here went up in 1895.[^1] Its tower was carried off by the spring freshet in 1901, and an ice jam destroyed the original tower in 1934.[^1][^6] The Canadian Coast Guard owns the present tower.[^1][^6]

## The place

LighthouseFriends says the village was called Grimrose until it was renamed for the British general Sir Thomas Gage, who had been granted land in New Brunswick.[^1] Acadians lived there. In the Saint John River campaign of 1758 and 1759, Colonel Robert Monckton's force of 1,150 soldiers forced fifty Acadian families out of Grimrose and burned every building in the village, according to LighthouseFriends.[^1]

The bend that the light marks was known as "No Man's Friend".[^1] Sailing vessels had to tack hard to get round it in the narrow river.[^1] A beacon at No Man's Friend was one of six placed along the river in 1869. LighthouseFriends says that by 1893 the tower there had decayed badly, and the decision was made to replace it with a light on the opposite shore, which became the 1895 Gagetown light.[^1]

In the late 1800s and early 1900s, LighthouseFriends says, steamboats carrying passengers between Fredericton and Saint John stopped at the village.[^1]

## Building the light

The 1895 light was a square wooden pyramidal tower with a square wooden lantern, set on a cribwork pier.[^1] It stood 14.3 m (47 ft) from the deck of the pier to the vane of the lantern.[^1] Its fixed white light was shown at an elevation of 16.2 m (53 ft), to guide vessels between Jemseg and Upper Musquash Island and round the bend.[^1]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends lists the keepers for No Man's Friend and Gagetown together.[^1] Edmund Buzza served from 1869 to 1880, Morris Scovill from 1880 to 1897, and David Purvis from 1897 to 1903.[^1] A second Purvis, given as "Jr." Purvis, served from 1903 to 1904, and Fraser Fox from 1904 to 1912.[^1] Charles Brooks served from 1912 to at least 1923.[^1] LighthouseFriends does not separate the earlier No Man's Friend beacon from the Gagetown light in its list for the years before 1895.[^1] The dates for Brooks rest on LighthouseFriends alone.

The Department's keepers' table in the annual report printed in 1904 lists "Purvis, jr" against Gagetown, appointed 8 August 1903.[^3] The table printed in 1908 lists "Fox, Fraser" at "Gagetown, St. John River", appointed 22 April 1904.[^4]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

On 10 April 1901 the spring freshet carried the tower away.[^2][^1] The Department's report for 1901 calls it "this fine new lighthouse" and says it was carried away by the spring freshet.[^2] A temporary fixed white light was shown from a lantern suspended from an elm tree within 6 m (20 ft) of the site.[^2] The tower was set back in place, and the permanent light was shown again on 22 June 1901. Rebuilding the foundation and repairing the tower, by day labour under the inspector of lighthouses, cost $268.73.[^2] LighthouseFriends gives the cost as $269.[^1]

An ice jam destroyed the original tower in April 1934.[^1][^6]

## Today

The present tower is a square cylindrical watch room and lantern, painted white with a red lantern and gallery, on a square pyramidal wood skeletal frame.[^6] Rowlett gives the watch room and lantern as 10 m (32 ft).[^6] The Coast Guard's list gives a fixed green light with a focal height of 11.9 m and a nominal range of 8 nautical miles. The tower is a white square tower with a red top, 9.8 m high, and the list marks the light as seasonal.[^5] LighthouseFriends says the lantern still holds a seventh-order drum dioptric lens, which a former Chance Brothers executive, quoted there, described as built for use in Canada.[^1]

The Coast Guard owns the light.[^1][^6] The site is open and the tower is closed.[^1][^6] Rowlett places the station at the ferry landing, just off NB 102. LighthouseFriends gives directions from Route 103 and Ferry Road.[^1] The ferry that once ran from the landing stopped in February 2016, according to LighthouseFriends.[^1]
