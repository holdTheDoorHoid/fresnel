Westport Cove Light is a white octagonal tower on a cliff on the north side of the cove at Westport, on the east side of White Bay in Newfoundland.[^1][^2][^3] Lighthouse Friends says a light was first shown there in 1906, and that the wooden tower standing now dates from 1911.[^1]

The first light was a lens lantern hoisted to an open framework and painted white.[^1] In 1911 the wooden octagonal tower was built and fitted with a sixth-order dioptric lens.[^1] Lighthouse Friends also calls the place Western Cove.[^1] The tower's fixed white light burned kerosene until 1969, when it was converted to solar power.[^1] The light now shows a white flash every four seconds.[^1][^2][^3]

The sources disagree on the tower's height. The Coast Guard list gives the structure as 5.5 m, with the focal height at 10.1 m.[^2] The Lighthouse Directory gives 5.5 m (18 ft) and a focal plane of 10 m (33 ft).[^3] Lighthouse Friends says the tower is only 2.7 m (9 ft) tall, and that its focal plane is thirty-three feet above the sea because of the cliff.[^1] This story uses the Coast Guard's figure.

Lighthouse Friends says four keepers minded the light from 1906 to 1989, but it names only three.[^1] J. Pearce is named from 1906 to at least 1912. Darius Jacobs is named as keeper at least in 1921. Cecil Jacobs is named from at least 1935 to at least 1945.[^1] The Coast Guard list and the Lighthouse Directory name no keepers.[^2][^3] The Coast Guard list marks the light as seasonal.[^2]

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the light and is responsible for its upkeep.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory and Lighthouse Friends both say the grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^1][^3] Lighthouse Friends describes a 4 km trail from the community wharf, with a steep set of stairs up to a picnic area near the light. It gives the distance from the parking area at the wharf to the light as roughly 0.8 km.[^1] Do not climb the tower.
