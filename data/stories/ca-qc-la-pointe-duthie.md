Pointe Duthie Light was a small, square, white wooden tower on a point at New Richmond, on the Baie des Chaleurs in the Gaspé region of Quebec. It stood on the east side of the mouth of the Grand Cascapédia River.[^2] It was built in 1903, lit in 1904 and went dark in 1914. The tower on the point today is a replica, built in 1989 near the original site.[^1][^2][^4][^5]

## The place

The point is named for George and John Duthie, who came with their families in 1755, according to Lighthouse Friends.[^1] The municipality of New Richmond says the point was first occupied by the Mi'gmaq, then by Acadians, and then by successive waves of Loyalist, English, Scottish and Irish settlers.[^6]

The lighthouse stood on the southwest tip of the point, 15 m (50 ft) back from the top of the bank.[^1][^2] The Quebec heritage register says the Baie des Chaleurs had a series of leading lights built from 1872, to keep ships safe around the bay's rich fishing grounds. It describes the Pointe Duthie light as a secondary marker along the coast.[^4] (Translated from the French.)

## Building the light

The Department of Marine and Fisheries' annual report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1904 describes the first tower as an enclosed wooden tower, square in plan, with sloping sides, topped by a square wooden lantern, all painted white. The report gives its height as 10 m (33 ft) from the base to the lantern ventilator, and the Quebec register gives the same height.[^2][^4]

The light was a fixed white shown 15 m (50 ft) above high water, with a seventh-order dioptric lens.[^2] The report says it "should be visible 12 miles".[^2] The report does not say whether those are statute or nautical miles, so this story repeats the figure as printed.

The building was put up by day labour under P. A. Perron, the Department of Public Works engineer, at a cost of $1,798.65.[^2][^1] Lighthouse Friends says Henry Carswell, a local farmer, gave up the land for the lighthouse in exchange for a Crown debt.[^1]

The Quebec register dates the first tower to 1903, while the Marine report says the light was put in operation "on the opening of navigation in 1904". Both dates can stand if the tower was finished in 1903 and the light first showed when navigation opened in 1904.[^4][^2]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends says the first keeper, B. V. Willett, lived four miles away.[^1] The Marine report's list of keepers gives "Willett, B. V" against "New Richmond, Duthie Point". The name, station and appointment date were matched by column position, because the OCR prints them in separate blocks. The page image confirms the row: the appointment reads "16, 1903" with a ditto mark for the month, which carries October from the line above, so October 16, 1903.[^3]

Lighthouse Friends says Willett's post was a political appointment, and that he was replaced in 1911 by James R. Doddridge, when the government's majority changed from Liberal to Conservative.[^1] No other source read here names Doddridge. Len V. Willett is listed by Lighthouse Friends until 1914, with no start date.[^1]

## After the keepers

From 1914, the register says, the bay silted up after timber was cut along the Grand Cascapédia River, and navigation became difficult. That year the light was moved to Maria, another harbour on the bay, and the site has had no navigation role since.[^4]

Lighthouse Friends tells the story differently in its details. It says the Department planned to move the lighthouse to the neighbouring parish, but John Duthie petitioned to leave the structure in place and move only the light. The tower was left and then dismantled in 1918.[^1] The register gives 1914 for the dismantling. The register and Lighthouse Friends both say the upper and middle sections went to the Carswell farm and the lower section to a property at Dimock Creek, and that the upper section was later lost in a fire.[^1][^4] Lighthouse Friends adds that the middle section became a shed.[^1]

In 1989 the Gaspesian British Heritage Centre was set up on an old shipyard beside the point. It obtained the surviving sections and reconstructed the lighthouse near its original site.[^1] The Quebec register dates the replica to 1989 and says it includes the surviving parts of the old tower. It describes the replica as a rebuilding of the "1911 lighthouse" (translated from the French "phare de 1911"), a date that no other source here gives for a tower.[^4][^5]

The Quebec register lists the lighthouse as located within a heritage site, with a status date of 8 April 2002.[^5]
## Today

The Town of New Richmond presents the site as Pointe Duthie / Ne'sueg. It covers 82 acres, with 18 buildings, forest trails, and a pebble beach at the mouth of the Grand Cascapédia River. The town rents some of the buildings by the day or week in summer, shows displays from its collections, and invites visitors to spend a night in the lighthouse.[^6] The listing carries no date, and the night stay has not been checked for this season, so confirm with the town before planning a visit. The address is 351 Boulevard Perron Ouest, on the west side of New Richmond.[^6][^1]

Lighthouse Friends is older. It says the grounds are open but the tower is closed, and that the British Heritage Centre "appears to have closed in 2017".[^1] It names the Gaspesian British Heritage Village as the owner.[^1] The town's own listing, which carries no date, presents the site as the town's.[^6] The Les amis des phares site lists the manager as Pointe Duthie Point – Nesue'g.[^7]
