Howards Cove Light stands on a cliff above the harbour at Howard's Cove, on the western shore of Prince Edward Island, looking out over Northumberland Strait.[^1] The Coast Guard's records call the place Seal Point, though neither the maps nor the local people use that name.[^1] It is a small square tower, 5.8 m (19 ft) high, painted white with a red top.[^2][^3]

The station was established in 1960.[^3][^4] Rowlett and the Prince Edward Island Lighthouse Society both date the present tower to 1978.[^3][^4] LighthouseFriends says the tower was built in 1976.[^1] The sources do not agree on that date, and this story cannot settle it.

The light went up in the same year that a new fishing harbour was being built below the cliff.[^1][^5]

The harbour works nearly cost the cove a landmark. The Giant's Chair, a sandstone rock shaped like an armchair, sat near the lighthouse and had become a little tourist attraction.[^1][^5] It was noticed missing in the summer of 1959.[^1] A newspaper report of July 1960, as LighthouseFriends quotes it, says the rock had been pushed off the cliff during the harbour work, had fallen into the sea and had been covered with fill to make a wharf. Workmen found it. It was then hauled back up and set on the cliff top, where it again overlooks the strait.[^5]

The Coast Guard's list of lights, last updated on 25 August 2026, still carries the light. It shows a two-second flash in every six seconds, a focal height of 14.2 m (46.6 ft) and a range of 12 nautical miles, and it is marked "Seasonal."[^1][^2] The Prince Edward Island Lighthouse Society's 2021 page says the Government of Canada owns the light.[^3] In 2011 the cove's port authority said it hoped to take the light over under the Coast Guard's divestiture program, so that it could be kept as a harbour feature.[^6] The Howard's Cove Harbour Authority has petitioned for ownership of the tower through the Parks Canada Heritage Lighthouse Program.[^3]

The grounds are open. The tower is closed.[^1][^3]
