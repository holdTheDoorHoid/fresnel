Petit-Rocher Lighthouse stands at the end of the wharf at Petit-Rocher, New Brunswick, on the south side of Chaleur Bay, beside the mouth of Nepisiguit Bay.[^2] It was first lit in 1879 on Elm Tree Point, and it was moved to the wharf in 1910.[^1]

Joseph Morrison won a contract in 1878 for a square wooden lighthouse on Elm Tree Point. LighthouseFriends gives its height as 9.4 m (31 ft) and its total cost as $952.60.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory gives the tower as 9 m (30 ft).[^2] Keeper Hilarion Roy first lit its fixed white light on 24 May 1879, and he was paid an annual salary of $150.[^1] LighthouseFriends names three keepers: Roy, from 1879 to 1895; Joseph B. Boudreau, from 1896 to 1911; and J. D. Laplante, from 1911 to at least 1923.[^1] The Department's keeper list published in 1905 gives Boudreau against Petit Rocher.[^4]

A wharf was built at Petit-Rocher in 1908. In 1910 the lighthouse was moved from the north side of the mouth of the Elm Tree River, about 2.5 km (1.6 mi) south, to the outer end of the wharf at Rochette (Church) Point.[^1] LighthouseFriends says that in 1912 a storm carried the lighthouse away, and a pole light was set up near the end of the wharf in its place.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory gives a different end for the original tower: demolition in 1929.[^2]

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the light today.[^2] Its list describes a red light flashing once every six seconds, shown from a square skeleton tower 6.9 m (23 ft) high at the outer end of the wharf. A green light on a pipe pole marks the breakwater.[^3] The sources do not give the date the present tower was built. The Lighthouse Directory says the light can be reached by walking the pier, where the site is open but the tower is closed. It describes the light as a yellow flash every five seconds, which differs from the Coast Guard's red.[^2]
