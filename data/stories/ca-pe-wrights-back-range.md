Wright's Back Range Light, also called the Wrights Rear Range Lighthouse, is a small white wooden tower on the shore of the Northumberland Strait, just west of Victoria on Prince Edward Island. Built in 1894 on land belonging to its first keeper, Charles L. Wright, it was the rear half of a pair of lights that lined ships up for the approach to Victoria Harbour.[^1][^2][^4][^6]

## Building the light

Lighthouse Friends records that in 1894 two new pairs of range lights, the Palmer Range and the Wrights Range, were added to the older Leards Range lights to guide vessels into Victoria Harbour, also known as Crapaud Harbour.[^1] The Wrights Range was first shown on 15 June 1894, and its rear light stood in the corner of an orchard on the farm of Charles L. Wright.[^1] That first rear tower was a square, open-framed wooden structure with a slatted seaward face, painted white and topped with a red lantern.[^1] The sources disagree about the lantern. The statement of significance in the Canadian Register of Historic Places calls the tower's lack of a separate lantern, with the light shining through a sea-facing window, unusual, and the Heritage Places file says it was built without one.[^2][^3] The Prince Edward Island Lighthouse Society, however, describes the original open-framed tower as topped by a red lantern and says it was later enclosed.[^4] None of them explains the difference.

## The keepers

Charles L. Wright was appointed keeper on 14 June 1894. He was paid $20 for the land under the rear light and received a salary of $100 a year.[^1] The tower takes its name from him.[^2] The Department of Marine and Fisheries' statement of lightkeepers for 1910 (printed page 253) lists the Wrights Range keeper, Charles Wright, on a salary of $130, still with with the 1894 appointment date.[^5] The Lighthouse Society records that he received an Imperial Long Service Medal for his many years of lightkeeping.[^4] He served until 1935, and his son Charles Evan Wright took over and kept the light until 1959.[^1] The Heritage Places file says Charles Evan Wright maintained it until electricity was installed that year.[^3] Lighthouse Friends lists two further names for 1959, Edward H. Boswell and Bryce Boswell, without saying more.[^1]

## After the keepers

The Lighthouse Society notes that major repairs were made in 1957 and that, before the light was decommissioned, a solar panel supplied its electricity.[^4] In 1986 the tower was moved a short distance because of shoreline erosion.[^2] Lighthouse Friends says the Canadian Coast Guard deactivated the range in 2011, and the Lighthouse Society gives August 2011.[^1][^4] The red light itself has been removed from the rear tower.[^1]

## Today

On 13 March 2025 the Government of Canada designated the Wrights Rear and Front Range lighthouses under the Heritage Lighthouse Protection Act.[^6] The tower stands at the end of Beach Light Road in a mixed residential and agricultural area, and the Lighthouse Society describes it as on private property.[^6][^4] Ownership is described differently: Lighthouse Friends says the Canadian Coast Guard owns it, and the Lighthouse Society says the Government of Canada does, with the Victoria Seaport Museum Inc. having petitioned for ownership under the Heritage Lighthouse Program.[^1][^4] Lighthouse Friends reports the grounds and tower closed.[^1] No source describes public access to the tower.
