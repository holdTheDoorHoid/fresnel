The Cedar Point range lights marked the entrance to Sandusky Bay on Ohio's Lake Erie shore. The first light there was built in 1839.[^6] The limestone lighthouse that replaced it in 1862 lost its tower and lantern after the ranges were discontinued in 1904, and it now stands in the grounds of the Cedar Point Amusement Park resort.[^1][^6]

## The place

Cedar Point is a peninsula near Sandusky, and the light marked the entrance to Sandusky Bay.[^6] Lighthouse Friends says that in 1832 leading citizens and shipowners asked Congress for a beacon there, although every other Ohio harbor on Lake Erie east of the Black River already had one. Congress allocated $2,500 on 3 March 1837 and another $3,000 on 7 July 1838 to finish the work.[^6]

Lieutenant Charles T. Platt described the planned light in 1838. As Lighthouse Friends quotes him, he warned that "the lake is making fearful inroads upon the shore," and he proposed cribwork filled with stone, extended from the Hog's-back to the point of the cape.[^6]

## Building the light

Lighthouse Friends says the light went up in 1839 as a rectangular stone dwelling with an octagonal tower. From the ground to the top of the lighthouse it measured just over twenty-eight feet (8.5 m).[^6] A "patent signal lantern" was placed on the point in 1849 to range with it. In 1853 it was replaced by a front-range beacon 265 feet (81 m) north of the lighthouse.[^6]

Work began in 1862 on a larger limestone lighthouse with a square tower. Lighthouse Friends says the lantern room and lens from the 1839 tower were moved into it, and the archive records the lens as installed in 1862.[^6][^1] Lighthouse Friends says a third front-range light, on cribwork in five feet (1.5 m) of water with a frame dwelling, followed in 1867.[^6]

Lake action was a threat to the front range. The Light-House Board's report for the year ended 30 June 1891 says the crib under the range dwelling had separated in the centre, so that the frame building settled unevenly, and that the crib work and structure were "liable to be further damaged at almost any time by the lake action." It proposed a triangular iron tower, 30 feet (9 m) high, in the centre of the crib. A contract for a stanchion tower was made on 29 June 1891, but the Board then decided to drive a row of piles around the outside of the crib and to rebuild its superstructure to full height.[^3]

The walk from the dwelling to the front beacon, 486 feet (148 m) long, was rebuilt in the year ended 30 June 1894.[^4]

## The keepers

The archive lists Leonard Beatty as the first head keeper, from 1839 to 1848.[^1] It then lists Sarah Beatty as head keeper from 1848 to 1849. The archive does not say how the two were related.[^1]

Between 1849 and 1872 the archive lists ten head keepers, most of them for only a few years each.[^1][^6] Hiram A. Lyman kept the light from 1872 to 1901, the longest term of any head keeper on the roster.[^1] Daniel Finn followed him, from 1901 until the range was discontinued in 1904.[^1][^6]

The first assistants on the roster were J. A. Youngs (1867 to 1868), Samuel D. Cuteliffe (1868 to 1870), Joseph A. Gibaut (1870 to 1874) and Alanson Youmans (1875 to 1882).[^1] The archive also lists Grace Chapman as a laborer in 1901.[^1]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

Lighthouse Friends says that in July 1910 fireworks shot from the steamer *Eastland*, as she left Sandusky for Cleveland, started a fire that destroyed the old front-range light.[^6] Rowlett's directory gives the same year, and says the front range was "destroyed by fire in 1910."[^7]

The Commissioner of Lighthouses' report for 1922 says that the tender *Crocus*, Fred Herbert commanding, rescued six people from the gasoline cruiser *Aurora*, of Cleveland. The cruiser had run aground on a sand bar of Cedar Point, Ohio, and the six were given food and shelter overnight.[^5] The report does not link the rescue to the light.

## After the keepers

Lighthouse Friends says both range lights were discontinued in 1904, leaving the outer and inner ranges to guide mariners into the bay. After its light was deactivated, the lighthouse lost its tower and lantern room.[^6] The archive lists the rear range as no longer an active aid to navigation.[^1]

The Coast Guard then used the site as a buoy depot and a radiobeacon station, established 19 April 1932, and finally as a boat station, until 1975.[^6][^7] The archive records the front range as no longer existing.[^2]

In 1987 Cedar Point Amusement Park acquired the northwest corner of the peninsula, which included the abandoned lighthouse, for $425,000.[^6] Lighthouse Friends says the park's planners wanted the restored lighthouse as the centre of a group of park cottages, and that Lighthouse Point accepted its first visitors in 2001.[^6] Rowlett says the light tower was replaced in a restoration of 2000 to 2001.[^7]

NPGallery holds two National Register records for the site. The 1982 record is titled "U.S. Coast Guard Building" in the Sandusky multiple resource area, with a date published of 20 October 1982.[^8] The lighthouse's own record, "Cedar Point Light," was published on 19 July 1984 in the Great Lakes Coast Guard multiple listing.[^10]

## Today

The archive lists the lighthouse's disposition as "Recreation Inside Amusement Park" and says it is not open to the public.[^1] Lighthouse Friends says the grounds are open and the dwelling and tower are closed.[^6]

Lighthouse Point, the park's cottage area where the lighthouse stands, offers 156 cottages and cabins and 145 RV sites.[^9]
