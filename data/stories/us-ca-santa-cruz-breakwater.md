Santa Cruz Breakwater Light, also called the Walton Lighthouse, is a harbor light at the end of the west breakwater of Santa Cruz Harbor, at the northern end of Monterey Bay.[^1][^4] The first light on the breakwater was a box-shaped structure in 1964. The tower that stands now was dedicated and lit in June 2002.[^1]

## The place

Santa Cruz Harbor was built in two phases, from 1962 to 1973, on the site of Woods Lagoon at the mouth of Arana Gulch.[^2] LocalWiki gives other dates: it says the lagoon was converted into the harbor in 1963 and that the harbor was dedicated in 1964.[^3] The Army Corps of Engineers made the breakwater, and the county developed the port facilities.[^3] The light stands at the end of the west jetty, where it marks the harbor entrance.[^1]

The U.S. Lighthouse Society's archive keeps a separate record for an older Santa Cruz light, established in 1869 and located on the "West Side Of Entrance To Harbor." It records that tower as wooden, demolished in 1947 and replaced by a replica tower.[^5]

## Building the light

The first light on the breakwater was a box light, which served from 1964 to 1996. It was replaced by a cylinder that was nicknamed "the water heater," used from 1996 to 1999. From 1999 until May 2002 a simple pipe structure held the light.[^1]

In 1998 the community proposed replacing the "unsightly harbor light" with a lighthouse of classic design. A major donation from Charles Walton of Los Gatos helped fund it, and construction began in 2001.[^1] Mark Mesiti-Miller designed the tower, and Devcon Construction, Inc., built it.[^1] A cylindrical inner core holds the electrical equipment and "a circular staircase of forty-two steps" leads to the top. Reinforcing rods carry a shotcrete shell that forms the conical shape, and the lantern room has a copper roof.[^1]

The tower stands about 41 feet (12.6 m) above the level of the west jetty.[^1] It was dedicated on 9 June 2002, when "a green light flashing every four seconds" was turned on.[^1] The lighthouse is named for Derek Walton, Charles Walton's brother, who "served in the merchant marines and was lost at sea during World War II."[^1] The county history wiki calls it a "memorial to Derek Walton."[^4]

## Today

The tower is white with a green band. Its first day mark was red, but after boaters objected that a green light should not have a red day mark, the band was changed to green in March 2003.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the grounds are open and the tower is closed, and it describes a sidewalk that leads out to the breakwater and the lighthouse.[^1] The county history wiki says nothing about visiting.[^4]
