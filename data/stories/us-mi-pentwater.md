Pentwater Light marks the entrance to the harbour at the mouth of the Pentwater River, where the village of Pentwater, in Oceana County, meets Lake Michigan. Charles Mears, who built lumber mills along this shore, put one at the river mouth in 1855 and improved the channel to Lake Michigan.[^1] In 1867 Congress appropriated $55,000 for the harbour work, and cribs built at Ludington were towed to Pentwater to make the piers.[^1] By 1884 the Life-Saving Service had secured sites at Pentwater for stations.[^4] Two towers stand on the piers today, a 1937 metal tower on the south pier and a 1987 tower on the north pier.[^1]

## Building the light

The first light was a square, open-frame wooden tower built at the outer end of the south pier in 1873. Its 33-foot (10 m) tower carried a sixth-order lens in an octagonal lantern, and the fixed red light was first shown on 20 June 1873.[^1] An elevated walkway let the keeper reach the tower in any weather, but no keeper's dwelling was provided. LighthouseFriends says the Lighthouse Board asked for $5,000 for a dwelling in 1873, that a dwelling was apparently never built, and that the Board was still asking for money for one in 1906.[^1]

In 1890 a post with a tubular lantern went up at the outer end of the south pier. With the pierhead light it made a range for entering the harbour, and it was first lit on 15 August 1890.[^1] The Board's report for the year ended 30 June 1900 lists the "Pentwater pierhead range" and records that an elevated walk 500 feet (150 m) long and 10 feet (3 m) high was built there that year.[^2]

Later towers followed. The Army Corps of Engineers finished replacing the timber piers with concrete in 1937, and a metal skeletal tower went up on the south pier that June.[^1] On 8 October 1987 a 30-foot (9 m) prefabricated tower was lifted onto the north pier by a National Guard helicopter, after an earlier attempt was postponed for high wind. LighthouseFriends says the tower carries a light, a foghorn and a radiobeacon, and calls its push a community project.[^1]

## The keepers

The sources disagree about the first keeper. The archive's roster lists John Davenport for 1873 alone, and Francis McGuire from 1873 to 1877. LighthouseFriends names McGuire as the first keeper.[^3][^1]

When McGuire resigned in 1877, his wife Annie took charge of the light. The archive prints her as "Annie (Anna) McGuire" and gives her as principal keeper from 1877 to 1885. LighthouseFriends says she was removed in 1885.[^3][^1] Barney Evers followed and served from 1885 to 1911. LighthouseFriends says he gave twenty-six years of service at Pentwater.[^3][^1] The archive then lists Andrew J. Davenport (1911 to 1912) and William P. Larson (1913 to 1915) as the last principal keepers it names.[^3]

The keeper's post ended in 1919. The front and rear lights were changed from oil to acetylene, the elevated walk was taken down, and "the position of keeper was eliminated."[^1]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

The first event below is not tied in the sources to the light itself. In its 1879 annual report the Life-Saving Service described "a particularly distressing disaster at Pentwater, Lake Michigan". On the evening of 20 November 1879 the schooner Mercury went ashore in a gale and snowstorm. The report says she was "ashore near Pentwater, which was 15 miles distant" from the Ludington station.[^5][^6] The 1880 report adds that the telegraph wires between Ludington and Pentwater were down, and the Ludington keeper did not get word until seven that evening.[^6]

The severe storm of 8, 9 and 10 November 1913 damaged the Pentwater Pierhead Range, a range light at the pier. The December 1913 Lighthouse Service Bulletin lists five light stations on the lakes that were damaged, among them the "Pentwater Pierhead Range, Mich.", with damage amounting to about $6,500 in all. The Bulletin does not give a separate figure for Pentwater.[^7]

## Today

LighthouseFriends says the yacht club's commodore enlisted a congressman's help in gaining ownership of the old wooden tower, so that it could be mounted on the roof of the club's clubhouse.[^1] The piers carry the 1937 and 1987 towers. The state park on the lake shore is Charles Mears State Park. Carrie Mears, daughter of Charles Mears, gave the land for the park to the State of Michigan in 1923, and LighthouseFriends says the lights can be seen from the park.[^1]
