The Haszard Point range lights are a pair of tapered wooden towers at Kinlock, on the south shore of Prince Edward Island just east of Charlottetown. They were built in 1889 to mark the way into Charlottetown Harbour, and both are still listed as active.[^1][^2][^7]

## The place

Two pairs of range lights were set up in 1889 "to guide vessels into Charlottetown Harbour along a channel clear of all obstructions."[^2] The outer pair stands southeast of the harbour entrance, on farmland owned by Hon. Francis L. Haszard (1849-1938), who was Premier of Prince Edward Island from 1908 to 1911 and a Supreme Court judge from 1911 to 1930.[^1][^2] The inner pair was at Brighton Beach, in the harbour.[^2]

The front tower is 17.3 m (56.8 ft) from base to vane. The rear tower is 14 m (45.9 ft), and stands 650 m (2,133 ft) north of the front, on a hill that gives it a focal plane of 46.3 m (151.9 ft).[^2] The Coast Guard list gives the same heights.[^6] Rowlett gives the front tower as 14 m (47 ft) and the rear as 12 m (37 ft), so the sources disagree on the heights.[^7]

## Building the light

The range lights first showed fixed red, and changed to fixed white in 1902.[^2] The Department's report for the year ended 30 June 1901 says that "On January 1, 1902, the range lights will be changed from fixed red to fixed white catoptric lights."[^4] The same report gives the front light as 13.7 m (45 ft) above high water, and the back light as 38.1 m (125 ft).[^4] LighthouseFriends says the front tower was moved back from the bank in 1902, and again in 1936.[^2]

The Department's report for the year ended 30 June 1910 lists a 25 mm vapour installation for each light.[^5]

Around 1948 the front tower was raised by 2.4 m (7.9 ft). LighthouseFriends says the change gave it "a somewhat awkward appearance."[^2]

## The keepers

Each light had its own keeper at first. LighthouseFriends lists the front, or outer, light's keepers as J.P. Wood from 1890 to 1893, George Wood from 1893 to 1899, and Daniel McRae from 1900 to 1910. The rear, or inner, light's keepers were Alexander Kennedy from 1890 to 1902 and Angus Beaton from 1902 to 1910.[^2][^3]

The Department's keeper list for the year ended 30 June 1907 has "Beaton, Angus S" at "Hazard Point Range, Black Light," with an appointment date of 21 November 1902.[^8]

In 1910 one keeper took over both lights. LighthouseFriends says Edwin Stewart was appointed to keep the range lights that year at a salary of $320 a year, and that before then a separate keeper had been assigned to each light.[^2] The Parks Canada register, which covers the rear light, names Alexander Kennedy, Angus Beaton and Edwin Stewart as its keepers, and gives Stewart's service as 1910 to 1961.[^1] LighthouseFriends says he retired in 1958, earning $1,020 a year, and then served as caretaker of the automated lights for $75 a year.[^2] The two sources disagree on the end date.

## After the keepers

LighthouseFriends says the Town of Stratford petitioned for ownership of the lighthouse under the Parks Canada Heritage Lighthouse Program.[^2] Rowlett says the Town of Stratford has applied for the rear light, and that an application for the front light has been filed.[^7]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns both lights, and Rowlett's directory lists each as active.[^2][^3][^7] The Coast Guard's list gives the front light a fixed white characteristic, "F W," a focal height of 18.3 m and a range of 15 nautical miles, and says it operates all year and 24 hours a day.[^6] Rowlett and LighthouseFriends describe a yellow light instead. Rowlett calls it a "continuous yellow light visible only on the range line," and LighthouseFriends says the lights "currently show a fixed-yellow light."[^7][^3] The sources disagree on the colour, and this story does not settle it.

The setting is residential. The register describes it as "the setting of the lighthouse within a residential area."[^1] Rowlett says the site and tower are closed, and "the tower is in a gated neighborhood." It says the rear tower is on private property.[^7] LighthouseFriends says the access lane is private, and that visitors "are not openly welcome."[^3]
