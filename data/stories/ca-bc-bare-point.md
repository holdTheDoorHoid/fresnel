Bare Point Light stands at the end of the peninsula that closes Chemainus Bay, on the east coast of Vancouver Island between Nanaimo and Victoria. It has been an aid to navigation since 1897.[^1] It began as a wooden house with a lantern on its roof, and it now shows a green light from a cylindrical tower.[^4][^21]

## The place

For many years timber left Chemainus Bay by ship.[^2] In 1896 the directors of the Victoria Lumber Manufacturing Company asked James Gaudin, the Marine Agent in Victoria, for a light on Bare Point to help lumber vessels reach the harbour at night.[^3] Gaudin sent the request to Ottawa and added that there were plans to ship coal from newly opened mines at the same point.[^3]

## Building the light

The Department's 1897 report, as LighthouseFriends quotes it, describes a square wooden house with a square lantern on its roof.[^4] The light showed a fixed white beam from 11 m (36 ft) above high water, through a seventh-order dioptric apparatus.[^5]

The Department's statement of light-keepers records the Bare Point appointment of James Crozier on 12 June 1897, at $120 a year.[^6] In the year ended 30 June 1900 a floating landing was built at the point for $36.[^7]

## The keepers

James Crozier rowed or walked out from Chemainus each night to get the light going, and returned the next morning to put it out.[^8] With the Marine Agent's permission he also worked as a lumber inspector at the Chemainus sawmill.[^8] LighthouseFriends says Crozier resigned in 1911, and that Philip R. Stevenson then took charge of the light.[^9]

The keeper list in the Department's 1913 report gives the name as "P. R. Stevinson" on the Bare Point line, with an appointment date of 14 October 1910.[^10] That date is a year earlier than the 1911 that LighthouseFriends gives for Stevenson taking charge, and the two accounts are not reconciled.[^9][^10]

Stevenson never returned to the lighthouse. LighthouseFriends names James Chalmers as the permanent keeper from then on.[^11] The Department's 1915 keeper list gives "J. C, Chalmers" on the Bare point line, with an appointment date of 5 August 1912.[^12]

A column in the Chemainus Courier of 5 September 2018, reprinted by the Chemainus Valley Museum, names another keeper, Charles Laurence, who would row across the harbour daily to light the lantern.[^13] The Department's 1913 and 1915 keeper lists do not name him, and the column gives no date for him.[^10][^12]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

In March 1911 a gale swept away the floating landing, according to LighthouseFriends.[^14] On 4 August 1912 Stevenson was delayed in returning from the light. That evening his wife had been seen pacing in the rain at the end of the Chemainus dock, and she was found drowned.[^15] Stevenson wrote to the marine agent in Victoria, Captain George Robertson, saying he had asked James Chalmers to take care of the lighthouse until after they had "put her away".[^15]

## After the keepers

In September 1926 mariners reported that the light had been out for a few nights. A party from Chemainus went out and found Keeper Chalmers dead on the floor.[^16] The Department did not hire a successor. It put an unwatched AGA acetylene light in the lantern room instead.[^17]

Russ Rowlett's Lighthouse Directory says the light was automated after that death, that the house deteriorated and was demolished about 1940, and that a skeletal tower replaced it. It dates the present cylindrical tower to about 1980.[^18] LighthouseFriends disagrees about the house. It says the dilapidated house was burned in 1940, and that a steel skeletal tower with a red lantern went up in its place, lowering the focal height of the flashing white light from 9.1 m (30 ft) to 7.9 m (26 ft).[^19] Around 1980, according to LighthouseFriends, a white circular tower replaced the skeletal tower.[^20]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard list gives the light as a flashing green light, every four seconds, 9.3 metres above the water and visible for five nautical miles. The tower is white and cylindrical with a green band near the top, and the light runs year-round.[^21]

Bare Point is occupied by a Shell Canada oil terminal, and LighthouseFriends says the light is best seen from a boat.[^22] Rowlett gives the site status as unknown.[^23]
