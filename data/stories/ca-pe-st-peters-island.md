St. Peters Island Lighthouse stands on a small island in Hillsborough Bay, off Rice Point south of Charlottetown, Prince Edward Island.[^1] The lighthouse is not visible from land, and the Prince Edward Island Lighthouse Society says it is best seen by boat or from the air.[^2]

## The place

Lighthouse Friends places the island on the western side of Hillsborough Bay, near the entrance to Charlottetown Harbour.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory calls the light a landfall for Charlottetown, for vessels approaching from the west.[^5] The sources do not say which shoal or channel it was meant to mark.

Lighthouse Friends, citing the Journal of the House of Assembly of Prince Edward Island for 1866, reports £32 paid that year for erecting a light on the island, and oil paid for the same year.[^1] It does not say what kind of light was set up.[^1] The Directory gives 1866 as the year the station was established, and says nothing is known about the form of the first light.[^5]

## Building the light

In 1880 Joseph Egan began work on a new lighthouse on the island under a contract for $500.[^1] It was finished in time to start at the opening of navigation in 1881, and in all $765.87 was spent on it.[^1] The tower was a square wooden building 11.6 m (38 ft) tall, on the island's most westerly point. Its fixed white light showed 14.6 m (48 ft) above high water.[^1] The original tower had no dwelling.[^2]

In 1882 the light was changed from fixed white to fixed red, so that it would not be confused with the Blockhouse Point and Point Prim lights.[^1] In the winter of 1883–84 the tower was moved 1.6 km (about a mile) south-east, to the south side of the island.[^1] The Directory dates the move to 1884.[^5] In 1893 the tower received a 20 cm (8-inch) pressed-glass lens and a Hincks duplex burner lamp.[^1] From the opening of navigation in 1898 the light showed white for six seconds in every thirty, an occulting light.[^1]

## The keepers

William Hewson kept the light from 1881 until 1897.[^1][^2] He was followed by three keepers named Taylor.[^1][^2] The keeper list in the Department of Marine and Fisheries report for fiscal 1909–10 has James W. Taylor at St. Peters Island from 1 May 1897, at a salary of $400.[^3] Lighthouse Friends and the Society both give his years as 1897 to 1912.[^1][^2] James J. Taylor followed him. The list in the report for fiscal 1913–14 places a J. J. Taylor at the island.[^4]

James J. Taylor fell at the lighthouse on 9 November 1945, and a substitute was placed in charge from 13 November while he had treatment for an infected ankle.[^1] The Society's list ends his stint in 1947 and notes that he "died March 11."[^2] William A. S. Taylor is listed as keeper in 1947.[^1][^2]

The Society also lists James MacGrath from 1913 and William Geldert from 1939, with no end dates.[^2] Lighthouse Friends does not name either man.[^1] The same 1913–14 list has a J. McGrath at the St. Peters range, a separate station with its own entry, so the sources do not link him to the island.[^1][^2][^4]

## After the keepers

The light became an unwatched electric light on 3 October 1947.[^1] In 1964 it was mounted on top of a metal tube that looked like a culvert pipe.[^1][^2]

The sources disagree about the tower in use now. Lighthouse Friends says the 1881 tower is active again.[^1] The Directory's text says the light was moved back to the original tower, though its heading calls the station inactive.[^5] The Society says the culvert was replaced in the 1980s by a tower similar to the original, and its technical data gives 1990 as the date built.[^2] The sources do not settle which tower stands.

## Today

Lighthouse Friends says the Canadian Coast Guard owns the lighthouse.[^1] The Society says the Government of Canada owns it, and that a community group has petitioned for ownership under the Parks Canada Heritage Lighthouse Program.[^1][^2] Lighthouse Friends lists the grounds as open and the tower as closed.[^1] The Society says the light is best seen by boat or from the air.[^2]
