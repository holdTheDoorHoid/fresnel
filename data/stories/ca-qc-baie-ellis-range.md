Baie Ellis Range is a pair of range lights that lead ships into Ellis Bay, on the southwest coast of Anticosti Island, where the village of Port-Menier now stands.[^1][^3] The island's owner, the French chocolate maker Henri Menier, had the two towers built in 1905 and 1906, though a Quebec society dates the work to 1904.[^1][^7] Modern range lights now mark the bay.[^1][^2]

## The place

Before Menier, the bay was known as Gamache Bay.[^1] LighthouseFriends describes Ellis Bay as the only site on the island suited to a deep-sea port.[^1] The Canadian Coast Guard's list of lights still uses the older name: it places the Port-Menier fairway light at "Anticosti Island, S. of Baie Gamache".[^2]

Menier was the island's proprietor by 1900.[^6] The Department's report for the year ended 30 June 1900 refers to "Mr. Menier, proprietor of the Island of Anticosti".[^6] LighthouseFriends says he bought the island in 1895 as a private hunting ground, and that under the leadership of his friend Georges Martin-Zédé the village of Port-Menier was formed on the bay's shores.[^1]

In the year ended 30 June 1923 the Department took tidal observations at Ellis Bay, one of three places in the Anticosti region, in co-operation with the Hydrographic Survey. The report describes the region as "somewhat difficult of access without a surveying steamer".[^5]

## Building the light

LighthouseFriends says two prefabricated range lights were ordered from the French firm Sautter & Harle to mark the pier and guide ships into port. The pieces arrived in 1905 and the front light was put up that year. Work on the rear light began in July 1906, and the range was first lit on 11 August 1906.[^1] Les amis des phares du Saint-Laurent, a Quebec lighthouse society, dates the construction to 1904.[^7] The sources disagree on the first year, and this story gives both.

The Department's report for the year ended 30 June 1907 describes the pair. The front tower stands on the outer end of the breakwater and is a cylindrical cast-iron tower. Its light is "fixed white dioptric of the fourth order", 10.7 m (35 ft) above high water and visible 11 miles.[^3] The back tower stands on land at the bottom of the bay, 1,524 m (5,000 ft) from the front one. It is 15.8 m (52 ft) high on a stone foundation, and its light, 24.1 m (79 ft) above high water, is visible 14 miles.[^3]

LighthouseFriends says the lights were listed as private property, but were shown to visitors when a vessel's arrival was reported.[^1]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

In February 1917 heavy ice kept one steamer from reaching the bay.[^4] The Department's report for the year ended 30 June 1917 records that the steamer Montcalm left on 28 February for the north shore, with 45 passengers and about 90 tons of freight for Murray Bay, Tadousac, Seven Islands and Ellis Bay. She "was unable to reach the last place on account of heavy packed ice" and got back to Quebec on 5 March.[^4]

## After the keepers

LighthouseFriends says the towers were moved in 1927 because the wharf was being altered to improve docking, and were set on a new alignment at the head of the bay.[^1]

The dates of Menier's death and of the island's sale are disputed. LighthouseFriends says Menier died in 1915, that his heir then backed away from the project, and that the island was sold in 1926 to the Anticosti Corporation, which kept the logging going.[^1] A Montreal history site gives 1913 as the year of his death, citing the Quebec place-name commission.[^8] The Anticosti tourism timeline dates the sale to the Anticosti Corporation to 1908.[^10] A 1980 study titles the Menier period as running from 1895 to 1926.[^9]

The historic range lights were decommissioned some time after 1985 and replaced by a modern set.[^1][^2] In the winter of 1989 the rear tower was lowered from its crumbling base and dragged to a new site near the Menier chateau. It was set on a new concrete foundation in the summer of 1990, and its lantern was replaced with the front tower's.[^1]

## Today

The Coast Guard's list still gives a "Baie Ellis Main front range light" at the northwest corner of the bay, and a "Baie Ellis Main rear range light".[^2] LighthouseFriends says the modern range lights remain in operation.[^1]

The historic rear tower stands on the Chemin du Château, just beyond the foundations of the Menier chateau.[^1][^7] LighthouseFriends says the front tower still stands in the woods off the road to Baie-Sainte-Claire, and that the lighthouse is owned by Port-Menier, with the grounds open and the tower closed.[^1] The Quebec society says what remains of the front tower can be seen in the woods along the same road.[^7] Its page also says the rear tower remains at its original place, which contradicts LighthouseFriends' account of the 1989 move.[^7]
