Cheboygan Crib Light marks the west side of the mouth of the Cheboygan River, in Cheboygan, Michigan.[^1] The light was lit on 1 November 1884 from an iron tower set on a crib, a platform built out into the lake at the river mouth.[^1] Today the crib is gone, and the iron tower stands on shore.[^1]

## The place

In the 1888 season about 100 million board feet of lumber left the harbor, but the river mouth had been shallow: before a dredging project was adopted in 1871, the water over the bar was only about seven feet (2 m) deep.[^1]

## Building the light

The pier under the light was built in 1881, in sixteen feet of water, and had been repaired several times by 1902, LighthouseFriends says.[^1] It also quotes the Lighthouse Board's report for 1885, which describes an iron pierhead beacon erected by contract on 15 October 1884, on an oak-ring foundation, on the crib at the end of the dredged channel.[^1]

The tower was octagonal, and LighthouseFriends gives its height as thirty-three feet (10 m).[^1] Its lamp was a fourth-order Fresnel lens, used to produce a fixed red light.[^1]

The Board's report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1892 records that "Iron boat davits were placed on the crib."[^2]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends says the crib never had a dedicated keeper. Instead, an assistant keeper's post was added to the Cheboygan River Range Lights, and that station's keepers looked after the crib as well.[^1]

The Board's report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1897 records that "Quarters for the assistant keeper on the crib were built," together with pile protection for the boat.[^3] LighthouseFriends dates the two-room dwelling to 1897.[^1]

## After the keepers

The Board's report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1899 says that after the crib's foundation was riprapped, "The timberwork was leveled and the deck was relaid."[^4]

LighthouseFriends says the iron tower was painted white in 1901. A diver examined the crib in July 1902 and found its base sound, though the upper works needed repair. In 1903 a brick and concrete pier replaced the wooden upper crib.[^1] A fog bell hung from a skeleton tower on the crib in 1910.[^1]

In June 1925 the light's character was changed to a red flash every two seconds.[^1] The gas-operated fog-bell striker is recorded as installed that same month, according to LighthouseFriends' quotation from the Bulletin.[^1] The Lighthouse Service Bulletin for 1932 reported on the striker under the heading "PERFORMANCE OF AUTOMATIC FOGBELL STRIKER AT CHEBOYGAN CRIB."[^5] LighthouseFriends says the light was automated in the late 1920s.[^1]

By the 1980s the crib had developed a significant lean. The Coast Guard judged that demolishing it would cost less than repairing it.[^1] The crib was taken down. The iron tower was barged to the river mouth and set on a new base at the foot of the pier in Gordon Turner Park. A $40,000 grant that Cheboygan received from the state paid for that foundation.[^1]

In August 2001, after members of the Great Lakes Lighthouse Keepers Association offered their labor, the windows were replaced, the tower recaulked and the lighthouse repainted.[^1] In 2003 the Coast Guard listed the light among its nonessential property, and the formal process of transferring it to the city began.[^1]

## Today

The City of Cheboygan owns the light. The grounds are open, and the tower is closed.[^1] A boardwalk in Gordon Turner Park leads to the river and the light.[^1]
