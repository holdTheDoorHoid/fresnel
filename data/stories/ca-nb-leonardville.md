Leonardville Lighthouse is a white tower with a red top, on a cliff at the entrance to Leonardville Harbour on the east side of Deer Island, in Charlotte County, New Brunswick.[^1][^2][^3]

## The place

Deer Island lies in Passamaquoddy Bay.[^2] Rowlett's directory says the location of the border between Canada and the United States in the bay was in dispute for many years, until the Passamaquoddy Bay Treaty of 1910 settled it.[^2]

The harbour is a very small, shallow bay between Deer Island and Bar Island, a small island just offshore.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the tower marked the western side of its entrance.[^1]

## Building the light

The Department of Marine's report for the year ended 30 June 1914 lists a new aid at Leonardville, with the work "Erection of a wooden lighthouse tower and installation of a 6th order dioptric lens." The contract went to A. L. Mury, of West Arichat, at $896.[^4]

LighthouseFriends says the tower was erected in 1913 under that contract. It describes a white square tower with sloping sides, 8.2 m (27 ft) high, and a sixth-order dioptric lens. The focal plane was 18 m (59 ft) above high water.[^1] Rowlett dates the light to 1914. It gives the tower as 8 m (28 ft) and the focal plane as 20 m (66 ft).[^2] The sources disagree on the year. The Coast Guard list gives no date for the tower.[^3]

## The keepers

The Department's keepers' list for the year ended 30 June 1915 gives Alonzo Conley an appointment date of 20 December 1913, at a salary of $230.[^5] The list prints names and stations in separate blocks, so the link to Leonardville is made by position.

LighthouseFriends calls Conley the first keeper and gives his years as 1913 to at least 1936.[^1] In 1936, according to that page, he wrote to J. C. Chesley, the Department's agent, to report a large meteor. It had fallen into the sea off Harbour-de-Lute on Campobello Island in daylight. A resident had told Conley the water steamed and bubbled, and large waves washed ashore. Chesley replied that he would pass the information on to the meteorological department in Toronto.[^1]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the light.[^1][^2] Its list gives a fixed white light with a focal height of 20 m, visible 10 nautical miles, and shows it lit year round.[^3]

The site and tower are closed to visitors. LighthouseFriends says the grounds and tower are closed.[^1] Rowlett calls the site private property and says the light can be seen through the trees from NB 772.[^2] Deer Island can be reached by ferry from L'Etete and from Campobello Island.[^1]
