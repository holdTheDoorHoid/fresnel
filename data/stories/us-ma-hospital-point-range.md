Hospital Point light stands on a rocky point on the west side of Beverly Cove, at the main channel into Salem Harbor, in Essex County, Massachusetts.[^1] It has shown a light since 1871.[^3] From 1927 it was half of a range: a second light in the steeple of the First Baptist Church, about a mile away, guided ships into Salem and Beverly harbors.[^2][^6]

## The place

The point was used for defense and for disease long before there was a light. The 1981 form says the point was first called Paul's Point because a Paul Thorndike owned it. A watch house was built there in 1711, and in 1755 Col. John Glover was authorized to build the earthwork fort that still remains on the upper level.[^1] In 1801 a smallpox hospital was built on the point, and the place took its name from it. The hospital became barracks during the War of 1812, and it burned in 1849.[^1]

The lights came from a request to Congress. In 1869 the Lighthouse Board asked for money "to complete the lighting" of Salem Harbor, and on July 15, 1870, Congress appropriated $30,000 for the lights the Board had recommended, Hospital Point among them.[^2]

## Building the light

The town of Beverly transferred about 1.8 acres to the federal government in 1871, and a temporary light was set up. The 1981 Massachusetts Historical Commission form notes that there is "no deed of record" for the transfer.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the temporary light first shone on May 1, 1871, and that in 1872 it moved into a ten-sided black lantern room on a square brick tower of forty-five feet (14 m).[^2] The 1981 form gives the brick tower as 40 feet (12 m) high and 14 feet 7 inches (4.4 m) across, built in 1871 and 1872.[^1] The sources differ on its height: the Digital Archive gives 45 feet (14 m) from base to lantern center.[^3]

The Digital Archive gives 1872 as the year the tower was established and the year the first lens was installed. It describes that lens as a third-and-a-half-order Fresnel.[^3] The 1981 form says a Fresnel lens was replaced in 1976 and that an acrylic optic was installed in 1981.[^1] The Digital Archive and LighthouseFriends describe a third-and-a-half-order Fresnel in the lantern now, so the sources disagree on what it holds.[^3][^2]

The rear light was not in the station's tower. On May 1, 1927, the front light was renamed Hospital Point Range Front Light, and a beacon was placed in the steeple of Beverly's First Baptist Church, using equipment from a lightship.[^2][^6] The Digital Archive records the steeple as the location of the rear light and gives 1927 as its year.[^4] LighthouseFriends says the spire's electric beacon had two lamps, one a spare, and an astronomical clock that turned it on and off at sunset and sunrise.[^2] The form dates the electrification of the front light to 1928.[^1]

## The keepers

William A. Friend was the first keeper, from 1871 to 1874, according to the Digital Archive roster. Joseph H. Herrick followed and served until 1917, the longest term on the roster for this station.[^3]

Levi B. Clark was keeper from 1917 to 1926. The efficiency flag went each year to the lighthouse with the highest general efficiency in its district, and the Bulletin lists Clark's station among the flag winners for 1924 and again for 1925.[^8][^9] The Lighthouse Service index also records the award under his name.[^7]

Richard F. Dixon kept the light from 1926 to 1939, and Arthur A. Small from 1939 to 1945.[^3] LighthouseFriends says Small transferred to Hospital Point in 1939 from Palmer Island, after surviving the Great New England Hurricane of 1938, which claimed his wife, Mabel.[^2] Carl D. Hill was the last keeper on the roster, from 1945 to 1947.[^3]

In 1901 the Lighthouse Board reported that the station had been connected to Beverly's water supply and that the dwelling had been repaired.[^5] LighthouseFriends says volunteers later found the logs kept by the station's first and second keepers, "that record weather observations and important events."[^2]

## After the keepers

The Digital Archive gives 1947 as the year the front light was automated.[^3] The dwelling was altered in 1941 to house twenty men as barracks, and in 1947 it was designated the quarters for the commander of the First Coast Guard District.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the dwelling has housed that commander since 1947.[^2]

A fire swept the First Baptist Church in 1975, but the spire survived, and the rear light stayed active.[^2] In 2009 the Coast Guard agreed that Coast Guard Auxiliary volunteers could open the lighthouse to visitors more often than the annual open house.[^2]

## Today

Hospital Point is Coast Guard property.[^3] The Digital Archive lists the owner as the Coast Guard and the station as not open to the public.[^3] LighthouseFriends says the lighthouse is open to the public only as part of the week-long Beverly Homecoming, that the dwelling is closed, and that the grounds and tower open once a year.[^2] Its account of the 2009 agreement, which let Coast Guard Auxiliary volunteers open the lighthouse more often, does not say how often it opens now. A 1981 form describes the approach as a private road, with the property surrounded by a fence and a gate.[^1] Please do not go past the gate.

The front light is still an active aid to navigation, according to the Digital Archive.[^3] In 1981 the form recorded its status as "Unmanned/Active."[^1] The rear light is in the church steeple. The Digital Archive lists the First Baptist Church as owner of the building and the Coast Guard as owner of the optic, and records that it is not open to the public.[^4]
