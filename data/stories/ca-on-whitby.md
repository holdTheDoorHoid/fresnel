Whitby Lighthouse was a wooden tower on the west pier of Whitby Harbour, on the north shore of Lake Ontario about 48 km (30 mi) east of Toronto.[^1][^2] Its octagonal tower of 1857 served for just over a century before it was removed in 1958.[^1] Two small lights on the harbour's piers mark the entrance today.[^1][^7]

## The place

Whitby Township was surveyed in 1792. Its chief asset was the natural harbour on Lake Ontario, from which grain grown on the farmland to the north was first shipped in 1833.[^1]

An 1899 history of the Great Lakes, as LighthouseFriends quotes it, calls the harbour one of the best on the north side of the lake. Its entrance lies between piers 76 m (250 ft) apart, with about 4.3 m (14 ft) of water in the channel.[^2] "A strong current sets in and out of this harbor," the history says.[^2]

The harbour changed hands several times. In 1850 the harbour and a road to Lake Scugog were sold for $80,400 to a road company, which defaulted. The government took the harbour back in 1863 and sold it in 1864 to the Port Whitby Harbor Company for $35,150.[^2]

## Building the light

The 1899 history says the lighthouse on the west pier was established in 1844 and was "not under the Marine Department."[^2] LighthouseFriends says a much larger octagonal wooden tower was built on the west pier in 1857. It measured 4.3 m (14 ft) at the base and stood about 12 m (nearly 40 ft) tall.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory gives the 1857 tower as 12 m (39 ft) tall, with a lantern and gallery, built by the municipal government.[^3]

LighthouseFriends names Harbour Master Christopher McDermott as the tower's designer and Hugh Chisholm as its general contractor. The page cites no document for either name.[^1]

At the opening of navigation in 1915 a fifth-order dioptric lens replaced the pressed lens in the lantern, improving the fixed white light, according to LighthouseFriends.[^1]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends says that the Department of Marine did not build or run the light for much of its existence, so the names of its keepers are not in government records.[^1] The Department's 1910 keeper list does print a keeper for Whitby, however. It names the company as "Port Whitby Hbr. Co. Ltd." (the Port Whitby Harbor Company), with an appointment date of 1 May 1905.[^4] The two sources disagree on whether the records name the keepers, and the account below follows the list for the company years.

LighthouseFriends lists the company as keeper from 1905 until 1911. Richard Goldring became keeper in 1911 and served until 1923.[^1]

In fiscal 1916-17, during Goldring's time, the Department spent $206.82 on repairs to the Whitby lighthouse. The work was done by day labour, and the table names M. J. Egan in its "contractor or foreman" column.[^6]

## After the keepers

In 1923 an automatic Aga light was installed in the lighthouse. Its characteristic changed from fixed white to occulting white.[^1] The Department's report for fiscal 1923-24 lists the Aga light installation at Whitby among its year's works.[^5] LighthouseFriends names no keeper after Goldring.[^1]

In September 1958 the wooden lighthouse was removed from the west pier when the harbour was improved. LighthouseFriends says it was later taken to a farm west of Cranberry Marsh. The Lighthouse Directory says it was demolished when the piers were rebuilt that year.[^1][^3]

Also in 1958 a skeletal tower with a flashing red light, which flashed every six seconds, went up on the new concrete east pier. Its light stood 7.6 m (25 ft) above the lake.[^1] The 1961 Light List shows a pair of range lights, both fixed red, marking the port entrance in place of the pier light. The rear light stood on shore about 200 m (658 ft) from the front one.[^1]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard's list gives the Whitby Harbour East Pierhead light as a red light on a white cylindrical tower with a red upper portion, 6.5 m high. The light is 10.2 m above the water, and its nominal range is 6 nautical miles. The list marks it as seasonal.[^7] The Lighthouse Directory describes the red light as one second on and one second off.[^3]

The West Breakwater light is a green light flashing every four seconds, on a 4.0 m mast.[^7]

LighthouseFriends reports that in 2021 a white tower at the outer end of the east pier was displaying a flashing red light, and a mast at the south end of the west breakwater a flashing green light.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory says the east pier can be walked and the site is open, but the tower is closed.[^3]
