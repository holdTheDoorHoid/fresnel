Nakalele Point Light stands on the northern tip of Maui's western lobe, down the hill from a pullout on Highway 30 just past mile marker 38, 15.7 miles north of Lahaina.[^1][^2] Its light was established in December 1908, and it has been an automatic, unattended light since October 1923.[^4][^1]

## The place

A Light-House Board notice printed on 21 June 1908 named the site as "the most northerly point of the westerly part of the island of Maui" and said the establishment of the light "will be postponed until further notice."[^5] Six months later, a December notice reported that the light had been established on 10 December 1908.[^4] Neither notice says what hazard the light was meant to mark.

## Building the light

The December notice describes a fixed white light, 20 feet (6 m) above the ground, on a platform on top of a small white house with a red roof.[^4] It gives the position as about 21 degrees 2 minutes north and 156 degrees 35 minutes 30 seconds west, taken from Coast and Geodetic Survey Chart No. 4100.[^4]

The sources do not agree on what stood at the point in the first years. Lighthouse Friends says the Board chose the point in 1908 for a forty-foot wooden mast to carry a temporary light, and that by 1910 a keeper's dwelling stood there, with a boxlike platform on its roof for the fixed white light.[^1] Rowlett says the original light on a mast was replaced in 1910 by a light on a platform atop a keeper's house.[^2] The December 1908 notice already describes a light on a platform on a house, which fits neither account as the first structure.[^4] This story does not choose between them.

## The keepers

The Lighthouse Society's roster names six principal keepers for the station between 1908 and 1923.[^3] The first, George N. Kanaulalena, served from 1908 to 1909. John H. Kanekoa followed for a single year, 1909.[^3]

John M. Hanuna came next. The Society lists him from 1909 to 1915. Lighthouse Friends gives 1910 as the start of his service in its narrative and 1909 in its list of head keepers.[^1][^3] In 1915 John K. Mahoe took charge of the light, and the Society's roster has him there from 1915 to 1916.[^1][^3]

Luther K. Kalama was appointed keeper in 1917 and served until 1923.[^1][^3] John Makahi became keeper in July 1923 and served until October that year.[^1]

## After the keepers

The station was changed to an automatic, unattended light in October 1923, ending Makahi's service.[^1] Lighthouse Friends says the light's characteristic changed to flashing white on automation.[^1]

## Today

The Coast Guard owns the light.[^1] Rowlett lists it as active and gives its access note as "Site open, tower closed."[^2] Lighthouse Friends agrees that the grounds are open and the tower is closed, and says the light sits down the hill from the pullout, by the shore.[^1] The 2025 Coast Guard Light List gives a flash of white every 2.5 seconds, with a range of 6 nautical miles.[^6] Rowlett gives a focal plane of 142 feet (43 m).[^2]

A note on the Lighthouse Friends page describes numerous rock stacks and large rings of white coral at the point.[^1] Visitors should keep to the grounds, since the tower is closed.
