Port Medway Lighthouse is a wooden tower at the outer end of the breakwater in Port Medway harbour, on Nova Scotia's South Shore in Queens County.[^1][^4][^5] It was lit in 1899, and it has stood in a park since 2002.[^1][^4]

The name first belonged to the lighthouse on Medway Head, the headland at the harbour entrance, which was established there in 1851. Lighthouse Friends says the name was transferred to the wharf tower in 1899.[^1]

## The place

Port Medway lies near the mouth of the Medway River. Loyalists from Massachusetts settled it in the mid-eighteenth century, and logging, shipbuilding and fishing followed. At its peak it had six wharves and a population of over 5,000.[^1]

The Region says the light was built to mark the outer end of the breakwater and to help ocean-going ships using the harbour.[^4]

## Building the light

Lighthouse Friends quotes the Department's 1899 report: a light on the eastern end of the breakwater was put in operation on 1 April 1899.[^1] It was a square wooden tower with sloping sides and a square wooden lantern, 10 m (33 ft) high to the vane.[^1] Its fixed red light was 9.4 m (31 ft) above high-water mark and was visible six miles, from a seventh-order dioptric lens.[^1]

The Department's keepers' table for fiscal 1909-10 lists "Port Medway Harbour" with Samuel T. Foster at $100 a year.[^2]

## The keepers

Samuel T. Foster was appointed the first keeper on 17 February 1899, at $100 a year.[^1][^2] Lighthouse Friends gives his service as 1899 to 1919.[^1] The municipality's heritage file says only that he assumed his duties on that date, and that there is no record of how long he served.[^3]

George Hopkins followed, from 1919 to at least 1923.[^1] The Region's park page names Chelsey Whynot among the lightkeepers, without dates.[^4] The heritage file says four keepers followed Foster, and that Cecil Earl Dolliver was the last.[^3]

According to Lighthouse Friends, George McConnell started at $150 in 1942, and was making $345 when he retired on 7 October 1959.[^1] The heritage file gives his years as 1942 to 1959.[^3] Cecil Dolliver then served as caretaker, paid $40 a year, until 1967.[^1][^3][^4]

## After the keepers

Vinyl siding went on the tower in 1979, and the Region says it adorned the walls for several years.[^1][^4] The light was automated on 1 January 1967. The Region says the light changed from red to green that same year.[^4]

The sources do not agree on when the light was decommissioned. The Region's park page and the Lighthouse Directory give 1987.[^4][^5] The heritage file says the Coast Guard decided in January 1989 to decommission it, and Lighthouse Friends gives 4 January 1989.[^3][^1]

For nearly ten years the building stood neglected, according to the heritage file.[^3] Lighthouse Friends says the Medway Area Communities Association set out to save the tower in 1998.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory says the association began restoration in 1988 and that by 1998 the tower was in poor condition.[^5] That 1988 start comes before the 1989 decommissioning date given by the heritage file and Lighthouse Friends.

In July 2000 the Municipality bought the lighthouse on the association's behalf.[^1][^3] In late 2001 the tower was moved several metres to allow the breakwater to be restored.[^3] The restoration and its surroundings cost $602,000.[^1][^5] The park opened in October 2002.[^3][^4]

## Today

The tower stands in Port Medway Lighthouse Park, at the end of Long Cove Road, beside the Harbour Authority wharf.[^1][^4] The Region of Queens Municipality owns the site.[^5] The park is open, and the tower is closed to visitors.[^1][^5]
