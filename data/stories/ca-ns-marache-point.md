Marache Point light stands at the southern entrance to Arichat Harbour, on Isle Madame, Nova Scotia. It was called Arichat Lighthouse until the name was changed after the light was automated.[^1] The Canadian Coast Guard lists it as a fixed white light, with a nominal range of 12 nautical miles, from a square tower with a red upper portion.[^5]

## The place

A petition from merchants and ship-owners, asking for lighthouses at the southern and western entrances to Arichat Harbour, was read in the Nova Scotia House of Assembly in 1848. The government did not build them, and in 1849 local interests were allowed to put up a beacon on Marache Point instead.[^1] The Commissioners of Lighthouses inspected it and found it "quite unsuitable for the object intended". They were confident the beacon's proprietors, with government money, would put it right.[^1]

## Building the light

The beacon was lit in 1851 with Joseph DeCoste as first keeper. Lighthouse Friends says he "lived a half mile away", and no dwelling was built at the light; two people generally slept in a small room in the tower.[^1]

In 1865 the inspector John H. Kendrick called the building "unworthy of the name". He wrote that it had been temporary when first put up, was very small and "leaks badly", and he recommended a new lighthouse with the keeper's dwelling in the same building.[^1] Isle Madame Lighthouses says a larger lighthouse with living space was built by 1869.[^2] Lighthouse Friends gives its contract price as $1,395 and says it was put in operation on 1 October 1869.[^1]

Lighthouse Friends says the lamps and reflectors gave way to a fifth-order lens in 1909.[^1] The Department's report for the fiscal year 1909 to 1910 says a "270° 5th order dioptric illuminating apparatus" was substituted for the catoptric apparatus, under the heading "Arichat, Canso Harbour".[^4] The Preservation Society's history list gives 1922 for a fifth-order lens, which is later than the Department's report.[^3][^4]

## The keepers

Joseph DeCoste kept the light from 1851 to 1875, when his son Christopher took over.[^1] An inspection report of 1877 refers to Keeper DeCoste, who wanted the unfinished upper storey made into bedrooms. Christopher and Adele DeCoste had four children at the time.[^1] In 1896 the keeper was given a hand-operated foghorn to sound in thick or foggy weather.[^1]

Christopher DeCoste kept the light until October 1898, when William LaVache took over. Isle Madame Lighthouses spells the name Lavashe.[^1][^2] Michael McGrath followed in 1918, and Jeffrey LeBlanc in 1929.[^1][^2] Harry LeBlanc was the last keeper, from 1950 until May 1970.[^1][^2]

## After the keepers

In 1949 a new lighthouse was built at Marache Point, with a dwelling, a boathouse and a storehouse.[^1][^2] Commercial power reached the station in 1954, but the light stayed staffed until 1970, when a battery system was installed for power failures.[^1] The other station buildings were declared surplus and sold, and the lighthouse's name was officially changed from Arichat Lighthouse to Marache Point Lighthouse "a few months after automation".[^1][^2]

The Preservation Society's list gives other dates. It has the light automated by 1973 and, in its date box, "Automated by 1993".[^3]

## Today

Lighthouse Friends, citing The Reporter of 12 October 2017, says residents approved a Coast Guard proposal to replace the old lighthouse with a modern modular one, at a public meeting in Arichat on 3 October 2017.[^1] The Isle Madame Lighthouse Preservation Society backed the plan. Its chair said restoring the old tower would cost more than $50,000, and that a replacement resembling the old one was chosen because no source of funding was available.[^1]

Isle Madame Lighthouses says a modular, aluminum-framed building was brought in by road in January 2018, and that it "looks like the original pepper pot lighthouse that preceded it".[^2]

The station is owned by the Government of Canada.[^3] The Coast Guard's List of Lights gives the present tower as 7.9 m high, with a focal height of 10.2 m and a nominal range of 12 nautical miles, lit year round.[^5] Lighthouse Friends says the grounds are open and the tower closed. Visitors must walk the last part of the route, because a bridge has been removed.[^1] The Preservation Society's access entry reads "Requires Hike".[^3] Isle Madame Lighthouses says the keeper's house is now a private dwelling in Arichat.[^2]
