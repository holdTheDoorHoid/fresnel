Horse Island Light stands on a small island about a mile west of Sackets Harbor, on Lake Ontario, in Jefferson County, New York.[^1][^2] A light has been shown from the island since 1831. The brick tower that stands there now was begun in 1870 and lit in 1871, and the station was discontinued in 1957. The island is closed to the public.[^2][^8]

## The place

The island lies about a mile west of the village of Sackets Harbor, and LighthouseFriends describes it as joined to the mainland by a causeway at low tide.[^1][^7] In the War of 1812 British troops landed there on 29 May 1813 and drove the militia back across that causeway, though the attack on the port itself failed.[^7]

The light was for the approach to the harbor. In 1838 Lieutenant Charles T. Platt wrote that "It is by means of this light that the mariner finds his way to Sackett's Harbor," a line LighthouseFriends quotes from his report.[^1]

## Building the light

Congress appropriated $4,000 for a lighthouse near Sackets Harbor in March 1831. The state gave up its jurisdiction over the island that spring, and the federal government bought it from T. L. Ogden for $500. Later that year a one-and-a-half-story keeper's dwelling with a tower rising from the middle of its roof was built on the island.[^1][^2]

A fifth-order Fresnel lens went into the lantern room in 1857.[^1][^2] By the late 1860s the buildings were in poor condition. A Lighthouse Board report of 1869, as LighthouseFriends quotes it, called them "in such dilapidated condition that further repairs cannot be made," and recommended a new dwelling with a tower attached.[^1] Congress provided the money on 15 July 1870. Work on a new brick tower, on a limestone foundation, began that August about 300 yards (274 m) southeast of the old one. The tower was not finished until April 1871, and the old lighthouse was then torn down.[^1][^2][^8]

LighthouseFriends says the worn-out lens was replaced by a new one from the general depot in 1890.[^1] In August 1899 the square tower received a ten-foot (3 m) addition. The Board's report for the year ended 30 June 1900 records that "The tower was built 10 feet higher."[^1][^3]

## The keepers

The U.S. Lighthouse Society's archive lists nine principal keepers, from John McNitt, who served from 1832 to 1841, to George H. Ward, who is listed until 1926.[^2] Samuel McNitt kept the light from 1844 to 1860. LighthouseFriends says he had been a militia captain whose company held its ground in the 1813 landing, that he was regarded as a local hero for it, and that he was later offered the keeper's post.[^1]

Horace G. Holloway is listed from 1884 to 1909. LighthouseFriends says he was appointed in 1883.[^1][^2] His son-in-law George H. Ward took over in 1909.[^1] Ward was the keeper in August 1918, when he went to the aid of a power boat's occupants.[^4]

When Ward was transferred to Oswego in July 1926, the government hired Schuyler S. Simmons as a caretaker with the title of lamplighter. He lived on the island and farmed its seventeen acres until his death in 1933.[^1][^8] The archive's roster does not name Simmons. It lists a lamp lighter, James P. Westcott, from at least 1939.[^2]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

LighthouseFriends tells of a rescue during Horace Holloway's keepership. A sailboat with two military men aboard disappeared off the island in a strong northwest wind. The keeper launched the station boat and reached the men after they had been in the water for nearly an hour.[^1] The account gives no exact date.

On 22 August 1918 a power boat sank after striking rocks near the light. The Lighthouse Service Bulletin reports that George H. Ward, "keeper of Sacketts Harbor Light Station," went to help the boat's occupants.[^4]

## After the keepers

In July 1919 the tower was repaired. The Lighthouse Service Bulletin reported that the cracked mortar and damaged bricks had been removed and the tower painted with a waterproofing compound.[^5] In 1925 the Lighthouse Service changed the light from oil to acetylene and from fixed to flashing.[^6] LighthouseFriends says it became an automated light at that time.[^1]

In 1957 the lighthouse was declared excess and sold by the General Services Administration to a private buyer.[^1][^8] LighthouseFriends says the light's work had by then passed to an automated light on a metal skeletal tower.[^1] Private owners kept the lighthouse as a vacation home until 2017.[^1]

## Today

In July 2017 Campaign 1776, a project of the Civil War Trust, announced the purchase of the 24-acre island, the first War of 1812 land the Trust had saved. The Trust planned to transfer it to the New York state park system to join the Sackets Harbor Battlefield State Historic Site.[^7] Lighthouse Digest reported in 2019 that the state's Office of Parks, Recreation, and Historic Preservation had finalized ownership, and that the lighthouse had been off-limits to the general public since 1957.[^8] A regional director for the state Office of Parks said the state might add trails and a dock, and was looking into week-long rentals of the lighthouse, a process that may take a few years.[^8]

Trees have grown up on the island, and LighthouseFriends notes that only the top of the lighthouse can be seen from Sackets Harbor.[^1] The island is closed to visitors. It should not be approached or landed on.
