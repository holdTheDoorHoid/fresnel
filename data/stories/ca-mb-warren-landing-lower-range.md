The lower Warren Landing range is a pair of lights that mark the mouth of the Nelson River, at the north end of Lake Winnipeg in Manitoba, where the lake empties into the river.[^3][^9] The front light stands on Purvis Island, and the rear light is 780 m (about half a mile) away.[^10][^9]

## The place

The entrance was the problem before the lights. In its report for 1903 the Department of Marine and Fisheries listed the small harbours at Warren's Landing, "entrance to Nelson river," and said that "the narrow, crooked and none too deep entrances" had been "marked out by range beacons."[^1] The settlement, Lighthouse Friends says, took its name from John Warren, a Hudson's Bay Company man who died there of a wound suffered in the Red River Rebellion.[^8]

## Building the light

The Department erected two pairs of range lights at the mouth of the Nelson River, and its report for 1908 describes both.[^3] The lower pair, it says, were "two inclosed wooden towers, square in plan, with sloping sides." The front tower stood "on the southeast end of the island, opposite Warrens landing," 7.6 m (25 ft) high to the ventilator on its lantern, with its light shown 9.8 m (32 ft) above the water. The back tower stood a third of a mile from the front, 9.1 m (30 ft) high, with its light at 11.6 m (38 ft).[^3]

The report gives the visible distance as "eleven miles."[^3] The Coast Guard list gives the front light a focal height of 13.0 m and a nominal range of 14, with no unit stated in the entry.[^10]

The sources also disagree on where on the island the front tower stands. The 1908 report says the southeast end, the current list says the southwest end of Purvis Island, and Rowlett places it on the east side of the river entrance.[^3][^10][^9]

The Department's report for 1905 says M. J. Egan had been employed as foreman of works on the upper lakes and Lake Winnipeg.[^2] The 1908 report says the towers were "erected by day's labour, under the supervision of" Egan, and gives one cost for them, $1,725.51.[^3]

In 1909 the Department listed the front light's equipment as "A 7th order 180° Chance lens and brass stand with duplex lamp," and named the pair the "Warrens Island Range."[^4]

## The keepers

One keeper, Hugh McDonald, is listed for both pairs of range lights. The Department's report for 1911 gives "Warren Landing Ranges Hugh McDonald . . Aug. 14, 1907," and its report for 1914 gives "H. McDonald Sept 14,1907."[^5][^6] The two reports differ on the month of his appointment. The Department's 1908 statement of lightkeepers gives "Hugh McDonald" at Warren's Landing with an appointment date of 25 August 1905, and none of the reports explains the difference.[^11] The report for 1913 lists "Warren Landing ranges" as one station.[^7]

Lighthouse Friends says McDonald was the first keeper and served until 1924, when J. Robertson took charge. It dates his service from 1908, not 1907, and gives the later keeper's name as "Roberts" in one place and "J. Robertson" in another, responsible for the lights "through at least 1937."[^8]

## After the keepers

Lighthouse Friends says that at some point skeletal towers replaced the enclosed towers used for the back lights of both pairs, but that the original wooden front towers remain in use. It gives no date for the change.[^8] The Coast Guard list gives the rear light as a square skeleton tower 13.8 m high, with a focal height of 17.0 m, and gives the front light a focal height of 13.0 m.[^10] Rowlett describes the rear light as a "square pyramidal steel skeletal tower" of 13.5 m (44 ft).[^9]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns and manages the site, Rowlett says.[^9] Rowlett describes the lower pair as "Accessible only by boat," with the "Site open, tower closed."[^9] The Coast Guard list marks all four Warren Landing range lights "Seasonal," while Rowlett lists them as active.[^10][^9] Lighthouse Friends says the range can be seen from Warren Landing itself.[^8]
