Sangster Island Light marks the south-east tip of a small island in the Strait of Georgia, south of Lasqueti Island in British Columbia.[^1][^2] It is a white cylindrical tower with a red band at the top.[^2] The Coast Guard list gives its characteristic as a quick-flashing red light, a focal height of 17.1 m, and a range of 5 nautical miles.[^2]

The Lighthouse Directory gives the tower as 5 m (17 ft) round, painted white with a red top. It says the light stands opposite the Ballenas Island lighthouse, "marking a narrow passage in the Strait of Georgia."[^1] The Directory gives no date for the light.[^1] The island is reached only by boat.[^1] The site is open, but the tower is closed.[^1]

The island's name appears in *The Encyclopedia of Raincoast Place Names*.[^3] Its entry for Sangster Island goes on to describe James Sangster (c. 1812 to 1858), a native of Port Glasgow, Scotland, who joined the Hudson's Bay Company's marine service in 1827 as an apprentice aboard the *Eagle*. Eventually he was given his first command, the paddle steamer *Beaver*.[^3] The text available online does not say how the island got its name, and this story does not link the island to him.[^3] The BC Geographical Names Office has not yet transferred its origin note for the name to its website.[^4]

The Coast Guard list gives the light as lit year round, with a radar reflector fitted.[^2] The Directory also lists it as active.[^1]
