Bass River Lighthouse is a white wooden tower on the western side of the mouth of the Bass River, where the river meets Cobequid Bay in Colchester County, Nova Scotia. It was built in 1907 as the easternmost beacon on the bay, and since 1994 it has been in private hands.[^3][^4]

## The place

The light was the most easterly beacon on Cobequid Bay, in the eastern part of the Minas Basin.[^3] According to Lighthouse Friends, economic activity in Bass River in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries was focused on lumber-related industries, with furniture production, shipbuilding and timber export the main industries.[^3] Shipbuilding took place at Saint's Rest, the area around the light.[^3][^4]

## Building the light

The Department's chief engineer reported on the new light in its report for the twelve months ending 31 March 1908. The light stood on the west side of the mouth of the river, in an enclosed wooden building, square in plan, with a square wooden lantern. The whole was painted white, and the building was 9.8 m (32 ft) high to the ventilator.[^1] The tower stood on the shore about 122 m (400 ft) inside the point.[^1]

The light was a fixed red light, shown 11.9 m (39 ft) above high water. The report gives its range as 7 miles from all points of approach by water, and its apparatus was a sixth-order dioptric lens.[^1]

The contract went to S. Beekwith of Bass River. The report spells it that way; Colchester's page gives Samuel Beckwith.[^1][^4] The Department allowed him an extra $200 because the site was changed after he had brought all his materials to the old site and had started construction work.[^1]

Lighthouse Friends gives 1907 as the year of building and says David Vance was appointed the first keeper on 24 October 1907.[^3]

## The keepers

David Vance was appointed the first keeper at $100 a year.[^3] The Department's statement of light-keepers, printed in 1912, lists David Vance at Bass River.[^2] Lighthouse Friends says he kept the light through at least 1924, when his salary had risen to $150.[^3] No other keeper's name appears in the sources read.

## After the keepers

The sources give different dates for the light's end. Colchester says it was deactivated in 1992, Lighthouse Friends says around 1992, and the Nova Scotia Lighthouse Preservation Society (NSLPS) lists it as discontinued in 1991.[^4][^3][^5]

NSLPS also gives two dates for automation. Its history list says "automated by this date" against 1973. Its summary says "Automated by c. 1992".[^5] The same page records a move in 1966: the light was shifted about 300 m (1,000 ft), and its focal height was raised from 11.9 m to 12.5 m (39 ft to 41 ft).[^5]

NSLPS's history lists a sixth-order dioptric lens showing a fixed red light from 1922 to 1941, and a fixed white light in 1990.[^5]

The light went out of government hands in 1994. Colchester says it passed from the Crown into private ownership that year, and Lighthouse Friends says it was sold in 1994.[^4][^3] Lighthouse Friends says a one-storey dwelling was later built into the base of the tower as a summer home.[^3]

## Today

The lighthouse is privately owned. NSLPS asks visitors to take their photographs from the road, and Lighthouse Friends says the grounds and tower are closed.[^5][^3] NSLPS records that the light still stands but no longer operates.[^5]
