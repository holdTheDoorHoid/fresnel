The Balache Point light was first lit on 1 December 1905 on the Cape Breton side of the Gut of Canso, the narrow strait between Cape Breton Island and the Nova Scotia mainland.[^1][^2] The strait, about 25 km long, narrows to 832 m between Balache Point and Cape Porcupine on the mainland, and the light was built to mark that stretch.[^2] Since 1963 a pair of range lights on the point has guided ships to the northern entrance of the canal at the Cape Breton end of the causeway.[^2]

## Building the light

The point has had more than one name. The Department's report calls it "McMillan point (Balache point)", and its list of new lights and changes for 1907-8 calls it McMillan's Point. The Coast Guard's current list uses Balache Point.[^1][^4][^6]

The Department's report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1905, submitted in March 1906, records that a tower was erected on McMillan point and the light put in operation on 1 December 1905. The tower was a square wooden building, 9.8 m (32 ft) high to the top of its ventilator. It showed a fixed white dioptric light of the seventh order, 16 m (53 ft) above high water and visible 12 miles. Jas. McDonnell, of Margaree, built it by contract for $545.[^1]

By 1907-8 the apparatus had changed. The Department's list of new lights and changes to existing lights gives McMillan's Point a 270-degree, fifth-order French lens with a duplex lamp, showing white.[^4] The list does not say why.

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends names John B. Chisholm as the first keeper, hired at an annual salary of $205, and gives his service as 1905 to 1912.[^2] The Department's keepers' tables place him at McMillan's Point. The table in the report labelled 1907 has "Chisholm, John B" against it, and the table in the report labelled 1911 has "John B. Chisholm" beside McMillans Point. Both give his appointment as 2 December 1905.[^3][^5]

Lighthouse Friends continues with Angus McMaster (1912 to 1919), Daniel McLellan (1919 to 1921) and Allan Nicholson (1921 to 1942). It lists Catherine Nicholson last, as keeper from 1942 to 1954.[^2] These later names and dates rest on Lighthouse Friends alone.

## After the keepers

Lighthouse Friends says the light was discontinued as the causeway across the strait neared completion, and that the causeway was finished on 13 April 1955.[^2] In 1963 a set of range lights was established on the point to guide vessels to the northern entrance of the canal. Both range lights were first shown from square wooden towers, and a skeletal tower was put up for the front light in 1991.[^2]

The Coast Guard's current list shows both range lights as occulting red lights, lit for 10 seconds and dark for 1 second in every 11. The front light is on a square skeleton tower and the rear on a white pyramid tower. Both are marked seasonal, not year-round.[^6]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the light.[^2] Lighthouse Friends says the grounds and the tower are closed, and it suggests watching the range lights from the south side of the swing bridge at the canal.[^2] The rear range stands on a grassy knoll that is home to a cemetery.[^2] The oldest of its six headstones marks a child who died in 1795, at the age of six.[^2] The grounds are closed, so keep off them.
