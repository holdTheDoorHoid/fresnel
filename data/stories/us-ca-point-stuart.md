Point Stuart Light stood on the west side of Angel Island, in San Francisco Bay.[^1][^2] Rowlett's directory dates it to 1915 and lists it as inactive.[^1]

The first "lighthouse" on the island was not a lighthouse at all. It was a manned fog bell on Point Knox, at the southwest corner of the island, struck in fog by clockwork machinery driven by a weight that an attendant rewound every few hours.[^3] Point Stuart lay about half a mile (800 m) north of Point Knox, on the west side of the island, where it marked the entrance to Raccoon Strait between Angel Island and the mainland.[^1]

The Commissioner of Lighthouses reported for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1915 that electric sirens had been established at "Point Blunt and Point Stuart, Angel Island, San Francisco Bay, Cal."[^2] The Lighthouse Service Bulletin said the remote-control stations there, with 500-watt incandescent lamps in lens lanterns and electric sirens, had been in successful operation at Point Blunt and Point Stuart since 11 February 1915.[^4]

The controlling station was at a keeper's dwelling, about a mile from Point Stuart and two miles from Point Blunt. The Bulletin does not name the building.[^4] The same Commissioner's report records electric lights and fog signals "controlled from Angel Island Station", and work to enlarge the keepers' quarters there.[^5]

The Angel Island Conservancy says that in 1915 a second light was added on Point Stuart, and another keeper with it.[^3] Rowlett says the light was kept by keepers from Point Knox and later from Point Blunt.[^1]

The Conservancy says the Coast Guard decided to build modern facilities on Point Blunt, on the island's south-east corner. It says the new facility, completed in 1960, "did not have a lighthouse in the traditional manner." Instead there was a light and a foghorn on a low building, a watch structure, and quarters for four keepers.[^3] Rowlett dates the Coast Guard station there to 1961, when it had several keeper's houses.[^1]

Rowlett says the Point Stuart light has since been replaced by an offshore light buoy, USCG 6-4350.[^1] The sources read do not give a year for the light going out, or for the buoy.[^1][^3] The US Lighthouse Society archive records Point Stuart as not existing.[^6]

According to Rowlett, the site belongs to California State Parks and is managed by Angel Island State Park. The site and tower are closed. The light sits on a platform halfway up a steep bluff, which Rowlett describes as practically inaccessible.[^1]
