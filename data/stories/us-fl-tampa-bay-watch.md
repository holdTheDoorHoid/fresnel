The Tierra Verde Lighthouse, also known as the Tampa Bay Watch Lighthouse, is a lighthouse-inspired building on Cunningham Key in Tierra Verde, Pinellas County, Florida.[^2][^3] Tampa Bay Watch built it in 2005.[^1][^2] The Coast Guard permits its light as a private aid to navigation.[^1][^2]

Cunningham Key was "just another small spot of land" in southern Tampa Bay until the 1960s, when the Department of Transportation began dredging nearby and building roads and causeways. The key became part of a causeway system connecting Tierra Verde to Fort De Soto.[^1] It once held a single family residence and Rita's Bait Shop, which was eventually abandoned. The property was put up for sale in 1997. After Pinellas County and the State of Florida's Greenways Program worked together to secure it, the property was purchased in 2000, and Tampa Bay Watch runs its programs there under a management agreement.[^1]

Tampa Bay Watch's own history says the tower follows the design of the Boca Grande light. It gives the tower's height as 74 feet (22.6 m) and says its light, a quarter-second flash every four seconds, shows over the mouth of Tampa Bay. The history calls it the first privately held aid to navigation that the Coast Guard had permitted in Florida in 54 years.[^1] A 2011 blog post says the light was modeled on the Boca Grande Lighthouse and was "the first privately held aid to navigation allowed in Florida."[^3]

The Florida Lighthouse Association lists the building among its faux lighthouses. It notes that the apparent lantern is an atrium inside the education center, and that the Coast Guard maintains the light.[^2] Visits depend on Tampa Bay Watch's programs and facility policies, and the association says there is no public tower-climbing program.[^2]

The education center was renamed the Auer Marine Education Center in 2021. Visitors can buy a Lighthouse Passport book in Tierra Verde and have it stamped.[^1]
