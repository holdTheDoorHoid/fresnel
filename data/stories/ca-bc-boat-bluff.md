Boat Bluff Lightstation sits on the southwest point of Sarah Island on British Columbia's Inside Passage, marking the southern entrance to Tolmie Channel. Its light began in 1907 as an unwatched gas beacon, and the keepers' houses came later.[^1][^2]

## The place

Ships travelling north on the Inside Passage have to change course here to enter narrow Tolmie Channel, where the tidal currents run fast; Lighthouse Friends says the manoeuvre has been compared to threading the eye of a needle and is especially dangerous at night.[^1] Parks Canada's statement for the Canadian Register of Historic Places describes the same water as a narrow cross-entrance to Tolmie Channel and Sarah Passage, on a route along which "canneries, lumbering camps and mines had begun to develop" around the time the light was built.[^3]

## Building the light

The Department of Marine and Fisheries report printed in 1908, which covers the nine months ending 31 March 1907, lists "Boat bluff, Sarah island" among seventeen gas beacons placed in service in British Columbia, and its table of beacons describes Boat Bluff as "steel framework, gas-light."[^2][^4] The heritage statement calls the first light an unwatched steel frame tower, one of a type then favoured as economical and suited to narrow channels.[^3] Lighthouse Friends describes the 1907 light as a white tank on a white steel truss, with a red steel frame above it holding the lantern; it was unwatched and eclipsed automatically at short intervals.[^1]

The first watched tower came in 1930, according to the heritage statement, and the present aluminium skeleton tower of 1979 is the third on the site.[^3] That tower is 7 m (24 ft) tall, with the light at 11.6 m (38 ft) above the water.[^1] The register entry also lists five contributing buildings, among them the 1961 assistant keeper's dwelling, the 1968 principal keeper's dwelling and the 1973 engine room.[^3]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends' roster begins with John William Webster (1932 to 1934), followed by Tom White, who was keeper from 1934 to at least 1937.[^1] It lists seventeen more keepers from the 1960s on, from Clayton Ralph Marshall to Spencer Wilson, who was there in 2024.[^1] A 1960 notice in the Department of Transport staff magazine, as quoted by Lighthouse Friends, names H. Shorson as the lightkeeper.[^1]

In 2003, families lived with the keepers at every staffed British Columbia light except one rotational station, and children were schooled at home.[^5] That year the keeper, Mike Higgins, and his assistant, Gerry LaRose, split the day into two shifts so that one of them was always on duty. The day began at 0330 with the local marine weather, and reports went out every three hours until 2130, work the heritage statement ties to the people of Klemtu, who depend on the bulletins for float-plane supplies and medical flights.[^3][^5] Relief keeper Chris Mills, who worked there in the mid-1990s, remembered "the sound of the fog horn echoing off the hills."[^5]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

On 21 March 1914 the steamship *British Empire*, entering North Passage, Finlayson Channel, struck a rock on the east side of Boat Bluff and was badly damaged at the bow below the waterline. She continued to Vancouver for permanent repairs.[^6]

On 26 February 1936 the schooner *Maid of Orleans* ran onto the island at 4:00 a.m. in a heavy snowstorm, unable to see the light or hear the fog signal. Lighthouse Friends, citing *The Vancouver Sun* of 2 March 1936, says she slid off six hours later and sank, and the fifteen aboard rowed ashore and were looked after by keeper Tom White and his wife until a passing ship took them off.[^1]

## Today

Lighthouse Friends' keeper list runs to 2022 and at least 2024, and it gives the owner as the Canadian Coast Guard; it lists the grounds as open and the tower as closed.[^1] The station is the keepers' home, and in 2003 keeper Mike Higgins said visitors were welcome if they came by small vessel or helicopter.[^5] The lighthouse is best seen from the water, and is visible from the Inside Passage ferry between Prince Rupert and Port Hardy.[^1]
