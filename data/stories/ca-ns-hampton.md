Hampton Lighthouse is a white wooden tower with a red roof, standing on a hill above Chute Cove on the Bay of Fundy shore of Annapolis County. It was built in 1911, and since 2001 it has been owned by a local historical society rather than the federal government.[^1][^2][^3]

## The place

The light stands in Hampton, on the Bay of Fundy shore.[^6][^2] It overlooks Chute Cove, which Lighthouse Friends describes as a place where, depending on the tide, "vessels bob lazily on the water or rest on the gravel bottom."[^2] The Coast Guard's list places the light east of the western breakwater at Chute Cove, and gives its name as Hampton Breakwater.[^6]

## Building the light

The lighthouse was built in November 1911 by Joseph Marshall, on a parcel of land bought from Lloyd Brooks.[^3][^2] Lighthouse Friends says the tower was built under a contract to J. F. Titus for $585, and that a further $89 went on inspection fees and the purchase of the site.[^2] The Nova Scotia Lighthouse Preservation Society names the contractor John Titus.[^3] The Department's report on works for the year ended 31 March 1912 describes "a 33-foot high square wooden tower with sloping sides, surmounted by a square wooden lantern," and lists the contractor as J. F. Titus, Hampton.[^4] The tower stands about 10 m (33 ft) high.[^2]

The light was a kerosene lamp, which had to be lit each night at sunset and extinguished at daybreak. It was fitted with a sixth-order dioptric lens, which is still in place.[^1] No dwelling went with the light, so the keeper lived in the village.[^2][^1]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends calls Herbert Foster the first keeper, hired at an annual salary of $180, and says he looked after the light until at least 1923.[^2] The Department's statement of lightkeepers, printed in its 1913 annual report, lists "H. Foster" against Hampton, appointed 2 December 1911 at $180 a year.[^5] That salary matches the figure Lighthouse Friends gives for Herbert Foster, which suggests the two entries are the same man.[^2][^5] The Preservation Society gives a different start. It names Tom Burton as the first keeper, who served only briefly before Herbert Foster took over.[^3] The Preservation Society says that oil and other supplies came once a year by ship, usually in June, aboard the buoy boat "Dollard". A tender then brought them ashore, and the men rolled the oil drums up the hill to the light.[^3] Lighthouse Friends gives later keepers as Vernon Dunn (from the 1920s into the 1950s), Claude Hamilton (to 1957) and Frank Copley (from 1957).[^2] The Preservation Society spells the last name Cropley.[^3]

## After the keepers

The light was electrified in the 1940s, which soon ended the need for a full-time keeper.[^1][^3] In 1973 it was fully automated.[^1] Between February and May 1993 the Coast Guard re-shingled the sides and repaired the lantern.[^3]

In 2001 ownership passed from the federal Department of Fisheries and Oceans to the Hampton Lighthouse Society, for $1. The Register of Historic Places calls it the second lighthouse in Canada transferred under the 96-1 Directive, and the first given to a non-profit community group.[^1][^2] The agreement was signed at a community celebration in the Hampton Community Hall on 30 November 2001.[^3] The lighthouse was designated a Provincial Heritage Property on 15 November 2007, and the society changed its name to the Hampton Lighthouse and Historical Society in 2008.[^2][^1]

## Today

The Hampton Lighthouse and Historical Society owns the lighthouse.[^3][^2] The Coast Guard's list still shows the light as operating: a red light that flashes every four seconds, 18.9 m above the water, with a range of two nautical miles.[^6] The Preservation Society's page disagrees. It gives the light's characteristic as fixed white, and its summary table says the light is not still operating, although its light details say it is operational.[^3] Lighthouse Friends reports that the society opens the lighthouse from 1 to 4 p.m. on Saturdays and Sundays from July to mid-September, and that the grounds and the tower are open.[^2]
