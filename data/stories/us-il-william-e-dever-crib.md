The William E. Dever Crib Light is a navigation light on a water-intake crib in Cook County, Illinois, about 2.6 nautical miles (three miles) offshore from Chicago. The crib is named for William Emmett Dever, who was mayor of Chicago from 1923 to 1927.[^1]

## The place

LighthouseFriends says the city's water intakes were placed out in Lake Michigan, beyond a shoreline that had grown polluted.[^1] By 1860, it says, sewage and waste from the city's slaughterhouses and other industries had made the river a cesspool.[^1] The Dever Crib is one of those intakes. It stands beside an older one, the Carter H. Harrison Crib, completed in 1900 and named for Carter Henry Harrison, who was mayor from 1879 to 1887.[^1]

## Building the light

The light had been on the Harrison crib before it moved.[^1] LighthouseFriends describes a red skeletal tower there, with a gray lantern room, that once showed one red flash every three seconds.[^1]

LighthouseFriends quotes the Commissioner of Lighthouses as noting, in 1932, a submarine cable from Chicago Harbor Lighthouse to the Harrison crib, and as recording that the crib's tower and structure had been changed to hold machinery, an air whistle and a revolving beacon.[^1] The Lighthouse Service's 1934 Bulletin describes two power and control cables laid to the Carter Harrison crib. They were laid separately to space them about 300 feet (91 m) apart.[^3]

A photograph on LighthouseFriends is captioned "Harrison Crib in 1931 with Dever Crib under construction", so the new crib was already being built by then.[^1]

The archive gives 1935 as the year the Dever Crib was established.[^2] LighthouseFriends says it was placed in operation that year, and that the navigation light moved from the Harrison crib to the top of the taller Dever crib. The Harrison crib was deactivated, then reactivated when the city's water needs kept growing.[^1]

In 1998 the tunnel system that served the older Harrison crib collapsed, and that crib was taken out of service.[^1]

A lighthouse-enthusiast site gives the reason for building the Dever crib beside the Harrison crib: the Harrison crib "showed aging signs after 30 years."[^4]

## Today

The crib belongs to the Chicago Department of Water Management, and LighthouseFriends reports that its tower is closed.[^1] The US Lighthouse Society's archive lists the crib as existing and as not open to the public.[^2]

Security systems have covered all the intake cribs since 2002, and vessels are kept out of the buoyed zone that surrounds each one.[^1] LighthouseFriends notes that the crib is best seen by boat or plane.[^1] The same lighthouse-enthusiast site says the NOAA Great Lakes Environmental Research Laboratory maintains a weather station at the crib.[^4]
