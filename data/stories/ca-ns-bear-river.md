Bear River light is a wooden lighthouse on Winchester Point, where the Bear River enters the Annapolis Basin in Nova Scotia.[^1] It was put into operation in 1905 and stopped serving as a navigation aid in 2001.[^3] A local historical society has plans to turn the site into a park.[^3]

## The place

The register's statement of significance calls it a secondary coastal light for the approach from the Annapolis Basin. It says the light warns vessels of the rocky shoals near the channel into the river, and that it supported the development of the scallop-fishing and lumber industries of the nearby town of Digby.[^2] When it was built the lighthouse stood in an open area. Lighthouse Friends says it is now surrounded by dense woods.[^3]

## Building the light

The Department of Marine and Fisheries recorded the new light in its annual report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1905. The report says a lighthouse "was erected on Winchester point, western side of entrance to Bear river, south side of Annapolis basin," and was put in operation on May 1, 1905.[^1]

The building stood on ground 13.7 m (45 ft) above high water and 30.5 m (100 ft) back from the water's edge. It was a square wooden tower with sloping sides, surmounted by a square lantern, painted white, and 9.8 m (32 ft) high to the lantern's ventilator.[^1] The light was a fixed red dioptric light of the seventh order, 21.9 m (72 ft) above high water, visible for nine miles from all points of approach by water.[^1] The municipality's heritage registration, made in 2022, gives the height as 21 m (69 ft), so the two figures differ by about three feet.[^6] The work was done by contract for $494, by a contractor from Granville Ferry, Nova Scotia.[^1]

## The keepers

William Hunt was the first keeper. Lighthouse Friends gives his appointment as 10 April 1905, at a salary of $150 a year.[^3] The Department's 1906 list of light-keepers puts him at Bear River.[^7] He kept the light until 1913, when H.B. Warren was appointed keeper.[^3] Lighthouse Friends has Warren in post until at least 1923.[^3] The sources give nothing more about either man.

## After the keepers

The light was automated in 1973 and decommissioned as a navigation aid in 2001.[^3][^5] The Lighthouse Directory describes it as "inactive since 2001."[^4] The Smith's Cove society says that afterwards the site grew over with scrub and trees, and the tower fell into disrepair.[^5]

Ownership changed slowly. On 14 December 2009 Digby Town Council voted to acquire the lighthouse, with the Bear River Lighthouse Society, formed in 2002, to go on caring for the structure. The transfer was delayed for many years.[^3] The federal government recognized the tower as a heritage lighthouse on 15 February 2015 under the Heritage Lighthouse Protection Act.[^8] The municipality took ownership in the spring of 2021, and the Smith's Cove Historical Society signed a lease that year to care for the light.[^5][^9] Restoration of the tower started in August 2022, Lighthouse Friends says.[^3]

## Today

The municipality owns the lighthouse, and the Smith's Cove Historical Society is the site manager.[^3][^4] The grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^3] The society's plans include removing trees so that the park has a view over the Annapolis Basin.[^3] A trail runs from the light down to the river, 13 m below.[^3] The Nova Scotia Lighthouse Preservation Society lists the tower as still standing.[^9]
