St. Johns Light is a concrete lighthouse at Mayport, less than a mile south of the mouth of the St. Johns River in Jacksonville, Florida. The Coast Guard established it in 1954 to replace a lightship offshore. It stands inside Naval Station Mayport, and the public cannot visit it.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]

## The place

The river mouth was a changing place. The first river light, built in 1830 near what is now the south jetty, stood where the nomination describes a vicinity "characterized by dynamic natural forces including river channel shifting and shoreline erosion."[^7] The first light's foundation was undermined as the shoreline changed, and it collapsed in 1833.[^8] The third river light, a brick tower of 74 feet (23 m), was discontinued in 1930.[^9][^10]

In the 1920s mariners reported problems with the third light. The most serious was that it was hard to see in dense fog. The nomination says this contributed to several maritime accidents at the river's mouth, and the government decided to replace the light with an offshore lightship.[^11]

That lightship was LV 84. It was built in 1907 and served off Brunswick, Georgia, until 1929.[^12] It reached the St. Johns station in 1929 and needed a crew of 15.[^13][^14] The nomination puts it five miles offshore and gives its light a range of 14 miles in clear weather.[^13][^12] LighthouseFriends puts the lightship seven miles offshore instead.[^6]

A 1934 Lighthouse Service Bulletin reports a night sighting. On 30 November 1933 the master of relief lightship No. 109, at Tybee Light, Georgia, was on a voyage from the St. Johns station to Charleston, South Carolina. The moon was nearly full, the sky clear and the wind calm, and the bulletin says the St. Johns lightship "was visible 21 miles."[^15][^16] The two figures disagree. The bulletin's is a single sighting on one clear night.

A naval air station was set up at the river mouth in 1941. It operated through the war, was then turned over to the Coast Guard as a training camp, and went back to the Navy in 1948 when it was commissioned as Naval Station Mayport.[^17][^18] It has been an aircraft carrier homeport since 1956.[^19]

## Building the light

In 1952 the Coast Guard's Civil Engineering Unit-Miami contracted with Steward & Skinner, a Miami architecture firm, to design the new light. The plans were approved in April 1953.[^20][^21] John Llewellyn Skinner's design put modern classical elements on the tower and functional modernist styling on the two one-story wings.[^22] The St. Johns Light was Skinner's only lighthouse.[^23][^24]

The Coast Guard bought the land from private owners between May and November 1953. The James H. Small Construction Company of Jacksonville built the station from late 1953 into 1954, for $78,897.[^25][^26][^27] The buildings were a lighthouse, a storage building and two duplex dwellings with garages.[^28] The light was officially established on 1 October 1954.[^29]

The light was built to run its fog signal and radio beacon by remote control. The nomination calls it the only Florida light designed that way from the start.[^30] The fog signal stood on a tower in 37 feet (11 m) of water, seaward of the south jetty, with an equipment room 40 feet (12 m) above the water.[^31][^32] The radio beacon broadcast a Morse "R" (dot-dash-dot) on 306 kilocycles, through a 4,800-foot (1,463 m) cable to a 175-foot (53 m) antenna on land.[^33][^34]

The tower's height is in dispute. The nomination gives 49 feet (15 m) of reinforced concrete, while LighthouseFriends gives 64 feet (20 m).[^35][^36] The nomination gives the focal plane as 83 feet (25 m) above sea level.[^37] Inside, a 60-step concrete stair climbs the tower's interior wall to the service room.[^38]

## The keepers

When the light opened in 1954 it had a crew of four Coast Guard personnel who served as lighthouse keepers. Their families lived in two duplex dwellings about 300 feet (90 m) west of the lighthouse.[^64] The nomination does not name them.

The nomination says the light was the last lighthouse in Florida built to be run by resident keepers,[^42] and that it was the last manned lighthouse constructed in the state.[^43]

The U.S. Lighthouse Society's archive lists keepers for the light from 1959 to 1964. It names two principal keepers: Clarence L. Warmack (at least 1959-1961) and DeWitt T. Abell, Jr. (1961-at least 1964).[^39][^40] LighthouseFriends names the same two as head keepers.[^41] The archive also lists nine more men under the heading "USCG," with documented spans of one to three years, several of them marked "at least." The roster table gives all eleven.

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

In 1957 a passing ship struck the fog-signal tower, seaward of the south jetty. The damage was so extensive that the structure was demolished, and it was not rebuilt.[^44][^45]

The lightship it replaced had weather troubles too. Inclement weather sometimes forced it off station when its mooring broke or dragged, and each time it needed several hours to return to its position.[^46][^47] The lightship was discontinued in 1954, when the new light began operating.[^48]

## After the keepers

The light was upgraded to automatic operation in June 1967, and resident keepers were no longer needed.[^49] The Coast Guard later transferred the two keepers' duplexes and their land to the Navy, and the duplexes were demolished.[^64] LighthouseFriends says the same.[^50][^51]

The wings were remodeled in the 1980s.[^52] In 1994 the Coast Guard changed the optic and installed the VRB-25 marine beacon that is in use today.[^53]

The nomination gives two accounts of the radio beacon's antenna. It says the antenna was later shortened to 110 feet to reduce the hazard to aircraft using the naval airfield.[^54] It also says that when the radio beacon was discontinued, its transmitter was removed and its antenna tower demolished.[^65] The nomination does not date either change.

The nomination describes the light as having statewide significance for maritime history, architecture and engineering.[^55]

## Today

St. Johns Light is still an operating Coast Guard aid to navigation, listed as light 575 on the Coast Guard's regional list. It is visible for 19 miles in clear weather and signals four white flashes every 20 seconds.[^56][^57][^58]

It stands in a fenced grassy clearing on a 5.2-acre parcel inside Naval Station Mayport. The lighthouse is accessible on foot but is not open to the public.[^59][^60][^5] LighthouseFriends says the lighthouse is best viewed from within the station, and that base access has been restricted since 9/11 to people with a military ID or a sponsor on the base.[^61][^62]

The nomination says the Coast Guard owns the parcel.[^60] LighthouseFriends says the Coast Guard owns the lighthouse.[^63] The U.S. Lighthouse Society's place record, however, lists the owner as the U.S. Navy.[^39]
