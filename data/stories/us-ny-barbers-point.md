Barber's Point Light stood on a point on the eastern shore of Lake Champlain, just south of Westport, New York, from 1873 until 1935.[^4] It was a two-story house, its lower story faced with blue limestone, with an octagonal tower.[^3][^4] Its fifth-order Fresnel lens had a focal plane 83 feet (25 m) above the lake.[^3][^4] It is now a private home.[^5]

## The place

The point sits about halfway between Split Rock Point to the north and Crown Point to the south, on a stretch of the lake only about two miles across.[^4] Hezekiah Barber began clearing land there in 1785, and records indicate that he operated a ferry across to Arnold Bay, Vermont.[^4]

The Lighthouse Board asked Congress for money for a light here in 1868, according to LighthouseFriends.[^4] Funds were appropriated on 15 July 1870, and in 1871 the Treasury reported that work would begin once "a certain judgment debt is removed and a valid title to the United States can be secured."[^1] LighthouseFriends says the title difficulties delayed construction.[^4]

## Building the light

By the Treasury's report for 1871, a contract had been made for the light "as provided by the appropriation of July 15, 1870."[^1] LighthouseFriends says work did not begin until 1872, and that the light made its debut at the opening of navigation on Lake Champlain in 1873.[^4]

The Treasury's report for 1874 describes the Bluff Point light on Valcour Island as "identical in construction to that at Barber's Point."[^2] LighthouseFriends describes a two-story building with a Mansard roof and an integrated tower.[^4] The Society's record gives the tower as 36 feet (11 m) to the centre of the lantern, and calls the keeper's quarters a Second Empire cottage.[^3] The keeper needed a horse to fetch supplies, so a barn was needed, and LighthouseFriends reports that $1,000 was requested for it in 1873.[^4]

## The keepers

The first keeper was James E. Barnes, from 1873 to 1883.[^3] LighthouseFriends says that in June 1864 a rebel shell landed near him during the Siege of Petersburg and shattered his left leg. Two comrades lost their legs too, and the three became known as the "three one-legged Jims." Barnes passed away in 1890 at his sister's home in Fort Ann.[^4]

Charles E. Stevens followed, from 1883 to 1911.[^3] LighthouseFriends says the Lighthouse Board gave preference to disabled veterans after the Civil War, and that the first two keepers at Barber's Point were both veterans.[^4]

Hans C. Anderssen kept the light from 1911 to 1934.[^3] LighthouseFriends reports that he was an assistant keeper at Fire Island when a newspaper of June 1911 described his request for help finding a wife.[^4] By November he had moved to Barber's Point with a new wife, and a year later she filed for divorce, claiming that he treated her cruelly.[^4] He raised two sons at the station, and in 1941 he ended his career at Cumberland Head Light.[^4]

## After the keepers

In 1935 a steel skeletal tower with an automatic light replaced the old house as the working light.[^3][^4] The lighthouse was sold by sealed bid in April 1936.[^4] An advertisement in the *Ticonderoga Sentinel* offered "a two-story stone dwelling, frame barn and shed," and set a restriction on trees and buildings: "You will not be allowed to grow a tree or erect a structure that will obstruct the visibility of the light."[^7]

The house has changed since. Its brown-shingled roof has been painted white with black trim, a small addition has gone on the rear, and the barn is now a garage. The Society's record lists a 1950s addition to the house.[^3][^4]

## Today

Barber's Point has been a private home since 1936, and the Society's record says it is not open to the public.[^3][^5] LighthouseFriends says the light is included in the Camp Dudley National Historic District. The National Register listing for the Camp Dudley Road Historic District is dated 21 October 1993.[^4][^6] The property is a private home: please do not go onto it.
