Brigus Lighthouse stands on North Head, at the entrance to Brigus Harbour on the southwest coast of Conception Bay, Newfoundland.[^1][^4] It was first lit on 1 March 1885.[^1] The Coast Guard still shows its light.[^3]

Until around 1800 the people of Brigus made their living almost entirely from the fishery.[^1] Then the town also became an important harbour for the seal hunt.[^1] L. G. Chafe, quoted by LighthouseFriends, said that the harbour's wide mouth and deep water made Brigus ideal for a sealing base.[^1] The headland at the entrance was bought from Thomas Hawe in 1883 for sixty dollars.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the land had been in the family for forty years.[^1]

Inspector John T. Neville reported in 1882 that four small cast-iron towers had been manufactured for four sites, one of them the northern head of Brigus.[^1] The tower was landed on the headland in 1883, but nothing more was done that year.[^1] Neville wrote that the tower would be of no use without a keeper's dwelling, and that a keeper had to live on the spot because the head was so far from any houses and the country between was rough.[^1] In 1884 the tower was put up and a keeper's dwelling was built.[^1] The light first showed a fixed red light, from an Argand lamp inside a sixth-order lens, at a focal plane of 34.5 m.[^1]

The first keeper was William W. Horwood, who served until 1897.[^1] Samuel S. Wilcox followed him in 1898 and kept the light until 1915.[^1] His son Walter took over around 1916.[^1]

The light was electrified in 1931 and afterwards run from a switch near the government wharf in Brigus.[^1] LighthouseFriends names the operator only as "Water Wilcox", which may be a misprint for Walter. No other source read here names him.[^1] The keeper's dwelling on the head was pulled down after the electrification.[^1][^4]

Parks Canada's register counts Brigus among about 26 lighthouses of prefabricated iron and steel construction, which it thinks are the only surviving examples of their type in Canada.[^2]

The Coast Guard list gives the light as a white flash every three seconds, 34.4 m above the water, with a nominal range of 7 nautical miles.[^3] The tower is painted in red and white vertical stripes.[^3] The sources disagree on its height. The Coast Guard list and LighthouseFriends give 9.5 m,[^3][^1] and the Lighthouse Directory gives 9 m (31 ft).[^4]

The tower is closed to visitors, and the grounds are open.[^1][^4] A hiking trail starts from Battery Road in Brigus, and LighthouseFriends says it is not clearly marked.[^1][^4] The lighthouse is owned by the Canadian Coast Guard.[^1]
