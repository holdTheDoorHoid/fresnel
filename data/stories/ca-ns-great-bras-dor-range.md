The Great Bras d'Or range lights are two wooden towers on Noir Point, at the eastern entrance to the Great Bras d'Or channel in Cape Breton, Nova Scotia. Lined up one behind the other, they give a ship its course through the shoals, and they have done that since the navigation season of 1903.[^3]

## The place

Bras d'Or Lake, a large inland sea, lies at the heart of Cape Breton. It meets the Atlantic through two natural channels, and the Great Bras d'Or channel, the northern of the two, runs north of Boularderie Island.[^5]

In 1901 the Department of Marine and Fisheries chose sites at Duffus Point for range lights to guide ships between the shoals at the entrance. The department expected that, once they were built, the older lights at Black Rock Point and Carey's Beach would be of little use.[^1]

Before the range, a fixed red mast light stood on the north side of the entrance at Carey Point. The 1903 report says it was permanently discontinued when the range lights opened, because they made it unnecessary.[^3]

## Building the light

The two reports disagree about where the range went. The 1902 report says two range lights were "in course of construction at Duffus point, on the northern side of Boularderie island", and names the contractor as Peter McFarlane.[^2] The 1903 report puts the front tower on Noir Point, on the west side of Boularderie Island, and names the contractor as P. L. McFarlane of Baddeck, who built the range buildings for $1,500.[^3] Neither report says why the site or the contractor's name changed.

The 1903 report describes the front tower as a square wooden building, 9.4 m (31 ft) high from base to vane and painted white. It carried a fixed white light of the seventh order, dioptric, 12.5 m (41 ft) above high water, which the report says was visible 11 miles; the report does not say whether those are nautical miles.[^3] The rear tower stood 515 m (1,689 ft) from the front on land 7 m (23 ft) above high water. It was 14 m (46 ft) high, with a fixed white catoptric light 19 m (63 ft) above high water, visible 13 miles along the line of range.[^3] Lined up, the two lights kept a ship clear of Middle Shoal and Carey Point bar to starboard and of Blackrock shoal to port.[^3]

## The keepers

Malcolm McLean was the first keeper of the front light, and Alexander Fraser of the rear light. Both were paid $100 a year to begin with.[^5][^6] The 1904 keepers' table in the Marine Department's report lists "McLean, Malcolm" against the front light.[^4] Fraser's pay rose to $130 in 1909, five dollars more than McLean was then earning.[^6]

R.L. McLennan kept the front light from 1912 to at least 1923, and O.S. Douglas kept it until 1958. On the rear light, Alexander Fraser served until at least 1923, and John R. Squires kept it until 1958.[^5][^6]

## After the keepers

The range lights were electrified on 7 November 1956, and their colour changed from fixed white to fixed green. Lighthouse Friends says the change was meant to make them easier to tell apart from other lights in the area.[^5]

In March 1958 both towers were fitted with sun switches and battery backup lights, and the range was de-staffed. Squires became caretaker of the range. Douglas took over that work in 1959 and held the post until it was abolished about a decade later.[^5]

Between 1968 and 1970 the front tower was lowered by one foot. In 1984 it received a sixth-order dioptric lens, and that same year both towers were given red vertical stripes.[^8][^5] The Nova Scotia Lighthouse Preservation Society lists the front light as automated by 1993.[^8]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns both range lights.[^5][^7] Neither tower is open to visitors. The front tower stands behind a private house at the end of a lane, and the Lighthouse Directory marks its site and tower as closed, private property.[^5][^7] The rear tower stands on Richards Lane, off Great Bras d'Or Ferry Road. The directory says its site is open and its tower closed, while Lighthouse Friends says the grounds are closed too. The two sources disagree about the grounds, and both say the tower is closed to visitors. Check before going onto the grounds.[^7][^6]

The Coast Guard's List of Lights gives both lights a fixed green character. It describes the front light as seasonal and operating 24 hours.[^9]
