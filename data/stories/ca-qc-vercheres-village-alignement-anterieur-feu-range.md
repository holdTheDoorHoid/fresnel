The Verchères village range lights are a front light and a rear light on the south shore of the St. Lawrence, about 25 km (15 mi) north-east of Montreal.[^6] The Department of Marine and Fisheries set them up on 1 May 1902 to mark a newly dredged ship channel, and the Canadian Coast Guard still lists them as the Verchères Village range lights.[^1][^7]

## The place

The Department's annual report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1902 describes the channel as dredged between Île Bouchard and the south shore of the river.[^1] The 1902 report says the village range was to lead ships from the point where its line crosses the Contrecœur range to the point where it crosses the Verchères traverse range.[^1]

The front light stood on a cribwork pier off the outer end of the village wharf.[^1] Below the village stood an old stone windmill. In 1902 the lantern of the Verchères traverse back light was set on its summit.[^1]

The Department's annual report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1910 prints notes from the parish priest at Verchères about the old mill. The notes say it was built in 1690, and they suppose that the fort which Mlle de Verchères defended in 1692 is no other than the mill.[^4] Rowlett dates the mill to about 1700.[^6] The report presents the notes as the work of the parish priest, who had researched the matter.[^4]

## Building the light

The front light was a fixed white catoptric light, 13.4 m (44 ft) above the summer level of the river and visible 12 miles. It was shown from a square wooden lantern on a square wooden tower, 9.4 m (31 ft) from the pier to the ventilator. The pier and tower were white, and the lantern roof red.[^1] The rear light was a fixed white catoptric light, 25.9 m (85 ft) above the summer level and visible 15 miles. It was shown from a square tower with sloping sides, of open steel framework painted brown, 19.8 m (65 ft) to the ventilator on the lantern.[^1]

The Lighthouse Directory describes the earlier rear light as a 20 m (66 ft) square pyramidal skeletal tower. It dates the present rear light to 2021 or 2022.[^6] In 1929 the front tower was rebuilt as a skeletal tower. LighthouseFriends says it has an enclosed lantern room; the Lighthouse Directory describes a square wooden lantern.[^5][^6]

The Department acquired the old windmill on 1 April 1909, after two years of negotiation.[^4] The Department's 1910 report gives two reasons: the old stone mill made a good signal station, and the building was worth saving from destruction.[^4] LighthouseFriends says the signal station had an 18 m (60 ft) mast and a private telephone line linking it with eight other signal stations between Quebec City and Montreal.[^5] LighthouseFriends says the windmill served as a signal station until 1949. The Lighthouse Directory says the signal station ran from 1909 to around 1940.[^5][^6]

## The keepers

Joseph Guyon was the first keeper of the front light, at $80 a year.[^5] The Department's list for the fiscal year 1904 gives Guyon at the Verchères Village front light.[^3] LighthouseFriends gives his service as 1902 to at least 1923.[^5]

Felix Bousquet was the first keeper of the rear light, at $70 a year.[^5] The Department's list for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1902 gives a "Vercheres Village (Back)" appointment dated 12 April 1902. The OCR of that list does not print a name beside the date, so any link to Bousquet rests on the column position.[^2] The list for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1904 names Felix Bousquet at the Verchères Village (Back) light.[^8] LighthouseFriends gives Bousquet's service as 1902 to 1912.[^5] Ed. Chagnon followed him from 1912 to 1915, and A. St. Pierre kept the rear light from 1915 to at least 1923.[^5]

## Today

LighthouseFriends says the Canadian Coast Guard owns the front light. It stands on the eastern pier of the marina, with the grounds open and the tower closed.[^5] The rear light is on private property. Its site and tower are closed, but the Lighthouse Directory says the light can be seen from the foot of the Rue de l'Aqueduc.[^6]

The old windmill stands on the Rue St.-Étienne.[^6] The municipality restored it in 1991, and LighthouseFriends says it is open in summer as an art gallery.[^5][^6]
