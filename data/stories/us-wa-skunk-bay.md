Skunk Bay Lighthouse is a privately owned tower near Hansville, Washington, on the shore of Puget Sound.[^2][^4] It was built by the former Coast Guardsman and author Jim Gibbs, who topped it with the lantern room from the Smith Island Lighthouse.[^2][^3][^5] It has shown a light as an aid to navigation since the mid-1960s.[^1][^2]

## The place

The lighthouse stands about a mile west of Hansville, in Kitsap County.[^3][^4] The sources read here describe no hazard that the tower was built to mark. LighthouseFriends calls it a memorial light, and it was not a legitimate aid to navigation at first.[^2][^4]

## Building the light

Gibbs was one of the keepers at Tillamook Rock Lighthouse for a year, from 1945 to 1948, and later edited Marine Digest until 1972.[^5] The tower was built on plans from the Mukilteo Lighthouse.[^2]

The lantern room came from the Smith Island Lighthouse, which stood at the far eastern end of the Strait of Juan de Fuca.[^3] That light became operational in October 1858 and was abandoned in 1957, when the encroaching cliff threatened it.[^3] Gibbs obtained the Coast Guard's permission to take the lantern room before the building fell into the sea.[^2][^4]

The sources disagree on when the tower was built. LighthouseFriends gives 1959, and Salish Sea Lighthouses gives 1964.[^2][^4] HistoryLink, citing a 1963 Seattle Times article, says the lantern room was already at Skunk Bay by then.[^3] LighthouseFriends dates the Coast Guard-required change to a fixed red light to 1965.[^2] This story has not settled the year of construction.

The sources also disagree on the Smith Island lens. HistoryLink says the Puget Sound Historical Maritime Society salvaged it, and that it is at the Museum of History and Industry in Seattle.[^3] Salish Sea Lighthouses says Gibbs rescued it, and gives the same museum location.[^4]

## After the keepers

The archive lists no keeper's quarters and no keepers for the station.[^1]

A Coast Guard officer visited after a light was left on by accident.[^2][^4] The Coast Guard told Gibbs to switch it off or meet the rules for a private aid to navigation.[^2] Gibbs applied and passed inspection.[^2] The light was changed to fixed red and reduced in power in 1965, and LighthouseFriends says it has operated ever since.[^2][^4]

In 1970 about twenty people formed the Skunk Bay Lighthouse Association and bought the structure.[^2] They enlarged it, adding bedrooms, a bathroom, a living and dining room and a loft.[^2] The finished building was dedicated in 1982.[^2] A 1940 fog bell was added during the remodeling.[^2]

A replica fourth-order Fresnel lens was installed in 2021.[^1][^2] A pair of association members commissioned it to mark the association's fiftieth anniversary, and gave ownership of it to the U.S. Lighthouse Society.[^2] Salish Sea Lighthouses describes the lens inside as a replica.[^4]

## Today

The lighthouse is privately owned by the Skunk Bay Lighthouse Association.[^2][^4] LighthouseFriends says the association is now limited to twelve members, each holding a share, and that each share includes a one-week stay each quarter.[^2] The grounds, dwelling and tower are closed. The archive lists the station as not open to the public, and Salish Sea Lighthouses says no access is allowed.[^1][^2][^4] Do not try to land or to climb the tower. The archive lists the light as an active aid to navigation but not as a private aid, while LighthouseFriends describes it under the Coast Guard's rules for private aids to navigation.[^1][^2]
