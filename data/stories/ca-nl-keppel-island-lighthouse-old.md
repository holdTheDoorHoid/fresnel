Keppel Island Lighthouse marks the harbour mouth at Port Saunders, on the west coast of Newfoundland.[^1][^2] The 1901 wooden tower still stands on Keppel Island, about 500 m off shore and reached only by boat.[^2][^4] Its tower is closed to visitors.[^2] A modern skeletal tower beside it has carried the working light since 1992.[^1][^2][^3]

## The place

Captain James Cook charted the western shore of Newfoundland in 1766 and named Keppel Island after Keppel, whom LighthouseFriends describes as a member of the British House of Commons who later became First Lord of the Admiralty.[^1] The island marks the south side of the harbour entrance at Port Saunders.[^1] Port Saunders takes its name from Admiral Saunders, who came there with a ship, the town says.[^5]

The French had fishing premises on Keppel Island in the 1860s, where they salted and dried their fish before sending it to France.[^1][^5] Quarrels followed. On 17 June 1889 the French warship *Bison* destroyed several lobster traps at Port Saunders, and the next summer another French warship destroyed 500 traps nearby. The dispute lasted until France gave up its claims in 1904.[^1]

## Building the light

The tower dates from 1901. LighthouseFriends describes it as a square, wooden, pyramidal tower with an octagonal lantern. The Directory adds red and white horizontal bands.[^1][^2] LighthouseFriends says it first showed a fixed white sixth-order dioptric light, 32.6 m (107 ft) above high water.[^1] The town's history says the light burned kerosene when it was first built in 1901.[^5]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends names C. Pike as keeper from 1901 to at least 1912, and Walter Pike from at least 1921 to at least 1945.[^1]

LighthouseFriends says Moses Lavers was appointed keeper of the new fog alarm, which began operating in September 1958. He served for nearly thirty years and retired in 1987.[^1] Its keeper list gives his years as 1950 to 1987, a start date its text does not explain.[^1] Stewart Hancock is the last keeper named, from 1987 to 1989.[^1]

## After the keepers

In 1931 the kerosene light gave way to a flashing white light fuelled by acetylene gas.[^1] In 1957 a keeper's dwelling, a fog alarm building and storage buildings went up. A diaphone fog alarm, worked by compressed air from an oil engine, began operating in September 1958.[^1] A second house for an assistant keeper followed in 1969. In 1976 an electronic fog signal replaced the diaphone, and the light was converted to diesel-generated electricity.[^1]

The station was staffed until 1989. A fully automated light went up in 1992, and the old tower's light stopped.[^1][^2]

The Directory says the town acquired the station buildings, except the light tower, and hoped to restore them. Those plans came to nothing.[^2] In June 2013 the town put the keeper's houses and other outbuildings up for sale for $250,000.[^1][^2] By 2015 the price had been lowered to $100,000, and the property was still for sale in 2017. The listing has since been removed, and by 2020 the town was listing the lighthouse as an attraction.[^2]

## Today

The working light shows a white flash every five seconds, from a focal height of 36.5 m (120 ft), with a nominal range of 11 nautical miles. It is carried on a square skeleton tower with an octagonal lantern house, 11.3 m (37 ft) high.[^3] The Directory gives two other readings of the modern light: a focal plane of 37 m (121 ft) with one second on and fifteen off, and a focal height of 17 m (56 ft) with a flash every fifteen seconds. This story follows the Coast Guard list.[^2]

The town owns the station buildings, except the modern tower, LighthouseFriends says.[^1] SaltWire's undated column says the lighthouse stands on land owned by the Coast Guard, and that 25 acres of the island are Crown land.[^4] The sources do not settle who owns the ground under the old tower.

The grounds are open, and the tower is closed.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the best views come from a private boat to the island from Port Saunders.[^1] The town's history page says tourists can visit the island and view the lighthouse.[^5]
