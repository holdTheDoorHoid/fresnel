Walpole Island Lower A32 is a square-tapered concrete tower with no lantern, standing in the South Channel of the St. Clair River beside Walpole Island, Ontario.[^1][^2] The register gives 1931 as the year it was built, and the Government of Canada recognized it as a heritage lighthouse in 2015.[^1][^2]

## The place

The St. Clair River carries international shipping between Lake Huron and Lake St. Clair.[^2] The register says the tower is one of a series of lights built for that traffic along the South Channel, and it ties the light to the Walpole Island First Nation community.[^1][^2]

## Building the light

The register says the Lower A32 and the Upper A34 "were established as a pair in 1920."[^2] Their original lights were poles. Reinforced-concrete towers replaced them before a major dredging of the channel in the mid-1930s.[^2] The register dates the Lower A32 tower to 1931.[^1]

The sources give different heights. The register describes the tower as "approximately 5.6 metres (18.4 feet) tall."[^2] The Coast Guard's list gives 9.9 m for the structure above ground, and puts the light 10.4 m above the water.[^3] This story does not choose between them.

In its report for the fiscal year ended 31 March 1909, the Department of Marine and Fisheries recorded two range lights set up on the northern end of Walpole Island to lead into the Chenal Ecarté. The lights were fixed white, in pressed-lens lanterns hoisted on poles; the front pole stood 2.4 m (8 ft) high and the back pole 6.1 m (20 ft).[^4] The Coast Guard's list now shows a Chenal Ecarté front range light "On Walpole Island, SE. of N. extremity," with a rear range light 61 m from it.[^3] The sources do not say whether those are the same lights as the Lower A32 and Upper A34, and this story does not treat them as the same.

## Today

The Coast Guard's list gives the Lower A32's characteristic as Iso R 4s, a red isophase light with a four-second period.[^3] Its description reads "White tower with red trim, marked “A32”," and its position is given as "Off NW. corner of wharf."[^3]

The register says the tower is located offshore, and that it is "closely associated to a number of key community facilities on Walpole Island."[^2] None of the sources describes public access to the tower. Walpole Island is a First Nation community.[^2] Do not land there or cross its land without permission.
