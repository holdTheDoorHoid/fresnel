Sand Point Lighthouse stands on the east bank of the Saint John River, on the west side of Kingston Peninsula, about 20 km north of Saint John, New Brunswick. A light has been shown here since 1869. The present wooden tower dates from 1898, and it is the tallest lighthouse on the river.[^1][^4]

## The place

The Saint John River runs "673 km (418 miles) before reaching the Bay of Fundy."[^1] The register text calls the river "a key shipping and passenger route." Steamboat service began in 1816, and by the middle of the century "over 50,000 people, along with substantial quantities of freight," travelled it each year.[^4]

Sand Point lay on the route upriver. The register text says ships bound upstream "travel directly towards the Sand Point Lighthouse to find the channel leading to the upper river."[^4] Six beacon lights went into service on the river on 20 August 1869, to aid the steamships operating between Saint John and Fredericton. Sand Point was one of them.[^1] By 1914 the register counts 21 lighthouses operating on the Saint John River system.[^4]

## Building the light

The 1869 lights were crude. John Duffy erected all six for $1,044, and the page describes them as "rather crude," each a lantern on a mast or on a mast atop an open framework.[^1] The first keeper at Sand Point was J. Cauldfield. He and the other five keepers hired on the river that year were paid an "annual salary of $80."[^1]

In 1895 a survey of the site was made to acquire land for a new enclosed tower. The purchase was complicated, because "the owner was outside Canada."[^1] The new tower was completed in 1898. G. W. Palmer of Kars did the work for a contract price of $699, and the register text calls it "the second tower on the site."[^1][^4] The new light was a seventh-order dioptric, fixed white light, elevated 18 m (60 ft) and "visible 13 miles from all points of approach by water."[^1]

The 1898 tower's lantern and ironwork were painted red, with the upper part of the tower white.[^1] In 1901 the Department painted the whole superstructure white. The annual report says this was "so as to make it a more conspicuous day mark."[^2] LighthouseFriends adds that the aim was to stand out against "the background of dark fir trees."[^1]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends lists the keepers at Sand Point in order. J. Cauldfield served from 1869 to 1873, James Clarke from 1873 to 1875 and Robert Clarke from 1876 to 1883.[^1]

Richard Wagner kept the light for thirty years, from 1883 to 1913.[^1] The keeper lists in the Department's reports published in 1906 and 1907 both name him against "Sand Point, St. John River."[^3][^5] J.F. McCloskey followed him from 1913 to 1921, and J.W. Wagner from 1921 to at least 1923.[^1]

## After the keepers

The Sand Point settlement grew up around the light. The register text says "A community grew up around the tower," with cottages, a wharf, a community hall, a beach and tennis courts.[^4] It calls the settlement "a small cottage community" that emerged after the lighthouse was built.[^4]

The tower was renovated thoroughly in 1991.[^1] The register records the lighthouse as "Recognized 2016" by the Government of Canada as a heritage lighthouse, and calls it "possibly the oldest extant example in Canada" of its design type.[^4]

## Today

LighthouseFriends says the Canadian Coast Guard owns the lighthouse, and that the grounds are open while the tower is closed.[^1] The wharf beside the lighthouse is private, and so is the cottage property around it. The page asks visitors to be respectful.[^1] The register text notes that the lighthouse is "a highly valued resource in the Sand Point community."[^4]
