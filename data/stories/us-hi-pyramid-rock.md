Pyramid Rock Light stands on the northwestern point of the Mokapu Peninsula, on the windward side of O'ahu, inside Marine Corps Base Hawaii at Kaneohe Bay.[^1][^2] It was established in 1941. The Coast Guard owns the light, but non-military visitors cannot go onto the base to see it.[^1][^2]

## The place

Kaneohe Bay is "the largest sheltered body of water in the Hawaiian Islands." Its mouth is 4.0 nautical miles (4.6 statute miles) wide and runs from Pyramid Rock on the Mokapu Peninsula to Kualoa Point.[^1] The name Pyramid Rock belongs to one of three hills at the seaward end of the peninsula. The others are Puu Hawaiiloa and Ulupau.[^4] Rowlett's directory places the light on top of a pyramid-shaped rock at the end of Palikilo Road.[^2]

## Building the light

Lighthouse Friends says dredging of the bay began in 1939, and that the light was established in 1941 on a natural feature at the northwestern point of the Mokapu Peninsula.[^1] Rowlett gives 1941 as the year the station was established, and does not date the light itself.[^2] The sources read for this story do not name who built the light or what it cost.

The Navy was building its air station on the same peninsula. Lighthouse Friends says the Navy bought the western side of the peninsula in 1939, and construction soon began on what became Naval Air Station Kaneohe.[^1] The air station was attacked on 7 December 1941.[^1] A Historic American Buildings Survey report on the station's administration building says that "the strafing and bombing damaged nearly all of the aircraft on the ground or in the water at the station."[^4]

The sources describe the light's characteristic differently. The Society's historical light list gives an isophase white light, with a two-second period, for 1950 to 1956.[^3] From 1971 to 1985 the list gives an occulting white light every four seconds.[^3] Rowlett also gives an occulting white light every four seconds, without a date.[^2] The 2025 Coast Guard Light List gives a flashing white light every six seconds, visible 10 nautical miles.[^5]

## Today

The Coast Guard owns the light, and Marine Corps Base Hawaii is the site manager.[^2] The light sits on the roof of a square concrete workhouse, painted with black and white diagonal stripes.[^1] Rowlett gives the workhouse as 15 feet (4.5 m) square.[^2]

After World War II the Army left the peninsula, and the Navy followed in 1949. The Marines established a Marine Corps air station on the combined Navy and Army sites in 1952, and the facility was renamed Marine Corps Base Hawaii in 1994.[^1]

Visitors cannot go to the light. The light is on an active base, and non-military visitors are not allowed access. Rowlett lists the site and tower as closed.[^1][^2]
