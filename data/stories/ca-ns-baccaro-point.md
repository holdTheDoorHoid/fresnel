Baccaro Point Light stands on the southern tip of the Baccaro peninsula, on the east side of the entrance to Barrington Bay, in Shelburne County, Nova Scotia.[^3][^4] The square white tower there was built in 1934 to replace a lighthouse that burned that January.[^2][^3]

## The place

Barrington Bay is an inlet with Cape Sable Island on its western side and Baccaro Point on the east.[^1] Baccaro Point is the southernmost point of the Nova Scotia mainland, though islands such as Cape Sable Island lie farther south.[^1] The register of historic places describes the point as low, just above sea level and "surrounded by a flat grassland and bog".[^2]

The point had other uses in the twentieth century. Lighthouse Friends says a LORAN station was set up there in 1943 by the Royal Canadian Navy, and that a radar station, run by the United States Air Force from 1957, passed to the Royal Canadian Air Force in 1962 and was decommissioned in 1990.[^1] The register says the lighthouse is "noticeably overshadowed" by the NORAD radar station built nearby in 1957.[^2]

## Building the light

The first lighthouse, a square tower with a dwelling attached, was built on the point in 1850. The Preservation Society gives 1850 as the year work began.[^4] Lighthouse Friends says the materials were landed near the point, and that the light "commenced operation on January 16, 1851, with James S. Smith as its keeper".[^1] The Preservation Society gives the same date as the day it was lit.[^4] To mark the tower against others in daylight, black circles were painted on three faces of it.[^1]

The light changed several times. In 1870 it went from a revolving white light to a fixed red, which Lighthouse Friends says mariners disliked.[^1] In the report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1901, the chief engineer reported that an English quick-flashing light had been ordered to replace "the fixed red light complained of".[^6] The same volume's Nova Scotia notes say hand fog horns had been supplied to Baccaro and several other stations to answer the fog signals of ships heard nearby.[^7]

On 11 July 1903 the light was changed from fixed red to a revolving white light, with a 30-second interval between its points of greatest brilliancy. The apparatus had come from the Cape Sable light, which the 1903 report describes as "formerly in use in Cape Sable".[^8]

The first tower burned in January 1934, and a temporary light was shown from a pole while the present square, tapered tower was built later that year.[^1][^2] The Preservation Society gives the new tower as the replacement for the 1850 tower.[^4]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends gives the keepers in order: James S. Smith from 1851 to 1881, George H. Snow from 1881 to 1893, and Joel W. Fisher from 1893 to 1907.[^1] William L. Smith followed from 1907 to 1913, and the Department's table for 1910 pairs "Baccaro" with "Wm. L. Smith".[^9] Herbert Ross kept the light from 1913 until the fire of 1934.[^1]

W. Trueman Nickerson was appointed in August 1934, and Lighthouse Friends says he rode a bicycle out to the light from Port La Tour. Lighthouse Friends has him keeping the light until at least 1937.[^1] A. Blache is listed from 1935 to at least 1943.[^1] The register of historic places says the lighthouse is directly associated with two keepers "of importance to the local community", Nickerson and Brenton Reynolds.[^2]

Victor Crowell came to Baccaro in 1964, after about twenty years in charge of the light and fog signal at The Salvages, and was killed in a road accident in 1965 that Lighthouse Friends says a drunk driver caused.[^1] Brenton Reynolds, his assistant, then took charge of the station and kept it until he retired in 1984. Lighthouse Friends reports Reynolds's account of the fog horn: it interfered with the annual moose rut, because the sound drew the animals out of the woods and down to the point.[^1]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

On 16 January 1934 fire destroyed the lighthouse. Lighthouse Friends says the fire drove keeper Herbert Ross and his family out of the dwelling in their nightclothes.[^1] The register says the earlier tower was destroyed by fire, and the Lighthouse Directory dates the fire to January 1934.[^2][^3]

## After the keepers

Lighthouse Friends says one of the two keepers' dwellings was moved to Seal Point and the other was torn down in 1986; the Lighthouse Directory dates the move to 1984.[^1][^3] Reynolds retired in 1984, and the station was de-staffed, according to Lighthouse Friends. The Preservation Society says the light was automated by 1986.[^1][^4]

The fog alarm was discontinued in 2014. Lighthouse Friends says a Facebook page was started to seek help getting the foghorn working again.[^1] In 2015 a non-profit group, the Keepers of the Baccaro Light, was formed to look after the lighthouse and its grounds.[^1][^3] Grants of $10,000 from the Municipality of Barrington and $3,344 from the National Trust of Canada funded the 2016 repairs and repainting, and the lantern room was restored in the fall of 2018 with the Coast Guard's help.[^1][^3]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the lighthouse, and Lighthouse Friends says the grounds are open and the tower closed.[^1][^3] The Preservation Society says the site is maintained by the Keepers of the Baccaro Light.[^4] The tower is a federal heritage building, DFRP 02293, listed with Parks Canada.[^2]

The Coast Guard's list gives the light a Morse letter D, a long flash and two short flashes, repeated every ten seconds, from a focal height of 15 m, visible 15 nautical miles. The list describes a white square tower with a red upper portion, 14 m high.[^5] The Lighthouse Directory gives the tower as 13.5 m (44 ft) and its focal plane as 16 m (52 ft).[^3]

To reach it, Lighthouse Friends gives the drive as Route 309 south from Clyde River to Port La Tour, then a gravel road to the light.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory notes parking.[^3]
