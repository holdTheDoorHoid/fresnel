Margaretsville, on the Bay of Fundy shore of Annapolis County in Nova Scotia, has a square wooden lighthouse built in 1859.[^1][^3] It was one of the first lighthouses on the Nova Scotia side of the bay.[^1][^4] The Canadian Coast Guard still owns it, but a local society holds a lease.[^2]

## The place

The tower stands on a small point that projects into the Bay of Fundy, at the end of the point, south of the breakwater.[^1][^3] Residents began asking for a light to mark the local pier in 1856, according to Lighthouse Friends.[^2] The Committee on Navigation Securities recommended a beacon on the pier, without a building.[^2] In 1858 the superintendent of the Board of Works, William Condon, visited Margaretsville, found the plan unacceptable to the people with an interest in navigating the bay, and dropped it.[^2]

Sir Brenton Halliburton, Chief Justice of Nova Scotia, donated the land, and the lighthouse was built in 1859.[^1][^2] At the time Margaretsville was a transport hub. The register says it linked the shipping route from Saint John, New Brunswick, with Nova Scotia's railways.[^1] Lighthouse Friends adds that it was connected to Saint John by steamship and to other parts of the province by rail.[^2]

Ships coming up the bay could not make out the light until they were nearly abreast of the pier.[^2] Lighthouse Friends says that on a fair wind and a flood tide, they were often carried past before they could take in sail.[^2]

## Building the light

William Early supervised the construction and then served as the first keeper, from 1859 to 1887.[^2] The register says the tower was built of heavy timbers with a broad base and a slight taper, and that it could be put up quickly by local contractors using local materials.[^1]

At first the light showed two colours.[^2] Vessels would see a fixed white light from about 8 km away, and then, nearer in, a red light.[^2] In 1864 the light was moved from a window in the side of the tower to a new twelve-sided iron lantern on top, and the window was closed up.[^2] The register calls the broad black daymark and black lantern roof "unique in Nova Scotia."[^1]

In 1915 a fourth-order lens with a single lamp replaced the lamps and reflectors in the lantern.[^2] The light's characteristic was changed from fixed red to occulting white at the same time.[^2]

## The keepers

John Early took over from his father in 1887 and kept the light until his death in 1907.[^2] Lighthouse Friends says his wife, Ruth, then took over the light for another three years.[^2] The register calls her Ruth Earley, keeper from 1907 to 1910, and says she was one of the first women to serve officially as a keeper in Nova Scotia.[^1] Lighthouse Friends spells her name Early and gives her service as 1907 to 1909 in its keeper list, though its text says three years.[^2] The sources agree that she kept the light. They differ on how her name is spelled and on when her service ended.[^1][^2]

Wesley W. Gaucher kept the light from 1909 to 1912, and Joseph L. Cleveland from 1912 to 1937.[^2] The Department of Marine's list of lightkeepers for the year ended 31 March 1911 has Capt. W. W. Gaucher at Margaretville.[^6] Lighthouse Friends then lists Lehan H. Celeveland, as the site prints the name, from 1937 to 1940, Reagh Hall from 1940 to 1956, and Gordon Aldred, the final keeper, from 1956 to 1963.[^2]

## After the keepers

Electricity replaced the oil lamp in 1963, when the light was automated, according to Lighthouse Friends.[^2] The Coast Guard still maintains the light, although there is little activity at the wharf now.[^2]

The Friends of the Margaretsville Lighthouse Society formed in 2013 to gain ownership of the lighthouse.[^2][^5] In 2018 the society obtained a lease. Lighthouse Friends says the lease came from the Department of Fisheries and Oceans, while the Lighthouse Directory says it came from the Coast Guard.[^2][^5] In a 2015 crowdfunding contest for Nova Scotia lighthouses, the society entered the lighthouse and earned $1,780 in its fundraising portion.[^2]

The register gives 21 March 2022 as the date the lighthouse was recognized as a heritage lighthouse.[^1] Parks Canada announced the designation on 2 June 2022.[^4]

## Today

The Coast Guard owns the lighthouse, and the society leases it.[^2][^5] Lighthouse Friends says the grounds are open and the tower open in summer.[^2] The Lighthouse Directory says the site is open but the tower closed.[^5]

From the community hall in Margaretsville, Lighthouse Friends says, the lighthouse is on the left as you continue straight past the hall, and there is parking nearby.[^2] The Coast Guard list gives the light as an occulting white light in a 20-second pattern, 11.2 m above the water, with a nominal range of 10 nautical miles.[^3] The Lighthouse Directory gives a focal plane of 9 m (30 ft) and a tower of 7 m (22 ft), which do not match those figures.[^5] The tower is 9.7 m high and carries a black horizontal band, according to the register and the Coast Guard list.[^1][^3]

The register says the lighthouse "continues to attract artists, tourists, and locals alike who use it for weddings and community events."[^1]
