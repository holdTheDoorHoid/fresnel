Racine North Breakwater Light is a square steel skeletal tower, painted red, on a rip-rap breakwater on the north side of the harbor entrance at Racine, Racine County, Wisconsin. It was built in 1901 as the North Pierhead Light at the end of the north pier and moved to the breakwater in 1924. It has been dark since 1987, and the City of Racine owns it.[^1][^2]

## The place

Racine's harbor is at the mouth of the Root River, which LighthouseFriends calls the Racine River.[^1][^3] From 1845 and 1846 piers ran out into Lake Michigan from both sides of the river, and they were lengthened over the following decades.[^1] In 1899 a Corps of Engineers officer, Captain J. G. Warren, reported that wind-driven seas often broke into the harbor and wrenched ships loose from their moorings at the city docks.[^1] A detached breakwater off the north pier, 600 feet long, was completed in November 1900.[^1]

## Building the light

A wooden pierhead beacon was set up at the outer end of the north pier. A Lighthouse Board work party arrived in July 1872 to build it, and it first showed a fixed red light on 5 September 1872.[^1][^3]

In 1894 the Board replaced the beacon with a lantern that ran in and out along a conduit on the pier, 288 feet long.[^4] The conduit light proved less than reliable, and a sixth-order light was re-established on the pier on 18 June 1896.[^3][^5]

In 1901 the Lighthouse Board decided to discontinue the Racine Harbor Lighthouse and to put its fourth-order Fresnel lens into a new metal tower at the end of the north pier.[^1] That tower was 46 feet (14 m) tall.[^2] LighthouseFriends dates its first light to 23 November 1901, and Rowlett's directory also gives 1901, but Terry Pepper dates the first light to 23 November 1902.[^1][^2][^3]

In 1912 the tower was moved from the north pier to the outer end of the south pier, where it showed a white flashing light from 29 May 1912.[^1] The Lighthouse Service Bulletin lists the Racine Pierhead among lights changed from fixed to flashing, with an acetylene light.[^6]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends lists the head keepers of the Racine harbor lights from 1839 to 1932 and their assistants from 1896 to 1933.[^1] The early names belong to the harbor lights before this tower existed. Martin N. Knudsen is listed as head keeper from 1899 to 1917, and Edward W. Knudsen from 1917 to 1932.[^1]

LighthouseFriends' end date for Martin Knudsen does not fit the Bulletin. In 1921 the Bulletin printed a letter from "Martin Knudsen, keeper, Racine Pierhead Light Station," who wrote to the superintendent about drying out damp masonry towers by systematic ventilation.[^7] The Bulletin also names an Edward W. Knudsen as keeper of the Racine Reef light, a different light off the harbor, in its 1915 storm report, so these may be different men or the dates may be wrong.[^2][^8]

LighthouseFriends lists Therse W. Knudsen as an assistant in 1907.[^1]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

In November 1913 the steamer *Nyanza* arrived at Racine loaded with coal, and a storm rose while she was unloading at the dock. Her captain's account, quoted by LighthouseFriends, says the crew "parted every working line" they had and that the sea rolled at least 2 feet over the docks. LighthouseFriends notes that the north breakwater alone had not given the harbor enough protection.[^1]

## After the keepers

In 1933 telephone and control cable was laid between Racine Reef and the breakwater, so the breakwater light could be controlled remotely.[^1]

The light was discontinued in 1987, and the Coast Guard planned to remove the tower. The public protested and the tower was saved.[^1][^3] Rowlett's directory says the tower is inactive since 1987 and was charted as a landmark.[^2]

## Today

The tower is owned by the City of Racine, and its site and tower are closed.[^1][^2] It is floodlit at night and serves as an ornament of the city's waterfront park.[^2] Pepper says the tower was incorporated into the new Reefpoint Marina complex.[^3] LighthouseFriends says a metal pole now serves as the official light for the north breakwater.[^1] The active green light at the harbor entrance belongs to a different structure, the West Harbor Entrance light, which Rowlett lists as privately maintained.[^2] LighthouseFriends says the tower can be seen from a viewing platform in Racine Harbor Park across the entrance.[^1]
