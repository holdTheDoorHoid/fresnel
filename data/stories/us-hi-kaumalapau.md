Kaumalapa‘u Light is a small white light on the south side of the entrance to Kaumalapa‘u Harbor, on the west coast of Lāna‘i, in Hawaii.[^1]

The harbor was built to ship the pineapple crop.[^2] James Dole bought most of Lāna‘i from Harry and Frank Baldwin in 1922, and he eventually devoted 15,000 acres of the island to pineapple.[^2] To transport the harvest, the bight at the mouth of Kaumalapa‘u Gulch was made into a small harbor by a breakwater on its north side, on the island's southwest shore.[^2] An account of the harbor works dates them to 1923 through 1926.[^3]

The first navigational light was set at the end of the breakwater in 1924.[^2] In 1925 the Bureau of Lighthouses took over that beacon and set up a second light on the south side of the harbor entrance.[^2] The light standing there today is that second light.[^2] It began as a beacon on a small wooden building.[^2] LighthouseFriends says it has since been replaced by a metal pole with a daymark placard and a solar-powered light.[^2] The Directory gives the present light as a 26 ft (8 m) post, with a white flash every four seconds from a focal plane 68 ft (21 m) up, and dates the station to 1925.[^1]

Today the light is Coast Guard property.[^2] LighthouseFriends reports the grounds and tower closed, and says the light can be seen from the breakwater, which it describes as made of giant concrete jacks (tetrapods).[^2] The Directory lists the site status as unknown.[^1] Kaumalapa‘u Harbor still functions as the island's main working harbor.[^2] No keeper is named for this light in the sources read.[^2]
