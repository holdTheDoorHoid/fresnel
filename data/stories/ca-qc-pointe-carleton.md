
Pointe-Carleton Light stands on the middle of the north shore of Anticosti Island, in the Gulf of St. Lawrence, where a dangerous reef reaches out from the coast.[^1][^2] Its octagonal concrete tower was built around 1919 and automated in 1970. It still shows a green flash, though the Coast Guard lists the light as seasonal.[^2][^3]

## The place

The north side of Anticosti is a hard approach. The Québec heritage record describes the Jacques-Cartier strait as difficult to navigate because of its many small islands, and says the island is ringed by a dangerous reef of large rocks that extends more than three kilometres from its coast.[^1]

The tower was part of a programme of improvements to aids to navigation in the Gulf of St. Lawrence. The Québec record says that building the three north-shore lights, Pointe-Carleton, Cap-de-la-Table and Cap-de-Rabast, completed the island's network of lights.[^1] The national register calls the tower's lighting of the north coast a help to vessels taking the shorter route from the Strait of Belle Isle to the mouth of the St. Lawrence.[^4] The Department of Marine and Fisheries said in its report that, with the three stations in permanent operation, many vessels now passed on the north side of the island.[^5]

## Building the light

The Québec record dates the station to between 1917 and 1919, and marks that date as uncertain.[^1] The Department's report of 1918 lists the "lighthouse and fog alarm plant" at Charleton point, Table Head and North point, and says it "will be completed next year".[^6] LighthouseFriends says the three north-shore lights were established in 1919.[^2]

The tower is built of reinforced concrete on an octagonal plan. The Québec record says it is one of the first uses in Québec of a standard concrete lighthouse plan drawn up by the Department of Marine, and that a fog-alarm shelter went up with it.[^1] The national register praises its octagonal gallery and lantern, its concrete construction and its pedimented doorway cap.[^4]

LighthouseFriends gives the tower as 12 m (40 ft) from base to vane, with a focal plane of 38.4 m (126 ft). The Québec record gives the tower as 15.9 m (52 ft).[^1][^2] Its first signature was four white flashes, followed by a long eclipse.[^2]

A double dwelling for the keepers and a fog-alarm building were built in 1918, and the diaphone foghorn sounded a blast of three and a half seconds in each minute when needed.[^2]

The Department's reports of the early 1920s record small works at the station: an oil tank was installed at Charleton point, oil storage tanks followed, and repairs to the cistern, drain and foundation came later.[^7][^8][^9]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends lists the keepers from the opening onward. J. H. Delisle served from 1919 to 1922, C. A. MacDuff from 1922 to 1923, A. C. Riley from 1923 to 1928, A. R. Côté from 1928 to 1930, and Ernest Carbonneau from 1930 to 1937.[^2] Carbonneau and his wife raised a family at the isolated station in those years.[^2] The same source lists Eugène Francis (1948 to 1962), Armand Lafrance (1962 to 1964), Edmour Carré (1964 to 1966) and Bertrand Carré (1966 to 1970) as keepers.[^2]

## After the keepers

The light was automated in 1970.[^2][^10] The keeper's duplex was replaced by two separate houses in 1956, and a new fog-alarm building went up that year, according to LighthouseFriends. The Québec record dates the replacement of the keeper's house to the 1950s.[^2][^1]

Sépaq, the Québec park operator, rented out the keepers' houses on Anticosti. LighthouseFriends says the buildings were abandoned by Sépaq in 2006, after a new lodge was built nearby, and that the plans are to raze the dwellings.[^2][^11] The Québec record says the accessory buildings have since been adapted to serve fishers and tourists, and the Amis des phares say some of the adjacent buildings have been altered or demolished.[^1][^10]

## Today

The light shows a green flash every six seconds, with a focal height of 38.4 m and a range of 5 nautical miles. The Coast Guard list marks it as seasonal.[^3]

The lighthouse is owned by the Canadian Coast Guard. The site is reached by road from Port-Menier, and the grounds are open while the tower is closed.[^11][^2]
