The tower at the northern tip of Roosevelt Island is a gray stone lighthouse about fifty feet tall.[^2] New York City had it built in 1872, when the island was still called Blackwell's Island, to light the asylum and the tip of the island for boats navigating Hell Gate.[^2][^4] The city commissioned it.[^4] The federal Lighthouse Board described it as a light exhibited "by the municipality of New York City," over which the Board had "not the slightest control."[^6] The tower has been on the National Register of Historic Places since 16 March 1972.[^1]

## The place

The city bought the East River island in 1828.[^1] By the nineteenth century it held "a complex of workhouses, a general hospital, an almshouse" and other institutions, and a penitentiary was built there in 1832.[^1] The lighthouse was meant to light the asylum and the boats passing Hell Gate.[^2][^3] The Roosevelt Island Historical Society says that in May 1872 a city official resolved to light the asylum and the tip of the island.[^2] A walking tour of the island says the tower was used to help ships navigate the currents of Hell Gate.[^3]

## Building the light

The Historical Society says the tower was completed in September 1872 under the direction of the Board of Governors of the Commission of Charities and Correction, which administered the island's institutions.[^2] The supervising architect was James Renwick, Jr., who also designed other buildings on the island.[^2]

The stone is gray gneiss, quarried on the island, largely by convicts from the island's penitentiary.[^2] The Historical Society describes the plan as octagonal.[^2]

The sources describe different things the federal service supplied. The Historical Society says the federal service furnished lamps for the finished tower.[^2] LighthouseFriends says the federal government, which it reads as the Lighthouse Board, furnished a fourth-order Fresnel lens for the lantern room.[^4] Both may be true; neither source gives a date for the lens.

LighthouseFriends says the light went into operation in September 1872 with a fixed red light at a focal plane of 54 feet above the river.[^4] The US Lighthouse Society archive's record for a "Blackwell Island" light gives 44 feet above water and an establishment year of 1904.[^9] The sources read here do not reconcile those figures.

A legend also surrounds the tower. The Historical Society records a local story that an asylum patient was allowed to build a stone fort on the point, that he later built the lighthouse, and that a stone once carved with the name John McCarthy stood by it until the late 1960s.[^2][^3] The Society says the tower cannot be credited to McCarthy, although the asylum warden's 1870 report describes an "industrious but eccentric" patient who had built a large section of seawall near the asylum.[^2]

## The keepers

The Board's reports describe the city's light as one it did not control.[^5][^6] Its report for fiscal 1894 says: "One of the best of these private lights is that exhibited from Blackwells Island by the municipality of New York City." The light "has gone out a number of times recently," and the Board "has not the slightest control over that light." It suggested that lights shown by corporations or private parties be "prohibited by law."[^6] The Board's report for fiscal 1891 prints the same passage, repeating a recommendation first made in its 1883 report.[^5]

The Archive's place record for a "Blackwell Island" light station lists principal keepers from 1885 to 1895, among them Daniel W. Fox, Thomas Lang, Arthur Donnelly and Patrick Whalen, with assistants.[^9] LighthouseFriends lists most of the same men, Fox, Lang, Donnelly, Noble and Foley among them, as keepers of the federal Hell Gate light on Hallet's Point, about 2,300 feet northeast of the tower, under the heading "Hell Gate Keepers."[^4] This story does not count those men as keepers of the city's tower. The sources read here name no keeper of the city's light.

## After the keepers

LighthouseFriends says the light was decommissioned in the 1940s.[^4] The walking tour says it "remained in operation as a navigational aid until 1940."[^3]

The city designated the tower a New York City landmark.[^3][^4] LighthouseFriends gives 1975 and the walking tour gives 1976, and this story does not settle the year.[^3][^4] The walking tour dates an anonymous donation of $120,000 to 1998 and says it paid for a complete restoration. LighthouseFriends says an anonymous grant of $120,000 funded a complete restoration.[^3][^4] A later restoration costing $3.1 million, as LighthouseFriends reports it, included a new glass and steel lantern.[^4]

The federal service also listed a Blackwells Island Reef light in the East River.[^7][^8] The Lighthouse Service Bulletin for 1917 lists it among lights whose illuminant was changed, from two fixed red oil lanterns to one flashing white acetylene lantern.[^7][^8] The US Lighthouse Society record for "Blackwell Island" may describe that federal light rather than the city's tower. The sources read here do not settle it.[^9]

## Today

The tower stands in Lighthouse Park at the northern tip of Roosevelt Island.[^4] The National Park Service says the island is managed by New York City.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^4] Visitors should keep to the grounds.
