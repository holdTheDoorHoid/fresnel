Black Rock Lighthouse stands on the south shore of the Minas Channel in Kings County, Nova Scotia, west of Canada Creek.[^2] The present tower is white and cylindrical, 10.4 m (34 ft) high, and shows two white flashes in each twelve-second period from 15.9 m (52 ft) above the water, with a nominal range of 12 nautical miles.[^3] The NSLPS puts the light about 15 m (49 ft) above the water, a little lower than the Coast Guard's figure.[^2] The Coast Guard's list describes one red horizontal band on the tower, while Lighthouse Friends and the NSLPS describe two.[^3][^1][^2]

A light has stood on this shore since 1848. Lighthouse Friends describes the first one as a square wooden building that served as both tower and keeper's home, with its lamp shown from a three-sided bow window on the shoreward side of its pyramidal roof.[^1] The NSLPS gives the same date and a lantern on the roof of a square wooden dwelling, with the light about 14 m (45 ft) above the water.[^2] Lighthouse Friends says John Crotty kept the light from 1848 until he retired at the age of eighty in 1872, when James Robinson took over, and that the station was kept by a string of three Robinsons until at least 1930.[^1] The Department of Marine's keeper tables print Charles Robinson against Black Rock with an appointment date of 16 March 1885, and the 1912 table still lists him there.[^4][^5] Lighthouse Friends gives C. D. Robinson as keeper from 1913 to at least 1930.[^1]

The present fibreglass tower, which the NSLPS dates from 1967, replaced the 1848 building; Lighthouse Friends gives the same year.[^1][^2] The NSLPS records the station as automated by 1973.[^2] The NSLPS also records a fourth-order dioptric lens on the earlier light from 1922 until 1941.[^2]

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the light. Lighthouse Friends reports that the grounds are open, the tower is closed, and there is a circular turnaround just past the light at the end of a gravel road.[^1] Black Rock Point Lighthouse, at the entrance to the Great Bras d'Or Channel in Cape Breton, is a different station with a similar name.[^6]
