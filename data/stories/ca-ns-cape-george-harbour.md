On the south side of Bras d'Or Lake, at the west side of the entrance to St. Peter's Inlet, a white tower with a red lantern stands on a pebble beach. The tower dates from 1950, but the light is older: it was established in 1875, and the Murray family kept it for ninety-four years.[^1][^2][^4]

## The place

Cape George sits where the lake's main body meets the northern end of St. Peter's Inlet, the arm that leads to St. Peter's Canal.[^2] LighthouseFriends says the canal was finished in 1869 to link the lake with the Strait of Canso and the Atlantic.[^2] Parks Canada says the light was established shortly after the canal was finished, and that the new route served a growing mining, lumber and farming economy in the interior of the island.[^1]

## Building the light

LighthouseFriends quotes a Department of Marine announcement from 1874 that "A light-house is now nearly complete at this point."[^2] The tower it described was square, wooden and 6.1 m (20 ft) high, with a lantern glazed with plate glass. The light was to show a fixed white light 14.9 m (49 ft) above high water, and to be seen, the Department said, about ten miles.[^2] Duncan McRae built it under a contract of $590, and in 1875 trees were felled near it to increase the range of its light.[^2]

The tower standing today is a later one. In 1949 H. E. McDonald took a contract for a new tower and a detached keeper's house, and LighthouseFriends says the work was finished in March 1950.[^2] Parks Canada calls the design "a typical mid-20th century Department of Transport design", simple and inexpensive, and easily erected with local materials and expertise.[^1] The tower is 8.2 m (27 ft) tall.[^3]

In 1968 the lamp at the top was changed from oil to an electric bulb, powered by ten large batteries.[^2]

## The keepers

Norman Murray started as the first keeper on 3 August 1875. When he died in 1882, John Murray was appointed.[^2] A keepers' list in the Department's report for the year ended 30 June 1903 places "Murray, John" against "Cape George, Great Bras d'Or Lake".[^6] LighthouseFriends says John kept the light for thirty years, but its own dates, 1882 to 1922, span forty.[^2]

After John, LighthouseFriends lists Mrs. A.M. Murray from 1922 to 1924. It presumes she was his wife, and the sources give nothing more about her.[^2] A. J. Murray kept the light from 1924 to 1930, and William L. Murray from 1930 until automation, a service of thirty-nine years.[^2] Parks Canada counts the family's keeping as ninety-four years, "until it was automated in 1969."[^1]

## After the keepers

The light was automated in 1969.[^1][^2] In 1975 the dwelling, barn and oil store were declared surplus. LighthouseFriends says that former keeper William Murray bought them and removed them from the site.[^2]

Between 2012 and 2014 a square skeletal tower carrying a light went up beside the wooden one.[^2] The Lighthouse Directory marks the old tower "Inactive since 2013 (?)" and describes the active light as standing on the skeletal tower.[^5] The Coast Guard's current list gives a seasonal white light that flashes every four seconds, with a range of six nautical miles.[^4]

In October 2020 the federal government designated the lighthouse a heritage lighthouse under the Heritage Lighthouse Protection Act.[^3]

## Today

Parks Canada calls the lighthouse a well-known landmark for mariners on Bras d'Or Lake, and describes its "isolated setting" as largely unaltered since it was built.[^1] The 2020 announcement counts Cape George among the 59 designated lights "managed by new, non-federal owners", but it does not name the owner.[^3] LighthouseFriends says in one place that the Canadian Coast Guard owns the lighthouse, and in another that a new, non-federal owner manages it.[^2] The Lighthouse Directory names the Canadian Coast Guard as owner and site manager.[^5] The sources disagree about who holds it now.

The two guides give different ways in. LighthouseFriends describes a drive along the lake shore from Saint Peters and then a walk of about a kilometre over a hill from Oldys Road, and notes that in 2011 the trail was overgrown.[^2] The Lighthouse Directory describes a short walk from a campground car park off Nova Scotia Route 4.[^5] Both say the grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^2][^5]
