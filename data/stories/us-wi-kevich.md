Kevich Light is a private lighthouse on a bluff above Lake Michigan in Grafton, Wisconsin, a Milwaukee suburb. It was built in 1981, attached to a home, and the Coast Guard lists it as a private aid to navigation.[^1][^2]

## The place

The light stands at Port Ulao, now part of Grafton, a lakeshore port that was never much more than a pier.[^1] James T. Gifford started a cordwood business there in 1847.[^1] He bought felled trees from nearby farmers and milled them into cordwood.[^1] He sent it down chutes from the bluff top to the beach and sold it to steamers from a pier 1,000 feet (305 m) long.[^1]

In 1850 the Wisconsin Legislature told the U.S. Senate that Port Ulao's commerce was fast increasing, and asked Congress for a sum to build a light-house and improve the harbor there.[^1] Congress appropriated $3,500 for a lighthouse at Port Ulao on 28 September 1850, LighthouseFriends says.[^1] A realtor.com article says the light was never built with public money, although Congress had appropriated the $3,500 in 1850.[^3] In 1854 Congress allocated $1,000 instead, for a "small beacon light on or near the pier at Port Ulao." LighthouseFriends says that beacon was never built, that steamers soon stopped burning wood, and that the port was abandoned.[^1]

## Building the light

In 1981 Brana Kevich, an Orthodox priest, and his wife Neva built a lighthouse on the bluff, attached to their home. LighthouseFriends says they wanted to own and live in a lighthouse.[^1]

The tower is 50 feet (15 m) tall, and the light sits at a focal plane of 163 feet (about 50 m) above the lake. LighthouseFriends says only Boyer Bluff Light, among active lights on the Great Lakes, is higher.[^1]

The light uses two metal halide lamps, a 400-watt lamp above a 1,000-watt lamp, behind a shield that turns around them. LighthouseFriends describes four seconds of light and four of darkness. WMHS, whose writer visited it, describes eight seconds of light and eight of darkness, so the two accounts differ.[^1][^2] The Coast Guard certified the light as a Class II private aid to navigation in 1990, light number 20765.[^1][^2]

## Today

The light is not open to the public, and WMHS asks visitors not to go onto the grounds.[^2] The US Lighthouse Society's record calls it a private lighthouse that is not open to the public. It also says the light is neither an active aid to navigation nor a private aid. That disagrees with the Coast Guard's private-aid listing, with LighthouseFriends, which says the light "continues to serve as an official aid to navigation," and with WMHS's account of a functioning light.[^4][^1][^2]

The house was sold in 1995 or 1996. LighthouseFriends gives 1995 in one passage and 1996 in another, WMHS gives 1996, and realtor.com gives 1995.[^1][^2][^3]

LighthouseFriends says the property returned to the market in 2026, listed for $1.28 million, after roughly three decades with one family.[^1] The realtor.com article, which LighthouseFriends dates to 12 August 2026, says the lighthouse home is being offered for sale for only the second time in its history. It also says the property was already under contingent offer less than a month after it was listed.[^3][^1] The sources do not name the buyer.
