Wawatam Light stands at the end of an old railroad dock in St. Ignace, Michigan, where it marks the entrance to the city's marina on the Straits of Mackinac.[^3] It is a steel tower about 52 feet (16 m) tall,[^2][^4] and it was never a lighthouse in the usual sense.[^3] It began as a decorative landmark at a state welcome center and became a working aid to navigation only after it was moved north and re-erected in 2006.[^2][^4]

## The place

The light stands on the dock where the railroad car ferry *Chief Wawatam* loaded its cars.[^1][^3] The ferry carried railroad cars across the Straits of Mackinac from 1911 until the mid-1980s.[^2][^3][^4] It was a 338-foot (103 m) ship, and the city named the light after it.[^1]

Although the tower had been built as a decoration, city officials wanted its light to mark the marina entrance.[^3] The Coast Guard could not act on the application until the structure had a name, because the light would be shown on navigation charts.[^3] City officials polled people at City Hall, and Wawatam was the first choice.[^3] In winter the light also guides snowmobilers crossing the ice bridge from Mackinac Island.[^1][^2]

## Building the light

Ed Morris, a welder from Bay City, Michigan, designed the tower in five sections on a base about 12 feet (3.7 m) across. A crew of eight took roughly three months to finish the job.[^1] His first blueprint called for a 36-foot (11 m) tower, but he chose to build higher.[^1]

The sources disagree on the year. The St. Ignace News and LighthouseFriends place Morris's work in 1997.[^1][^3] The Chamber of Commerce and the local tourism site say the tower started life in 1998 as a welcome-center landmark near Monroe.[^2][^4] The height is disputed too: the St. Ignace News gives 54 feet, while the Chamber of Commerce and Discover St. Ignace give 52.[^1][^2][^4] Morris built two other welcome-center towers, at New Buffalo and Clare.[^1]

The Monroe welcome center was remodeled in 2004, and the tower was due for removal. The state donated it to St. Ignace that fall.[^1][^3] Shipping it cost about $20,000. The whole job, moving, repairing and erecting it, came to about $50,000. Half came from the Michigan Waterways Commission and the rest from fifty donors giving $500 each.[^1][^3] The tower was reassembled at the end of the old railroad dock in June 2006.[^2][^3]

The light was first lit on August 20, 2006.[^2][^3][^4] The Coast Guard assigned it a white flash every five seconds.[^2] The Chamber of Commerce gives the lens as a 250-millimeter Fresnel with a 152-degree arc, and the light is seen for more than 13 miles over Lake Huron.[^2][^4] The Chamber also notes that the tower is 52 feet tall, while the Coast Guard measures the height from the water to the focal plane as 62 feet (19 m).[^2]

The light has never had a resident keeper.[^3]

## Today

The light stands at the end of the pier in Chief Wawatam Park, overlooking Moran Bay and the St. Ignace waterfront.[^4] Visitors walk out the pier for a closer look.[^4] The park has waterfront seating, picnic areas, a fishing platform, a seasonal splash pad and nearby parking, and it links to the Huron Boardwalk.[^4] Viewing is free and the pier is open year round, although weather, ice or maintenance can affect access.[^4]

A $137,500 Michigan Natural Resources Trust Fund grant helped extend the Huron Boardwalk by more than 1,000 feet (300 m) to the light.[^3] The marina director said the tower had not been cleaned since it went up in 2006, and it was cleaned in May 2022.[^3]
