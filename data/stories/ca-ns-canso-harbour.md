Hart Island, in Canso Harbour at the north-eastern tip of Nova Scotia, got its first light on 10 July 1872.[^1] Its two range lights, on square skeleton towers, now guide vessels into the harbour.[^8] The original 1872 lighthouse was removed in 1928, and the building that replaced it has been inactive since the 1960s.[^1]

## The place

Canso is a small community on the north-eastern tip of the mainland, and its harbour is protected by the Canso Islands. The islands were designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 1925.[^1]

## Building the light

Lighthouse Friends quotes the Department's annual report for 1872, which describes a new light on "Cutler's or Hart's Island" in Canso Harbour. It was a fixed red catoptric light with four flat-wick lamps and 46 cm (18-inch) reflectors, seen at 12 miles. It was first exhibited on 10 July 1872, and John Langley was appointed keeper at $200 a year. Construction had cost $814.42 by 30 June.[^1]

In 1901 a Department surveyor who had made a survey of the harbour wrote that arrangements were being made for range lights to guide ships into the south entrance.[^2]

At the north entrance, through False Passage, a fixed green light on a rock was put in operation on 30 December 1902, about a mile N. 73° W. of the Hart Island lighthouse.[^6] The report says a storm carried the pole away "last winter". The pole was replaced that year by one rising from a concrete pier, and the light first lit on 10 November 1903 stood 25 feet (7.6 m) above high water.[^6]

The Department's report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1905, submitted in March 1906, records that range light buildings were erected on the south side of the harbour. The front tower was 9.8 m (32 ft) high and stood about 15 m (50 ft) back from the water's edge, south of Lanigan Beach near the south entrance. It showed a fixed red catoptric light 14 m (46 ft) above high water, visible 8 miles in the line of range. The back tower, 13.4 m (44 ft) high, stood 383 m (1,256 ft) away and showed a fixed red dioptric light of the seventh order, 33 m (108 ft) above high water, visible 10 miles. E. F. Munro, of Westville, built both by contract for $2,590.[^3]

The sources do not say whether these 1905 towers are the range lights that stand today. The Coast Guard list shows a Hart Island pair and a separate Canso Harbour pair, the front one on the north end of the breakwater, and no source cited here ties the 1905 towers to either pair.[^8]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends gives the keepers of the 1872 light as John Langley (1872 to 1877), his son Thomas Langley (1877 to 1879), Lawrence Power (1879 to 1888), John Power (1888 to 1896) and Joseph Long, from 1896.[^1]

The Department's keepers' table in the report labelled 1909 gives Joseph Long's appointment as 31 December 1896.[^4] The 1905 table lists Long twice, once against Canso Harbour and once against False Passage Ledge. The 1911 table has him against "Canso Harbour and False Passage".[^9][^7] Lighthouse Friends says he kept the light for about forty years, and its list runs to 1936 at least.[^1]

The 1911 table also names Wm. J. Mathews against the station "Canso Harbour Range", and the 1909 table gives his appointment as 17 December 1904.[^7][^4] The 1905 table has him against "Canso Range" with the same date.[^9]

## After the keepers

The Department's report for the fiscal year ended 31 March 1910 records that a 5th-order, 360-degree dioptric apparatus had been substituted for the catoptric apparatus at Canso Harbour.[^5] Lighthouse Friends dates that change to 1909.[^1]

In 1928 the original lighthouse on Hart Island was removed and a combined dwelling and light was built in its place. Lighthouse Friends says that building has been inactive since the 1960s, and its lantern room has been removed.[^1]

The Coast Guard list gives the Hart Island front range as a fixed red light with a nominal range of 17 nautical miles, on a square skeleton tower. The rear range shows the same red light from a higher focal height, 9.3 m against the front's 4.1 m.[^8] Starling Rock, on a crib in the harbour, shows a quick-flashing green light.[^8] The Canso Harbour front and rear range lights, the front on the north end of the breakwater, show fixed green lights of 7 nautical miles.[^8]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the light.[^1] Lighthouse Friends says the grounds are open and the tower closed. It adds that the light can be seen from the waterfront in Canso, where a harbour walk on Water Street has signs about Hart Island.[^1] Its directions lead to that waterfront and do not describe a way onto the island, so check access before planning a visit.
