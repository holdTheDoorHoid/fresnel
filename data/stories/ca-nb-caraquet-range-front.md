The Caraquet Range Front Light is the front tower of a pair of range lights that lead ships through Caraquet Channel into the harbour at Bas-Caraquet, New Brunswick.[^1][^2] The Department of Marine and Fisheries reported that the lights went into operation on 24 September 1903.[^1] The Department, which called the harbour Lower Caraquet, placed the front tower close to the shore, on the point below Stoke Point.[^1] The Coast Guard lists the light under its Chaleur Bay section.[^5]

## Building the light

A contract for the range towers was awarded in 1902 to J. R. Chiasson, of Lower Caraquet. He could not do the work, and John Kelly, the Inspector of Lights, completed it. The Department reported that the accounts for the work were still unsettled.[^1]

Both towers were built of wood, with square plans, sloping sides and square lanterns, and both were painted white.[^1] The front tower was 9.1 m (30 ft) from its base to the top of the lantern ventilator. Its fixed white light stood 9.4 m (31 ft) above high water and used a seventh-order dioptric lens. The Department expected it to be seen for 10 miles by vessels approaching by water.[^1] The back tower stood 1,158 m (3,800 ft) from the front one. It was 13.7 m (45 ft) high and carried a catoptric light.[^1]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends says Fred F. Doucett, Jr. was hired as the first keeper of the front light, and Patrice L. Legere as the first keeper of the back light.[^2][^3] The Department's keeper lists print Doucett against the Caraquet Front Range Light in its report published in 1905, and Legere against the Caraquet Back Range Light in the report published in 1907.[^6][^7]

LighthouseFriends gives the succession. Doucett was followed at the front light by J. O. Chaisson, from 1912 to at least 1923, and then by Theodore DeGrace, from 1925 to 1961.[^2] At the back light, Legere's service ended in 1913, when P. A. Lantaigne took over. LighthouseFriends lists Lantaigne to at least 1923.[^2]

## After the keepers

In 2000 the Canadian Coast Guard erected an 11.3 m (37 ft) skeletal tower to serve as the rear light. The old 1903 back tower was given to the Town of Bas-Caraquet.[^2][^4] It was moved to a municipal park at the foot of Rue Lanteigne, where a roofed patio was added to one side.[^4]

The Coast Guard's list marks both range lights as seasonal, operating 24 hours.[^5]

## Today

The front light belongs to the Canadian Coast Guard. It stands at the end of a private lane off Route 145 in Bas-Caraquet. Its grounds and tower are closed, but it can be viewed from Route 145.[^2] The Lighthouse Directory says it is easy to see from the road and from the beach.[^4]

The 2000 rear light stands on Rue Saint-Paul, Route 145, about 1.1 km (0.7 mi) southwest of the front light. Its site and tower are closed, but the light is easily seen from the road.[^4]

The old 1903 rear tower stands in its park, where the grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^3]

The sources do not agree on the front light's colour. The Coast Guard's list gives a fixed green light.[^5] LighthouseFriends says it shows a fixed yellow light, and the Lighthouse Directory says a continuous yellow.[^2][^4]
