
Bayswater Lighthouse stands beside Route 845 on the Kingston Peninsula, near Milkish Creek on the lower Saint John River. It is a square, tapered wooden tower, built in 1913 and deactivated in 2005, and since 2018 it has been a federally designated heritage lighthouse.[^1][^2][^3][^7]

## The place

The 2018 federal release says the lighthouse served traffic on the Saint John River, including a ferry, recreational, fishing and touring vessels.[^2] A twin tower stands about 2.8 km (1.7 mi) away at McColgan Point, and the two were built the same year and look nearly identical.[^1][^3]

## Building the light

Both lights were built in 1913 by B. R. Palmer of Tennant's Cove. LighthouseFriends gives the contract price as $825 for Bayswater and $700 for McColgan Point, and says the Bayswater tower carried a fifth-order lens.[^1]

The Department of Marine and Fisheries' annual report for the year 1913–14, published in 1915, lists New Brunswick works of this kind. In the Bay of Fundy group it includes a wooden lighthouse tower with a fog bell and a fifth-order dioptric lens. It also lists a 27-foot wooden tower with a fourth-order lens, and a wooden tower with a sixth-order lens.[^4][^5] The same pages name B. R. Palmer, of Tennant's Cove, against a contract price of $825, but the scan does not show which row that price belongs to. The station column is hard to read, so the report does not show which of these rows is Bayswater and which is McColgan Point.[^4][^5]

A covered bridge dating from 1920 spans the nearby Milkish Creek.[^1]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends lists a single keeper, F. E. Currie, from 1913 to at least 1923.[^1] The Department's keepers' table in its 1916 report lists an F. E. Currie with an appointment date of 31 December 1913.[^6] The station column of that table is hard to read in the scan, so the report does not confirm that he was the Bayswater keeper.

## After the keepers

The light was deactivated in 2005.[^1][^3] In 2010 a local community group was given a two-year lease on the lighthouse. It painted the tower and landscaped the grounds in 2011.[^1] In 2012 the group began the process of taking over the tower under the Heritage Lighthouse Protection Act.[^1]

In autumn 2016 the beams in the tower were found to have rotted beyond repair, and demolition was judged the only safe option.[^1][^3] LighthouseFriends says the Department of Fisheries and Oceans made the finding in October 2016; the Lighthouse Directory says the Coast Guard made it in November.[^1][^3] Community residents rallied to stop the demolition, and the department agreed to salvage the tower.[^1][^3]

In June 2018 the federal government designated the tower a heritage lighthouse. Its release described it as a traditional square tapered wooden tower in the village of Bayswater.[^2] The MP for Fundy Royal, Alaina Lockhart, said in the release that the lighthouse had been in the community since 1914.[^2]

Ownership passed to the Bayswater Lighthouse Non Profit Group in 2019. With $80,000 in federal funds, the internal beams were replaced that summer and the tower was repainted.[^3][^1]

## Today

The tower stands beside Route 845, and LighthouseFriends lists the grounds as open and the tower as closed.[^1] The light is inactive.[^3] The Lighthouse Directory gives the Bayswater Lighthouse Non Profit Group as owner and site manager, and describes the site as open.[^3] LighthouseFriends, which is undated, still names the Canadian Coast Guard as owner.[^1]

Heights differ. LighthouseFriends gives 8.2 m (26.9 ft) and the Lighthouse Directory 8 m (27 ft).[^1][^3] Historic Places Days gives 8.8 m and also converts it to 26 feet, although 8.8 m is about 29 feet.[^7]
