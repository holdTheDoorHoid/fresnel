The Prescott Breakwater Light, which the Canadian Coast Guard lists as the Prescott Heritage Harbour light, is a small octagonal tower at the mouth of the harbour at Prescott, Ontario, on the St. Lawrence River.[^1][^2] The Coast Guard's list describes a white octagonal tower with a green upper portion, 6.8 m (22 ft) high. It shows a green light, equal on and off every four seconds, from a focal height of 8.4 m (28 ft), with a nominal range of 6 nautical miles. The light is listed as seasonal.[^1]

The Town of Prescott says the light "is located at the mouth of Prescott's Harbour, and continues to serve as a functional navigation aid", and that it can be seen for up to 10 km.[^2] LighthouseFriends places the tower on the breakwater at the harbour and gives the grounds and tower as closed.[^5]

No source gives the year the breakwater light was first shown or built, or who designed it. LighthouseFriends calls the tower modern and gives a build date, "the late 1908s", that is not a usable year.[^5] The Town's pages date only the Rotary Lighthouse, a separate structure built in 1989 as part of Prescott's Waterfront Redevelopment Project.[^2]

Prescott was also the home of the Dominion Lighthouse Depot, which the department set up there in 1903 to make and distribute lighthouse apparatus.[^4][^3] The depot supported aids to navigation from 1903 to 1985, when the Coast Guard moved to a new base.[^3] No source ties the depot to the breakwater light. The Lighthouse Digest, writing in 2004, says the Rotary Light, which it calls the Prescott Harbor Inner Light, holds a lantern that was on top of the old depot building and a lens used in training there. It also says a light stands on the breakwater.[^3]

The Coast Guard owns the breakwater light.[^5] The tower and grounds are closed to visitors, and LighthouseFriends suggests viewing it from the Sandra S. Lawn Harbour and Marina.[^5]
