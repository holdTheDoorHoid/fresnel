Point Aux Roches Light was an octagonal blue-limestone lighthouse on the New York shore of Lake Champlain, about eight miles north of Plattsburgh, where ships had to turn east to clear a reef.[^1] The US Lighthouse Society's record and the Town of Beekmantown both date it to 1858,[^2][^3] and the town says it kept its light in service for 131 years before it was replaced by a buoy in 1989.[^3] The tower still stands. It is closed to visitors.[^3][^4]

## The place

Ships near the northern end of Lake Champlain had to turn east at La Roche Reef, just south of Isle La Motte, to avoid the shoals.[^1] On August 31, 1852, Congress appropriated $5,000 for a light to mark that hazard, and the government bought 10.4 acres of shore near La Roche from Nathan Perry for $440.[^1] The town's history says that after the first lighthouse on Cumberland Head went up in 1838, several more were judged necessary, including one to get past an east-west reef and the easternmost point of Beekmantown.[^3]

## Building the light

The sources do not agree on when the light began to shine. The Town of Beekmantown says the lighthouse was built in 1858, and the US Lighthouse Society gives 1858 as the year the station was established and the original lens installed.[^3][^2] LighthouseFriends says the light was not activated until the opening of navigation in 1859, and that a temporary beacon had been shown from the point since 1856.[^1]

The tower was octagonal, of blue limestone, and stood 50 feet (15.2 m) to the center of the lantern.[^1][^2] Its light was 54 feet (16.5 m) above the lake.[^1][^2][^3] A sixth-order Fresnel lens gave a fixed white light, which the Town says could be seen for 13 miles.[^1][^2][^3] An attached wooden cottage in the Cape Cod style housed the keeper.[^3][^1] The Town counts it as one of three "sister" lights, built the same year as the lights at Windmill Point and Crown Point.[^3] A barn and stable were put up for the keeper in 1868, and a cistern was built in the cellar of the dwelling in 1879.[^1]

## The keepers

The US Lighthouse Society lists ten principal keepers, from 1856 to 1933, and LighthouseFriends gives the same names.[^2][^1] The first on the list, Warren Clark, is dated 1856 to 1859. Those are the years in which LighthouseFriends says a temporary beacon was shown from the point.[^2][^1]

William A. Nicholls held the post longest on the roster, from 1866 to 1886, twenty years.[^2] Amos Walker and then Frank E. Diamond followed, and Peter Savage kept the light from 1901 to 1919.[^2]

Grover C. Oliver had served as first assistant keeper at Eaton's Neck on Long Island Sound for several years before he was placed in charge here in 1919.[^1] He resigned in 1924.[^1] In that year William H. Bailey came to Point Aux Roches from the head keeper's post at Fire Island Lighthouse.[^1]

As LighthouseFriends quotes it, Bailey's 1928 report describes the routine.[^1] At sunrise the light was put out and the lens was covered, with curtains hung at the windows.[^1] From 8 a.m. he cleaned and polished the lamps and lens, then the lens room and tower stairs.[^1] At sunset he lit the oil lamp, set it in the lens and watched it for a time before leaving.[^1] During the night the lamp was checked at intervals.[^1] Apart from the lighting, his work was keeping the dwelling, the outbuildings and the grounds in repair.[^1] For about three months of the year, little outside work was possible apart from clearing snow.[^1]

The Light-House Board's reports for 1893 and 1899 mention the station only in one-line entries.[^5][^6] The report for the fiscal year ended June 30, 1893 lists a survey of the reservation and various minor repairs.[^5] The report for the fiscal year ended June 30, 1899 says the tower's deck and coping were repainted inside and out.[^6]

## After the keepers

In 1919 the Lighthouse Service asked for money to replace the oil lights on Lake Champlain with acetylene lights, except at stations with a fog bell.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the change did not begin until the early 1930s.[^1] At this light an electric light was installed on top of the tower in 1933, and Bailey was transferred to Robbins Reef that July.[^1] The Town's page gives a different date, saying the light was automated in 1934.[^3]

In 1934 most of the property was sold for $2,100 to Grover C. Oliver, a former keeper.[^1] He moved the keeper's cottage a short way north and used it as a private residence.[^1][^3] The two sources disagree on the land that was kept. The Town says the tower stands on 50 square feet, while LighthouseFriends describes a fifty-foot-square lot around the tower.[^3][^1]

LighthouseFriends says the shoreline in front of the tower eroded and the masonry deteriorated.[^1] It also says the Coast Guard decided in 1989 that the tower was unsafe and replaced the light with a buoy on La Roche Reef.[^1] The Town gives the buoy, but says the General Services Administration declared the lighthouse obsolete in 1989, after 131 years of continuous service, and put it up for sale.[^3]

## Today

The sources do not agree on who owns the tower. LighthouseFriends says the Coast Guard owns the tower, and the dwelling and surrounding land are privately owned.[^1] The Town says that after several options were weighed, the lighthouse "became privately owned."[^3] Cyberlights, whose page was last visited in November 2002, reports that the General Services Administration was seeking to transfer the light to the Town of Beekmantown, which wanted to preserve it.[^4] The same page says a study of the work needed to stabilize the stone tower was funded by the Lake Champlain Basin Program.[^4]

There is no public access.[^3] The US Lighthouse Society's record lists the station as not open to the public.[^2] Cyberlights says permission must be given by the owners of the land next to the station.[^4] LighthouseFriends marks the grounds, dwelling and tower as closed.[^1] The tower and lantern can be seen through the trees from Point Au Roche Road, and more clearly from the lake opposite La Roche Reef.[^3][^4]
