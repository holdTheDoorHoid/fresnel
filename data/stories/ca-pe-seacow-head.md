Seacow Head Light is a wooden octagonal tower on the coast of Prince Edward Island, built in 1864 near the turning point for ships bound into Summerside Harbour. Its volunteer owners now run it as a museum.[^1][^6]

## Building the light

The tower was built in the summer of 1864 under a contract of £314 by David McFarlane and John Rankin. Land and right-of-way cost another £86, and Thomas Robinson was paid £85 for the lantern.[^1] It is an octagon of heavy timber frame, 18.3 m (60 ft) high and 3.4 m (11 ft 3 in) a side at the base, and it originally stood on a stone foundation with its light about 27 m (88 ft) above the water.[^1] Lighthouse Friends says it serves as both a harbour light and a gulf light.[^1]

In April 1902 a new cast-iron lantern replaced the "worn out and inferior" one; the tower was put in thorough repair, reshingled and given a new platform.[^4] The old fixed white catoptric (reflector) light was later replaced by a fourth-order double-flashing Fresnel apparatus, with two groups of two panels, made by Barbier, Bénard & Turenne of Paris. The Department's report volume for 1906-07 records the change, and Lighthouse Friends dates it to 1906.[^5][^1]

## The keepers

Malcolm McFarlane was the first keeper, from 1865. In 1867, after he had left, two commissioners appointed by the House of Assembly of Prince Edward Island looked into a charge that he had embezzled public property; they found the evidence so trifling that it would not have been sustained in a court of law, and he was cleared.[^1]

In 1877 William Mitchell, the Department of Marine and Fisheries' agent on the Island, visited with the General Superintendent of Lighthouses of Canada and had five of Sibler's patent lamps with deep reflectors fitted in the lantern. The keeper, Peter O'Ronaghan, was then living inside the lighthouse itself, which was very uncomfortable, and Mitchell encouraged the Department to consider building a dwelling.[^1] A contract for $777 went to James Barclay of Ellerslie, and the house was built in 1880.[^1]

Lighthouse Friends lists seven keepers: McFarlane (1865-1867), Thomas P. Huestis (1867-1872), James Wright (1872-1873), Peter (Patrick) O'Ronaghan (1873-1917), Emmett O'Ronaghan (1917-1918), Thomas James Ranahan (1918-1946) and Charles Walter Richards (1946-1959).[^1] The Department's 1903 table of keepers, which prints the name as "Ranaghan, Peter", lists him at Sea Cow Head, appointed April 21, 1873, at a salary of $250, which matches the start of O'Ronaghan's long tenure.[^2]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

The Department's report for the year ended June 30, 1903 describes the winter ferry steamer *Stanley*, which left Summerside wharf on 12 January 1903 and with great difficulty got outside the harbour. With a great deal of ice coming down the Gulf of St. Lawrence, she "was forced into the board ice off Sea Cow head" and could not make further way; efforts to free her failed. On 14 January a large part of the shore ice broke off and carried her out into the strait, where wind and tide drove her about until 17 March.[^3]

## After the keepers

The light was automated on 12 November 1959 and the dwelling was sold in March 1960 and removed. Charles Richards, the last official keeper, was transferred to Caribou Lighthouse in Nova Scotia; William Sherry was hired as caretaker at $150 a year, against the $2,820 Richards had been paid.[^1] The tower was moved back from the eroding bank in 1979.[^1]

The lighthouse was recognised as a heritage place under the provincial Heritage Places Protection Act on 3 October 2012.[^1] When the federal government moved to dispose of it, neighbour Tom Sherry gathered signatures from the community, a public meeting was held at the lighthouse on 18 July 2013, and residents formed a Friends group.[^1] The Friends of Seacow Head Lighthouse Inc. took possession from the Department of Fisheries and Oceans on 7 September 2022.[^6] The tower also served as the model for the lighthouse in the television series *Road to Avonlea*.[^1]

## Today

The Friends, a non-profit charity, own and operate the lighthouse as a museum at 198 Lighthouse Road, Fernwood, with a gift shop and washrooms in a new outbuilding; it opened to the public on 10 July 2024.[^1][^6] Its website gives seasonal daily hours (10 am to 6 pm, mid-June to September when last read), so check before travelling. Overnight camping on the property is not permitted.[^6] An earlier Lighthouse Friends entry described the grounds as open and the tower as closed; it also still named the Coast Guard as owner, which the Friends' site supersedes.[^1]
