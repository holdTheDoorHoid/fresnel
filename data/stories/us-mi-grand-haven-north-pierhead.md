The Grand Haven North Pierhead Light marks the outer end of the north pier, where the Grand River empties into Lake Michigan.[^3] LighthouseFriends describes the outer end of the north pier as marked by a D9 tower.[^3]

In 1837 Lieutenant G. J. Pendergrast, sent to choose lighthouse sites on the Great Lakes, wrote that the river had "two fathoms water on the bar at its mouth" and was "the only safe shelter for large vessels on the east shore south of the Manitou islands," as LighthouseFriends quotes him.[^3]

The Commissioner of Lighthouses' report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1915 lists "Grand Haven North Pierhead, Mich." among the aids in commission that used illuminants other than oil, under the heading of acetylene lights.[^1] So by 1915 the pier light burned acetylene.[^1] The report for fiscal 1918 lists a "Grand Haven Pierhead, Mich." among lights whose illumination was improved to electric incandescent. That entry does not say which pier it means, so it cannot be tied to this light.[^2]

LighthouseFriends says the station's dwelling was enlarged to give improved quarters to "the keepers of the pierhead lights," but it does not name a keeper for the north pier light.[^3] The Society's record lists the north pier as a minor aid to navigation and names no one.[^6]

The Michigan lighthouse directory describes an older light, "Grand Haven North Pierhead (1?)," as a white skeletal tower about 30 feet (9 m) tall, rising from a square metal equipment shelter. It says the light was replaced on the pier by a standard D9 tower, and that the old tower now stands at the entrance to Coast Guard Park on North Shore Drive in Ferrysburg. The directory gives no date for either change.[^4]

In March 2026 WGHN reported that the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers would start an $8.25 million project in April that would shut down the north pier for up to two years. About 325 feet (99 m) of the pier is to be replaced, and a new walkway built for pedestrian access.[^5] WGHN's report does not say whether the light itself is affected. The Society's record says the north pier is not open to the public.[^6] The available sources do not say whether visitors can reach the light.
