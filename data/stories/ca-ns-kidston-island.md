The Kidston Island Lighthouse stands on the northeast point of Kidston Island, at the entrance to Baddeck Harbour on the north side of the Bras d'Or Lake, in Victoria County, Cape Breton.[^1] It can be reached only by boat, but it is visible from the Baddeck waterfront.[^2]

## The place

The light was put up for Baddeck Harbour.[^1] The Department of Marine's 1875 announcement describes the new tower as being at "the entrance to Baddeck Harbour."[^1]

The island had a trading history before the light. James Duffus set up a home and mercantile business on nearby Mutton Island in 1819, to serve settlers at River Baddeck and Grand Narrows.[^1] After Duffus died in Halifax in 1833, his executors sent William Kidston to the island in 1836, and Kidston moved the business to the mainland in 1840.[^1]

## Building the light

In 1875 the Department of Marine announced that a new light tower had been erected on the north-east point of Kidston's Island, and would be put in operation on 8 November.[^1] The light was a fixed red, 9.4 m (31 ft) above high water, which "should be seen a distance of about seven miles" in clear weather (the report does not say which miles).[^1]

The building was "a small square tower painted white, without a dwelling," and its keeper lived in his own house in Baddeck.[^1] The lantern was iron, about 1.7 m (5.5 ft) across, and the light used three flat-wick lamps with 43 cm (17 in) reflectors.[^1]

The building contract went to Neil W. McKenzie for $674.[^1] The lantern room and lighting apparatus cost $561.90, the site and the deed $150.53, and the total came to $1,474.63.[^1]

## The keepers

Donald McRae was appointed keeper at $200 a year, a salary that included an allowance for a boat.[^1] LighthouseFriends gives the keepers as Donald McRae from 1875 to at least 1877, Duncan McRae to 1892, and Donald McRae again from 1892 to at least 1937.[^1]

The 1911 list names Donald McRae as the Kidston Island keeper, appointed 17 May.[^6] The 1909 list gives that appointment as 17 May 1892, at a salary of $240 a year.[^5] No Department list cited here names Duncan McRae.

The station was improved in small ways. In 1878 the shelf that held the lamps was raised six inches, so that the burners sat in the middle of the lantern-room glass.[^1] In 1885 a separate oil store was built, which took the oil out of the tower.[^1]

In 1909 a fourth-order lens replaced the reflectors, according to LighthouseFriends.[^1] The Department's report for the year ended 31 March 1910 describes the same change as a 270-degree fourth-order dioptric apparatus, replacing the earlier reflector apparatus.[^4]

## After the keepers

In 1911 a new square wooden tower replaced the original.[^1] LighthouseFriends says it was built by L. Mury of West Arichat, and that Victoria Foundry Co. of Ottawa supplied the octagonal lantern room for $626.50.[^1] The same source says the new tower raised the focal plane from 9.4 m (31 ft) to 14.6 m (48 ft).[^1] Rowlett also dates the present tower to 1911.[^2]

The sources give different focal heights. LighthouseFriends' 14.6 m (48 ft) is for the 1911 tower, while Rowlett gives 13.5 m (44 ft) and the Coast Guard list gives 13.3 m for the present light.[^1][^2][^3] The sources do not explain the difference.

The original tower, a shorter one, was not demolished until 1959, according to Rowlett.[^2] LighthouseFriends says it was then destroyed after falling into disrepair.[^1][^2]

The light was red in 1875.[^1] The Coast Guard list now gives a long-flashing green light, with 2 seconds of flash and 10 seconds of eclipse, and the sources cited do not say when the colour changed.[^1][^3]

Ownership passed to the Village of Baddeck in 2005.[^2] Rowlett records the transfer and a restoration in 2013 and 2014, and LighthouseFriends says the village re-shingled and painted the tower in 2014.[^1][^2] The village had acquired the island itself in 1959 and leases it to the local Lions Club, which runs a summer ferry in July and August.[^1][^2]

The sources cited do not give the date the light was automated. LighthouseFriends' keeper roster runs to at least 1937.[^1]

## Today

The Coast Guard list gives the light a nominal range of 6 nautical miles and a focal height of 13.3 m.[^3] It is seasonal, and the list describes it as a "private aid" rather than a Coast Guard aid.[^3]

Ownership is given two ways. Rowlett names the Village of Baddeck as owner and site manager, while LighthouseFriends' visitor notes say the Canadian Coast Guard owns the light.[^2][^1]

The island is reached only by boat.[^2] The grounds are open and the tower is closed, and the Lions Club ferry runs in July and August.[^1] It is visible from the Baddeck waterfront, and LighthouseFriends points visitors to the marina for a view.[^1][^2]
