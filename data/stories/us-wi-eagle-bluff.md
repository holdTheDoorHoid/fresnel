Eagle Bluff Light stands on a bluff in Peninsula State Park, near Fish Creek on the Green Bay shore of Door County. Its keeper's house and square tower are one building of cream-colored brick, built in 1868, and since 1963 the Door County Historical Society has run it as a museum.[^1][^2]

## The place

Congress appropriated $12,000 on 28 July 1866 for a light to help commercial ships pass through Strawberry Channel, between the Strawberry Islands and the Door Peninsula. That October President Andrew Johnson set aside twelve acres of public land for it.[^1] The National Register nomination says the light "marked the east passage of Green Bay to Lake Michigan," and that the Green Bay water route was difficult for transporting both travelers and goods.[^2]

The work was to begin in 1867, but the crew was diverted to Sand Point Lighthouse at Escanaba. Construction began only after navigation opened in 1868.[^1]

## Building the light

Masons built a two-story dwelling of cream-colored Milwaukee brick, twenty-six by thirty feet (7.9 by 9.1 m), with a kitchen wing attached.[^1] The tower, which LighthouseFriends measures at nine feet four inches (2.8 m) square, was built into one corner of the house. The two sources disagree on which corner: LighthouseFriends says northwest, and the nomination places it at the northeast end.[^1][^2] The lantern was built by the Detroit Locomotive Works, which by then had stopped making locomotives, and shipped in pieces.[^1]

A third-and-a-half-order Fresnel lens, made in France by Henry-Lepaute, was installed in the lantern.[^3] Henry Stanley first exhibited the light on 13 October 1868, six months after work began. LighthouseFriends gives its focal plane as seventy-six feet (23.2 m) and its range as sixteen miles in clear weather.[^1]

The station grew slowly. The Board's report for the fiscal year 1893 says a barn was built, "16 by 24 feet in plan, and one and a half stories high."[^4] LighthouseFriends dates the brick oil house to 1890, but the Board's report for the fiscal year 1899 says the metal for an Eagle Bluff oil house had been bought and stored at the Sturgeon Bay Canal "for delivery here as soon as practicable."[^1][^5] The two sources do not agree on when the oil house was built.

## The keepers

Henry Stanley, the first keeper, was born in Norway in 1823 and came to the United States in 1844. After a failed try at prospecting in California he sailed the Great Lakes and became part owner of several ships. He took the Eagle Bluff post with his wife Katherine and their two children, John, then ten, and Elizabeth, eight.[^1] LighthouseFriends says Stanley kept detailed logs, which survive from 1872. The logs do not record John's death, so LighthouseFriends assumes that he died between 1870 and 1872.[^1]

Elizabeth's death is recorded in the keeper's own words. In November 1881 Elizabeth, by then married to a doctor, died at Fort Howard. LighthouseFriends quotes Stanley's log for 14 November 1881: "I received a Telegraph Dispatch from Fort Howard that Mrs. F. L. Lewis was Dead."[^1] Mrs. Stanley had reached her daughter's home three hours before Elizabeth died, and she was unable to return to the lighthouse for more than six months.[^1] Stanley was transferred to the new Sherwood Point light in September 1883.[^1]

William H. Duclon took charge on 20 September 1883. He had served at Waugoshance and Beaver Island, and his wife Julia and seven sons came with him.[^1][^3] The roster gives Duclon as principal keeper until 1918.[^3] LighthouseFriends says the boys helped their father catch more than 2,000 pounds of lake trout through the ice near the station in the spring of 1887.[^1] Duclon's last log entry, on 15 December 1918, closed thirty-five years at Eagle Bluff. He settled in Fish Creek.[^1]

Peter Coughlin came from the Green Island light in February 1919, when he was sixty-two. LighthouseFriends says he kept a much more detailed log, recording the weather and the station's activity almost every day, and that he repainted every building on the property.[^1][^3] His last log entry, on 15 September 1926, reads: "Green Is light not vis. Rain and fog N wind."[^1]

## After the keepers

In 1917 the Lighthouse Service Bulletin listed Eagle Bluff among the lights changed from oil to incandescent oil vapor.[^6] LighthouseFriends says the lens was replaced in 1918 by a fifth-order lens, because the brighter oil vapor light made the larger lens unnecessary.[^1]

The light was converted to unmanned acetylene in 1926, the year of Coughlin's last entry.[^1] Maintenance of the acetylene light fell to the Cana Island keepers, LighthouseFriends says.[^1] The site was empty until 1930, when the Fruit Growers Union Cooperative leased the dwelling as an office.[^1]

In the 1930s the state acquired 10.98 acres of the property for Peninsula State Park, and the federal government kept the light and buildings. The lighthouse was vacant in the 1950s and suffered vandalism.[^1] The nomination says it was "faithfully restored in 1961-1963" from the original plans, and that a nearby lumber company had enough old wainscoting in storage to replace the original parts where needed.[^2] The Door County Historical Society began the work in 1961, after the Coast Guard gave it permission, and opened the lighthouse as a museum in 1963.[^1][^2] Several Duclon family members donated furniture and a piano that had been used in the lighthouse.[^1]

The Coast Guard replaced the acetylene light with a battery-powered electric light, and later a solar beacon on the railing outside the lantern room. The fifth-order lens is still in the lantern.[^1] The nomination was prepared in 1970, and it describes the Coast Guard and the state as owners at that time.[^2] LighthouseFriends says the lighthouse passed to the State of Wisconsin in 2003.[^1]

## Today

The Door County Historical Society runs Eagle Bluff Light Station as a museum. Self-guided tours are open seven days a week, from 11 a.m. to 4 p.m., from mid-May to mid-October, and the admission is $7 per person. A state park sticker is required to enter the park.[^7] The Society's site describes a capital campaign for restoration.[^7] The light itself is automated, with a solar beacon on the railing outside the lantern room.[^1]
