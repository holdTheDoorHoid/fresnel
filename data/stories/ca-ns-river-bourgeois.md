River Bourgeois light marks the entrance to Bourgeois Inlet, an arm of the sea on the south coast of Cape Breton Island, whose entrance is only fifty metres wide. It lies along Lennox Passage.[^4] The village stands on the inlet, not on a river as its name suggests.[^4]

## Building the light

The Department of Marine's annual report for 1902 describes a small wooden tower being built at the mouth of the inlet, in Richmond county, by Edward Doyle of Poulamon, under contract for $415.[^1] The light began showing in 1903, when navigation opened, on the north side of Lennox Passage.[^2] The Department described a square wooden tower, 8.2 m (27 ft) high from its stone foundation, with a fixed white light 7.6 m (25 ft) above high water. The light, it said, "should be visible 10 miles from all points of approach by water."[^2]

## The keepers

Martin Burke is the first keeper LighthouseFriends names. It gives his service as 1902 to 1912, and the Department's keeper table for 1904 lists his name beside Bourgeois Inlet, with an appointment date of 1 December 1902.[^3][^4] D. Sampson followed, from 1912 to at least 1923.[^4] A local history says the keeper was paid $90 a year.[^7]

## After the keepers

By 1962, LighthouseFriends says, the tower had fallen into disrepair. Its lantern top was taken off and storm lanterns were mounted on the tower instead.[^4] Electricity replaced the kerosene light in 1966.[^7] On 2 June 1989 the Coast Guard put an aluminium skeletal tower on the point and burned the old wooden tower. Residents were upset that they had no chance to save it.[^4][^5] The Coast Guard explained that the stairs and timber frame were rotten and the tower was hazardous to maintain. The local history gives the cost of maintenance as the reason.[^4][^7] The Nova Scotia Lighthouse Preservation Society dates the replacement to 1990 or 1991, and the local history says 1989.[^6][^7]

## Today

The light station was sold in 2003 to a local group, now the River Bourgeois Community Services Society, which built an exact replica of the 1903 tower.[^5][^7] Rowlett describes the replica as an 8 m (26 ft) pyramidal wood tower, square in plan, with a red lantern.[^5] The Coast Guard list shows the light as an isophase red light every four seconds, while Rowlett describes a continuous red light.[^8][^5] It is privately maintained and lit in season.[^4][^6] The tower is reached across private property, so LighthouseFriends suggests viewing it from the south side of the church at Church Point.[^4][^5] Rowlett adds that the tower can be reached at low water, though high tide may cut off the way in, and lists the site as open with the tower closed.[^5]
