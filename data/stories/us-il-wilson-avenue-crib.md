The Wilson Avenue Crib is a granite water-intake crib in Lake Michigan, off Montrose Harbor, five miles north of downtown Chicago.[^1][^2] A light was set up on it in October 1915, while the crib was still being built, and the crib was finished in May 1918.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree on how far out it stands. LighthouseFriends gives "roughly three miles" in one place and "just over two miles" in its map note, and Preservation Chicago places it 2.1 miles east of Montrose Harbor.[^1][^2]

The superstructure is square-hewn granite on a 90-foot (27 m) steel caisson, around a 40-foot (12 m) inner well. Work began in 1915 with the sinking of the caisson.[^1][^2] On 23 October 1915 a temporary light went up to mark the works: a fixed white light hung from a wooden post on a timber platform, with a focal plane of 35 feet (11 m).[^1] When the crib neared completion, the superstructure was found to be 18 inches (46 cm) out of plumb. Crews blasted away the concrete that joined it to the caisson and used 300 jacks to level it. That delicate work held completion back until 1 May 1918.[^1]

The permanent light stands on a red circular tower with a lantern room, above brick walls that rise from the stone.[^1][^2] Its first light was an acetylene light visible for up to eight miles. A fog bell struck a double blow every twenty seconds in low visibility.[^1] The Coast Guard's 2025 Light List gives the signal as a horn, one blast every twenty seconds, and the light as a quick white light (QW) on a cylindrical tower.[^3]

The crib originally supplied water through eight miles of tunnel to an onshore pumping station at Wilson Avenue.[^1][^2] For many years the city kept isolated crews on the water cribs around the clock. The cribs still in use are staffed remotely.[^2] Preservation Chicago reported in 2017 that the city planned to demolish this crib under its four-year Capital Improvement Program, announced in 2015, after many years on standby service.[^2]

The city's water department owns the crib, LighthouseFriends reports. Its tower is closed, and the crib is best seen by boat or plane.[^1] The 2025 Light List still carries the Wilson Avenue Intake Crib Light.[^3] These sources do not show whether the demolition has gone ahead.
