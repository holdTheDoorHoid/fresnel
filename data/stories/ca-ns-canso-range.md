The Canso range lights are two white towers on the south side of Canso Harbour, Nova Scotia.[^1] Set one behind the other, they have guided ships into the harbour's south entrance since 1905.[^3][^6]

## The place

Canso is the easternmost settlement on the Nova Scotia mainland, and the harbour is sheltered by several islands.[^1] In the fiscal year to June 1900, the Department of Marine's hydrographic notes recorded a shoal in the harbour, discovered by the steamer Minia.[^4]

After a survey of the harbour, the Department's officer issued a notice to mariners correcting the sailing directions for entering it.[^5] The report for fiscal 1900–01 adds that "arrangements are being made for the erection of range lights to guide into the south entrance of this fine harbour."[^5]

## Building the light

The Department's report for fiscal 1904–05 says: "Range light buildings were erected on the south side of this harbour."[^6] Both towers were enclosed wooden buildings, square in plan, with sloping sides, topped by square wooden lanterns and painted white.[^1][^6]

The front tower was 9.8 m (32 ft) high to the lantern vent, and its fixed red catoptric light stood 14.0 m (46 ft) above high water. It was visible for 8 miles in the line of range.[^6] The rear tower stood on land 21.3 m (70 ft) above high water and was 13.4 m (44 ft) high. Its fixed red seventh-order dioptric light was 32.9 m (108 ft) above the water and visible for 10 miles.[^1][^6] The work was done by contract by E. F. Munro of Westville for $2,590.[^6]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends names William J. Mathews as the first keeper, appointed on 17 December 1904 at a salary of $200 a year.[^1][^2] He is listed until 1930. H. Horton kept the lights for 1930 and 1931. Robert M. Eustace took charge in 1931 and kept them until the range was electrified in 1958.[^1][^2]

NSLPS lists a keeper named Bill Ringer for the front light from 1986 to 1996.[^3] The same page says the lights were automated by 1993, so the listing does not fit its own dates, and the sources read do not settle it.[^3][^9]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

On 20 October 1912 the schooner Clara was wrecked in a gale at the entrance to Canso harbour. The Canso lifeboat went to her, and after "a very hard pull" it saved four men and two women.[^8] The report does not connect the wreck with the range lights.[^8]

## After the keepers

The range was electrified in 1958.[^1]

In 1968 the towers were fitted with 400-watt mercury-vapour floodlights, and the characteristic of both lights was changed from fixed red to fixed yellow.[^1][^2] When the floodlights were installed, the lantern room on the front tower was found to be rotten and was removed. The top of the tower was decked over, and the light was mounted on the open deck.[^1][^2] NSLPS dates the yellow light to 1992, so the sources disagree on when the change came.[^3]

In late 2008 new concrete foundations were built under both towers, and both were clad in vinyl siding. The front tower was moved back from the water and given a new lantern room matching the original design.[^1][^2]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns both towers, and the grounds and towers are closed to visitors.[^1][^2] The rear tower stands behind a seaside building on Union Street, in the town.[^1]

The Coast Guard's current list gives both lights as fixed red, from white square towers with a red upper portion and a red vertical stripe. The front light has a focal height of 11.9 m (39 ft) and a nominal range of 13 nautical miles. The rear light has a focal height of 28.5 m (94 ft) and stands on a 13.8 m (45 ft) tower.[^7] NSLPS marks both lights as not operating, but the Coast Guard list gives both as year-round lights.[^3][^7][^9]
