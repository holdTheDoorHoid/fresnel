Pointe à Brideau Range Rear Light, on the shore of Caraquet Bay in the New Brunswick town of Caraquet, is a white tower with blue trim: the rear light of a range the Canadian Coast Guard put up in 1978.[^1][^2] The town later wrapped the Coast Guard's skeletal tower in wood.[^1][^2] The light has been inactive since September 2020, but the tower still stands in a town beach park, where the grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^1][^2]

## The place

The tower stands behind the Place Caraquet shopping centre, at the end of Rue Foley, where the road runs down to the shore.[^1][^2] A range is a pair of lights, one behind the other, that a ship keeps in line to hold a channel.[^5] This tower is the rear light. The sources mention a front light only in passing: the Lighthouse Directory has a 2019 photograph of a former front light that has since been removed.[^2]

Caraquet had range lights before this one.[^3] The Department of Marine's towers at Lower Caraquet were built to lead ships into the harbour through Caraquet channel, and they were placed in operation on 24 September 1903.[^3][^4] LighthouseFriends places that older pair in the separate Town of Bas-Caraquet, where the old wooden rear tower was moved to a municipal park and refurbished.[^4] The sources read for this story do not connect the 1903 towers with the Pointe à Brideau tower. They date the Pointe à Brideau range to 1978, when the Coast Guard's skeletal tower went up.[^1][^2]

## Building the light

The Coast Guard set up a skeletal range tower at Pointe à Brideau in 1978.[^1][^2] Several years later the Town of Caraquet developed its beach area around the rear range tower.[^1] The town was not satisfied with the bare Coast Guard frame, and it won permission to put a wooden wrapper around it.[^1]

In 1991 the city added siding to the skeletal tower, which made it the centrepiece of a town park.[^2] LighthouseFriends calls the result a faithful picture of the range towers the Department of Marine once built.[^1] The tower that stands now is a 13 m (43 ft) square pyramidal wooden tower with a lantern and gallery.[^2] The Coast Guard looks after the navigational aid inside, and the town maintains the wooden shell.[^1]

Before 2019 the tower was painted white with red trim and a vertical red stripe on the range line.[^2] In 2019 the town restored and renovated the tower and gave it the white and blue scheme it has today.[^2]

## After the keepers

None of the sources read for this story names a keeper for the Pointe à Brideau light, and none gives a date for its automation.

The range was deactivated in September 2020. The tower still carries the red range daymark.[^2] The Lighthouse Directory's entry includes a 2019 photograph of the former front light, which was removed; the sources do not say when it came down.[^2]

## Today

The tower stands in the town park at the end of Rue Foley. The grounds are open and the tower is closed to visitors.[^1][^2] The Lighthouse Directory names the Canadian Coast Guard as owner and the Ville de Caraquet as site manager, while LighthouseFriends says the City of Caraquet owns the lighthouse.[^2][^1] The two accounts differ on ownership, and this story does not settle it.
