Gimli Light marks the harbour at Gimli, Manitoba, on the west shore of Lake Winnipeg.[^1] The Icelandic settlers who arrived in 1875 and founded the town needed a harbour for their fishing boats and for the freighters on the lake.[^3] A lighthouse was added to the pier in 1910.[^1] Its original lantern room now sits on a replica tower by the waterfront.[^1][^2]

## Building the light

A federal grant made the harbour possible, but the sources disagree on the year. The Manitoba Historical Society gives 1899 and LighthouseFriends gives 1898.[^1][^2] The harbour authority says Icelandic contractor J.J. Vopni built the first permanent pier in 1900, for $9,000 with federal help.[^3]

The lighthouse of 1910 was a 7 m (23 ft) wooden tower, square in plan, with a square wooden lantern. The Department of Marine and Fisheries recorded its erection, superintended by W. S. Young, Inspector of Fisheries at Selkirk.[^5] LighthouseFriends gives its cost as $661.33.[^2] The department's list of new lights gives the optic as a fifth-order, 360-degree lens with a duplex lamp.[^6]

In 1912 the tower was moved to the outer end of a new extension of the pier. LighthouseFriends says a Winnipeg firm, Buchanan & Fraser, was paid $100 for the move.[^2]

## The keepers

The first keeper was E. G. Thomson, hired in 1910 at an annual salary of $80.[^2] The department's keeper table for the year ending March 1911 lists "E.G.Thompson" at Gimli, appointed on 21 January 1910 at $80.[^7] LighthouseFriends says K. Samundsson replaced Thomson in 1912 and kept the light until at least 1923.[^2] The keeper tables for the years ending March 1913 and March 1914 also give Samundsson's appointment as 9 April 1912.[^10][^8]

Pete Thompson kept the light in the 1930s and 1940s, from 1933 to at least 1946. He also worked as a scaler at the Armstrong-Gimli fish processing plant.[^2] The sources do not say when the keeping ended.

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

The harbour was in use before the light stood. On 29 August 1907 a northeast gale caught the steamer Viking, of Winnipeg, lying at the dock at Gimli, and wrecked her. The department's casualty record says she "became a total wreck by pounding against the dock."[^9] The light was not built until 1910.[^2]

In April 1943 an ice pile-up pushed the lighthouse over and damaged it.[^1][^2] The sources do not say how it was repaired, or what became of the original tower.

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard's list gives the Gimli light at the outer end of the breakwater. It shows a flashing red light every four seconds from a square skeleton tower with a red and white daymark. Its focal height is 7.7 m (25 ft), and its nominal range is 5 nautical miles. The list marks it as seasonal.[^4]

In 1973 the original lantern room, which a keeper had saved, was set on a replica tower.[^1][^2] The Manitoba Historical Society says the replica stands in the fishing-village area as a tourist attraction, and that the New Iceland Heritage Museum manages the lighthouse.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the replica is attached to buildings near the foot of the pier that house the Lake Winnipeg Visitor Centre and the harbour master, and that the lighthouse can be seen along the waterfront.[^2] The sources do not say whether the breakwater is open to walkers.
