Crisp Point Light stands on the Lake Superior shore of Luce County, Michigan, about 13 miles west of Whitefish Point. Its brick tower was first lit on 5 May 1904, for a point where ships bound down the lake in thick weather could be run ashore.[^1][^2][^3]

## The place

The Lighthouse Board's report for 1896 and 1897 described the danger. Ships bound down the lake in thick weather tried to make Whitefish Point, and "a slight variation in their course from the nearest point of departure will run them ashore near Crisps Point." The report adds: "Several wrecks have occurred here."[^3]

The Board asked for a light and fog-signal station "on or near Crisps Point," estimated at $18,000, and repeated the request each year.[^1][^3] Congress appropriated the money on 28 June 1902.[^1]

The Life-Saving Service also kept a station on the point. Its 1888 report mentions the "Crisp's Station, (Tenth District,) Lake Superior."[^1][^4]

The fog here was heavy as well. A 1931 Bulletin fog list, printed after its account of the year ending 30 June 1930, gives Crisp Point 644 hours of fog, against an average of 491 hours over 26 years.[^9]

## Building the light

The site was surveyed and its title obtained in August 1902, and the metalwork and materials went north during the 1903 season.[^1] Work closed for the season on 18 December 1903. A crew came back in May 1904 and finished on 10 June.[^1]

The tower is brick and 58 feet (18 m) tall.[^2][^1] It tapers from fourteen feet (4.3 m) across at the base to nine feet (2.7 m) at the shaft, and a gallery goes round the octagonal lantern. The keeper's duplex stood 60 feet (18 m) west of the tower.[^1]

The lens was a fourth-order Fresnel from Sautter, Lemonnier & Cie.[^1] The Society's record gives it as "FOURTH ORDER, FRESNEL."[^2] The keepers first showed its "fixed red light" on the night of 5 May 1904.[^1]

The first head keeper, John E. Smith, wrote on 30 April 1904 that he had "found the station in good condition or as good as could be expected under the circumstances." The dwelling's floors were not yet finished, so he "decided to live for the present in the workmen's quarters."[^1]

In 1905 a second fog-signal plant was installed, with a ten-inch chime whistle so that its tone would not be confused with the one at Whitefish Point.[^1]

In 1914 the Bulletin reported that Crisp Point's light had changed from oil to incandescent oil vapor.[^6] In 1933 the fog signal changed from steam to a six-inch siren, driven by air compressed by a diesel engine.[^1]

## The keepers

The archive's roster for the station runs from 1904 to 1940 and lists the head keepers and their assistants.[^2] Herbert P. Crittenden was head keeper from 1917 to 1922, and Joseph N. Singleton from 1923 to 1939.[^2] Albert F. Brown held the post in 1940, the last year in the roster.[^2]

Singleton retired "at the end of the season in December 1939." He had spent thirty years in the Lighthouse Service, with postings at Charity Island and Two Harbors.[^1] The family lived at the station, and LighthouseFriends calls the Singleton boys "lighthouse kids."[^1]

In April 1933 a newspaper article, quoted by LighthouseFriends, described First Assistant Keeper Brown's plan for reaching the station. He would drive as far as Eckerman, take a stage north to Whitefish, and then make "a 20 mile snow-shoe trip to the Crisp Point Light."[^1]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

On 13 September 1914 the launch *Endress* capsized off Whitefish Point. The keeper of Crisp Point was a passenger, and his report, printed in the Bulletin, says: "Seven of us managed to get in the lifeboat that was being towed."[^5] The Soo Times, quoted in the same Bulletin, credits the rescue of eleven men to Robert Carlson, keeper of Whitefish Point Light Station, and two fishermen, who went out in a small flat-bottomed skiff.[^5]

On 12 June 1920 Crittenden and two assistants at Crisp Point went to help the steamer *F. R. Hazard* after a collision with another vessel.[^7]

In 1921 the Commissioner reported that Keeper H. P. Crittenden towed the motor boat *Gladys L*, with four passengers aboard, to shore. He also assisted the Coast Guard crew that helped the *Sea Fox* in a severe gale.[^8]

## After the keepers

The light was electrified and automated in 1941, after two years of planning, and the fog signal was discontinued before the last keepers were withdrawn.[^1] The Society's record gives 1947 as the year the station was discontinued.[^2] LighthouseFriends ties the automation to 1941, which leaves the 1947 date unexplained.

In 1965 the Coast Guard removed every building except the tower and its attached service room.[^1]

In 1988 Don and Nellie Ross found the abandoned light and founded the Crisp Point Light Historical Society to save the tower. The Coast Guard decommissioned the light after the 1992 season, and the Society got a lease for the tower in 1994.[^1]

A violent storm in November 1996 destroyed the service room at the base of the tower.[^1] The tower went to Luce County in 1997, and the Society spent $42,000 hauling in stone to protect it the following year.[^1] A new service room was built between 2006 and 2008, and a visitor center was built inland in 2009.[^1]

In 2012 the Coast Guard gave the Society a permit to operate the light as a private aid to navigation, and a 300 mm lens was installed in the lantern on 23 November 2012.[^1][^2]

## Today

The Society's record lists the station as open to the public, a private aid, and an active aid to navigation.[^2] The Society says "The lighthouse grounds are always open."[^10] Its lighthouse and visitor center hours "vary depending on volunteer availability and weather," and the site said "CURRENTLY CLOSED FOR THE SEASON" when it was last updated on 17 October 2026.[^10]

The last seven miles of Luce County Road 412 are seasonal. The road "is not snowplowed in winter and is a seasonal road," and the Society warns that "Crisp Point does not have trash service."[^10] Camping is limited: "No camping is allowed except for the volunteer lighthouse keepers."[^10]

The original boathouse, barn, oil house and two outbuildings are recorded as "ALL DEMOLISHED."[^2]
