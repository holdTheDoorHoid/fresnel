Mahukona Light stands on a promontory in the north-west corner of Hawai'i Island, south of Mahukona Harbor in North Kohala.[^1][^2] Lighthouse Friends and Rowlett date the station to 1889, though the US Lighthouse Society archive gives 1904 as its year of establishment.[^2][^1][^3] The tower standing today, of concrete, dates from 1915.[^1][^2]

## The place

North Kohala's sugar plantations had rough landings on an exposed coast.[^2] The Hawaiian Railroad, complete by January 1883, moved the plantations' sugarcane byproducts over the hill to Mahukona, a sheltered cove on the island's lee side.[^2] Steamers called at the port, but in thick weather they had trouble finding it. In 1889 Charles L. Wight, president of the Hawaiian Railroad Company, asked for a light to mark the port.[^2]

The islands' lights were then kept by the monarchy. The 1936 Lighthouse Service Bulletin says navigational aids in the islands were maintained by the monarchy up to 1893, by the provisional government between 1893 and 1894, and by the Republic of Hawaii from 1894 to 1900.[^4] The same Bulletin says lighthouses in the islands have been maintained by the federal government since 1904.[^4] The port closed at the start of the Second World War for security reasons, and the train stopped after the 1945 season.[^2]

## Building the light

Wight suggested building the light on a ridge south of the harbor. He estimated it could cost about $250 and be maintained by a local man for $12.50 a month, and he argued against sending men from Honolulu.[^2]

The tower was a truncated cone, 12 feet (3.7 m) across at the base and 3 feet (0.9 m) at the top. It was built of rock with a thick coat of mortar, topped with solid concrete, and a five-sided lantern sat on it.[^2] On 5 August 1889 the surveyor general, W. D. Alexander, reported the tower complete, with a focal plane of 75 feet (23 m) and a light visible for ten miles.[^2]

The dates disagree. The US Lighthouse Society archive gives 1904 as the year the place was established.[^3] Lighthouse Friends and Rowlett give 1889, and the 1936 Bulletin lists Mahukona among lights "established prior to 1890".[^2][^1][^4] The Bulletin's 1904 is the start of federal maintenance of lighthouses in the islands generally. That may be what the archive means, but no source read says so.[^4]

On 20 June 1907 a lens-lantern light went up 51 feet (16 m) east and 11 feet (3.4 m) west of the old tower, on a white mast. It was shown 35 feet (11 m) above the ground and 64 feet (19.5 m) above the water.[^2]

In 1915 a concrete tower 22 feet (6.7 m) high went up near the original light, topped by a flashing acetylene light.[^2][^1] The Bulletin for 1915 lists Mahukona among lights changed from oil to acetylene, and from fixed to flashing.[^6] The Commissioner of Lighthouses' report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1915 lists it among the lighted aids in commission on that date.[^5]

## The keepers

The US Lighthouse Society archive names four people for the station. Its principal keepers were C. S. Wright (1904–1906) and Edward Madden (1913–1915). Its laborers were Ed. A. Fraser (1904–1906) and Robert R. Elgin (1906–1912).[^3]

Lighthouse Friends calls Wright, Elgin and Madden the head keepers. It gives Elgin's years as 1906 to 1912, the same years the archive gives, so the two sources differ only on his job.[^2] The sources read name no keepers for 1889 to 1903, or for the years after 1915.[^2][^3]

## Today

Rowlett lists the light as active, with a focal plane of 64 feet (19.5 m) and a white flash every four seconds.[^1] The US Coast Guard owns it.[^1][^2] Rowlett and Lighthouse Friends describe the site as open and the tower as closed.[^1][^2] The Society's place record reads "Open to Public: No", which disagrees.[^3] A short walk south from Mahukona Beach Park, off Highway 270, reaches the light.[^1] The remains of the 1889 tower can still be seen just north of the present light, and the harbor is now part of Mahukona Beach Park.[^2]
