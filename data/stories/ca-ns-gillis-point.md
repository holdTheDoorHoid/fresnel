Gillis Point Lighthouse is a square wooden tower at the south entrance to Maskell's Harbour, on the Bras d'Or Lakes in Victoria County, Cape Breton.[^1][^3] It was first lit on 1 January 1895 and was discontinued in 2011.[^1][^3]

## The place

Gillis Point stands on the Bras d'Or Lakes, about 11 km (seven miles) north of Uniacke Point.[^1] Grand Narrows, also called Barra Strait, is just over a kilometre wide and connects the northern and southern basins of the lakes.[^1] The Department of Marine had built a light on Uniacke Point in 1874 to guide mariners through Barra Strait.[^1] The Gillis Point light helped mariners transit the strait, and it also marked the entrance to the anchorage at Maskell's Harbour, which lies between Gillis Point and Ponys Point.[^1] The register calls it the only light built to guide vessels north into or out of Grand Narrows and to and from Barra Strait.[^2]

Most of the lights on the Bras d'Or Lakes, the register adds, served the coal and other goods carried from Sydney.[^2]

## Building the light

Lighthouse Friends says that after the Grand Narrows bridge was completed in 1890, the Department decided to discontinue the Uniacke Point light and replace it with one at Gillis Point.[^1] The Department's report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1904 gives different dates for the Uniacke Point light. The light at Iona, near the extremity of Uniacke Point, had been maintained from 1874 to 1894.[^5] In December 1901 it was put back into operation to mark Grand Narrows, in addition to the lights on the railway bridge.[^5]

The Intercolonial Railway built the bridge in the late 1880s. Its red and green lights on the swing span were not sufficiently conspicuous for mariners in bad weather, and the railway placed a fixed white light directly above them.[^1]

The Gillis Point light went into service on 1 January 1895.[^1] Lighthouse Friends quotes the Department's annual report for 1895 as describing a wooden building: a square pyramidal tower with a small dwelling for the keeper attached, painted white, under a red iron lantern.[^1] The height from base to the vane was 11.6 m (38 ft).[^1] The light was fixed white and seventh-order dioptric, elevated 22.6 m (74 ft) above high water. The report said it should be visible 14 miles from all points of approach by water; it does not say whether these are nautical miles.[^1] The register gives the tower's height as 11.3 m (37 ft), a little less than the 1895 base-to-vane figure, and its build date as 1895.[^1][^2]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends says F.X. McNeil was transferred to Gillis Point from the Grand Narrows light on Uniacke Point, and that Philip E. McNeil took over as keeper that same year.[^1] Hector MacLean replaced Philip in 1897.[^1] Two of the Department's keepers' lists print "McLean, H" against Gillis Point: the one for the year ended 30 June 1902, and the one for the year ended 30 June 1905.[^7][^6] That fits Lighthouse Friends' account of Hector MacLean.[^1]

Hector MacLean died in 1916, aged sixty-eight, and his wife Catherine became keeper.[^1] The register says she kept the light from 1916 until 1939, and that she was one of the few women to keep a light in Nova Scotia.[^2] A Nova Scotia Archives photograph of the tower, taken in 1937, is captioned as kept by "Mrs. Catherine MacLean, the only woman light-keeper in Nova Scotia."[^4] The two sources do not agree on whether she was the only one.[^2][^4] Her son Andrew M. MacLean took charge in 1939 and kept the light until 1967.[^1]

## After the keepers

In 1978 the dwelling, unused since Andrew MacLean retired in 1967, was demolished, and the tower was renovated and covered in vinyl siding.[^1] The Nova Scotia Lighthouse Preservation Society's list says the light was automated by 1973, and ran seasonally from April to December from 1980.[^3] The same list says a new tower replaced the original tower of 1895 in 1984,[^3] and that an electric lantern in 1992 showed a flashing white light (4 s), seasonal from May to December.[^3]

The register gives the tower's build date as 1895 and describes its current, as-built form.[^2] The sources disagree on whether the tower standing today is the 1895 one.[^2][^3]

The light was discontinued in 2011.[^1] In 2017 it was recognized as a heritage lighthouse under the Heritage Lighthouse Protection Act.[^1] The register gives the recognition date as 20 October 2017.[^2]

## Today

The Society for the Preservation of the Gillis Point Lighthouse owns the lighthouse.[^1] The register says it is clearly visible from the water, and that it has been a meeting place for the surrounding communities and a cherished landmark for local residents.[^2] The list gives the site access as by car.[^3] The sources do not say whether the tower is open to visitors.
