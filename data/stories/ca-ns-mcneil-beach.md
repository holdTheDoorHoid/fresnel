The McNeil Beach lighthouse stands on the shore of the Great Bras d'Or channel, just south of the east end of the Seal Island Bridge on Boularderie Island, Cape Breton.[^1][^6] It is a square wooden tower, 10 m (33 ft) tall, built in 1909 to replace a pole light of 1884.[^1][^7]

## The place

Boularderie Island lies at the entrance to Bras d'Or Lake, and the Great Bras d'Or Channel runs along its northwestern shore.[^1] Between Seal Reefs and McNeil Beach the channel is only 180 m wide, with depths of 12.8 to 23.8 m.[^1] The Department's list of lights for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1903 includes a "McNeil Beach Pole Light".[^2]

## Building the light

In 1884 the Department of Marine set up a beacon light on McNeil Beach.[^1] The first light was a dioptric lantern on a pole 7.6 m (25 ft) high, with a white shed at its base. Lighthouse Friends says its fixed red light stood 10 m (33 ft) above the water and could be seen for about eight miles.[^1]

In 1909 a new tower replaced the pole.[^1][^3] The Department's report for fiscal 1909-10 describes the new tower as square in plan, with sloping sides, and says it "replaces the old pole light formerly exhibited here."[^3] Lighthouse Friends says L. Mury of West Arichat was paid $780 to build it.[^1] The report says the light was fixed red, with a sixth-order apparatus, and "should be visible five miles from all points of approach by water."[^3]

The Nova Scotia society dates a sixth-order dioptric lens to 1922, in operation from 1930, and gives 1909 only as "alteration of some form."[^6]

## The keepers

Lauchlin McNeil was placed in charge of the light on 6 August 1884.[^1] The Department's keepers' table for fiscal 1910-11 has a line for "McNeil's Beach" with Lauchlin McNeil's appointment on "Aug. 6." The year is not legible in the text version.[^4]

Lighthouse Friends says he kept the light until 1927, when he retired at the age of 92.[^1] He died in 1937 at 102, and was one of the oldest people on Boularderie Island when he died.[^1]

## After the keepers

Construction of the Seal Island Bridge began in 1960, and the bridge was completed in 1961. When it opened, lighted buoys were placed to mark the channel beneath it, and Lighthouse Friends says the lighthouse was then discontinued.[^1]

Lighthouse Friends dates the discontinuance to 1961. The Nova Scotia society, the Rowlett directory and the Boularderie Island history all record the light as not operating from 1955 to 1962.[^6][^7][^5] The Rowlett directory adds that it has been inactive since 1965.[^7]

In 1965 a private owner bought the lighthouse for $250 and built a one-storey addition on one side of the tower. The addition was later removed.[^1] The Boularderie Island history gives 1966 for the private purchase.[^5]

In 1998 the owner gave the lighthouse to Acadia University, which had no long-term use for it and returned the deed to the owner.[^1][^7] The Boularderie Island history gives 1996 for the gift to Acadia University.[^5] The restoration of 2004 and 2005 used money from the J. M. Kaplan Fund.[^1][^7]

The owner died in 2015. The owner's daughter has offered to donate the lighthouse to the Boularderie Island Historical Society.[^1]

## Today

The sources disagree on who owns the lighthouse. Lighthouse Friends' visitor note says it is owned by Acadia University, while its history says the university returned it to the owner. The Rowlett directory names the Boularderie Island Historical Society as owner.[^1][^7]

The light is no longer operating.[^6] The Boularderie Island Lighthouse Preservation Project restored the building.[^1] The grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^1][^7]

The trail to the light descends through private property, according to the Rowlett directory.[^7] Lighthouse Friends reports that visitors in 2020 found the right-of-way blocked and a "No Lighthouse Access" sign.[^1] Because of that, ask the society before going, and do not cross private land without permission.
