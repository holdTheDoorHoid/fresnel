The Cape Negro Island light stands at the southeast end of South Island, one of the two lobes of Cape Negro Island, at the entrance to Negro Harbour in Shelburne County, Nova Scotia.[^1][^2] Lights have shown on the island since 1872, and the concrete tower standing now dates from 1915.[^1][^2]

## The place

Cape Negro Island is made up of two lobes, known locally as North Island and South Island, which a narrow strip of land links.[^1]

The first light stood on the northeast side of South Island. In 1915 the Department decided that navigation would be better served by a lighthouse on the southeast end of South Island than on the northeast tip.[^1]

The heritage statement ties the new tower to changes in technology that let fishermen travel farther to the fishing grounds, and to growing coastal traffic.[^2] It says a light was needed that could be seen by vessels approaching Negro Harbour from both the east and the west.[^2]

## Building the light

Lighthouse Friends quotes the Department of Marine’s report for 1872, which describes a light recently erected “at the entrance of Negro harbour, County of Shelburne.”[^1] It was first exhibited on 6 September 1872. The square wooden lighthouse was 8.8 m (29 ft) tall.[^1]

James D. Coffin was contracted to build the first lighthouse for $970. About $429 went on its lamps and revolving apparatus.[^1] Two years after the tower went up, the Department decided it was too small to serve as a dwelling, and $600 was provided for a separate house for the keeper.[^1]

In 1886 the old light was found to be too decayed to repair. Lighthouse Friends says a $975 tender from a Mr. DeChamp was accepted, and work began that year on a wooden octagonal tower 13.4 m (44 ft) tall.[^1] The heritage statement dates that wooden tower to 1887, and says both of the earlier lights stood 1.3 km north of the present one.[^2] The two sources differ by a year, and both dates are given here.

The present tower was built in 1915 at the southeast end of South Island.[^1][^2] Lighthouse Friends gives the contract to D. C. Mulhall for $11,200 and gives the tower’s height as 12.8 m (42 ft).[^1][^2] The heritage statement gives the same 12.8 m, measured from base to vane.[^2] The Coast Guard list gives the height of the structure above ground as 12.0 m.[^3]

## The keepers

James McKinnon was appointed keeper in 1872 at a salary of $100 a year. Lighthouse Friends gives his service as 1872 to 1897.[^1]

Byron Nickerson followed him, from 1897 to 1916.[^1] The Department’s keepers’ table for fiscal 1901–02 lists Nickerson at Negro Island,[^4] and so does the table in the 1907 report.[^5] The table in the report published in 1906 gives his appointment date as 26 July 1897, which fits the year Lighthouse Friends gives.[^6]

Freeland Perry, who fished and also cut hair on the island, kept the light from 1916 to 1943.[^2] His son-in-law, Harry Perry, kept it from 1943 to 1963.[^2] The heritage statement says the lighthouse is significant for the community “because of its association with the Perry Family.”[^2]

Lighthouse Friends lists two later keepers, Radford Malone in 1964 and Carmen Cook in 1965. It gives no other dates for either.[^1]

## After the keepers

The heritage statement says the concrete tower belongs to a group of towers built between 1906 and 1915 by the Department of Marine and Fisheries, and it gives the light historical and architectural value.[^2] Lighthouse Friends says the light was listed on the Canadian Register of Historic Places in 2009.[^1]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the light.[^1] Lighthouse Friends says the grounds are open, the tower is closed, and the light is best seen by boat.[^1]

The Coast Guard list calls the light Long Rock Light and Fog Signal and places it on the southeast end of the island.[^3] It describes a white light with a long flash every six seconds, a focal height of 28.3 m (93 ft) and a range of 11 nautical miles.[^3] Its fog horn sounds for six seconds, then falls silent for 54 seconds.[^3]

Lighthouse Friends gives a focal plane of 27.7 m (91 ft), a little below the list’s 28.3 m.[^1][^3]
