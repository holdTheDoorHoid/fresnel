Battery Point Breakwater light is a white square tower with a red upper portion, standing at the outer end of a breakwater on the eastern entrance to Lunenburg Harbour, in Lunenburg County on Nova Scotia's South Shore.[^3][^2] The point it is named for has carried a light since 1864, and the tower on the breakwater today dates from 1951.[^1][^2]

## Building the light

Lunenburg's settlers came mainly from Germany, Switzerland and the Montbéliard region of France, starting in 1753, and the town built its living on farming, fishing and shipbuilding.[^1] The first light on Battery Point was a dwelling with a twelve-sided lantern room on its roof, which Lighthouse Friends says held five lamps in twelve-inch reflectors.[^1] The NSLPS dates that first light to 1864 and describes its lantern as sitting on the roof of the dwelling.[^2]

The sources do not agree on what came next. Lighthouse Friends says a light was established near the end of the breakwater in 1937, and that the original lighthouse apparently kept operating beside it until at least 1948.[^1] The NSLPS describes the 1937 light as a square wooden tower on the dwelling, and records that the original tower was replaced between 1883 and 1922, adding that a new tower may have been built on the roof.[^2] Both sources date the present tower to 1951, when Lighthouse Friends says a new breakwater was built and the tower placed on it.[^1][^2] The Canadian Coast Guard list gives the tower as 8.0 m (26 ft) high, with its light 7.5 m (25 ft) above the water.[^3] The NSLPS's general description gives the light's elevation as 6.7 m (22 ft), while its own details give 25 ft.[^2]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends says John A. Ernst was appointed keeper in November 1864 at an annual salary of $240, and that he built two structures near the light, 6.7 by 5.2 m (22 by 17 ft) and 4.3 by 4.3 m (14 by 14 ft), to store wood and coal.[^1] Lighthouse Friends then names a run of keepers, from Mrs. John A. Ernst (1897, with no first name given) to F. Lohnes (1934 to 1935), and the station roster gives each name, role and years.[^1]

The Department of Marine's keeper tables differ from that list on how long Henry Naas served. The table printed in 1910 gives Henry Naas's appointment at Battery Point as 12 March 1897, which agrees with the start of his term.[^4] The tables printed in 1911 and 1912 still print his name against Battery Point, after the 1908 end date that Lighthouse Friends gives.[^5][^6]

Harvey L. Huskins came to the station in January 1962 from service on Little Hope Island and Spectacle Island, and stayed for twenty-five years.[^1] Lighthouse Friends quotes Huskins saying there is "no money in it", and adds that he would go back to the work if he were young.[^1]

## After the keepers

Huskins retired on 19 June 1987, when the station was officially de-staffed.[^1] The six-room dwelling was declared surplus and offered for sale, but the terms of sale required the owner to move the structure, and it sat vacant for eight years before a buyer was found.[^1] The NSLPS says the light was automated by 1993.[^2]

In 2015 the Lunenburg Waterfront Association received $5,000 from the This Place Matters competition, plus $2,544 through crowd-funding, and raised $2,000 more from the sale of reproductions of a painting of the lighthouse.[^1] That money paid for millwork and metalwork repairs in 2016 and for painting the tower, and the roof was re-shingled in 2018.[^1]

## Today

Lighthouse Friends says that all that remains of the light station is the 1951 breakwater tower, and that the Canadian Coast Guard owns it.[^1] The Coast Guard's list gives the light as isophase red, equal periods of light and dark every four seconds, shown round the clock.[^3] The NSLPS notes a fog signal of one blast every twenty seconds.[^2]

The grounds and tower are closed to visitors. There is no public right-of-way to the lighthouse, and as access to the breakwater is not permitted, Lighthouse Friends advises viewing the lighthouse from a distance.[^1] The best views come from the water or from the nearby shore.[^1]
