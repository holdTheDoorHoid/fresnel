The Cap-à-l'Est light, known in English as Cape East, stands on the Saguenay River at Sainte-Rose-du-Nord, 31 km (19 mi) below Saguenay, where a white octagonal concrete tower with a red lantern room rises 10 m (33 ft) above the ground.[^3][^1][^2] The Coast Guard's list gives the tower as 8.1 m high.[^5] Quebec's heritage register quotes a 2014 inventory of the Fjord-du-Saguenay regional county municipality, which calls it the last light of its traditional type built on the banks of the Saguenay.[^1]

## The place

The lower Saguenay is the stretch LighthouseFriends calls Saguenay Fjord National Park, and it is roughly 180 metres deep there.[^3] The sources disagree on the bank the light stands on. LighthouseFriends places it on the north side of the river, while the Friends of the St. Lawrence Lighthouses (Les amis des phares du Saint-Laurent) say the south bank.[^3][^6] Les amis des phares also say the light marks a critical turn in the fjord, on the route to Port-Alfred and Baie des Ha! Ha!.[^6] The Quebec register says the light was probably set up to serve the navigation that the region's industry needed, and the Port-Alfred port works at the head of Baie des Ha! Ha!, early in the twentieth century.[^1]

Lights were not new on this river. LighthouseFriends says a lighthouse went up on Lark Islet at the river's mouth in 1872, and that the government set up five pairs of range lights to mark the turn below Chicoutimi the following year.[^3]

## Building the light

The Quebec register dates the first light here to 1907, when a light was lit on an open wooden structure.[^1] Its other entry for the site is more cautious, saying a first wooden light is said to have been built that year and was replaced in 1910 by the present concrete tower.[^2] LighthouseFriends dates the concrete tower to 1909 and says day labour built it under the supervision of H. de Haan, at a cost of $2,636.16.[^3] The register and Les amis des phares both give 1910.[^1][^6]

The lens was of the sixth order.[^3][^4] The Department of Marine and Fisheries' annual report for the fiscal year ended 31 March 1911 lists "Cape East, Saguenay river" under "New Lights" for Quebec, as a "Fixed white light" whose "illuminating apparatus is dioptric of the 6th order."[^4] LighthouseFriends says the lens first showed a fixed white light, under the first keeper.[^3]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends names one keeper, G. Sergerie, who served from 1910 to 1917. It calls him the station's first and only keeper.[^3] The sources give nothing more about him at this light.

## After the keepers

In 1917 Aga equipment was installed, LighthouseFriends says. It changed the light to a white flash every three and a half seconds and allowed the light to be automated.[^3] The Coast Guard's current list gives the light one flash of one second, then five seconds of darkness, a six-second period, a focal height of 17.0 m above the water and a nominal range of 5 nautical miles, lit at night only and all year.[^5] The register gives a range of 8 nautical miles and a height of 23 m (76 ft) above the water, so the two sources disagree on both.[^1][^5]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the lighthouse and services it by helicopter, using a landing pad beside the tower.[^3][^2] LighthouseFriends says the grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^3] The trail to the lighthouse starts at La Pourvoirie du Cap au Leste, a private outfitter's lodge. Ask at the lodge before setting out.[^3]
