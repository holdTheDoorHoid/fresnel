The Souris East Lighthouse is a white wooden tower on Knight Point, above red sandstone cliffs at the harbour of Souris, on the eastern end of Prince Edward Island. It has marked the approach to the town's port since 1880, and it still stands beside the harbour used by the Magdalen Islands ferry.[^1][^2]

## The place

The Souris River runs into Northumberland Strait on the island's eastern shore. Lighthouse Friends gives the town's story as Mi'kmaq first, then Acadians, who were expelled by the British in 1758, with the name coming from a plague of mice (*souris*) said to have struck the settlers in 1724.[^1] John Knight, who settled at East Souris in the 1800s and bought a wharf, built a breakwater and ran a shipbuilding business, is credited there with making Souris a port.[^1] Today the harbour is home to many local vessels and to the ferry service to Quebec's Magdalen Islands.[^2]

## Building the light

The Department invited tenders for a lighthouse on Knight's Point in 1879 and let the contract for $630 to Peter Alyward, as Lighthouse Friends spells him.[^1] The Department of Marine and Fisheries designed it in 1880, and the provincial heritage record spells the builder's name Peter Aylward.[^2] The square pyramidal tower was finished that year and lit in November, with Angus McDonald as first keeper. Its fixed white light showed from 26 m (85 ft) above high water.[^1] In October 1881 the light was altered to show a streak of red over the anchorage sheltered by the breakwater, and a keeper's dwelling was built against the tower that year for $732.[^1]

The breakwater itself got a light in 1889: a fixed red lantern on a 12.5 m (41 ft) mast. A south-east gale on 21 August 1893 broke a 46 m (150 ft) section out of the breakwater and carried the mast and its shed away, and a south-west gale in autumn 1895 took the light again.[^1] In its report for 1903 the Department recorded the breakwater light being strengthened with a cut-glass lens of the 7th order in place of a pressed-glass one.[^3] In 1907 an open steel skeleton tower 7.6 m (25 ft) high, built at the Department's Sorel shipyard, replaced the mast.[^1][^4] The Department's report put the tower's cost at $650 and the erection at $599.98.[^5] Lighthouse Friends dates a fourth-order double-flashing Fresnel lens in the main tower to the same year, giving two flashes every five seconds; the Department's report for 1907-08 records only that such a light had reached Charlottetown for Souris East and would be erected immediately.[^1][^6]

## The keepers

Angus McDonald held the light from 1880 to 1905, followed by J. D. Lavie, and Lighthouse Friends lists a run of keepers after that.[^1] Frank McIntosh (listed in the keeper roster as Michael Francis McIntosh) took over in 1920 and looked after both the point light and the breakwater beacon; his son Andrew helped when his health failed and was appointed in 1935.[^1] Another McIntosh came later: Francis McIntosh, whose father and grandfather had both kept Souris East, began as assistant under Donald Osborne and became head keeper in 1974.[^1]

Osborne grew up partly on Saint Mary Island, Quebec, where his father kept a light. He took the Souris post in 1956 so his children could attend public school.[^1] The family found the attached dwelling infested with rats; he spread crushed glass around the tower, which helped, though traps and a good cat were still needed.[^1] That dwelling was removed in 1959 and a new house built about 100 yards away.[^1][^2] Until 1974 the keeper on duty had to climb the tower every three hours and fifteen minutes to wind the weights that turned the lens.[^1] Osborne later remembered that his duties had become "practically nil" as stations were electrified, and it hurt him when people said he had it easy.[^1]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

Souris had a lifeboat as well as a light. The Department's report for 1908 notes lifeboats placed at Souris,[^7] and an inspection report found the boat and gear in good condition, "a very good coxswain and crew", though the boathouse roof was too low.[^8] On 15 November 1911 the schooner *North America* went ashore at Souris in a heavy gale and the lifeboat rescued her crew.[^9] On the same date a rocket brigade from Priest Pond, 12 miles (19 km) away, covered the distance in about three hours over very heavy roads to help with a schooner ashore and breaking up; when they arrived the crew had already been taken off by the lifeboat.[^9] The report does not say whether this was the same vessel.

## After the keepers

The Fresnel lens installed in 1907 was replaced by another of the same order in 1961, the year the light was electrified.[^1] The provincial record says the lighthouse "became automated in 1961", when Souris East became one of only four lights in Canada with a 400-watt mercury vapour lamp.[^2] Keepers nonetheless stayed on: Francis McIntosh retired on 18 June 1991 as the last active keeper in Prince Edward Island, and the fog alarm ran until 1999.[^1] The lighthouse passed to the Souris Harbour Authority in 2009.[^1]

## Today

On 19 July 2010 the lighthouse reopened as an interpretive centre and gift shop, telling the story of the light, the port and sea glass.[^1][^2] It was designated a provincial heritage place on 1 November 2011.[^1] Lighthouse Friends says the grounds and tower are open to visitors in season, reached by Breakwater Street from the harbour; check the season and hours with the Harbour Authority before you go.[^1] The light now shows two seconds of white followed by two seconds of dark.[^1]
