West Point Lighthouse stands on the southwest point of Prince Edward Island.[^1] Its square, tapered tower is 20.6 m (67 ft 8 in) from the ground to the vane, the tallest of its kind on the island.[^2][^5] William MacDonald, its first keeper and often called "Lighthouse Willie," kept it for fifty years.[^5][^1][^2] The light was automated in 1963.[^1][^5] Since 1984 the tower has been part of an inn and museum, now run by a community corporation.[^1][^2]

## The place

West Point was a working community.[^5] The Prince Edward Island Lighthouse Society says it had shipyards, farms, fishing, lumbering and a number of lobster factories, and that MacDonald came there as a young man to work in the shipyards.[^5]

Parliament voted $5,000 for a lighthouse on West Point in its 1874 session, and added $2,500 the following year.[^1] The Society's account names the reef at West Point, where several boats still ran aground after the light was built.[^5] None of the sources cited here says what the light was built to mark.

## Building the light

The contract went to Mugridge and Company of Shediac, New Brunswick, for $4,559.59.[^1] HistoricPlaces says the work was the first lighthouse built on Prince Edward Island by the federal government after Confederation, and that construction began in 1875, very likely to federal Department of Marine plans.[^2]

Because the island had no building stone, the foundation rock came by schooner from Wallace, Nova Scotia, and the tower stood on a cribwork of logs.[^1][^2] The tower was square and tapered, which HistoricPlaces calls a departure from the largely octagonal lighthouses before it.[^2] It was attached to a one-and-a-half-storey dwelling.[^1] The bottom floor of the tower was the parlour, and the floor above held two bedrooms.[^1] The total cost came to $8,240.17.[^1]

The light commenced operation on 21 May 1876.[^1] HistoricPlaces names William MacDonald as its first keeper.[^2] Its eight lamps and reflectors made a revolution every three minutes, showing one red flash and then three white flashes every ninety seconds.[^1] The tower was painted white with red bands. In 1915 the red bands were changed to black, which was thought to be more visible.[^1][^2]

## The keepers

The Department's report for the year ended 30 June 1902 says the island's lighthouse division had lights at 39 stations and one fog-horn, under the charge of 45 keepers.[^3] Its statement of names and stations of light-keepers lists "McDonald, Wm" at West Point, with an appointment date of 1 December 1875.[^4] The Society says he was appointed in 1875 and helped build the lighthouse.[^5]

He and his wife, Maria, owned a farm 3.2 km (two miles) from the lighthouse.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the family lived on the farm through the winter and moved into the lighthouse as soon as the ice melted and navigation opened.[^1] The girls helped their mother with the housekeeping, and took over much of it when Maria died in 1905, at the age of 53.[^5]

The Society describes the work in detail. The eight lamps had to be lit early enough to have their full effect by the time twilight ended. In the morning the lamp chimneys were washed, the reflectors polished, and the glass of the lantern cleaned inside and outside. The weights had to be wound up every four hours through the night.[^5] The keepers were supplied with a manual such as the 1879 Rules and Instructions for the Guidance of Lighthouse Keepers, the Society says.[^5]

People loved to drop in for a visit and enjoy the family's hospitality, the Society says. The door was never locked, because sailors might need to come in for shelter from a storm.[^5] The MacDonalds bought an organ when they moved to the lighthouse. Much of the visiting was done in the big kitchen, and before people left they would all go into the parlour, where Maria or one of the children would play "Let the Lower Lights Be Burning."[^5] LighthouseFriends dates a pump organ in the parlour to 1876, and says many evenings ended with everyone gathered around it singing the hymn.[^1]

William Mitchell, the Department's agent for Prince Edward Island, visited in 1878 and found the premises clean and comfortable. LighthouseFriends quotes his report.[^1] In 1888 the island's lightkeepers were also named fishery guardians. A notice in the *PEI Agriculture* of 21 May 1888, as the Society quotes it, said there would be "no extra pay."[^5] A granddaughter's letter, which the Society also quotes, says that when the First World War ended in November 1918, William raised the flag on the lighthouse.[^5]

MacDonald retired in 1925, and an advertisement was run to find a replacement. Benjamin MacIsaac, who had served in England, France and Belgium in the First World War before being wounded, answered it.[^1] The Society gives 9 April 1925 as the day MacIsaac replaced him.[^5]

During the Second World War the light had to be kept burning all year, because it guided pilots flying out of the British Commonwealth Air Training facilities at Mount Pleasant and Summerside.[^1] MacIsaac had a radio-telephone to talk to aircraft, the Society says.[^5] The light was automated on 19 May 1963 according to LighthouseFriends; the Society gives 29 May.[^1][^5] MacIsaac retired after thirty-eight years as keeper, and only two men were official keepers at West Point in the eighty-eight years it was staffed.[^1][^5]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

Despite the light, several boats still ran aground on the reef at West Point.[^5] The Inveresh, a square-rigged ship, was wrecked there in 1879.[^5] The North Star came ashore with a load of coal, and the Roseneath and the Ruth Robinson were also lost.[^5] Crew from the shipwrecks took refuge at the lighthouse, and the keeper looked after them until they could go home.[^5]

In mid-November 1938 the Norwegian ship *Nandi* grounded near the lighthouse, the Society says.[^5] Keeper Benjamin MacIsaac's car got stuck, and he walked 4.8 km (three miles) through a blinding snowstorm, nearing the lighthouse about 3:00 pm.[^5] He lit the light, but could not see a thing. It was not until about 11:00 pm that the storm cleared enough for him to see the ship's lights, stranded on the beach a mile or two north of the lighthouse.[^5] The crew had thought they were near the shores of New Brunswick, and had crossed the West Point reef on an extremely high tide before swinging in towards the shore.[^5] Neither the ship nor the crew was injured, and the ship stayed aground until the following spring, when it was refloated.[^5]

A fierce winter storm in the mid-forties destroyed the wharf, the Society says, but the lighthouse escaped damage.[^5]

## After the keepers

The keeper's dwelling was torn down soon after automation, and the barn and oil house went too.[^1] For several years a grandson of William MacDonald, Frank MacDonald, was paid to act as caretaker.[^1]

In 1983 a volunteer group, the West Point Development Corporation, leased the light from the Canadian Coast Guard.[^5][^1] The keepers' dwelling was rebuilt in 1983 and 1984.[^2] LighthouseFriends says it was reconstructed from the original cloth blueprints.[^1] On 1 July 1984 the place opened as Canada's only Inn in a Lighthouse.[^1]

A restaurant and seven more guest rooms were added in 1987. In 1988 the second floor of the tower, which had been a museum, was converted into a bedroom.[^1]

Ownership changed on 21 May 2002. The Society calls it the first lighthouse on Prince Edward Island to pass from the federal government to a community group.[^5][^1] In November 2009 it was announced that about $1.5 million would relocate the restaurant and craft shop to the Harbourside.[^1] A grand opening celebration for the renovated lighthouse was held on 21 August 2010.[^1]

The province recognised the lighthouse as a heritage place under its Heritage Places Protection Act on 1 November 2012.[^1]

## Today

The West Point Development Corporation owns the light and runs the inn and museum.[^1] LighthouseFriends' visiting notes say the grounds and tower are open, that the museum, restaurant and inn open from late May to early October, and that a guest can spend the night in the tower if booking far enough ahead.[^1] Those notes may be out of date, so check hours with the operator before going.
