Cap-Alright Lighthouse is a small white tower with a red lantern, standing on a red sandstone cliff at the east end of Île du Havre aux Maisons in Les Îles-de-la-Madeleine, Quebec.[^4] It was built in 1928 and is the smallest light in the archipelago.[^1][^4]

## The place

The tower stands on a rocky point that juts into the Gulf of St. Lawrence and looks out at Entry Island.[^1] The land around it is steep, with high hills and sandstone cliffs.[^2] The fishing port of Pointe-Basse lies nearby.[^3]

The heritage record says the waters around the cape are considered particularly dangerous.[^1] It lists at least ten wrecks in those waters, but gives no dates, names or rescues for them.[^1]

## Building the light

According to LighthouseFriends, Grégoire Lafrance, a lobster fisherman who owned property at the cape, asked for a light there to help local fishermen find their way home.[^4] The light was built in 1928.[^1] LighthouseFriends calls it the last light set up in Les Îles-de-la-Madeleine,[^4] and the heritage record says it is the last new light built in the archipelago, leaving out rebuilt towers.[^1]

The tower follows a design that Marine Department engineers had drawn up in the last quarter of the nineteenth century.[^1] The heritage record says that type of small, square wooden tower was often used in Canada, and it names similar towers at Bonaventure (1902) and on Deep River island in the Ottawa River (1873).[^1][^2]

The heritage record gives the tower's height as 8.3 m (27 ft).[^1] LighthouseFriends and Rowlett give 8 m (27 ft).[^4][^5] Its focal plane is 24 m (79 ft).[^4] LighthouseFriends says it shows a white flash every five seconds, visible for twenty nautical miles.[^4]

## The keepers

The Corporation pour la Sauvegarde du Patrimoine des Îles lists the keepers as follows: Grégoire Lafrance and his son Alphonse together from 1928 to 1935; Alphée Arseneau from 1935 to 1940; Grégoire Lafrance again from 1940 to 1946; Albert Arseneau from 1946 to 1970; and Albin Leblanc, with no dates given.[^3]

Two sources name a Grégoire Lafrance. LighthouseFriends describes the fisherman who asked for the light, and the Corporation's list gives a keeper of the same name. Neither source says they are the same man, so this story does not link them.[^3][^4]

No source here describes the keepers' daily work at Cap-Alright.

## After the keepers

The sources do not agree on when the light went out. The Corporation says it went out in 2011,[^3] and Rowlett gives 2015 as the year it became inactive.[^5]

Ownership changed too. LighthouseFriends and Rowlett both name the Canadian Coast Guard as owner.[^4][^5] The Corporation says the lighthouse became private property in 2015, and that in 2025 it was sold to a non-profit organisation, the Société de conservation des Îles-de-la-Madeleine.[^3] The municipal heritage citation for the tower is dated 17 January 2006,[^1] and the Government of Canada gave it heritage site status in 2015.[^3]

The light appeared on a Canadian postage stamp in 2003.[^4]

## Today

The Corporation's page says the lighthouse passed to the non-profit in 2025.[^3] LighthouseFriends and Rowlett both describe the grounds as open and the tower as closed.[^4][^5] No source here describes the site after the 2025 sale.

To reach it, LighthouseFriends advises leaving the ferry landing at Cap-aux-Meules and turning right onto Chemin Principal (QC-199).[^4] After 5 km, turn right onto Chemin de la Pointe Basse and follow it for 3.2 km.[^4]
