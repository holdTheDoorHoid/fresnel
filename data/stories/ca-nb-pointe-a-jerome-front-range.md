Two range lights on Pointe à Jérôme, on the north side of Bouctouche Harbour in Kent County, New Brunswick, were first lit in 1883 to guide ships into the harbour.[^1] The lights were set in line so that a vessel keeping them in line would stay in the deepest channel.[^1] The front tower of 1916 still stands, on residential land.[^2][^3] Its rear partner was taken down in 2022.[^4][^5] The sources do not agree on whether the front light still shows.[^5][^2]

## The place

Pointe à Jérôme is one of three points of land on Bouctouche Bay, and it was among the first places settled in the area.[^1] The harbour lies between Dixon Point and a long sand dune that shelters most of it.[^1] Range lights went up on Dixon Point in 1881 to guide vessels to the harbour entrance.[^1] Two years later a second pair was set on Pointe à Jérôme, which was also known as Church Point or Indian Point.[^1]

The Department of Marine's keeper lists called the station "Indian Point, Buctouche".[^6] A different light, on the southern end of the Buctouche sand bar, was put into operation on 10 October 1902.[^7]

The original lighthouse dates from 1883, the same year the Moncton & Buctouche Railway was founded.[^8] The register suggests the railway may have prompted better navigation aids at a harbour it expected to see more traffic.[^8]

## Building the light

LighthouseFriends gives the first lights, from the Department's 1883 annual report, as fixed white lights with reflectors (catoptric), shown from small square wooden towers 7 m (23 ft) high.[^1] The front light could be seen 9 miles along the range line, and the back light 12 miles.[^1]

On 26 September 1903 the Department changed the colour of the front light from red to white.[^9] The fixed white front light was then visible for 10 miles from every approach by water.[^9] The mast carrying the back light was raised 3 m (10 ft).[^9]

The tower that stands today is a 6.3 m (20.6 ft) square, tapered wooden tower, painted white with a red daymark. The register dates it to 1916.[^2][^3] The Lighthouse Directory gives 4 m (14 ft) for the same tower, against the register's 6.3 m, so the sources disagree on its height.[^5][^2]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends names Dosithee O. Maillet as the first keeper, paid $150 a year, and dates his service from 1883 to 1921.[^1] The Department's keeper list for the year ended 30 June 1900 gives a Maillet at Indian Point, Buctouche, with an appointment date of 7 July 1888.[^10] The list for 1901 spells the name D. O. Maillet.[^6] The sources do not agree on when he started.

After Maillet, LighthouseFriends lists J. D. Mailett (1921 to 1922), Henry Argartine Cormier (1922 to 1955) and Gerald Maillet (1955 to 1963).[^1] Electrification in 1963 ended the need for an official keeper, and Gerald Maillet became caretaker.[^1]

## After the keepers

LighthouseFriends says the range was discontinued in 2014, after the Canadian Coast Guard gave notice that it meant to put the lights out and allowed mariners three months to comment.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory lists the front light as inactive from that year.[^5]

The two sources describe the rear tower differently. LighthouseFriends gives a skeletal tower of 13.6 m (44.6 ft) with an enclosed upper section.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory gives a 13 m (44 ft) square pyramidal wooden tower with a gallery and lantern.[^5] LighthouseFriends says the rear tower was demolished in 2022; the Lighthouse Directory says the Coast Guard removed it by helicopter that year.[^4][^5]

The front tower was recognized as a heritage lighthouse under the Heritage Lighthouse Protection Act on 21 March 2022. The Government of Canada announced the designation on 2 June 2022.[^2][^3]

## Today

The register describes the front tower as surrounded entirely by residential property, so the land around it is private and visitors should not assume they may go onto it.[^2] LighthouseFriends reports that the Coast Guard owns the lighthouse and that its grounds are open while the tower is closed.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory also gives the site as open and the tower as closed.[^5]

The 2022 register text says the light "continues to serve as a marker for mariners navigating the channel into Bouctouche Harbour".[^2] The Parks Canada release of 2 June 2022 repeats that line.[^3] It does not match the 2014 discontinuance, so whether the light still shows remains disputed.
