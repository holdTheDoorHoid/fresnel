Whitlocks Mill Light stands on the south bank of the St. Croix River, a few miles below Calais, Maine.[^1][^3] Its brick tower and keeper's house were built in 1909, and the National Register nomination calls it "the last station built in Maine."[^1][^2] The river bend it guards had been marked since 1892 by a red lantern, first hung from a tree and later mounted on a post.[^2]

## The place

The St. Croix was a working river.[^1] The nomination says it "was a heavily trafficked waterway on which vessels of all types carried a variety of cargoes," and that "Beginning in the 1820s Calais" developed into an important lumber-shipping terminal.[^1]

The hazard was a turn called the Narrows, a few hundred yards above the mill.[^4] The Light-House Board's report for the 1893–1894 volume says "A light was needed at this place to enable the steamers, plying between Eastport and Calais, and especially towboats, to make the difficult turn at the Narrows." The Canadian government had two lights on its own bank, and "another light was needed on the right or American bank to make the navigation safe at this difficult turn."[^4]

LighthouseFriends says the question went back to 1853, when the local customs collector asked whether the river needed "any lighthouse, buoy, beacon, or monument."[^2] An earlier light was built on Dochet Island. The nomination dates it to 1856 and says it was destroyed.[^1] LighthouseFriends gives 1857 for a light on St. Croix Island. The sources do not say whether the two names belong to one island or two lights.[^1][^2]

## Building the light

The 1892 aid was a red lantern.[^2] LighthouseFriends dates it to 15 July 1892 and says it was "intended only as a temporary aid."[^2] A notice to mariners from the Lighthouse Board, printed in the Augusta *Daily Kennebec Journal* on 16 September 1892, describes a fixed red beacon that had "recently been established on the south side of the St. Croix river, about 2 1-2 miles below Calais."[^7] The Board asked for money to buy land at the site: "each year from 1894 to 1899, the Lighthouse Board requested $250 to purchase land at the site for a proper light."[^2] The Board's 1894 appropriation list carries a "Whitlocks Mill light-station, Maine 250.00" line.[^4] LighthouseFriends says a 100-square-foot site was bought in 1901.[^2]

LighthouseFriends says Congress appropriated money in 1907 and 1908 "that were used, starting in 1909, to construct a keeper's dwelling and brick tower at the station."[^2] The nomination says "The entire complex was built in 1909."[^1] The tower is 25 feet (7.6 m) tall and stands on a granite base.[^2] The archive records the fog signal building as built in 1910.[^3]

The archive gives the lens as a fourth-order Fresnel installed in 1906.[^3] LighthouseFriends describes a fourth-order Fresnel lens "originally used in the new tower to exhibit a fixed red light until the light's characteristic was changed to green in 1931."[^2] The sources do not say whether the lens moved from an earlier lantern. The tower was painted red at first and received its white daymark in 1914.[^2]

The nomination names the designer as the Army Corps of Engineers: "U. S. Army Corp .of Engineers , Designer."[^1] LighthouseFriends dates electrification to about 1931, "around the time the station was electrified."[^2] It also says the light took its name from Colin C. Whitlock: "As Whitlock owned a lathe mill at nearby Bog Brook," the name followed.[^2]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends says Colin C. Whitlock "tended" the 1892 lantern, and the archive lists him for that year.[^2][^3]

Aaron V. Tucker was in charge of the lantern from 1892 and became the first keeper of the new tower in 1909.[^2][^3] LighthouseFriends says he was dismissed in 1912 "for lack of attention to duty." The local corpus does not hold the record of that dismissal, so the charge is unverified.[^2]

Frank N. Jellison, who had come "up the river from St. Croix Lighthouse," was keeper from 1912 to 1920. In 1912 he was commended for caring for the body of the mate of the dredge Bay State, killed in a collision on 16 June.[^2][^3] His brother Joseph W. Jellison followed him in 1920, having been, LighthouseFriends says, "head keeper at Tenants Harbor for over twenty years."[^2][^3]

Frank B. Ingalls, who had replaced Frank Jellison at St. Croix Lighthouse, came to Whitlocks Mill in 1923. LighthouseFriends says he "was in charge of the lighthouse for over two decades."[^2][^3] The Lighthouse Service Bulletin index for 1924–1929 lists "Ingalls, Frank B., keeper Whitelocks Mills, efficiency flag awarded" and gives the page as 29.[^6]

The archive then lists Alvah Robinson (1946–1948), Edgar M. Wallace (1948–1949), Jasper L. Cheney (1949–1955), Charles M. Montanese (1959–1961), Howard Bryant Clement (1961–1964), Russell W. Reilly (1964–1967) and Richard G. Caravan (1967).[^3] LighthouseFriends calls Reilly "One of the last Coast Guard keepers."[^2]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

LighthouseFriends describes the river at the bend as "extremely difficult and dangerous, especially during the spring and fall freshets."[^2]

The one accident the cited sources tie to the station is a collision on 16 June 1912, in which the mate of the dredge Bay State was killed. LighthouseFriends says keeper Jellison "was commended in 1912 for caring for the body of the mate of the dredge."[^2] No wreck at this station appears in the sources cited.

The need persisted in the Board's reports.[^5] In the year ended 30 June 1897 it says "The following recommendation made in the Board's last three annual reports is renewed."[^5]

## After the keepers

The light was automated in 1969, the year its Fresnel lens went to the Maine Lighthouse Museum in Rockland.[^3][^2] The station was deeded in 1977 to the Washington County Vocational Technical Institute, and it was added to the National Register on 21 January 1988.[^2]

In 1997 the St. Croix Historical Society was "awarded ownership of Whitlocks Mill Lighthouse" under the Maine Lights Program.[^2] The archive's current optic is "VLB-44 (LED), SOLAR POWERED (INSTALLED 2009)."[^3] The keeper's dwelling sold in 2004.[^2] In 2014 the society "mounted a fog bell on the bell tower at Whitlocks Mill," which LighthouseFriends identifies as the station's original bell.[^2]

## Today

The tower is owned by the St. Croix Historical Society, and the Coast Guard still maintains its flashing green light.[^3][^2] The keeper's house and outbuildings "are privately owned and closed to the public."[^2] The archive records the station as "Open to Public: No."[^3]
