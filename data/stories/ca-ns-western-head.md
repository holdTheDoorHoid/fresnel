Western Head Lighthouse is a white octagonal concrete tower with a red top, on the western side of the entrance to Liverpool Bay in Queens County, Nova Scotia.[^4][^1][^2] The point had a fog signal for decades before the tower was built in 1962.[^1][^3] NSLPS says the station was originally established as a foghorn station only.[^2]

## Building the light

The sources do not agree on when the fog signal began. Lighthouse Friends says a fog alarm building was put up under contract in 1924, along with a dwelling and an oil store, and that the fog alarm "was placed in operation on October 1 of that year".[^1] NSLPS dates the fog alarm building to 1928.[^2] The Region of Queens dates the fog station to 1930, describing it as "in existence since 1930".[^3]

The signal was a diaphone foghorn, and NSLPS lists it as in operation in 1930.[^2][^1] Lighthouse Friends says that in 1936 the ell at the back of the fog alarm building was removed, a radio room was built in its place, and it controlled a radiobeacon established at the point that year.[^1]

In 1961 the keeper's dwelling at Coffin Island had fallen into serious disrepair.[^3] The Region of Queens says the decision was made to destaff Coffin Island and "add a tower to the existing Western Head fog alarm station".[^3] The tower was built in 1962.[^1][^2][^3]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends lists the keepers from the start of the fog station as Avery Covey (1924 to 1927), H. Covey (1927 to 1928), F.J. Curry (1928 to 1930) and Carl M. Hatt (1930 to 1959).[^1] NSLPS gives a different first operator, Avery Curry, and says Carl Hatt took the station over in 1930 and ran it until 1959. It also says Hatt's son Doug was his assistant from 1947 to 1959.[^2] Lighthouse Friends says Covey had come from Flint Island Lighthouse.[^1]

After the tower went up, the keepers were W.R.R. (Roy) Murphy, 1962 to 1978, and Lawrence Wentzel, from 1962 into the 1970s.[^1][^2] Lighthouse Friends names Lemuel Moreau as the last keeper, up to 1988, without giving the year he started.[^1] The Region of Queens says that when the station had three keepers, the keeper on duty had to call the weather in to Bedford every hour, and that in high winds the keepers sometimes had to use the back door, because the front one could not be opened.[^3]

## After the keepers

The Region of Queens says Western Head was destaffed in 1988, and NSLPS says it was automated by that year.[^3][^2] Lighthouse Friends describes the change: by 1988 the light was monitored from elsewhere, and a keeper who wanted to go into the tower had to call the keepers at Cape Forchu to switch off the intrusion alarm.[^1] Lighthouse Friends says all that remained of the old foghorn building was a concrete pad, and that today the fog detector, the backup engines and the radios are housed in the tower.[^1]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the site.[^1] Lighthouse Friends says the grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^1] The Coast Guard list gives a single white flash every 15 seconds, from a 16.8 m (55 ft) focal height, visible 15 nautical miles, and a tower 13.8 m (45 ft) tall.[^4] Lighthouse Friends gives the tower as 19.5 m tall,[^1] and NSLPS gives 15.2 m (50 ft),[^2] so the heights in the sources disagree. This account uses the Coast Guard list for the light's figures.[^4] The Region of Queens warns that the foghorn is very loud on foggy days, and that visitors may want hearing protection as they approach.[^3]
