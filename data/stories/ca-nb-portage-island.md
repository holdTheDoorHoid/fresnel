Portage Island light is a wooden range light first lit in 1869 at the entrance to Miramichi Bay, New Brunswick. Since 1986 its tower has stood on a boardwalk in Shippagan, beside the New Brunswick Aquarium and Marine Centre, rather than on the island it was built to mark.[^1][^4]

## The place

Two barrier islands border the main entrance to Inner Miramichi Bay: Portage Island to the north and Fox Island to the south.[^1] According to John Vickers, writing in a Northumberland Free Press Substack post, fishermen used the island in season in the early 1800s to dry cod and mend their nets. Before the light, he writes, the island was unlit and unmarked.[^6]

## Building the light

Work on a square lighthouse began at the southern end of Portage Island in September 1869, and the light was first exhibited on 6 October that year.[^1] The lantern room was built from the old lantern room of the Point Escuminac light, and four lamps with silvered reflectors also came from there.[^1] The Department's 1909 report still calls the old light a "catoptric" light.[^3]

Lighthouse Digest gives 1908 as the date the present tower was built.[^4] Lighthouse Friends and Tourism New Brunswick date the tower to 1869, and the Department's 1909 report describes a mast light and a new lantern rather than a new tower, so the sources do not agree.[^1][^5][^3]

The original buildings cost $604.40, and an extra $300 was asked for a small house for the keeper. No title to the land had been secured before construction began.[^1]

The Department's report for the twelve months ending 31 March 1909 describes a mast light on the south end of the island, as the front light of a range with the old light. It was a light on a pole 8.2 m (27 ft) high.[^3] Lighthouse Friends dates the mast to 1908.[^1] The same report says the old fixed white catoptric light was replaced by a new 4th-order dioptric light in a new lantern. The new light was visible for 7 seconds, with a 2-second eclipse.[^3]

## The keepers

George Davidson was the first keeper. He had no dwelling, only a small shed, which Lighthouse Friends says the Department itself admitted was "not sufficient" in cold weather.[^1] A house for him was finished in May 1872, and fire destroyed it in February 1875.[^1]

Davidson was dismissed in August 1875 and replaced by Hugh Murray. Lighthouse Friends quotes an inspector who visited that year as finding the light "not so well kept as formerly."[^1] On a visit in 1876, an inspector found that the station's tidiness differed very much from similar stations, and put this down to "the want of female assistance."[^1] The next year's report, as Lighthouse Friends quotes it, said the keeper had "two female assistants, and everything looks neat and clean." Those assistants are not named.[^1]

Murray retired in 1881. Duncan Robertson was appointed in April that year and died within weeks, and James Stymest followed him. Keepers were paid $200 a year at that time.[^1]

Peter Morrison, Jr., kept the light from 1892 to 1924, according to Lighthouse Friends.[^1] The Department's statement of light-keepers lists "Morrison, Peter, jr" at Portage Island from 1 July 1892.[^2] After him Lighthouse Friends names J.W. Gratten (1925 to 1935), Harold Gratten (1935), Daniel Savoy (1936 to at least 1953), Thomas LeBreton, Joseph Samuel Savoy (1955) and Joseph Venance Savoie (1955 to at least 1961).[^1] Tourism New Brunswick says keepers served until 1960.[^5]

## After the keepers

In the 1980s a modern tower replaced the old lighthouse, according to Lighthouse Friends.[^1] Lighthouse Digest says the two towers of the range were replaced by "skeleton structures," and that the rear light was moved to Shippagan in 1986.[^4] Lighthouse Friends says a local fisherman tried to buy the old tower and move it to Neguac, but the New Brunswick Aquarium and Marine Centre won the bidding that year.[^1]

Donald Boudreau, of the Village Historique Acadien, led the work of taking the tower down in three pieces. It was carried by boat to Burnt Church and rebuilt on a wharf near the aquarium.[^1] Tourism New Brunswick describes the tower as dismounted piece by piece and moved by boat and road to Shippagan.[^5] Lighthouse Digest gives c. 1986 as the year the light was deactivated.[^4]

## Today

The tower stands on a public boardwalk near the aquarium in Shippagan, and belongs to the New Brunswick Department of Tourism and Parks.[^1]

The sources disagree on whether it is open. Lighthouse Friends says the grounds are open and the tower closed, and that the lantern room, once a visitor centre, is now empty.[^1] Lighthouse Digest lists it as a tourist attraction and information centre, open to the public.[^4] Do not go into the tower unless it is confirmed to be open.
