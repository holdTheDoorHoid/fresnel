Point Roberts Light is a navigation light on a skeletal tower at the southwestern tip of Point Roberts, Washington, an American exclave.[^3][^4] The federal government set the tip aside for a light station in 1908.[^3][^4] Sources disagree about what was built there, and when.[^1][^3][^4]

## The place

Point Roberts is a 4.9-square-mile "unincorporated American exclave" in the southern part of Canada's Tsawwassen Peninsula.[^3] The US government vacated its unused military reserve there in 1892.[^3] Homesteading on the point was not fully opened until 1908. Its southwestern corner was set aside for a lighthouse station.[^3]

## Building the light

In 1908 the federal government bought 21 acres at the end of the peninsula for a light station.[^4] The US Lighthouse Society's record gives 1908 as the year the tower was established.[^1] It describes a pyramidal, wood-frame tower about 30 feet (9.1 m) from its base to the lantern, on a concrete foundation, with a lens lantern installed that year.[^1] The record lists no keepers' quarters.[^1]

The Point Roberts Historical Society dates a wood-framed lighthouse on the point to 1910, and says it stood for half a century.[^5] Two other sources say no lighthouse was ever built there.[^3][^4] The Lighthouse Service's 1918 bulletin, though, lists "Point Roberts, Puget Sound, Wash., fixed to flashing, oil to acetylene" under "Important changes in aids to navigation" that year, so a light was operating by then.[^2]

## After the keepers

According to the Historical Society, the 1962 Columbus Day Storm badly damaged the wood-framed lighthouse, and the navigation light seen today replaced it.[^5]

Whatcom County's Lighthouse Marine Park was dedicated on the 21 acres in 1973.[^3][^4][^5] The two sources give different reasons for the name. HistoryLink says the park takes its name from a lighthouse that was never built.[^3] The Historical Society says it is named for the wood-framed lighthouse, which it says was damaged in 1962 and replaced.[^5] In 2000 the Point Roberts Lighthouse Society was formed to build a new lighthouse on the point.[^4] In December 2018 it announced that it would abandon the project and disband.[^4]

## Today

The Lighthouse Directory describes the present light as a 26-foot (8 m) square skeletal tower with a red-and-white checkerboard daymark, two white flashes every 15 seconds, and a focal plane of 30 feet (9 m). It says the site is open and the tower closed.[^4]

The sources disagree about whether the light is active.[^1][^4][^6] The Lighthouse Directory lists it as active.[^4] The US Lighthouse Society's record says it is not an active aid to navigation.[^1] A tourism site calls it decommissioned and says the lighthouse is not open to the public.[^6]

Point Roberts is not physically connected to the United States. HistoryLink says reaching it takes either a 14-mile boat trip from the Blaine border crossing or a drive of nearly twice that distance through Canada.[^3] The tower is closed to visitors, and it should not be climbed.[^4]
