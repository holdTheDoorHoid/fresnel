Richibucto Head Lighthouse, also called the Cap-Lumière light, stands on a headland in the village of Cap-Lumière on the Northumberland Strait, New Brunswick. It is a wood tower, first lit on 2 May 1865, and it still shows a white flash.[^1][^4]

## The place

In the early 1800s Richibucto was, in the words of the Lighthouse Friends history, the third largest shipping port in New Brunswick, and one shipbuilding family, the Jardines, launched more than a hundred vessels there before business declined in the 1880s.[^1] The river mouth is the hazard here. Two sandbars, each several miles long, flank the entrance to the Richibucto River, and the bar between them is one the Lighthouse Friends history calls extremely dangerous.[^1]

In 1857 the House of Assembly of New Brunswick authorised a lighthouse on Richibucto Head at a cost of £250. Work did not begin, and in 1859 the Lieutenant Governor was asked whether a light should be established there.[^1]

In May 1861 John Wilkinson was sent to choose a site. He judged a sandy point about four miles south-east of the harbour entrance the most favourable position, since the land was of little use to anyone and could be bought cheaply, but its low ground would have needed a tall tower. The light was built instead on Richibucto Head, a short distance south of that point, where a shorter tower would do and where, with a settlement nearby, it could be kept up for less.[^1]

## Building the light

Amos Keith contracted for $592 to build the tower by 1 July 1863. The specification called for a quadrangular wooden tower on a masonry foundation 4.9 m (16 ft) square at the base, with the focal plane 12.8 m (42 ft) above the ground and about 21 m (70 ft) above high water.[^1]

The lantern and lighting apparatus were not ready for a British ship bound for Richibucto in the spring of 1864, so they went to Saint John instead. The fourth-order lens followed to Richibucto and was installed in time for the inaugural lighting on 2 May 1865.[^1] The lighthouse and land cost $1,346.85, and the lantern and lens cost $1,606.25.[^1]

Lighthouse Friends records two early changes. In 1869 the tower leaked in rainstorms, so it was re-shingled, repainted and fitted with a new lantern deck. In 1870 a round wick lamp replaced the flat wick lamp; the flat wick had burned only about a pint of oil a night, so no oil had to be sent to the station for the four years before.[^1]

## The keepers

Fabian Richard was the first keeper. In 1875 his family was counted at eight, including himself and his wife; a married son and the son's wife also lived with them.[^1] The Lighthouse Friends roster spells him Fabien (Robicheau) Richard and dates his service from 1864, a year before the light was first lit, so the start date is the roster's and not the text's.[^1]

Peter F. Richard became the second keeper in 1895 and served until 1934.[^1] The Department's list of lightkeepers for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1906 places a "Richard, Peter F" at Richibuctou Head.[^5] The Department's statement of light stations, printed with its report in 1913, still lists Richibucto head among the New Brunswick lights.[^6]

After him came Maxime P. Richard (1934 to 1935), Adolph J. LeBlanc (1935 to 1943), Maxime P. Richard again (1944 to 1947) and Fred Joseph Blanchard (1947 to 1965). Blanchard was the final keeper.[^1]

## After the keepers

The light was electrified in 1965, the year Blanchard's service ended.[^1] The keeper's house, which had been attached to the tower, was removed in the 1960s.[^4] The range lights that once marked the channel over the bars are gone; Lighthouse Friends says none of them survive, but the Head light remains active.[^1] The Chief Engineer's section of the Department's report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1901 describes south-beach range lights re-arranged to lead through the channels. Two new pole lights went up on 1 August 1901, two more range lights on 8 October, and the back light of the old harbour range was discontinued. The section does not mention the Head light.[^3]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the lighthouse.[^1][^4] Its List of Lights calls the station Richibucto Cape and gives a white square tower with a red upper portion, 10.5 m high above ground, showing a white flash every five seconds, with a focal height of 18.1 m and a nominal range of 11 nautical miles. The light operates all year.[^2] Rowlett's directory describes a 9 m (30 ft) square pyramidal wood tower, painted white with red trim, with its focal plane at 18 m (59 ft).[^4]

The grounds are open and the tower is closed to visitors.[^1][^4] The lighthouse is on Cap Lumière Road, off Route 505, in Cap Lumière.[^4]
