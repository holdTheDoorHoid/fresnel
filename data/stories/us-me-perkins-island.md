Perkins Island Light stands on a bold rock ledge on the west side of Perkins Island, in the Kennebec River off Georgetown, Maine.[^2][^3] Its short wooden tower went into service in 1898, one of the river lights the Lighthouse Board had asked Congress to pay for six years earlier.[^2][^4] The light was automated in 1959.[^2] It remains an active aid to navigation.[^3][^13]

## The place

The Lighthouse Board's annual report for the fiscal year ended June 30, 1892 made its case for lights on the lower Kennebec with the traffic on the river. The government's fiscal year ended on 30 June. The report counted 3,137 vessel arrivals in the river during the year, not counting the steamers that plied it daily.[^4]

Above Bath the buoys were taken up about 20 November, and the river was likely to freeze at any time after that.[^5]

The Board also described the fog.[^5] Sea fog extended as far up the river as Bath, and the river had a fog of its own, "dense and at times low down." On dark nights, the Board wrote, it was "sometimes impossible to tell where the water ends."[^5] The Light-House Establishment, the Board said, maintained no lights or fog signals on the river. For many years the Kennebec Steamboat Company and the towboat companies had hung lanterns on buoys at turning points.[^5]

In the same report the Board recommended a fixed red light in a lens lantern on the southwest point of Perkins Island, with a white sector to the north and a fog bell struck by machinery.[^4] Its recommendation grouped Perkins Island with the Kennebec stations at Squirrel Point, Doubling Point and Ames Ledge.[^4]

## Building the light

Congressional action was delayed until 1895, the nomination says.[^2] An appropriation of $17,000 for the Kennebec lights was made by an act approved on 2 March 1895.[^6]

The Board's annual report for the fiscal year ended June 30, 1897 records that title to Perkins Island had been acquired, that plans and specifications had been prepared, and that a contract had been made for the building.[^7] The report gives the schedule: "The station is to be completed by February, 1898."[^7]

The National Register nomination says the station was completed and placed in operation in 1898.[^2] New England Lighthouses says the light went into service in February 1898, with a 23-foot (7 m) octagonal wooden tower and a six-room frame keeper's house and barn.[^13]

The nomination describes a tapered octagonal frame tower sheathed in wood shingles, on a stone foundation, and gives its height as "seventeen feet from base to center of the lantern" (5.2 m).[^2] The Archive's record gives 23 feet (7 m) from the base to the centre of the lantern.[^3] LighthouseFriends gives the tower as twenty-three feet tall, with the lantern's focal plane at forty-one feet (12.5 m).[^1] The sources do not reconcile the nomination's 17 feet (5.2 m) with the 23 feet (7 m) of the other two.[^2][^3][^1]

LighthouseFriends says the tower was built to the same design as those at Doubling Point and Squirrel Point.[^1]

A white sector was placed in the lantern on 10 July 1899.[^9] The Board's report for the fiscal year ended June 30, 1899 also records a gallery with railing built around the lantern, a boat slip and a flight of landing steps.[^8]

The Board's report for the fiscal year ended June 30, 1900 says that a June 1900 appropriation was to be applied in part to a boathouse at the station.[^9] LighthouseFriends and New England Lighthouses both date the boathouse to 1901.[^1][^13]

In 1902 the lens lantern gave way to a fifth-order Fresnel lens.[^1][^13] LighthouseFriends gives the date as 15 January 1902.[^1] The same year a pyramidal wooden bell tower, with a 1,000-pound fog bell, went up just south of the light tower.[^1][^2][^13] A brick oil house followed in 1906.[^2]

## The keepers

The USLHS Digital Archive lists eight keeper's stints at Perkins Island from 1898 to 1957, held by seven men, because Eugene W. Osgood served twice.[^3]

Jacob W. Haley is the first name on the archive's list, with the years 1898 to 1927.[^3] LighthouseFriends says he was appointed the first keeper, and that his stay of nearly thirty years far exceeded any later keeper's.[^1] New England Lighthouses gives his service as 1911 to 1927.[^13] The sources agree on the end date and disagree on the start.

The Board's report for the fiscal year ended June 30, 1899 covers the four Kennebec River stations, Perkins Island among them.[^8] It says the appropriations were not enough to build boathouses at them, and that the keepers at all four stations "use boats almost exclusively for communication with the shore."[^8]

Eugene W. Osgood kept the light from 1928 to 1935 and again from 1941 to 1946, with Ruel Walter Powers in between.[^3] LighthouseFriends, citing a 1938 interview, says Ethel Osgood had lived with her husband at various Maine lighthouses for twenty-five years, and that their five children had grown up in lighthouses.[^1]

Powers fell ill in early 1941 and was taken to hospital. LighthouseFriends says he died there.[^1]

Clifton S. Morong and his wife Shirley were substitute keepers for a little over a month in early 1941.[^1][^3] New England Lighthouses says Clifton Morong "was keeper for several years in the 1940s," a longer stint than the Archive's single year.[^13]

According to LighthouseFriends, which quotes Shirley Morong's account, the dwelling's furnace was out when they arrived, and the kitchen stove was the only heat.[^1] On some snowstorm nights Clifton had to start the bell. The bell machinery, LighthouseFriends says, stood in a pyramidal tower on the shore, and a long cable was wound by means of a hand crank.[^1]

Clarence A. Skolfield kept the light from 1946 to 1955.[^3] LighthouseFriends says he had spent nearly ten years on Seguin Island and had chosen to stay a civilian keeper when the Coast Guard took over the lighthouse service in 1939.[^1] The station had no electricity. A gasoline engine ran the washing machine, propane ran the refrigerator and stove, and rainwater was stored in cisterns in the cellar.[^1] Skolfield smoothed the ground, built a picket fence along the cliff edge and started flower and vegetable gardens.[^1] LighthouseFriends, citing a 1955 Lewiston newspaper article, quotes a reporter who called the result a "miniature paradise."[^1] Anthony Fargnoli (1956) and Robert E. Morong (1957) completed the archive's list.[^3]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

The Commissioner of Lighthouses' table of life and property saved for the fiscal year 1916 lists two services by J. W. Haley, keeper of Perkins Island.[^10] The first was to stop a "waterlogged row boat, loaded with lumber and with man on board" from capsizing, and the lumber was saved.[^10] The second "furnished party which had taken refuge on island during heavy thunderstorm with dry clothing and shelter."[^10] LighthouseFriends gives the same two incidents for 1916.[^1]

On 29 August 1922 a launch caught fire with 20 people aboard.[^11] The Lighthouse Service Bulletin says that Haley, the keeper of Perkins Island, towed the party to their destination.[^11] LighthouseFriends says the party had landed on Perkins Island, and that Haley used his personal powerboat to tow the damaged boat and its occupants twenty miles to their destination.[^1]

On 16 June 1931 Osgood rescued a man from drowning at Phippsburg, Maine.[^12] The Secretary of Commerce commended him for the rescue.[^12] The Bulletin says the keeper, "who had gone to the post office for his mail," saw the man struggling in the water and reached him in his own boat.[^12]

Both sources describe a storm rescue in Osgood's time, without a date. New England Lighthouses says a party of nineteen, whose boat grounded near the island during a thunderstorm, was taken in, and that the keeper's wife fed them.[^13] LighthouseFriends, citing the 1938 interview, says Osgood set out in the station boat, located the party and brought them back to the lighthouse, where Ethel dried them out and fed them.[^1]

## After the keepers

LighthouseFriends says coastguardsmen were assigned to Perkins Island after Skolfield left in 1955, until the light was automated in 1959.[^1][^2] Yet LighthouseFriends and the Archive both name Anthony Fargnoli (1956) and Robert Morong (1957) as keepers in those years, so the sources do not agree on who tended the light before 1959.[^1][^3]

In the 1960s the buildings other than the tower went to the State of Maine.[^1][^2] The tower stayed in the Coast Guard's care.[^2]

LighthouseFriends says the fog bell tower and keeper's dwelling gradually fell into disrepair.[^1] In 2000 part of the roof over the dwelling's front porch had collapsed, and the chimney "appeared ready to topple over."[^1] The fog bell tower was restored that year, with grants from the Maine Department of Conservation and a New Century Program Preservation Grant.[^1][^13] In May 2000 the Coast Guard licensed the tower to the American Lighthouse Foundation.[^13]

The Friends of Perkins Island Lighthouse, a chapter of the foundation, was formed in 2003.[^1] The Friends group shingled the most damaged portion of the roof and painted the dwelling in 2005 and 2006.[^1] A $45,000 gift from local residents paid for a crew to spend eight weeks restoring and painting the dwelling's exterior in the fall of 2014.[^1]

## Today

LighthouseFriends says the Coast Guard owns the tower and leases it to the American Lighthouse Foundation, and that the State of Maine owns the dwelling.[^1] The Archive's record names the American Lighthouse Foundation as owner or manager.[^3]

LighthouseFriends lists the grounds as open, and the dwelling and tower as closed.[^1] The USLHS record says the station is not open to the public.[^3]

LighthouseFriends gives Parker Head Road as the route to a distant view of the light from land. The road is now marked private, and LighthouseFriends advises viewing the lighthouse from the water.[^1]

Public cruises from Bath and from Boothbay Harbor pass the lighthouse.[^1][^13]

The fog bell used at Perkins Island is on display at a school in Georgetown.[^1] LighthouseFriends says Georgetown Central School, and New England Lighthouses says Georgetown High School.[^1][^13]
