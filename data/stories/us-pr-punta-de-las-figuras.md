Punta de las Figuras Light, or Faro de Punta de las Figuras, stands on Point Figuras on the south shore of eastern Puerto Rico, beside the port of Arroyo.[^1] The Spanish government began it in 1892 and finished it in 1893 to guide ships into the ports of Arroyo and Patillas.[^2][^1] In 1938 the light was moved to another place, and the structure was abandoned and boarded up.[^1] The building has been on the National Register of Historic Places since 1981.[^1]

## The place

The 1981 National Register nomination places the light on the south shore of eastern Puerto Rico, on the east side of Arroyo port.[^1] It was built "as a local light to aid navigation into Arroyo and Patillas Ports in 1893." The nomination also calls it "an intermediate light to connect Caja de Muertos and Punta Tuna Lights," naming two other lights.[^1]

The nomination describes the shore around the light as "a swampy, marshy, stagnant, gray sand shore," and says the structure was built in "a once malaria-infected area."[^1]

## Building the light

LighthouseFriends says the Spanish work cost 18,300 pesos. It describes the light as a "fifth-order Fresnel lens" by Barbier, Benard & Cie.[^2] The nomination dates the lens to 1892 and calls it a "Barbier, Benard, & Cie. lens."[^1]

The keepers' dwelling had a Spanish kitchen.[^2][^1] The nomination says "The Spanish kitchen, remains of which still stands in situ," in the southeast corner of the house.[^1]

The United States Light-House Board's 1901 report describes the light in its own terms: "This fifth order fixed white light is 47 feet above high water, is visible 12 miles."[^3] The report does not say whether the 12 miles were nautical.

The dates disagree. The US Lighthouse Society's record gives 1889 as the year the station, its tower and its lens were established.[^10] The nomination, the Arroyo municipal page, the Spanish encyclopedia entry and LighthouseFriends all give 1893 for the light's completion or first lighting.[^1][^4][^5][^2] This story follows 1893, and the record's 1889 is listed among the corrections.

In November 1906 the building was repainted, according to a Light-House Board notice printed in San Juan's *La correspondencia de Puerto Rico* on 9 November. The Spanish text dates the change to "Noviembre 8, 1906." The English text says the coloring of the light "was changed to white," with green shutters and "a black lantern."[^6]

## The keepers

The US Lighthouse Society roster names three people at the light: Francisco Garcia, "at least 1901" to "at least 1903"; Lorenzo Castro-Gonzales, "at least 1905" to "at least 1921"; and Leopoldo Perales, the lamplighter, from "at least 1939."[^10]

LighthouseFriends quotes the Lighthouse Service's regulation that the efficiency flag went to the station in each district with "the highest general efficiency during a fiscal year."[^2] The 1918 to 1923 index lists "Point Figuras, receives efficiency flag," under a keeper printed as "Castro, Loenzo."[^9] LighthouseFriends says Castro-Gonzales received the flag in 1919, 1920 and 1921, and that by 1930 he had been transferred to Cabras Island.[^2]

The 1924 to 1929 index lists Point Figuras as "kept by Domingo Suarez."[^8] A 1925 notice in the Lighthouse Service Bulletin reports the death of Domingo Suárez Rosa, "retired keeper in the ninth" district, on 24 July.[^7] It says he had been retired on 1 July 1925 at the age of 62 "on account of physical disability." It counts 26 years in the United States service and 12 in the Spanish service, at stations that include Point Figuras, and praises his "faithfulness and loyalty to the service."[^7]

The same index lists Domingo Suarez as keeper of six stations: Port San Juan, Cape Rojo, Point Figuras, Mona Island, Guanica and Port Ferro, which are the six the notice names. It also has an entry, "Rosa, Domingo Suarez, retired keeper, dead." This story treats the two as one man on that basis.[^8][^7]

## After the keepers

In 1923 the light characteristics were modified.[^1]

In 1938 the light was moved away, and the structure was abandoned and boarded.[^1] The roster gives the station's discontinuance as "Year Discontinued: 1938."[^1][^10]

The building was reopened during the Second World War and used as a lookout.[^1] Some years after the war, the surrounding land was leased to the Army until 1963.[^1] The nomination says that "The lens and lantern were apparently destroyed by vandals in 1969."[^1]

The Arroyo municipal government's page says the Spanish government first lit the light in 1893, and that it was relocated in 1938.[^4] (Translated from the Spanish: "It was first lit by the Spanish government in 1893.")

## Today

The National Register nomination describes the building as "In ruins," and gives its classification as "Unoccupied. Unrestricted access."[^1] The access note comes from the nomination and does not describe the present.

A travel account says the building was rehabilitated between 2002 and 2003 by the Puerto Rican government, that Hurricane Maria damaged it again in 2017, and that "it is once again boarded up." It says the lighthouse is "not in use nor open to enter," but that visitors can stroll around it and take photos.[^11]

The sources disagree about access. LighthouseFriends names the owner as the Puerto Rico Department of Natural Resources and says "Grounds/tower open." It adds that the lighthouse is "reportedly open daily between 9 a.m. and 3 p.m."[^2] The US Lighthouse Society's archive lists it as "Open to Public: No."[^10] This story does not settle which is current.
