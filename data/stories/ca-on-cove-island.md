Cove Island Light stands on Gig Point, at the northern tip of Cove Island, where the channel from Lake Huron opens into Georgian Bay.[^1][^7] Its limestone tower was built in 1855 to 1857 and lit on 30 October 1858.[^1] Over the next 133 years its keepers included a family short of food in December 1860, a keeper's wife who kept the light dark to call for help, and a long line of men who served until 1991.[^1]

## The place

The light marks the entrance to the main channel into Georgian Bay from Lake Huron.[^7] LighthouseFriends says it was the first lighthouse lit on Georgian Bay.[^1] The register calls it the primary light at the entrance to Georgian Bay, and says it is the tower most depended upon by commercial traffic in the region.[^2]

## Building the light

Construction under the contractor John Brown began in the summer of 1855, using white limestone taken from the island.[^1] By October 1856 the tower had reached what LighthouseFriends gives as its full height of 24.4 m (80 ft), and the stonework was finished in 1857.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory also gives the tower as 24 m (80 ft), but LighthouseFriends' own data table lists 28.4 m above ground, so the sources do not agree on its height.[^1][^7] The register calls the tower a very good example of John Brown's work, and one of the best examples of the work of the pre-Confederation Canadian Board of Works.[^2]

A crew of French technicians under A. de St. Aubin assembled the prefabricated twelve-sided lantern room and the second-order Fresnel lens.[^1] Keeper George Collins lit the lamp for the first time on 30 October 1858.[^1] Engineer William Scott wrote on 2 November 1858 that the lens was revolving on its axis every ten minutes and giving a flash every thirty seconds, or twice a minute. From a distance, he wrote, the effect was "Grand beyond description."[^1]

## The keepers

Collins arrived with his wife, Sophia, and their son Charles on 22 May 1858, on a salary of $435 a year.[^1] While the tower was still going up, he maintained a light on a pole. He wrote that rain had been coming into the tower since the engineer left, and that there was no stove to dry it out.[^1] In September 1859 he was transferred to Nottawasaga Island, in a swap arranged with David McBeath.[^1]

McBeath lived at the station with his wife, Mary Jane, and their five children. In early December 1860 the provisions had not arrived, and the family had been rationing food for weeks. Staying put and hoping for supplies was one choice; the other was to risk the mainland in the station's boat.[^1] Only one steamer could cut through the ice, the *Rescue*, and she was on her last trip of the season.[^1] Her captain, James Dick, agreed to try.[^1] He reported that the family was about to commit itself to the lake on a very insecure raft.[^1] He sailed from Collingwood on 6 December, in a storm, and delivered the provisions.[^1] David McBeath died in 1872, and William McBeath took over.[^1]

George Currie served from 1878 to 1902, and Kenneth McLeod from 1903 to 1912.[^1] A departmental keepers' table for the year to 31 March 1911 names Kenneth McLeod at Cove Island.[^4]

In April 1946 Keeper Harold Banas and his assistant, his brother Earl, went out to test a new 6.1 m (20 ft) boat and motor.[^1] The engine broke down, and while they rowed back the oarlocks snapped, leaving them stranded.[^1] Harold's wife, Iris, had married him in 1945.[^1] Her only way of signalling for help was to keep the light dark.[^1] Hugh Rumley, the lightkeeper at Flowerpot Island, noticed that the light had stayed dark all night.[^1] At daybreak he sped across the rough water and was greeted by Iris.[^1] An aircrew spotted the station's partly submerged boat near Half Moon Island, and the two marooned keepers were seen waving from the island.[^1]

William Spears was keeper from 1949 to 1976. He reported the weather and other information to the Coast Guard three times a day, and when the weather allowed he would row out to help stranded boaters.[^1] Robert Nelder followed him from 1977 to 1981, and Jack Vaughan from 1982 to 1991.[^1] Vaughan became head keeper in 1982, when the work was mostly caretaking.[^1]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

On 17 November 1911 the steamer *Turret Chief*, of Newcastle, Great Britain, was upbound on Lake Huron when she went ashore on Cove Island in a gale. She was got off and repaired at Collingwood, with no casualties.[^5]

On 15 May 1923 the steamer *Morrow* ran aground on the south-west bank of Cove Island. She reported to the Tobermory station, and tugs *Harrison* and *Keenan* were sent to assist. The Department's report says she was released at 4 p.m. on 16 May.[^6] The report does not say which light was meant by the Tobermory station.

## After the keepers

A steam fog alarm was added in 1885.[^1] In 1905 the steam horn was replaced by a diaphone worked by compressed air, with a wooden addition 6.7 m by 8.5 m (22 ft by 28 ft).[^3] A fire later destroyed the original fog-alarm building. The present foghorn building was put up in October and November 1948.[^1]

Jack Vaughan, the last keeper that LighthouseFriends names, retired and left the island for good in 1991.[^1] In 2005 the Cove Island Lightstation Heritage Association was formed and signed a memorandum of understanding with the Coast Guard to restore and preserve the station.[^7] In May 2015 a restoration of $200,000 was announced.[^7] It was to include repairs to the keeper's house, restoration of the stonework and repainting of the light tower.[^7] No completion is recorded in the sources read.

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the lighthouse.[^7] The Lighthouse Directory names Parks Canada, through Fathom Five National Marine Park, as site manager.[^7] The island is reached only by boat.[^7] The Directory says the site is open and the tower closed.[^7] LighthouseFriends says the grounds and the tower are both closed, so check before going ashore.[^7][^1] The light is still in operation, and its original Fresnel lens shows a white flash every five seconds.[^1] Cove Island Lighthouse can be seen from the Chi-Cheemaun car ferry between Tobermory and South Baymouth.[^1][^7]
