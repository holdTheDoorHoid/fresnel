Jerome Point light stands on the east side of the entrance to St. Peter's Canal, where the canal opens onto St. Peter's Bay on the Atlantic side of Cape Breton Island, Nova Scotia.[^1] It is a white square tower with a red upper portion, and it shows a fixed red light.[^1][^3]

## The place

The isthmus between St. Peter's Bay and Bras d'Or Lake was a popular portage point long before there was a canal.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the Mi'kmaq carried their canoes across it, and that in 1650 the French fur trader Nicolas Denys built a haulover road across it.[^1] The canal took fifteen years to finish, from 1854 to 1869, because its channel runs through a granite hill about 20 m high.[^1]

The light was to mark the entrance to the canal from St. Peter's Bay.[^1] Traffic on the route grew: LighthouseFriends says more steamers were carrying mail and passengers from the railway terminus at Port Mulgrave, through Lennox Passage and the canal, into St. Peter's Inlet on Bras d'Or Lake.[^1]

## Building the light

In 1882 Lawrence J. O'Toole of St. Peter's began work on the lighthouse, under a contract for $1,300.[^1] John D. Matheson was hired in 1883 as the first keeper, at $200 a year.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory and the Nova Scotia Lighthouse Preservation Society both date the station to 1883.[^2][^4]

The same year the Department began work on six pole lights along the route, three in Lennox Passage and three in St. Peter's Inlet.[^1] LighthouseFriends says that with the light and the poles, mariners could make the run from Port Mulgrave to Sydney safely at night.[^1]

The first tower was square and pyramidal, with a two-storey dwelling attached to its landward side.[^1] In 1884 Matheson was given a Pilley's mechanical hand fog alarm, which he had to sound in answer to steamers' whistles.[^1]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends gives the keepers as John D. Matheson, 1883 to 1892; Joseph D. McNeil, 1892 to 1897; James McAskill, 1897 to 1913; James H. Barron, 1913 to 1920; A. Sampson, 1920 to 1935; and David A. McNeil, 1935 to 1958.[^1]

The Department's own keeper tables name a different man for part of that time.[^5][^6][^7] The 1902 list has "McAskill, Kenneth" at Jerome Point.[^7] The keeper table in the Department's 1909 report, printed in 1910, gives Kenneth McAskill's appointment as 30 July 1901, at $320 a year.[^5] The 1911 table names him at Jerome Point with the same date.[^6] The sources do not agree, and this story does not decide between them.

David A. McNeil had been keeper since 1935.[^1] He enjoyed the new dwelling for two years before retiring in 1958.[^1]

## After the keepers

In 1922 the light was given a seventh-order dioptric lens and a hand fog horn.[^2] In 1930 a fog signal was added, to answer vessels.[^2]

In 1956 the tower was rebuilt.[^1][^4] The new tower is a wooden square pyramidal tower, almost 11 m high, built with a detached keeper's dwelling.[^1] The Coast Guard list gives its height as 10.9 m, and the Lighthouse Directory gives 10.5 m (35 ft).[^3][^4] Both the lighthouse and the dwelling were connected to commercial power.[^1]

A temporary keeper worked until May 1960, when the light was de-staffed.[^1] A caretaker was then hired to keep an eye on the light until 1962, when that role was added to the canal lockmaster's duties.[^1]

In 1963 the keeper's dwelling and the lighthouse property were deeded to the canal authority.[^1] In 1965 an automatic changer and a sun switch were installed, and the light was made unwatched.[^1]

## Today

The Coast Guard list gives the light a fixed red character, a focal height of 15.5 m above the water, and a range of four nautical miles.[^3] It marks the light as seasonal.[^3]

The sources differ on ownership. LighthouseFriends and the Lighthouse Directory both name the Canadian Coast Guard as owner, and the Directory names Battery Provincial Park as site manager.[^1][^4] The Nova Scotia Lighthouse Preservation Society's list says the Government of Canada owns it.[^2]

The tower is closed.[^1] The season is also given differently. LighthouseFriends says the park, where the light stands, is open from mid-June through mid-October.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory gives mid-June to mid-September for the site and park.[^4] Check before going. A paved road reaches the site, with parking.[^4] Do not climb the tower.
