Oakville has two lighthouses. The wooden tower, built in 1888 or 1889 on the east pier at the mouth of Sixteen Mile Creek on Lake Ontario, stood there until 1960. It now stands on the grounds of the Oakville Yacht Squadron. The working light on the end of the east pier is a cylindrical tower.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

## The place

In 1827 William Chisholm bought the reserved land at the mouth of Sixteen Mile Creek at auction. The Dictionary of Canadian Biography says the reserve was "purchased by Chisholm that summer for £1,029."[^6] In 1828 he secured an act giving him the harbour's rights for fifty years.[^6] Within a year of the purchase he and his men had "laid out a town," and he later served as collector of customs and as postmaster.[^6]

## Building the light

In February 1837 the government of Upper Canada voted money for a lighthouse at Oakville and appointed three commissioners, one of them William Chisholm.[^2] The foundation was begun on August 22, 1837, and the commissioners completed the light on November 1, 1838.[^2] The tower was a wooden octagon, 11 m (36 ft) high, and LighthouseFriends says Chisholm paid for the overrun himself.[^2]

The dates do not all agree. The by-law says the first lighthouse "was built in 1837."[^1] The club's history says it was built in 1836 for $852.[^3]

After the storm of 1886 the pier was rebuilt in 1887, and the by-law says nothing was done about a light until 1889.[^1] LighthouseFriends names John George of Port Elgin as the contractor, at $937, and says the new hexagonal wooden tower went into service on June 4, 1888.[^2] The by-law tells it differently: a new lighthouse was built in the summer of 1889 by Henry George, contractor for Port Elgin, at a cost of $960; construction began on April 2 and the light was first lit on June 4.[^1] The club's history also credits Henry George and gives 1889, and the Doors Open Ontario listing gives 1889 and names him as architect.[^3][^5]

The by-law describes the tower as hexagonal, three stories high and sheathed in wooden shingles.[^1] Its light was first an oil lamp.[^1] A bronze plaque on the door reads "Built 1875 (incorrect)."[^1]

## The keepers

Robert Kerr Chisholm, William's son, was keeper from 1866, LighthouseFriends says, until he retired in 1894 at seventy-five.[^2] The Dictionary of Canadian Biography adds that "His son Robert Kerr succeeded him as postmaster and collector of customs."[^6]

Maurice Felan followed him, keeper from 1894 to 1921. The Marine department's list of lightkeepers for the year ended June 30, 1902, names "Felan, Maurice Oakville Pier."[^2][^7]

Frank McCraney was keeper from 1921 until at least 1940, LighthouseFriends says.[^2]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

The 1886 storm is dated April 7 in the Department of Marine report of that year, which LighthouseFriends quotes: "The great easterly storm of the 7th April last destroyed the east pier at the entrance of Oakville Harbour."[^2] The by-law dates it to "April 8, 1886."[^1] It gives the waves as 11 m (35 ft) high in the diary of someone named only as Summer, and says that "many children came down to the lake bank in time to see the lighthouse tumble into the channel."[^1]

Pier damage forced the tower to be moved 18 m (60 ft) shoreward in 1893 and another 11 m (36 ft) in 1894.[^2] LighthouseFriends says fire damaged the lighthouse in 1920 and again in 1922, and it cites photographs.[^2] The Marine report for 1922–23 lists "Oakville Repairs to tower."[^8]

In 1947 a storm washed the wooden pier away and left the light on what the club calls an island.[^3] The by-law says the piers "were washed away by the action of the lake."[^1]

## After the keepers

In 1960 the Canadian Department of Transportation replaced the lighthouse with a modern steel structure, the club says; LighthouseFriends describes a cylindrical cement tower instead.[^3][^2] The by-law says the old lighthouse had been slated for demolition, but was saved and moved to its current site on the squadron's grounds.[^1] The club's history says members and concerned local residents saved it.[^3] Doug Armour, the club's first commodore, petitioned Ottawa for permission to take it over; more than $11,000 went into rebuilding it, and Mayor William Anderson reopened it on June 19, 1963.[^3]

The town designated the lighthouse under the Ontario Heritage Act by By-law 1991-162, passed in July 1991.[^1] The club says the lighthouse was declared a Heritage Structure on January 9, 1991, about six months before the by-law was passed; the sources do not explain the difference.[^3] The club says it has been refurbished several times, including in 1990, 2007, 2012 and 2013.[^3] The by-law says the Town of Oakville now holds the lens.[^1]

The working light, on the south end of the east pier, is an isophase red light, Iso R 2s, from a "White cylindrical tower with red upper portion." The Coast Guard list gives the tower as 7.6 m high, with a focal height of 11.6 m, and says it operates "Seasonal."[^4]

## Today

The old lighthouse stands on the grounds of the Oakville Yacht Squadron, a private club. LighthouseFriends says the grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^2] Doors Open Ontario has listed the site in the past, but the listing says it "is not part of the current season's program."[^5] Ask the club before going onto its grounds, and do not enter the tower. By the club's account, few 19th-century wooden lighthouses survive on Lake Ontario, and this is one of them.[^3]
