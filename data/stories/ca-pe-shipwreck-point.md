Shipwreck Point Light stands on a point at Naufrage, on the northeast shore of Prince Edward Island, on the west side of the entrance to Naufrage Harbour.[^1] "Naufrage" is French for shipwreck.[^1] The tower there now is a white octagonal concrete tower with a red top, showing a white light three seconds in every five, all year.[^3] Beside it stands the older keeper's dwelling-and-lighthouse of 1913, its lantern removed and now privately owned.[^2]

## The place

Naufrage Pond, fed by the Naufrage River, empties into the Gulf of St. Lawrence between a pair of piers, and the light stands on the point at the harbour's west side.[^1] LighthouseFriends attributes to oral history the story that survivors of a shipwreck in 1719 came ashore in this area and became the first settlers of St. Peters Harbour.[^1]

## Building the light

The Department's annual report for the fiscal year 1913–14, printed in 1915, lists "Shipwreck point" among the new aids on Prince Edward Island. Its entry reads "Erection of a combined wooden dwelling and lighthouse and the installation of a 4th order dioptric lens," done by the Annandale Lumber Company.[^5] LighthouseFriends gives the cost as $3,213.81.[^1] The square two-storey building had eight rooms, and its hip roof carried a lantern housing the fourth-order lens.[^2] The dwelling was painted white and the lantern red.[^1]

Instead of keeping up the vacant dwelling that housed the light, LighthouseFriends says, a new concrete tower was built a short distance west of the 1913 building in 1967 by Schurman's Limited.[^1] It went into service on 27 September 1967.[^1] The 1913 building stayed in its original place.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory dates the tower to 1968.[^4] The Coast Guard's list and the Lighthouse Directory give the tower as 13.5 m (44 ft) high; LighthouseFriends gives 13.4 m.[^3][^4][^1]

## The keepers

Francis "Frank" MacKinnon was the first keeper.[^1] The keeper statement in the report for the fiscal year 1913–14 lists "Shipwreck point" beside "F. McKinnon."[^6] He held the post until 1917.[^1][^2]

After he drowned, his widow Sarah MacKinnon kept the light from 1917 until 1922. She married Gus Gallant in 1920.[^1][^2] Peter J. MacKinnon kept it from 1922 to 1939, Daniel Leonard O'Henley from 1939 to 1955, and Hugh Boniface MacDonald from 1955 to 1966.[^1][^2]

The Society says the light ran year-round from 1942 to 1945 to help wartime Commonwealth pilots training at Summerside and Mount Pleasant.[^2]

A cellar was dug under the dwelling in 1918, LighthouseFriends says.[^1] The Department's list of works for 1917–18 includes "Digging out the cellar under lighthouse and dwelling" among the Prince Edward Island entries, which fits that date.[^7]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

On 12 May 1917, LighthouseFriends says, Frank MacKinnon "drowned while setting traps on May 12, the first day of the lobster season." The mishap happened just offshore from Naufrage, and he did not know how to swim.[^1] The Society's account says the drowning came while he set lobster traps.[^2]

His son Neil, then eight, showed his mother how to look after the light the night his father died, the Society says. LighthouseFriends, by contrast, says he instructed "some gentlemen" how to light it that night.[^2][^1]

A story passed down in the MacKinnon family, as both sites tell it, has the light's turning mechanism failing on one stormy night, undated.[^1][^2] Sarah turned the lens by hand all night, and a captain later wrote to thank her.[^1][^2] The Society adds that the captain had thought he was at East Point, then counted the total flashes and realized he was 24 km (15 miles) west, at Shipwreck Point.[^2]

## After the keepers

The light was automated in 1966, when Hugh MacDonald retired.[^1][^2] The 1913 lens was on display at the Coast Guard base in Charlottetown for many years.[^2]

The Society says the old building was sold to the former keeper when the light was automated in 1966.[^2] It was sold again in May 2013, and the new owners planned to rent it as tourist accommodation.[^2] In 2015 a local group applied to own the tower, and in 2017 it received $28,540 for repairs. Rowlett says the transfer was still pending in September 2024.[^4]

## Today

The Coast Guard owns the 1967 tower and lists it as a year-round light, operating around the clock, flashing white for three seconds and dark for two.[^3][^4] The Friends of Shipwreck Point are the site manager.[^4]

The sources disagree on access. LighthouseFriends describes a wooden walkway, made by the Friends group, from a limited parking space on Harbour Road, yet it also lists the grounds and tower as closed.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory lists the site and tower as closed, private property, and says the light can be seen from a short distance away.[^4] The Society says the 1913 building is on private property and can be viewed from a public road.[^2] Look at the light from the road, and do not climb the tower or go onto the grounds unless the site is confirmed open.
