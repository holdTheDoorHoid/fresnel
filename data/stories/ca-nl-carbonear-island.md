Carbonear Island Lighthouse marks a small island at the mouth of Carbonear harbour, in Conception Bay, Newfoundland and Labrador. The light was placed in operation in 1878, and it still shows, now from a small skeleton tower.[^1][^2][^3]

Carbonear Island lies just off the entrance to the harbour.[^1] A plaque in Carbonear, as LighthouseFriends reproduces it, says that in the winter of 1697, after French raiders had laid waste most of the English settlements in Newfoundland, the residents of Carbonear and surrounding communities entrenched themselves on the island and repelled repeated attempts to take it.[^1] The same plaque says local people, led by William Pynne of Carbonear, defended it again in 1705 against a French force from Placentia.[^1] In 1954 what happened there was designated a national historic event.[^1]

The 1879 almanac, as LighthouseFriends quotes it, described the new light as "a fixed white light, 195 feet above sea level, and visible 16 miles in clear weather."[^1] Its tower and dwelling were wood, in one building, and the apparatus was dioptric, of the eighth order, with a simple argand burner.[^1] The current Canadian Coast Guard list gives the focal height as 54.3 m above the water and the nominal range as 7 nautical miles.[^3] The almanac's height, about 59 m (195 ft), and the list's 54.3 m do not agree, and neither source explains the difference.[^1][^3] In 1883 a small store was built at the landing place on the island, and the keeper was given winter sashes for the dwelling.[^1] A report of 1892, as LighthouseFriends quotes it, said that "the roof of the house is leaky during storms."[^1]

George H. Taylor was the first keeper, from 1878 until he died on the island in 1882.[^1] Henry F. Forward was appointed in his place and looked after the light until his passing in 1898.[^1] His son Henry G. Forward then took charge, raised his family on the island, and served until he died there in 1928.[^1] A local newspaper's notice of that death, as LighthouseFriends quotes it, said that for the past 44 years he "has lived with his family on the Island."[^1]

After Henry G. Forward's death the light was automated, and its characteristic changed from fixed white to a white flash every five seconds.[^1] LighthouseFriends reports that the original lighthouse stood at least until 1955, when a Light List still described the light as shown from a tower on the gable of a white wooden house with a red roof.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory says a 2010 photograph shows foundation ruins from the original lighthouse.[^2]

Today the light flashes white every six seconds from a rectangular skeleton tower with a red and white daymark, 4.3 m (14 ft) above ground.[^3] A note on LighthouseFriends says the island is now a bird sanctuary, and that digs there in 2010 and 2011 turned up artifacts from the years it was a refuge from French invaders.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory says the site is open, the tower is closed, and the island is accessible by a hiking trail.[^2]
