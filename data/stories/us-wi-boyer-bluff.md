Boyer Bluff Lighthouse stands on the northwestern tip of Washington Island, just off the northern end of Wisconsin's Door Peninsula.[^1] The Coast Guard owns the light. In 2012 it showed a white flash every six seconds from a tower that LighthouseFriends gives as 67 feet (20 m) tall, and the grounds and tower are closed.[^1]

The light was established on September 5, 1933, as a flashing white light of 280 candlepower on a white skeleton pyramidal tower. Its focal plane was 215 feet (66 m), and it was visible for eleven miles.[^1] Rowlett's directory says the present tower replaced a square pyramidal skeletal tower, but gives no date for the change. It describes the present one as an 80-foot (24 m) triangular cylindrical steel skeletal tower, with a focal plane of 220 feet (67 m).[^2] LighthouseFriends gives the tower as 67 feet (20 m) tall, which disagrees with Rowlett's 80 feet.[^1] The U.S. Lighthouse Society's list, which reproduces a 2004 Coast Guard light list entry, gives the characteristic as "Fl W 6s" on a skeleton tower.[^3]

Washington Island is reached by car ferry from Northport, at the tip of the Door Peninsula.[^1] The directory's section on these waters says most ships use the Rock Island Passage, north of the island, and that some take Porte des Morts, the passage between the island and the mainland, which it calls "the scene of many nineteenth century shipwrecks."[^2] The directory lists the status of the site as unknown.[^2]
