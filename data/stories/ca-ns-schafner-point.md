Schafner Point Lighthouse stands on the north side of the Annapolis Basin, facing Goat Island and just beyond the Port Royal historic site.[^2][^3] Built in 1885 to help ships past Goat Island's shoals, it is the oldest surviving lighthouse on the basin, and it is known locally as the Port Royal lighthouse.[^1][^2][^3]

## The place

Goat Island divides the river into two channels.[^3] The northern channel is the one ships prefer, because the southern channel has only 1.2 m (4 ft) of water at low tide, and a shoal runs roughly 600 metres around the island.[^3]

## Building the light

Lighthouse Friends prints the Department of Marine's annual report for 1885, which says a new lighthouse on "Shafner's Point" was put in operation on 24 September. John Wagstaff of Annapolis built it under contract for $900.[^3] The 1885 description gives a square wooden tower, white with a red iron lantern, 13 m (43 ft) from base to vane. Its fixed white light stood 17 m (55 ft) above high water and should be visible 11 miles.[^3] It used a small dioptric lens, and it was meant to show mariners the position of the Goat Island shoals.[^3]

The later work on record is limited. In 1887 a stone retaining wall went up in front of the tower against the eroding bank.[^3] In 1898 the entrance steps were repaired, a new lock was fitted to the door, and a new lamp was supplied.[^3] Lighthouse Friends notes that government records hold little more than this.[^3] The register calls the tower the first lighthouse on its site, but the second navigational aid on the Digby Gut–Annapolis Basin water corridor.[^2]

## The keepers

Joseph K. Healy was the first keeper, appointed in 1885.[^3] Twelve years later the appointment was cancelled after an official investigation found him, in the words Lighthouse Friends prints, "guilty of active and offensive political partizanship" during a federal election.[^3]

Jacob V. Roblee took the post in his place, and Lighthouse Friends dates his term from 1897 to 1912.[^3] The Department's keeper index for the year ended 30 June 1906 lists "Roblee, Jacob V" at Shafner Point, appointed 29 May 1897.[^4] In 1912 Healy returned, and Lighthouse Friends says he served until at least 1923.[^3]

The Port Royal Lighthouse Association says that before electrification the light was run by a keeper named Lloyd Shafner, whose house stood nearby.[^5] No dates are given for his time there.

## After the keepers

Until 2015 the Department of Fisheries and Oceans owned and maintained the lighthouse, according to the association.[^5] In the 2010s the federal government began to shed its lighthouses. Schafner Point was still a working light, and it was offered to non-profit groups willing to look after it.[^5]

The lighthouse was recognised as a heritage lighthouse on 12 February 2015, under the Heritage Lighthouse Protection Act.[^2] The Annapolis Heritage Society took ownership that year.[^3][^5] Reshingling began in 2019, and under the shingles the work found extensive wood rot in the corner posts. The society decided not to go ahead with the repairs,[^5] and Lighthouse Friends says the lighthouse was left without shingles until money could be raised.[^3] The estimate for the work was $89,000.[^3]

In September 2022 the Port Royal Lighthouse Association was founded and took ownership of the lighthouse property.[^5] By May 2023 the group had raised more than $120,000, through local fundraising, business sponsors, a matching grant from Parks Canada and grants from the county and the Nova Scotia Heritage Trust.[^5] The renovation ran from late July to early November 2023, and local volunteers gave hundreds of hours painting, shingling and milling lumber.[^5] A dedication ceremony on 9 December 2023 included the unveiling of the federal heritage plaque.[^5]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard's List of Lights gives the light as fixed white, 13.3 m above the water and visible 10 nautical miles, shown from a white square tower with a red upper portion, and it is lit year round.[^6] The same list gives the tower a height of 11.1 m, while the register gives 13 m (43 ft). No source cited here explains the difference.[^2][^6]

The association owns the lighthouse, and its sole purpose is to maintain the lighthouse and grounds.[^5] Lighthouse Friends says the grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^3] Granville Road runs about 10 km (6.3 miles) from Route 1 at Lower Granville to the lighthouse, and there is a small parking area beside it.[^3] The association places the lighthouse less than a kilometre from the Parks Canada Habitation, which it describes as believed to be the site of the first European settlement in Canada.[^5] Lighthouse Friends gives the distance as 1 km beyond the historic Port Royal Habitation, on the drive in.[^3]
