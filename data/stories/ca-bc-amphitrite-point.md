Amphitrite Point Lighthouse stands on the point at the entrance to Ucluelet Harbour, on the west coast of Vancouver Island, British Columbia.[^1][^2] A light was first established on the point in 1906, and the tower that stands now dates from 1915.[^2][^3]

## The place

The point stands at the entrance to Ucluelet Harbour, on the Pacific coast.[^1][^6] The Ucluelet and Area Historical Society says the Pacific around Ucluelet was known as the "Graveyard of the Pacific", because of the high number of shipwrecks along the coast.[^6] The Department of Marine had already put a buoy to work here before the light. Its annual report for 1905 records a red whistling buoy established in the Carolina Channel, off Amphitrite point.[^5] The marker says the point was named for the naval warship HMS Amphitrite, in 1859, by Captain George H. Richards.[^2]

## Building the light

The first light was a wooden tower.[^2] The Department's annual report for 1906 records that a light was established on the extremity of the point. It was a fixed white light, elevated 18 m (60 ft) above high water, and visible 13 miles from all points of approach by water.[^3] The report gives the figure in miles and does not say whether they were nautical miles.

The Ucluelet and Area Historical Society says the second tower's unusual design lets it cling to the solid rock through violent Pacific storms.[^6] Some people, it adds, think it looks like a barnacle.[^6]

## The keepers

The Department's 1906 keepers' list places a George Fraser at Amphitrite point, appointed on 2 April 1906 at a listed salary of $240.[^4]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

The Ucluelet and Area Historical Society says the first light was built at the point after the Pass of Melfort sank with all aboard.[^6] The marker dates the wreck to 1905 and says the first wooden lighthouse was built in 1906, after it.[^2] The marker records that storm waves destroyed the lighthouse in 1914. It was replaced by the present structure in 1915.[^2]

## After the keepers

The marker says the lighthouse was maintained by on-site personnel until 1988, when the Canadian Coast Guard began automating many of its lights.[^2] Amphitrite Point was one of those chosen for automation.[^2]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard's list of lights gives the light as operating year round, 24 hours a day, with a characteristic of a white flash every twelve seconds, Fl W 12s.[^1] The fog horn sounds a two-second blast every twenty seconds.[^1]

The District of Ucluelet describes the Lighthouse Loop of the Wild Pacific Trail as offering views of Barkley Sound and the Amphitrite Lighthouse.[^7] Its trailhead is at a parking area on Coast Guard Road, and a short walk around the station leads to the trail's signs.[^7] No source invites visitors into the tower.[^1][^7]
