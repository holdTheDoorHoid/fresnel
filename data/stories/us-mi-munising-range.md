The Munising Range is a pair of steel towers on Lake Superior, in Michigan's Upper Peninsula, that were built to show red lights along the channel into Munising Bay. The lights were first shown on 30 October 1908.[^1][^2][^3]

## The place

Ships coming into Munising Bay passed east of Grand Island, through a channel bounded on one side by the island's eastern lobe and on the other by the mainland's Sand Point.[^1][^2] Shoals run "several hundred feet" out from Sand Point.[^2]

In the summer of 1868 a wooden light, Grand Island Harbor Lighthouse, was built on a stone foundation at the southeast tip of the island's eastern lobe to guide mariners through the East Channel.[^2] By 1905 it was no longer doing that job: mariners using the East Channel "were unable to see the light until they were nearly upon it," and the old wooden tower was "difficult and expensive to maintain."[^2]

When storms arose on the lake, ships often used Munising Bay as a harbor of refuge.[^1] The 1908 report of the Lake Carriers' Association, as LighthouseFriends quotes it, describes the new range as opening the deep water south of Grand Island "as a refuge for the largest class of vessels plying on the Great Lakes" in northerly storms.[^2]

## Building the light

Congress provided $15,000 for the range on 4 March 1907.[^1][^2] The contract for the two metal towers went to the Champion Iron Company of Kenton, Ohio.[^1][^2] The plans put the front tower on low land close to the shore, and the rear tower on "the hillside overlooking Munising Bay."[^1]

The sources give different heights for the front tower. The National Park Service brochure says it "stands at 58 feet," and LighthouseFriends gives "the fifty-five-foot-tall tower."[^1][^2] The towers were built of steel plates, and the front one rests on a concrete foundation about five feet (1.5 m) deep.[^2]

A light keeper's dwelling of frame and brick was built beside the front tower.[^1][^2] Its builder was local.[^1]

The sources do not agree on what lit the range when it was new. The National Park Service brochure says the lanterns used "incandescent electric bulbs."[^1] LighthouseFriends says the towers "originally displayed fixed red lights" from Adams and Westlake reflector lanterns, and describes a brick oil house beside the front tower. It does not name the fuel, but the oil house suggests oil.[^2]

Keeper George Prior lit the old Grand Island light for the last time on the night of 29 October 1908. The next day he sailed to Munising to make the first lighting of the new range, which became operational on 30 October.[^1][^2] When the range was lit, the Grand Island Harbor light station was discontinued, as an Act of Congress required.[^2]

## The keepers

The Society's archive lists George Prior from 1908 to 1912, Thomas Hendrickson from 1912 to at least 1924, Alf Evensen from 1925 to 1940, Fred W. Hawkins from 1940 to 1947, and Floyd W. Miller from at least 1960 to 1961.[^3]

Alf Evensen retired on 1 April 1940, "after thirty-eight years of service on Lake Superior." He had started in 1902 as a second assistant at Whitefish Point, and he spent "the last fifteen years of his career" at the Munising range.[^2] His son Harold, LighthouseFriends says, was then working as "the second mate aboard the lighthouse tender Amaranth."[^2]

Fred W. Hawkins came from the Au Sable Point light to replace Evensen and kept the range until 1947.[^2]

Floyd W. Miller "retired from serving as the light attendant for Munising Range, as well as Grand Island Harbor Range and Grand Island Light, in 1961."[^2] After his retirement, a Coast Guard team at Munising took over the range, "responsible for not only aids to navigation but rescues as well."[^2]

## After the keepers

The range lights were electrified in 1941, "greatly reducing the work associated with minding them."[^2] The brochure's electric bulbs in 1908 and LighthouseFriends' electrification in 1941 do not fit together on their face, and the sources do not explain the gap.[^1][^2]

The Coast Guard transferred the range to the National Park Service in July 2002.[^1][^2][^4] The metal range towers were sandblasted and painted in the winter of 2008 to 2009.[^2][^4]

In 2019 the Coast Guard "replaced the front range beacon with a leading light" and "decommissioned the rear light."[^4] The keeper's house has since been "renovated for offices for Park Service science and natural resources staff."[^4]

## Today

The Society's archive lists the range as an active aid to navigation and not open to the public.[^3] The Visitor's Bureau describes the front tower as an "active white-painted tower" that "emits a continuous red light."[^5] Rowlett's directory, describing the 2019 leading light, gives it as "continuous light, white, red or green depending on direction."[^4] The sources do not agree on the colour now, and no Light List entry was read. Its tower is "currently closed to the public," but the grounds are open, and the Visitor's Bureau says restoration is underway.[^5]

The front tower is "located at the intersection of M-28 and Hemlock Street in Munising."[^5] The rear light was decommissioned in 2019.[^4]
