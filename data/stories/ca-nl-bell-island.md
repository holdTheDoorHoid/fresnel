Bell Island Light stands at the northeast end of Bell Island, the largest island in Conception Bay, Newfoundland. It has shown since 1940, and the tower that stands now dates from 1966.[^1][^2] The Coast Guard's list gives the light a one-second flash and a five-second eclipse, a focal height of 52.7 m, and a range of 17 nautical miles.[^3] Historic Wabana gives the same focal height as 52.7 m (172.9 feet).[^2]

## The place

The island's iron mines brought the shipping the light was built for. LighthouseFriends says rocks from the island were identified in 1893 as red hematite, and the Newfoundland 3D Lighthouse Gallery says large-scale mining did not begin until 1895.[^1][^4] Historic Wabana says the island was noted in 1896 as needing a lighthouse, because large ore carriers were entering Conception Bay.[^2]

The request for a light is dated differently by each source. LighthouseFriends says the Dominion Steel Company, which ran the mines, asked the Newfoundland government for a lighthouse in the early 1930s. The gallery gives the request as 1939.[^1][^4]

## Building the light

Work on a light tower, a fog alarm building and a keepers' duplex began in 1939 on the eastern tip of the island, LighthouseFriends says. The gallery places the site on the northern tip.[^1][^4] The station began operation on 1 September 1940, according to the Historic Wabana account.[^1][^2]

In 1966 a combined lighthouse and fog alarm building replaced the 1940 station.[^1][^4]

In 2004 erosion threatened the cliff, and the light and the keeper's house were moved. The sources differ on the distance. LighthouseFriends says roughly 200 metres, and the gallery says nearly 200 metres inland. Historic Wabana says the move was a few hundred metres west of the old location.[^1][^4][^2]

## The keepers

The keeper captions and Joe Dwyer's narrative on Historic Wabana name the keepers. The captions give John Flynn's service as 1940 to 1968, and Dwyer's narrative calls him the first keeper.[^2] The captions give Tom Brazil as keeper from 1962 to 1986, and Bill Clarke from 1978 to 2001. Dwyer's narrative gives Clarke's years as 1983 to 2001.[^2] The captions also say Fred O'Brien, Sr. kept the light for about 16 years between the 1960s and the 1980s.[^2]

The station was reduced to one keeper in 1980, and the old duplex was removed.[^1][^2]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

During the Second World War German submarines torpedoed the island's iron ore pier and sank four ore ships off the island.[^1][^4] LighthouseFriends says a memorial at Lance Cove Beach honours the sixty-nine men who died aboard them.[^1] The sources do not link the light to those attacks.

## Today

LighthouseFriends reports that the lighthouse was broken into and vandalized in early August 2010.[^1]

The lighthouse is still listed as a staffed station and is owned by the Canadian Coast Guard, according to LighthouseFriends. Historic Wabana says it is still manned.[^1][^2] Its grounds are open, but the tower is closed.[^1] In 2014 Tourism Bell Island obtained federal and provincial funding to refurbish the keeper's house. It opened in July of that year as a visitor centre, now called the Keeper's Cafe.[^2][^1]

Because the station is staffed and owned by the Coast Guard, visitors should go only where it opens its grounds.[^1]
