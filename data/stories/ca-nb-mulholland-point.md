Mulholland Point Lighthouse stands at the Campobello end of Lubec Narrows, the short passage between Campobello Island, New Brunswick, and Lubec, Maine.[^1][^2] The light was first shown in 1885. The Canadian Coast Guard decommissioned it in 1963, when the new bridge's lights took over its work, and the octagonal wooden tower now stands inside Roosevelt Campobello International Park, owned by the park commission, and its tower is closed to visitors.[^1][^2]

## The place

The Narrows were the short way through. A later account says the Lubec Channel route past the light was eight miles shorter for coastal shipping than going round the eastern shore of Campobello, and that the island sheltered vessels from Atlantic storms.[^3] A Campobello marker says steamships sailing between Boston, Portland and Eastport in the 1890s could pass through the Narrows only at high tide, and otherwise had to steam round the east side of the island. The same marker says that travel on the car ferries that later crossed the Narrows often depended on the tides and the fog.[^2]

According to another marker, Franklin Roosevelt, as Assistant Secretary of the Navy, took the helm of the USS *Flusser* on an inspection trip and piloted her through the narrow channel between Lubec and Campobello.[^4]

## Building the light

Parliament made an appropriation in 1882 for a light to guide vessels through Lubec Narrows. The contract, for $790, went to Angus Fisher of St. George in 1883. The tower was finished in 1884, but its fixed white light was not exhibited until 1885.[^1] E. Chanteloup of Montreal supplied the lantern and lighting apparatus for $683.26, and Malachi Parker was hired as the first keeper.[^1]

The marker gives the tower as 9.7 m (32 ft) tall, post and timber-framed and covered in cedar shingles, with a 3.7 m (12 ft) red iron lantern. The total height is 13.4 m (44 ft). The lantern held a 7th-order refractive lens.[^4] Lighthouse Friends says the light was visible for 13 miles when it was first shown.[^1] The marker puts the lens's visibility at 6.4 km (4 miles), so the two figures disagree.[^4] Sunrise County Architecture, quoted by a museum catalogue, says that in 1885 the light itself stood 60 ft (18 m) above sea level.[^3] The sources do not settle the light's height or range.

## The keepers

Malachi Parker kept the light from 1885 until 1901, according to Lighthouse Friends.[^1] In 1901 the Department's report for the year ended 30 June 1901 took up the light's future. It said the lighthouse "has been found to be of little use where it now stands, since the narrow channel has been straightened and improved by the United States government." The report proposed a removal to Cherry Island, which it describes as a move from "Mulholland point, Lubeck narrows." The sources do not say whether the move was made, and later reports still list the light at Mulholland Point.[^5]

Alvin Parker became keeper in 1901 and served until 1925, according to Lighthouse Friends.[^1] The Department's keeper table for the year ended 31 March 1911 lists his name, and its station list includes Mulholland Point. The two lists are in separate columns of a scanned table, so the match is by position.[^6] A. F. Newman served from 1926 until the light was discontinued in 1963, according to Lighthouse Friends.[^1]

## After the keepers

Construction of the Roosevelt Memorial Bridge between Lubec and Campobello began in January 1960. Its formal opening was on 13 August 1962.[^2] The bridge's navigation lights made the lighthouse obsolete, and the Coast Guard decommissioned it in 1963.[^1][^2] The tower and a shed beside it were sold to Campobello resident Clifford Calder, and the Look family of Washington County, Maine, later bought the light from him.[^1][^2]

The sea has worked on the point. By 1954 the tides had eroded the bank under the lighthouse, and stone and wooden retaining walls were built against the water. The United States Corps of Engineers completed an emergency shore-line protection project at the point in 1989.[^3] In 1984 the Look family donated the lighthouse to Roosevelt Campobello International Park, in memory of Clifford Calder. The park commission placed a plaque on it and built a picnic and parking area for visitors.[^1][^2][^3] The National Park Service's Cultural Resource Center in Boston completed a restoration of the lighthouse in 1991.[^3]

The Corps of Engineers, as quoted in the same catalogue, praised the "design, materials and workmanship" of the 1885 builders and recommended the lighthouse for the United States National Register of Historic Places. The sources do not say whether it was listed.[^3]

## Today

The lighthouse is not open to the public. Lighthouse Friends says the grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^1][^4] Visitors reach the point by crossing the Roosevelt Memorial Bridge from Lubec, passing through the customs station and taking the first left.[^1] Do not climb or enter the tower.
