The Cheboygan River Front Range Light stands on the Cheboygan River in the town of Cheboygan, Michigan.[^1][^11] The Light-House Board listed it in the Straits of Mackinac.[^1][^5] It was first lit in 1880 as the front light of a pair of range lights at the river's mouth.[^1][^2] The US Lighthouse Society's record says it is no longer an active aid to navigation, and volunteers now keep it open to visitors in season.[^2][^11]

## The place

The range lights were part of the harbor work on the river.[^1] Before that work, LighthouseFriends says, the water over the bar at the river's mouth was only seven feet deep.[^1] In 1870 Cheboygan had about 800 people and a growing number of lumber mills along the river. The Army Corps of Engineers surveyed the river that year to evaluate whether navigation could be improved.[^11] Congress answered the Corps' recommendation with $160,000 for the work, to begin the following year.[^11] In 1876 Congress allocated $10,000 for range lights at the mouth of the river, but litigation over one of the two sites on the west bank held construction back until June 1880.[^1]

## Building the light

The front light was first a lens lantern, shown from a nine-foot (2.7 m) square tower on the north gable of a two-story keeper's dwelling, and the rear light stood on a skeletal wooden tower. Their focal planes were 42 feet (13 m) and 68 feet (21 m).[^1] Both range lights were first shown on the night of 30 September 1880.[^1][^2]

In 1884 a sixth-order Fresnel lens replaced the lens lantern at the front light, and both lights changed from fixed white to fixed red.[^1] The US Lighthouse Society's record instead gives a sixth-order Fresnel as the original optic, installed in 1880.[^2] The two sources disagree on when the lens came, and this story does not settle the question.

In 1891 the front light and the keeper's dwelling were moved to a new site. The Light-House Board's report for the year ended 30 June 1891 says the work had begun, and its report for the year ended 30 June 1892 says it was completed.[^3][^4] LighthouseFriends describes the cellar of the dwelling as the problem: it often flooded, and the only way to drain it was over private land to the river.[^1] In 1890 the Board asked for $1,500 to buy that land. While it waited, the dwelling was moved to a new site on the lighthouse lot, and a pipe was laid from the cellar to the river.[^1]

The Board's report for the year ended 30 June 1894 lists $1,750 for additional land at the front light.[^5] LighthouseFriends gives Congress's appropriation of $1,750 as 1 July 1898, and says the deed to the land was not obtained until 1910.[^1] The Board's listing is four years before that appropriation, and this story does not explain the difference.

The old timber beacon of the rear range was replaced by a steel tower. The Board's report for the year ended 30 June 1901 says the erection of the structure was finished in December 1900.[^6] LighthouseFriends dates the steel tower to 1890.[^1] The two sources disagree. This story gives the Board's date, from the report for the year that includes December 1900.[^6][^1]

The Board's report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1899 records that the bulkhead at the Cheboygan River range "had bulged out" and was repaired and straightened.[^7] LighthouseFriends says the combined tower and dwelling was changed from brown to white in 1901, and that on 27 June 1914 the range lights' illuminating apparatus was changed to reflectors, which increased their intensity.[^1]

## The keepers

George P. Humphreys, the first keeper, resigned after two years, in 1882.[^1][^2] James W. Rich succeeded him. LighthouseFriends says Rich was transferred to Copper Harbor Lighthouse after four months.[^1][^2]

Ivory A. Littlefield kept the range lights from 1883 until he died in August 1894.[^2][^1] LighthouseFriends describes him as a Civil War veteran who had lost his left foot in the war.[^1] A notice in the Northern Tribune of Cheboygan on 30 October 1884 names him keeper of the range lights and gives him the supervision of the pier light as well.[^9] During Littlefield's time an assistant keeper was assigned to the station to help with the Cheboygan Crib light, which began operating off the river's mouth in 1884.[^1]

When Littlefield died, his wife, Philancy, whom the archive calls "Fanny," served as keeper for one month before she resigned.[^1][^2] John Sinclair, Jr., was head keeper from 1894 to 1899, and John Duffy then kept the station for 35 years, until 1934.[^2]

The archive lists 17 first assistants between 1885 and 1925. Otto H. Both was one of them, serving from 1908 to 1919.[^2] Clarence R. Land kept the station from 1944 to 1966.[^2] The archive's later keepers appear under a "USCG" heading, which begins with John N. Ashmore, listed to 1957.[^2]

## After the keepers

Through the winter, the Martin and Poe Reef lightships were often moored at the front light.[^1] Later, with permanent structures in place of those lightships, the front light supplied those stations and others offshore.[^1]

The Commissioner of Lighthouses' report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1921 lists, among the work completed that year, $4,834 for a "timber sheet-pile revetment for shore protection at front light."[^8]

In 1932 the Lighthouse Service Bulletin described a radiotelephone group of stations at the north end of Lake Huron, and the Cheboygan Range light was one of them. The Bulletin names Cheboygan as the shore point for communication with the whole group, twice each day.[^10]

In the 1980s the Coast Guard moved its operations from the Cheboygan River to St. Ignace. LighthouseFriends says the U.S. Geological Survey and the Department of Fisheries then moved into the front range dwelling.[^1] In 2001 the light was included in the first group of lighthouses excessed under the National Historic Lighthouse Preservation Act of 2000.[^1] The Great Lakes Lighthouse Keepers Association received the deed in June 2004. It then used a Michigan Lighthouse Assistance Program grant to commission an engineering study, and the lantern box and gallery have since been rebuilt using a $21,000 grant from that program.[^1]

## Today

The Great Lakes Lighthouse Keepers Association owns the light.[^1]

Volunteers open it in season. The association's 2026 schedule lists weekend hours in June and in the fall, and Thursday to Sunday in July and August, and it warns that hours may vary.[^11] Volunteer keepers stay in the lighthouse for a session and work in pairs, swapping between the gift shop and tours. They pay a small fee and must be members of the association.[^11] The light stands in downtown Cheboygan.[^11] The US Lighthouse Society's record still lists it as not open to the public.[^2]
