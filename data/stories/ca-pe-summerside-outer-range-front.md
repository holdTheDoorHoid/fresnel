The Summerside Outer Range front light is a white tower with red trim, on the shore at the western end of Summerside, Prince Edward Island, beside the town's boardwalk. It was built in 1991 and is one half of a pair of red range lights that guide ships to the mouth of Summerside Harbour, near the Indian Head Light.[^1][^2][^4]

The harbour opens on Northumberland Strait and is sheltered by the Indian Head peninsula.[^1] Rowlett dates the Indian Head Light to 1881 and lists it as active.[^1] Ships that find the outer pair can then use the harbour's own range lights to proceed up the harbour.[^4] Rowlett gives the inner front range as established in 1895.[^1]

The front tower is 9 m (29.5 ft) tall, square and pyramidal, with an enclosed lantern and a gallery.[^1][^2] Its light is fixed red, with a focal plane of 9.3 m (about 30.5 ft), and the Society says it is visible in the line of range all year.[^2][^3] The rear tower stands 430 m (1,412 ft) from the front, on a bearing of 028°57'. Rowlett gives it as 10 m (33 ft) tall, with a focal plane of 15 m (49 ft).[^3][^1]

Both lights began operation in 1991, and LighthouseFriends calls them the most recent set of enclosed range lights to be established on Prince Edward Island.[^4] The Society says the front light has never had a keeper and has always been electric.[^2] Rowlett says the pair were probably the last wooden lighthouses the Coast Guard built.[^1]

The sources differ on the nominal range. The Coast Guard's list gives 16 nautical miles, and the Society gives 18.[^3][^2] The Society also reports recent erosion of the bank, which was reinforced to Coast Guard standards.[^2]

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the lights. The grounds are open, and the tower is closed to visitors.[^4][^2] The City of Summerside has applied to take over ownership. The Society says the city has petitioned under the Parks Canada Heritage Lighthouse Program, and Rowlett records the same application.[^2][^1]
