Caribou Point light stands at the northeast end of Caribou Island, at the approaches to Caribou Channel on Nova Scotia's Northumberland Shore. A light has shown from the point since 1868. The tower standing now is a concrete tower with a red top, put up around 1970 and set in one corner of a fog-alarm building.[^1][^2][^3]

## The place

Travellers on the ferry to Prince Edward Island get a view of the light shortly after leaving Nova Scotia.[^1] The sources do not agree on which island the point is on. Lighthouse Friends puts it at the north end of Caribou Island, and the Coast Guard's list at the north-east end of Caribou Island. The Society puts it at the north-east end of Gull Island, and lists the body of water as Caribou Channel.[^1][^2][^3]

## Building the light

The Board of Works awarded a $1,450 contract to Robert Purves on 20 July 1867 to build the lighthouse. Alexander Munroe was appointed the first keeper that October.[^1] The light was first shown in 1868, and the Society gives the same year.[^1][^2] It was a combination dwelling and tower. Its revolving apparatus gave a white flash every minute, from three lamps set in reflectors on each of its two faces.[^1]

Lighthouse Friends says 18 m (59 ft) of land were lost from the point in the first ten years. A stone-ballasted timber crib went in to slow the erosion in 1877, and it was doubled in length the next year.[^1]

The second tower was built in 1915 or 1916. Lighthouse Friends gives 1915 and a contract to W. Talbot of Pictou for $5,103.11. The Society and a tourism guide give 1916.[^1][^2][^5] The Marine Department's report for the fiscal year 1915-16 lists the erection of "a wooden combined lighthouse tower and dwelling" at Caribou as a contract with W. Talbot of Pictou, with $2,650 spent that year. The sources do not say when the rest of the contract price was paid.[^4] The second tower was a square dwelling with an octagonal lantern on a hipped roof.[^1]

The third tower is the one standing. The Society dates it to between 1968 and 1970, and so does the tourism guide. Lighthouse Friends says it went up around 1971.[^2][^5][^1] It is a square concrete tower in one corner of a one-storey, flat-roofed fog-alarm building.[^1]

## The keepers

Six head keepers served from 1867 to 1956, from Alexander Munroe to Charles H. T. Baird. Lighthouse Friends' list spells the first name "Munore". Roderick McKenzie, Frederick Baird, D. Falconer and James A. MacFarlane came between.[^1] The Marine Department's keeper table for the report labelled 1911 pairs D. Falconer with Caribou Island.[^6] Baird kept the light from 1917 to 1956, close to forty years.[^1]

Archibald Baird followed in 1956, then Charles Walter Richards, who is listed to at least 1966.[^1] Foster Welsh, who came from Entry Island in the Magdalen Islands around the time the third tower was built, kept the light from 1971 to 1990. When he arrived there were two keepers, and the station was reduced to one when the other retired.[^1]

Welsh argued for staffing and cited occasions when human presence on the point had made the difference between life and death. Public meetings and petitions did not save the post, and the station was destaffed in 1990.[^1]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

Winter gales in 1892 almost totally destroyed the cribwork. Lighthouse Friends says the Department had to choose between rebuilding the breakwater and relocating the lighthouse, and it moved the light. Hugh Henderson of Pictou was paid $525 to move it.[^1]

## After the keepers

The station was destaffed in 1990, and the Society gives automation by 1990.[^1][^2] In 1992 the light was given a fourth-order dioptric lens, a mercury-vapour source and an electric lantern.[^2]

When the Coast Guard began to divest several of its lights in 2012, no group came forward for Caribou.[^1] The second tower was moved a short distance down the road and is now a private residence.[^1]

In October 2016 the one-storey fog-alarm building was demolished, leaving the tower standing. It had fallen into disrepair and contained asbestos. After the partial demolition, a Facebook group called Friends of Caribou Island formed to save what was left.[^1] The Coast Guard patched and painted the tower in 2018.[^1]

The original lantern room from the second tower is on display at the Northumberland Fisheries Museum in Pictou. A two-thirds scale model of that second tower, built by carpentry students at Nova Scotia Community College in 2007, is at the museum too.[^1]

## Today

The Government of Canada owns the light.[^2] The Coast Guard's list gives a white rectangular tower with a red top, which flashes once every five seconds, with a focal height of 10.8 m and a nominal range of nine nautical miles. The tower itself is 12.6 m high.[^3]

Visitors drive in.[^2] Caribou Island Road ends at the lighthouse, and the grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^1] Visitors should not treat the second tower, now a private residence, as part of the visit.[^1]
