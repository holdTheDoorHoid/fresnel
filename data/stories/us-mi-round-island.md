Round Island Light stands on a small island in the St. Marys River, between Lime Island and Point aux Frênes in Chippewa County, Michigan.[^1][^2] It was one of the permanent stations the Lighthouse Board built along the river in 1892. The Lighthouse Friends site says that a buyer who acquired the lighthouse around 1998 became only its third private owner.[^2]

## The place

The St. Marys River flows from Lake Superior to Lake Huron. After the locks at Sault Ste. Marie opened in 1855 the river became a vital transportation link, but it took several decades before it was adequately marked and lighted.[^2]

In its report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1891, the Lighthouse Board said the conditions in the vicinity of Round Island and Point Iroquois "are unsatisfactory to navigators of these waters." The cause, it said, was "the insufficient depth and the narrowness of the passage," which was "but a quarter of a mile wide between the 11-foot shoals."[^3] Congress answered that year. The act approved 3 March 1891 "appropriated $30,000 for the construction of a system of lights and ranges" along the river from Pipe Island to Sault Ste. Marie.[^3][^2]

Round Island is nine acres in the lower part of the river. The much larger Lime Island lies just to its south, and Munuscong Lake lies to its north.[^2]

## Building the light

A deed to a one-acre parcel on the island's eastern side was obtained on 5 May 1892. Construction began on 27 July.[^2] The Light-House Board's report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1893 notes that the steam launch Lotus "was used in building Round Island light-station, on St. Marys River."[^4] The light was first exhibited on 20 September 1892.[^2]

The tower is eight feet square and attached to the center of a one-and-a-half-story frame keeper's dwelling.[^2] The Lighthouse Friends site gives the tower's height as 40 feet. The USLHS record gives 35 feet from base to the center of the lantern, and 40 feet as the height of the light above the water.[^2][^1] The light used a lens lantern, not a formal lens, because of delays in procuring lenses from abroad, and the Board used U.S.-made lens lanterns in the river lights.[^2][^1] The light was a fixed white over 180 degrees, bounded on each side by red sectors. The southern sector covered shoals southwest of the island, and the northern one helped mark the turn into Munuscong (Mud) Lake.[^2] The Lighthouse Friends site says the lens lantern was still in place in 1909.[^2]

## The keepers

The USLHS archive names seven principal keepers, from 1896 to 1921: Xavier Rains, Gustav Kirsch, John E. Tucker, Frank Brannock, William J. Gramer, Otto M. Bufe and Clement E. Richardson.[^1] Neither source names a keeper before 1896.[^1][^2]

In the fiscal year ended 30 June 1918, the Commissioner of Lighthouses' report prints "C. E. Richardson, keeper, Round Island Light Station (St. Marys River), Mich." in its table of saving of life and property by Lighthouse Service vessels and employees. The text read here does not say what service he rendered.[^5]

In the fiscal year ended 30 June 1920, the Commissioner's report describes "a change in the location of the Cedar Point Range to Round Island in the St. Marys River."[^6] The Bulletin that year reports that a "new range on Round Island [was] completed and in commission."[^7]

## After the keepers

In the fiscal year ended 30 June 1922, the Commissioner's report says Round Island "has been converted to an unattended acetylene light."[^8] The Bulletin for that year says the "Round Island light changed to acetylene."[^9] The Lighthouse Friends site dates the change of characteristic to 1922, a white flash every two seconds. It says that on 30 June 1923 the light moved from the lantern on the dwelling to a steel tower 65 yards north, and that the station then became unattended.[^2]

The sources differ on the date the station became unattended. The Commissioner's 1922 report already describes the light as unattended, while Lighthouse Friends dates the move to the steel tower, and the station's change to unattended status, to 1923. Neither source says the light was switched off.[^8][^2]

## Today

Lighthouse Friends says the lighthouse is privately owned. It is best viewed from the St. Marys River, and the grounds, dwelling and tower are closed.[^2] The USLHS archive lists the station as not open to the public.[^1] The site dates the third private owner's purchase to around 1998. It says the lighthouse was for sale in 2016 at an asking price of $1,995,000, and was back at that price in 2021.[^2] None of the sources read says who owns it today.
