Pointe Bonaventure Lighthouse is a small wooden tower on the west side of the Bonaventure River, on the Gaspé shore of Chaleur Bay.[^4][^6] It was first lit in 1902 on a point called Pointe Échouerie, moved to Pointe Bonaventure in 1907, and has been dark since 1998.[^1][^4][^6]

Do not confuse it with the Cap-Alright light in Les Îles-de-la-Madeleine, which is also called L'Échouerie and dates from 1928.[^8]

## The place

The 1911 Marine report places the light on Chaleur Bay, under the heading "Bonaventure point, Chaleur bay."[^3] The Canadian Register statement ties the tower to the Canadian government's campaign to improve efficiency and safety for ship traffic on Chaleur Bay.[^5] LighthouseFriends gives the first site's name as Pointe Échouerie, also known as Souris Point.[^4]

## Building the light

The Marine Department's annual report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1902, written in December that year, records a lighthouse "standing 75 feet inside the extremity of this point" that "was put in operation on October 15, 1902."[^1] The report describes a square wooden building with sloping walls, painted white, under an eight-sided red iron lantern.[^1] It gives the height as 10 m (34 ft) to the ventilator, the fixed white light as 15.8 m (52 ft) above high water, and the range as 12 miles, without saying whether the miles are nautical.[^1] The tower was built by day labour at a cost of $1,667.73.[^1]

LighthouseFriends says the lens was a seventh-order dioptric apparatus showing a fixed white light.[^4] The first keeper was Louis Bourdage, paid $60 a year.[^4]

LighthouseFriends says that in 1906 a lighthouse inspector, whom it names as Captain O'Farelle, reported that ships coming from the east in Chaleur Bay could not see the light on Pointe Échouerie.[^4] In spring 1907 the tower was moved some two kilometres south-west to Pointe Bonaventure, at a cost of $440, according to LighthouseFriends.[^4] The Marine report for the year ended 31 March 1908 records the move: the lighthouse "which formerly stood on point Echouerie was moved to the extremity of Bonaventure point." The work "was done by day's labour, under the Quebec agency and cost $440."[^2] The Canadian Register statement also gives 1907 as the year of relocation.[^5]

The light changed again about 1910.[^4] The Marine report for the year ended 31 March 1911 says the fixed white light "has been changed to an occulting white light visible 15 seconds and eclipsed 5 seconds alternately," with fifth-order dioptric apparatus.[^3]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends lists the keepers as follows: Louis Bourdage (1902 to 1903); Pitre Bourdage (1903 to at least 1914), who had been keeper at Point Échouerie before the move; Ovide Bourdage (1914 to 1923); P. B. Bourdage (1923 to 1931); J. N. Babin (1931 to at least 1936); and Theodore Babin (1958 to 1965).[^4] No keeper is listed for 1936 to 1958.[^4] The Bourdage name recurs, but the sources do not say how the keepers were related.

Theodore Babin was the last keeper, serving from 1958 to 1965.[^4] On 31 October 1965 the atomizing oil burner was replaced by an automated electric light, after which no keeper was needed.[^4]

## After the keepers

By 1911 the Department had set up a fixed red light on a 6.1 m (20 ft) pole at the outer end of the Bonaventure River wharf.[^3] It is a separate light from the 1902 tower.[^3]

The tower's cedar shingles were covered with metal siding in 1976.[^4]

The tower was recognized as a federal heritage building in 1996.[^4] The Canadian Register statement calls it "a reminder of the strong sea-going tradition of the Gaspé Peninsula."[^5]

The light was deactivated in 1998.[^4] Rowlett gives the tower as inactive since then.[^6]

In 2005 the tower was moved 4.3 km to a peninsula on the west side of the Bonaventure River, where it is more accessible to the public.[^4] Rowlett says it went to a town-owned park, beach and campground.[^6]

## Today

The town of Bonaventure (Ville de Bonaventure) owns the tower and manages the site.[^4][^6] Rowlett describes the site as open and the tower as open for climbing in season.[^6] LighthouseFriends says the tower is typically open for climbing from 10 a.m. to 7 p.m.[^4]

The Canadian Coast Guard's list of lights shows separate seasonal range lights at Havre de Beaubassin, a front light and a rear light, both showing red.[^7] The front light stands on a 4.6 m skeleton tower and the rear light on a 7.9 m one.[^7] The 1902 tower itself is not lit.[^4][^6]
