Two Harbors Breakwater Light is a red-flashing light at the end of the east breakwater at Agate Bay, in Two Harbors, Minnesota, on Lake Superior.[^5][^6][^7] Lighthouse Friends dates its iron skeleton tower to 1906, and the Coast Guard owns the light.[^5]

## The place

The breakwaters were a harbor project.[^5] Lighthouse Friends says that "a project was adopted to improve the harbor at Agate Bay" in 1887, when the bay had "two elevated iron ore docks, and two merchandise docks."[^5] The lighthouse on the eastern side of the bay, a separate station from the breakwater light, "commenced operation on the eastern side of Agate Bay in 1892."[^5]

## Building the light

On 14 December 1895 a post light went up on the breakwater, from which "a white 8-day lantern light was exhibited." The Light-House Board's report places the post "on the west end of the breakwater," and it records that "Material for a boathouse was purchased."[^1]

In 1897 the Board recorded that the light's color had changed: "Color of light changed from white to red, April 10, 1897."[^2]

Lighthouse Friends says the post was moved in 1902, "300 feet" to "the outer end of the newly constructed ell," and that a Pintsch gas light was "first exhibited on September 10, 1902."[^5] Lighthouse Friends places the 1895 post at the outer end of the eastern breakwater,[^5] while the 1896 report places it "on the west end of the breakwater."[^1] The sources do not settle the first spot.

Four years later, the post was replaced by a tower. Lighthouse Friends quotes the Board's report that the combined light and fog-bell tower "was completed on August 4, 1906." The Board described it as a "square, pyramidal, skeleton iron structure."[^5] The archive gives 1895 as the year the tower was established, and it records the tower as steel.[^6] The sources here do not reconcile the two dates or the two materials.

The archive gives no keepers for the light, and Lighthouse Friends names none.[^5][^6]

## After the keepers

On 22 July 1915 the fog signal was "changed from a bell to an electric siren," Lighthouse Friends says.[^5] By 30 June 1916 the breakwater light was on the Commissioner's list of electric incandescent lights in commission: "Two Harbors Breakwater, Minn."[^3]

A second light, on the west breakwater, was established in fiscal 1917. The Commissioner's report lists "Two Harbors West Breakwater, Lake Superior, Minn." among the flashing acetylene lights set up in that fiscal year.[^4] Lighthouse Friends gives 23 September 1916 for that light.[^5]

In 1941 "an electric diaphragm horn replaced the electric siren," according to Lighthouse Friends.[^5] In 1947 the east breakwater light was "discontinued and removed" while the breakwater was rebuilt, and two light buoys marked the spot until the work was completed in 1950. Then the old light and fog signal were set up again at the outer end of the new east breakwater.[^5]

## Today

The light is owned by the Coast Guard. Lighthouse Friends reports "Grounds open, tower closed."[^5] The archive gives the light as not open to the public.[^6]

Lighthouse Friends says the light "remains in service today, exhibiting a red flash every six seconds."[^5] The Coast Guard's 2025 Light List gives the entry for Two Harbors East Breakwater, LLNR 16505, as Fl R 6s.[^7] The archive, by contrast, marks the light as not an active aid to navigation.[^6]
