Dickinson's Landing Lighthouse marked the upper entrance of the Cornwall Canal on the St. Lawrence River in Ontario, in what is now the South Dundas municipality.[^1][^2] The village that gave the light its name was flooded when the St. Lawrence Seaway was built, and the lighthouse was moved to Upper Canada Village, a heritage park near Morrisburg.[^2]

## The place

The Cornwall Canal was built to bypass the Long Sault Rapids.[^1] It ran along the north side of the river from Cornwall to Dickinson's Landing and lifted boats through locks.[^1] Lighthouse Friends counts six locks; the Lost Villages Historical Society's essay on the village describes seven.[^1][^6] The two sources also disagree on the opening: Lighthouse Friends gives 1843, and the essay says 1842.[^1][^6]

The lighthouse stood at the head of the canal.[^1] The essay, which is written from the memories of people who knew the village, says it was "home to many people until the year of the flood, 1958 A.D."[^6] A light was put up at Dickinson's Landing in 1865 to mark the south side of the upper entrance.[^1] Lighthouse Friends calls it the Cornwall Canal Lighthouse and says it showed a fixed white light.[^1] It was still under the Superintendent of Public Works in 1878, not the Department of Marine.[^1]

## Building the light

The Department of Marine took the light over from the Department of Railways and Canals on 1 July 1890. Lighthouse Friends quotes the Department's 1891 report, which called the old tower "very much out of repair" and had it replaced.[^1] The new tower was built under contract by Wm. H. Miller of Moulinette for $253. The work was finished on 5 October 1891 at a total cost of $326.33.[^1]

The new tower was a square wooden building, painted white, standing 6.7 m (22 ft) above the pier on the south side of the canal's west entrance. Its light was fixed white and dioptric.[^1] Rowlett gives the tower as 7 m (23 ft).[^2]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends says John A. Stoneburner had charge of the light from 1881 until 1906, when Remi Casgrain replaced him. Casgrain's service is given as 1906 to 1909.[^1] The Department of Marine's keeper lists give Stoneburner's appointment as 12 April 1890, which conflicts with the 1881 start in Lighthouse Friends.[^3][^4][^5] The lists for the years ended 30 June 1901 and 1903 name the station "Cornwall Canal, upper entrance"; the list for the year ended 30 June 1904 gives it as "Dickinson Landing".[^3][^4][^5] In 1906 the light's characteristic changed from fixed white to fixed.[^1]

## After the keepers

Through the 1920s the light was maintained by the Department of Railways and Canals. In 1936 control passed to the newly formed Canal Administration of the Department of Transport.[^1] The Seaway works flooded the district, and Rowlett says the St. Lawrence Parks Commission removed the lighthouse, with many other historic buildings, and moved it to Upper Canada Village.[^2] Rowlett describes the light as inactive at least since the 1950s.[^2] None of the sources cited here dates the move to Upper Canada Village.

## Today

The lighthouse stands on the grounds of Upper Canada Village, and the St. Lawrence Parks Commission owns it.[^1][^2] Lighthouse Friends says visitors cannot walk right up to it, and that it has to be viewed from across the small canal. A view is available from a grassy area beside Crysler's Farm Battlefield, and Upper Canada Village, which charges admission, has a canal barge that passes the lighthouse.[^1] Lighthouse Friends and Rowlett both list the grounds as open and the tower closed.[^1][^2]
