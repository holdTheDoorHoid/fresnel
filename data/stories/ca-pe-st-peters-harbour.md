St. Peters Harbour Light is a white wooden tower in the dunes on the west side of the entrance to St. Peters Bay, on the north shore of Prince Edward Island. It marked a fishing harbour that no longer exists in its old form, and it has been moved back from the shore more than once.[^1][^2][^9]

## The place

The Canadian Register of Historic Places traces the community of St. Peters Harbour to 1719, when it was the first French settlement on this shore, and says it grew into a fishing and shipbuilding place in the mid- to late 19th century.[^2] The St. Peters Harbour Lighthouse Society, which now owns the light, says the fishers campaigned in those years for a better channel across the sandbar and for permanent lights to mark it.[^4]

## Building the light

The sources do not agree on when the light began. The Lighthouse Directory gives 1881 for the tower, with the station established in 1865, and the Prince Edward Island Lighthouse Society also says range lights were established in 1865.[^3][^6] Lighthouse Friends says range lights, probably lanterns on masts and shown red, were first placed at the entrance in 1868.[^1] The federal heritage record dates the tower "around 1878".[^9] The Prince Edward Island Lighthouse Society says the exact date is uncertain, but reasons that a square tapered tower of this kind must postdate West Point Lighthouse of 1875, and that no record of a keeper's dwelling exists.[^6]

By Lighthouse Friends' account, Parliament voted $1,600 for range towers at the harbour entrance, and a $550 contract went to James Barclay of Ellerslie; the towers were finished in 1878 at a total cost of $1,031.12.[^1] The front light stood on a newly built breakwater on the west side of the bay.[^4] In 1888 the Department of Marine and Fisheries reported a new inner range tower and ten feet (3 m) added to the height of the old one.[^1]

The society ties the repeated moves of the rear light to the changing channels.[^4] In 1896, according to Lighthouse Friends, the back tower was moved north-westward along the sand beach and the front light was shifted 5.5 m (18 ft) south-westward from the end of the breakwater, work done by a Mr. Sennott for $70.[^1] Temporary range lights replaced the towers in 1921, and the society says the rear lighthouse disappeared some time afterwards, possibly in the October gale of 1923.[^4]

## The keepers

The keepers' list, compiled by Lighthouse Friends and reproduced by the society, runs: Martin McInnis (1868 to 1869), William McGrath (1874 to 1900), Albert Anderson (1900 to 1912), James F. McGrath (1912 to 1931), Stanis LaPerre (1931), William Geldert (1931 to 1955) and Eldon Wilson Drake (1955 to 1963).[^5] The Department of Marine's keepers' table for 1902 lists Albert Anderson at St. Peter's Harbour with an appointment date of 25 July 1900, which matches.[^8]

William Geldert's story is told by a grandson on the society's site. Geldert was born in Lunenburg in 1892 and his family moved to the St. Peters Harbour area in 1906. His grandson writes that William "had the contract to operate the lighthouse" while it was his children who were given the task of lighting it, and says the older ones made the trek from the family's house in Morell.[^5] The page also carries a picture of his pay ledger as keeper for 1931 to 1955.[^5]

Lighthouse Friends names Eldon Drake, a Second World War veteran, as caretaker when the lights were electrified in 1963, and says his annual salary fell from $490 to $150.[^1]

## After the keepers

By the early 1940s, the society writes, a new channel had broken through the Greenwich peninsula and the old one could not be navigated. Red Head Harbour became the usual landing, and the lighthouse site was left to the weather.[^7] The federal heritage record says the light became a warning light in 1951.[^2] In 1965 two skeleton structures served as range lights at the new harbour entrance, with the tower a few hundred metres farther west; the old light was discontinued in spring 2008.[^1]

## Today

The lighthouse is a 10.4 m (34 ft) square, tapered wooden tower with a gallery and a hexagonal lantern.[^2] The government of Canada recognised it as a heritage lighthouse on 19 August 2016, and Lighthouse Friends reports it was transferred in 2017 to the St. Peters Harbour Lighthouse Society.[^9][^1] The register notes that its moves back from the water show the changing coastline.[^2]

The grounds are open and the tower is closed. Lighthouse Friends warns that the end of Lighthouse Road can be too rough to drive, so visitors may have to walk the last part, and Rowlett's directory passes on a reader's suggestion to park at the end of St. Peters Harbour Road and walk about 1 km along the beach.[^1][^3]
