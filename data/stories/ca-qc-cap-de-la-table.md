Cap-de-la-Table Lighthouse, also known in English as Table Head, is a white octagonal concrete tower with a red lantern, near the east end of the north coast of Anticosti Island, Quebec. It was built in 1917 and 1918, and the Government of Canada recognized it as a federal heritage building in 1990.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

## The place

The register says the lights on the north coast of Anticosti made the passage safer for ships taking the shorter route from the Strait of Belle Isle to the mouth of the St. Lawrence.[^2] Before those lights, and one at Petite-Île-au-Marteau in 1915, navigation in the Jacques Cartier Strait was largely confined to the local fishing fleet and to ships bringing supplies to local residents.[^2]

LighthouseFriends says the steamship made the northern route usable for shipping. Before the twentieth century, the route had been seldom used because of its narrowness, irregular soundings and frequent icebergs.[^3] The same page names the steamers Vandalona (1878), Brooklyn (1885) and Melbo (1897) among the many shipwrecks at Baie-du-Renard, a few miles east of the light. The light was established in 1919.[^3]

## Building the light

The Heritage Character Statement says the Department of Marine and Fisheries built the tower in 1917 and 1918. It is about 12 m (40 ft) from base to vane.[^4] The Lighthouse Directory dates the light itself to 1919.[^9]

The Department's report for the fiscal year 1917-18 lists the "erection of lighthouse and fog alarm plant" at Charleton point, Table Head and North point, to be finished the next year.[^5] The report for the fiscal year 1918-19 says new fog-alarm plants were set up at Table head, Anticosti, and that new towers were erected there and at North point.[^6] LighthouseFriends dates the fog alarm plant to 1918.[^3]

The report for the fiscal year 1920-21 says the stations on the north shore of Anticosti at Cap de Rabast, Charleton point and Table head, "built during the war," were "now in permanent operation."[^7] In the fiscal year 1921-22, oil storage tanks were installed at Charleton point and Table head.[^8]

LighthouseFriends says the light, when first lit, showed two flashes, five seconds apart, followed by a ten-second eclipse.[^3]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends lists four keepers at the station: A. Duchesneau from 1919 to 1925, Elzéard Perry from 1925 to at least 1937, Réginald Beck from 1956 to 1961, and Albert Beck from 1961 to 1970.[^3]

## After the keepers

The light was automated in 1970.[^3] The station first included a double dwelling. The Heritage Character Statement says it was demolished in 1959-60 and replaced by two separate residences.[^4] A new fog alarm building went up in 1956, and LighthouseFriends says the fog alarm has since been discontinued.[^3] Those residences were demolished in 2011, after falling into disrepair.[^3][^9]

The station was later leased to a tour company that offered overnight accommodation to tourists.[^9] LighthouseFriends says Safari-Anticosti Outfitters, which controls the site, used to offer accommodation at the station to artists in exchange for works of art done on location.[^3]

## Today

The designation covers only the footprint of the building.[^1]

The sources disagree about who runs the site. The Heritage Character Statement says that in 1989 the Canadian Coast Guard managed the tower, and the Lighthouse Directory names the Coast Guard as owner and site manager.[^4][^9] LighthouseFriends says Safari-Anticosti Outfitters currently controls the site.[^3]

They also disagree on whether the light still shows. The Lighthouse Directory calls it inactive.[^9] LighthouseFriends describes the light as flashing red every six seconds in 2012, with a focal plane of 34 m (112 ft).[^3] The Coast Guard's 2025 Atlantic list for Anticosti includes Cap-de-Rabast and Pointe-Carleton, the two towers LighthouseFriends says were built alongside it to the same design, but it has no entry for Cap-de-la-Table.[^10][^3]

The Lighthouse Directory describes the site as open and the tower as closed.[^9] LighthouseFriends says a road runs 39 km east to the light, branching off shortly before Salmon River Lodge, which the outfitter owns.[^3] It advises checking with Safari-Anticosti Outfitters before using that road.[^3] Ask the outfitter first, and do not go onto the tower.
