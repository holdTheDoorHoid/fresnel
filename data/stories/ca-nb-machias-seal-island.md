Machias Seal Island is a treeless rock in the mouth of the Bay of Fundy, about 19 km (12 miles) from both Cutler, Maine, and Grand Manan, New Brunswick. Both countries have claimed it, and the lighthouse on it has been kept by Canada since 1832.[^1]

## The place

The island lies in the track of vessels bound to and from Saint John and Saint Andrews. In the Canadian federal heritage assessment, the lighthouse is described as the government's answer to growing shipping from those two ports.[^2] Its heritage statement describes an island of granite rock, broken boulders and a shallow layer of peat, with no trees.[^2] Fog was a standing problem: Lighthouse Friends says the island sat in the middle of a shipping channel and was often shrouded in fog in summer.[^1]

The Canadian Register entry for the lighthouse also treats the light as part of the sovereignty story. Because early treaties did not name the island, it says, New Brunswick took ownership of it when it set up the light station in 1832.[^2] The Canadian Register of Historic Places adds that the United States disputed the claim, which was not fully recognized in law until 1925.[^3]

## Building the light

New Brunswick's government allocated 750 pounds in 1831, after repeated petitions from the Saint Andrews Chamber of Commerce, on condition that the light could be told apart from others nearby.[^1] Two octagonal wooden towers and a keeper's dwelling were built in 1832, each tower showing eight lamps in reflectors, so that the pair would not be confused with the revolving light at Gannet Rock or the fixed light at Head Harbour.[^1]

A third-order Fresnel lens was first shown from a new tower on 6 November 1869, contracted to Clarke and Stackhouse for $2,450 because one of the original towers was worn out. The brighter light brought its own trouble: mariners sometimes saw only the stronger of the two lights, or took the weaker for the keeper's house and thought themselves off West Quoddy.[^1] A companion tower contracted to George Armstrong in 1877 never received its first lens, which was destroyed in the fire at the department's Saint John warehouse on 20 June 1877; Lighthouse Friends says it began operating on 1 November 1878 with a replacement.[^1]

The steam fog whistle arrived in 1873 and was, Lighthouse Friends says, the most powerful then on the Bay of Fundy.[^1] Its reach was noticed across the border. In 1894 a US Light-House Board report on fog-signal observations quoted the keeper at Little River light, Maine, who said the Machias Seal Island whistle, to the south of him, was heard best in a northeast snowstorm and plainly in any northeast or north wind, but not with southwest, northwest or west winds.[^4]

The present tower is an octagonal reinforced-concrete structure. Lighthouse Friends and the Grand Manan Archives date it to 1915.[^1][^5] On the old wooden towers they differ: Lighthouse Friends says both were demolished, while the archives say the western one was pulled down in 1905 and the eastern one was cut down to a lower height after 1915.[^1][^5] The federal heritage review says the lighthouse is an early use of reinforced concrete for a lighthouse, a thin shell of classical proportions.[^3] The Department of Marine's report for the year ended March 1915 lists, among that year's works at this station, demolition of old buildings, alteration of the fog-alarm building and a type "G" diaphone replacing the steam whistle; the table is OCR'd and the entries were matched by position.[^6]

## The keepers

John Pendlebury was the first keeper, on a salary of 130 pounds, according to Lighthouse Friends.[^1] The Grand Manan Archives names John Conley as the first, living there with his wife Elizabeth and their family, and says he died on the island about 1844, his body later moved to Saint Andrews; it credits him and his son Obadiah with saving the crews of at least seven shipwrecks.[^5] Lighthouse Friends, by contrast, gives Conley's service as 1837 to 1874.[^1] The sources do not agree on his end date and neither is confirmed here. An 1840 note quoted there calls Conley an excellent pilot with a cow, a garden and "every comfort"; since he often went off to pilot vessels to Saint Andrews, his wife was left in charge of the lights.[^1]

After Conley's years came a quick succession. James Ackroyd ran the fog whistle from 1873; J. H. Crosby resigned after six months because his family would not live on the island; Wright Edmonston left after a year over pay.[^1] The Marine Agent who removed Edmonston and brought in Alexander Eddy reported, in the words Lighthouse Friends prints, a station in "very unsatisfactory condition" with a burnt and leaking boiler.[^1] Later names include Charles Frederick Seely (1883 to 1901), W. Lincoln Harvey (1904 to 1916) and Don C. Daggett (1915 to 1923 in Lighthouse Friends' list).[^1] The archives name a Don Daggett as the man who, after the concrete tower was built, cut the eastern tower down to a lower height so the island would not be mistaken for a two-tower station; keepers and their children called that stub "the castle".[^5] The Department of Marine's 1905 staff list carries the keeper as W. L. Harvey.[^7]

In the 1940s, when Harvey Benson was principal keeper and Lawrence Benson his second, there were eleven buildings on the island.[^5]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

Storms threw drift lumber under the iron-railed tramway that carried coal from the landing to the fog-alarm building, and the line needed repair every couple of years, Lighthouse Friends says.[^1] The department's report for 1920 records that the oil shed was destroyed by fire at this station and rebuilt; the following year's report says piers damaged by seas were repaired and strengthened and the dwelling extensively repaired.[^8][^9]

## After the keepers

Lighthouse Friends reports that the light is automated but that the Department of Foreign Affairs covers the cost of keepers "for sovereignty purposes", pairs being flown in on a 28-day rotation.[^1] The Grand Manan Archives describes the same arrangement, calling it the only manned lighthouse in the Maritime Provinces at the time its note was written.[^5] The island became a bird sanctuary by Canadian order in 1944, and the tower was recoated in 2016 to 2017.[^1]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard List of Lights gives a white flash every 3 seconds from 25 m above the water, on a white octagonal tower with a red top 18.3 m tall.[^10] The lighthouse is a Federal Heritage Building, and five related buildings on the site are listed as contributing to its character.[^3][^2] Lighthouse Friends says the Coast Guard owns the light.[^1] Lighthouse Friends names puffin trips by boat from Cutler, Maine, and Grand Manan, and says the grounds are open for tours.[^1] No source read says the tower can be climbed, and it is a working, staffed station, so visitors should not expect to go up it.
