Dawson Point Light stands on a headland on Hermitage Bay, on the south coast of Newfoundland, beside the site of Pushthrough, a fishing community that was resettled in 1969.[^1][^2][^6] The Newfoundland Lighthouse Department established the light in 1916, and the Canadian Coast Guard still lists it: a white light with a focal height of 16.8 m (55 ft) and a nominal range of 16 nautical miles.[^3][^4]

## The place

Pushthrough had three sections, "the Bottom, Pushthrough proper, and Dawson's Point," and the Pushthrough Gut separated Dawson's Point from the main settlement.[^6] A bridge over the Gut, built in 1872, linked the point to the rest of the community.[^6] The first permanent settlers came about 1812. By 1884, when the population reached 209, the community had an Anglican church and a school. Its population peaked at 247 in 1961 and had fallen to 204 by 1966.[^6]

LighthouseFriends says the name Pushthrough describes the Gut, where tidal currents and surging waters run during southerly gales.[^1] The records for this station do not say what hazard the light was built to mark.

## Building the light

The Lighthouse Inspector's report for 1916, dated St. John's, 25 April 1917, lists Pushthrough among five new lights established that year.[^4] The report says the first four of the new lights were of a new type. For that type the illuminant was acetylene, and each of these lights carried a "sun valve" that let the flame be lit and put out without a keeper.[^4] The report adds that the type was largely used in American and Canadian waters.[^4]

LighthouseFriends says Pushthrough was one of those four.[^1] The report's passage on the four does not name them.

General context, not this station's own history: Putnam's 1917 history of American lights says some acetylene beacons had a sun valve, which cut the gas off automatically while the sun shone.[^5]

LighthouseFriends says the first tower here was a wooden, open-frame structure, and that by 1990 the light was shown from a square pyramidal tower.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory describes the first tower as a wooden skeletal frame. Its successor was a square pyramidal wooden tower, put up at a date the Directory does not give.[^2] The Directory describes the present tower as a 4 m (14 ft) square, cylindrical skeletal tower, partly enclosed by a red and white daymark.[^2][^3]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends says a keeper was stationed on Dawson's Point at some point, and that other buildings were put up around the station. It gives no name or date for that keeper.[^1]

## After the keepers

The Lighthouse Directory says the station was staffed until recently and was scheduled for automation. It gives no date for the change.[^2] Today the point holds a one-storey keeper's house and several utility buildings.[^2]

Pushthrough itself was resettled in 1969.[^6] Blake places the move within a wider government program that ran from 1954 to 1975, when some 300 rural communities in Newfoundland and Labrador were emptied.[^6]

## Today

Dawson Point Light is Canadian Coast Guard property, and LighthouseFriends says it is best seen by boat or from the air.[^1] The Directory says access by land and the station's status are unknown.[^2]

The Coast Guard list gives the light as a white flash of 2 seconds, eclipsed for 6, with the focal height and range above.[^3] The Directory gives a white flash every 5 seconds, and a fog horn that blasts every 20 seconds.[^2] The two disagree on the flash. This story follows the Coast Guard list.
