Wallace Harbour Sector Light is a square, tapering white wooden tower beside Route 6, on the east side of the harbour at Wallace, Nova Scotia.[^1][^2][^7] It was built in 1904 as the front light of a pair of range lights that guided vessels into the harbour.[^1][^3] It has been a sector light since 1990, and since December 2015 the tower has belonged to a local development association.[^2][^7][^8]

## The place

The harbour lies at the mouth of the Wallace River. Two sandy spits, Palmer Point and Caulfield Point, form the way into it.[^2] For a time vessels used it to haul lumber from the mills and stone from the quarries along the river.[^2] The Register of Historic Places says the light also helped the shipping of "the famous Wallace sandstone used for building and ornamentation."[^1]

The entrance was also buoyed. In June 1902 the Department of Marine placed three buoys over Ship bar to mark the entrance; they were to be kept in addition to the eight buoys already marking the channel over Oak Island bar.[^5] The Lighthouse Friends history says an earlier light was built on Mullins Point, northeast of Palmer Point, in 1873, with a companion rear light soon after.[^2] The Lighthouse Directory dates the Mullins Point rear light, later moved to North Wallace, to 1894.[^7] The sources do not say how the two dates fit together.

## Building the light

In 1904 the Department set up a second pair of range lights, this time on Macfarlane Point, on the south side of the harbour.[^2][^3] Its annual report for the year ended 30 June 1904 says they "were put in operation on the 20th October, 1904."[^3] The front tower stands on the north-eastern tip of the point, and the report gives it as "30 feet high from its base to the ventilator on the lantern," with the light "elevated 41 feet above high water mark."[^3] The two lights were fixed red and catoptric, and should be visible about 6.4 km (four miles) in.[^3]

The back tower stood 567 m (1,860 ft) from the front one, on higher ground, 12.2 m (40 ft) high to the ventilator, with its light 30.5 m (100 ft) above high water.[^3] The buildings were put up under contract by John D. Reid, of Head Wallace Bay, for $2,400.[^3]

## The keepers

The first keeper was George Boyle. The Lighthouse Friends history gives his appointment as 24 May 1905, at $150 a year.[^2] The lights had already been shown for seven months by then.[^2][^3] The Department's statement of light-keepers for the year ended 30 June 1905 lists "Boyle, Geo" against the Wallace Harbour Range.[^4]

Boyle served until 1912. The Lighthouse Friends history then gives A. D. Lawson for 1913 and 1914, Robert Langille from 1914 to 1939, and Noble Jamieson from 1940.[^2] Jamieson was the last keeper and stayed in charge until the lights were electrified in 1958.[^2] The sources give no birthplaces or family histories for these men, and name no woman keeper.

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

None of the sources records a shipwreck or storm damage at this light. The trouble they do describe came from trees growing up around the rear light. The Lighthouse Friends history says that several vessels ran aground at the harbour in 1985, and that forty-one people signed a petition in 1986 asking for foliage to be cut back so that the rear light could be seen. The Coast Guard tried to clear it, but the owner of the land was not cooperative. Rather than go to court, it turned the front light into a sector light in 1990.[^2][^6]

## After the keepers

The Coast Guard converted the front light in 1990 by replacing its square lantern with a rectangular one to house a modern light, and it changed the stripes on the seaward face.[^2][^7] The rear tower was sold in 1994 to a private buyer. The Lighthouse Friends history says it was cut into three pieces the next year and moved to Malagash Point, where it was made into a summer home.[^2][^7] The SaltWire report gives a different date for the rear tower: it says the rear range light was taken apart and moved to become a cottage when the front light was converted in 1990.[^8]

Efforts to obtain the front light began in 2012.[^8] The government declared it a heritage lighthouse in February 2015 under the Heritage Lighthouse Protection Act of 2008, which made the transfer possible.[^2][^8] In December 2015 the Wallace Area Development Association bought it for one dollar and received a $10,000 grant to keep it up.[^2][^7][^8] The association looks after the building and its landscaping, while the Coast Guard remains responsible for the beacon in the lantern room.[^1][^2]

## Today

The association owns the tower, and the Coast Guard operates the light.[^7] The Lighthouse Friends page still names the Coast Guard as owner, though its own account of the December 2015 purchase says the association bought the light.[^2] The Lighthouse Directory lists the site as open with the tower closed, and says no parking is provided.[^7] The Lighthouse Friends history says the grounds are open and the tower closed.[^2] The Coast Guard's list shows a fixed light with red, white and green sectors, marks it "Seasonal" and says it "Operates 24 h."[^6] The tower stands next to Route 6, and the Register notes that the road passes very close to the door.[^1]

Visitors should look at the tower from the road and keep off the tower itself. The Lighthouse Directory says the tower is painted white with horizontal red bands on the water side.[^7]
