Wickford Harbor Light was a square wooden lighthouse on Old Gay Rock, at the entrance to Wickford Harbor in North Kingstown, Rhode Island. It was first lit in November 1882, and it was discontinued and demolished in 1930, when an automatic light on a skeleton tower replaced it.[^1][^2]

## The place

Old Gay Rock lay about 200 yards (180 m) offshore from the 1831 lighthouse at Poplar Point.[^2] D'Entremont says ferry traffic between Wickford and Newport was increasing, and that the Lighthouse Board's annual reports for 1878 and 1879 asked for marks at the entrance: "Additional aids to navigation to mark the entrance to Wickford Harbor are required."[^2]

## Building the light

Congress appropriated $45,000 on 15 June 1880 for a lighthouse on Old Gay Rock, D'Entremont says.[^2] By 5 November 1881 the Narragansett Herald reported that "The contractors have begun work on the lighthouse" on "the old Gay rock, at the entrance to Wickford."[^3]

D'Entremont describes the lighthouse as an eight-room wood-frame Gothic Revival house, with a square tower attached to one corner of the dwelling, and a cistern in the basement for rainwater.[^2] The Archive's entry gives the tower as wood and square, 42 feet (13 m) from base to lantern, on an iron pier on riprap, with the keeper's quarters also of wood, built in 1882.[^1]

The light was to show from 1 November 1882. A Lighthouse Board notice printed in the Weekly Visitor in October 1882 said that "on and after November" a "fixed white light of the fifth order will be exhibited from the lighthouse recently constructed at" Old Gay Rock.[^4] D'Entremont says the fifth-order Fresnel lens was first lit on 1 November 1882, and that the old light at Poplar Point was discontinued at the same time.[^2] The Archive gives the fifth-order lens and its installation in 1882.[^1] It gives the light as 50 feet (15 m) above water, where D'Entremont gives 52 feet (16 m).[^1][^2] The two sources differ on that height. A fog bell and striking machinery were installed with the light.[^2]

The Board's repair lists name the station in several years. In the year ended 30 June 1899 the Board listed Wickford Harbor among the stations where "Repairs more or less extensive were made."[^7] It appears in the same kind of list for the years ended 30 June 1896 and 1897.[^5][^6]

## The keepers

The Archive's roster gives three principal keepers, and D'Entremont says the light served for 48 years.[^1][^2] Henry F. Sherman served from 1882 to 1885. D'Entremont says he had kept the Poplar Point light since 1874 and that his stint at Wickford ran to 1886, though the same article says Nathaniel Dodge succeeded him in October 1885.[^1][^2] Dodge served from 1885 to 1893.[^1]

Edward Andrews, whom D'Entremont gives as Edmund Andrews (and says was often reported as Edward), kept the light from 1893 until his retirement in 1930.[^1][^2] He was born in 1868 in Providence, went to sea out of Block Island, and was an assistant keeper at Whale Rock Light by 1891. He eventually became head keeper there before he came to Wickford in 1893, with a wife and one child.[^2] Three more children were born at the Wickford lighthouse, and D'Entremont says the family's son later remembered riding a bicycle in circles around the lighthouse.[^2]

Andrews was recognized for the rescue of a drowning man in 1898, D'Entremont says, and a 1905 inspection found the station in good order.[^2] In 1909 he was offered a transfer to Eatons Neck Light on Long Island, New York, and declined it. He asked instead for a shore station nearer Rhode Island, without a vapor lamp or an assistant keeper.[^2] He was reprimanded in 1918 for the condition of the station, and commended in 1927 for its condition.[^2] In June 1930 he asked to retire, citing his health, and D'Entremont says he retired that year after forty years of service.[^2] The Archive's roster runs his stint from 1893 to 1930, which is thirty-seven years.[^1] The two sources differ on the length of his service, and the dates here follow the Archive.

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

The Commissioner of Lighthouses listed Wickford Harbor in its fiscal 1918 estimates for riprap, asking $3,350 for the station. The report noted that ice that winter had damaged the foundations of many of the third-district stations on the list.[^8]

Andrews's rescue of 1898 is the only one in the sources read that names a rescue at this station.[^2]

## After the keepers

The Archive's entry gives the disposition as "Discontinued and Demolished 1930; Replaced by Skeletal Tower W/375mm Lens."[^1] D'Entremont says the lighthouse was destroyed in 1930 and, to save money, was replaced by a small automatic light with no keeper.[^2]

## Today

D'Entremont says a square skeleton tower with a flashing green light now stands on rocks at the old site.[^2] The Archive lists the station as not open to the public.[^1] Do not land on Old Gay Rock or climb the skeleton tower.
