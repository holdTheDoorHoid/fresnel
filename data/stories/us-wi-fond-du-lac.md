Fond du Lac Light, also called the Lakeside Park Lighthouse, is an eight-sided tower at the entrance to the marina in Lakeside Park, at the southern end of Lake Winnebago in Fond du Lac, Wisconsin.[^2][^4] Donors built it in 1933 at no cost to the city, and the city still owns it.[^2]

## The place

The tower guides boats into the marina.[^1] Before it was built, a plain red light marked the park's entrance, and the new tower was meant to replace it.[^3] The local yacht club paid $200 for the beacon in the lantern room.[^2] The Wisconsin Historical Society says the original beacon still sits on the cupola.[^1]

## Building the light

In 1932 the lumberman W. J. Nuss offered to build a lighthouse in Lakeside Park, using plans from the architect Roger A. Sutherland, and the park board accepted the offer.[^2] Nuss donated the materials, and Sutherland designed the structure.[^1] Construction started in May 1933.[^2][^3] The cornerstone was laid on 10 July 1933, and the top flight of stairs was finished that October.[^2] Frank Russell, the park department's superintendent, supervised the work.[^2] The lower ten feet (3 m) are flagstone, and the upper part is frame.[^2]

LighthouseFriends says the workers were "out-of-work" citizens of Fond du Lac, and that the city paid nothing for the building.[^2] A 2026 local blog says the men were hired through the WPA.[^6] The writer says they read the sign beside the tower, but the blog does not quote it. None of the other sources read mentions the WPA, so the claim is not confirmed here.

The sources disagree on the height. The Wisconsin Historical Society and LighthouseFriends give 40 feet (12 m).[^1][^2] Rowlett's directory gives 56 feet (17 m).[^4]

## After the keepers

The sources name no keeper for the light.[^2]

By 1965 the tower had fallen into disrepair. Park superintendent Carl Jacobs said then, "The first two floors are weakened."[^2] A restoration drive began in 1966, and the city paid most of the $8,000 in repairs.[^3] The tower was rededicated on 3 July 1968.[^2][^3] A plaque near it reads "Saved 1967, Restored 1993."[^2] Rowlett's directory says the tower was restored in 1968 and in 1993.[^4]

In September 2021 the city closed the lighthouse to the public, for water damage and other repairs.[^4][^5] Some wooden studs beneath the siding had rotted, but not so deeply that the structure was of concern, the city manager said later that fall.[^5] The city reopened the lighthouse in 2023 after the repairs, and a ribbon-cutting for the Lakeside Park improvements was set for 29 June.[^5]

## Today

The city owns the lighthouse, and the tower is open to self-guided tours in season. The site is open, and there is parking.[^4] LighthouseFriends says the tower is usually open from Memorial Day to Labor Day, and a wooden staircase inside leads to the observation deck.[^2] The Wisconsin Historical Society's record says the original beacon still lines up with another beacon for boats entering the harbor.[^1]
