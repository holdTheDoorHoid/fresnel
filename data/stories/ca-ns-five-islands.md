Five Islands lighthouse is a wooden tower on Minas Basin in Colchester County, Nova Scotia. It was built in the winter of 1913 to 1914 and lit in 1914. It was deactivated in 1993, and it has been moved four times, three of them to escape the eroding shore.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

## The place

From the lighthouse site, visitors look out over Minas Basin. The Colchester registry says the basin has the highest tides in the world, and that erosion there is considerable every year.[^1][^4] Lighthouse Friends gives a Mi'kmaq account of the islands offshore: the god Glooscap made them by throwing handfuls of sod at a giant beaver whose dam had flooded his medicine garden.[^1]

## Building the light

The lighthouse first stood at Sand Point, on about two acres of land bought from David Corbett.[^1] The Colchester registry gives the same site, just over two acres, and dates the building to the winter of 1913 to 1914.[^4]

The Department of Marine and Fisheries' report for the year ended 31 March 1914 lists the erection of a wooden lighthouse tower at Five Islands, done by contract with A. L. Mury of West Arichat, at $750.[^5] Lighthouse Friends gives the same contractor and price, for a combined wooden lighthouse and dwelling.[^1] The following year's report still carried, under Five Islands, the accounts outstanding from the year before for "the erection of a combined wooden lighthouse and dwelling," at $207.04.[^6]

The kerosene light was first lit in 1914.[^4] The tower was a tapered, square wooden structure, about 10 m (33 ft) high according to the Nova Scotia Lighthouse Preservation Society (Rowlett gives 30 ft), and painted white with red trim.[^2][^3] Its light stood about 13 m (43 ft) above the water.[^3] From 1922 to 1941 a fourth-order dioptric lens showed a fixed white light, seasonally from April to January.[^3]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends says Cyrus A. MacBurnie was hired as the first keeper on 6 April 1914 and served until his death in 1925.[^1] Its roster starts him in 1905, which conflicts with its own text. The Marine Department's 1915 keeper list gives "C. A. McBurnie" at Five Islands, appointed 6 April 1914.[^7] His son John took over the light and served until 1963. Roland Marsh was appointed keeper in 1963 and served until 1967.[^1]

Erosion kept advancing toward the light during the MacBurnie years. The light was first moved back from the eroding edge in 1952, and again five years later, when it was placed as far inland as the property allowed.[^2][^4] The Colchester registry says that about 1963 the oil lamp was taken out and a battery system put in.[^4] The sources differ on when the light changed colour. The registry says that in 1967, when electricity reached Sand Point, it changed from white to red.[^4] The Nova Scotia Lighthouse Preservation Society dates the flashing red light to 1992.[^3]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

Lighthouse Friends records that in the winter of 1995 to 1996 a storm undercut the concrete retaining wall near the lighthouse, and the wall collapsed. The tower was in danger of being lost.[^1] The registry describes the base as washing away after nearly all the land had eroded.[^4]

## After the keepers

The Coast Guard deactivated the light in 1993.[^1][^2][^4] By the time of the 1996 move, the Coast Guard was trying to dispose of it.[^3] In 1996 the county bought the lighthouse and moved it onto the property of Sand Point Campground.[^1][^2] The Colchester registry names the municipality as the buyer.[^4] The Five Islands Lighthouse Preservation Society was formed that year to look after it.[^3][^4] Lighthouse Friends credits the purchase to a community group led by two people, one of them from a family that had minded the light for fifty years.[^1]

The campground was later sold at auction. On 6 November 2008 the tower was moved on a flatbed trailer about seven kilometres to Broderick Lane, the site it stands on now.[^1][^3][^4] The Society received the Craig Harding Lighthouse Preservation Award in 2010.[^3][^4] The lighthouse was registered as a municipal heritage property in July 2013.[^4] Rowlett says the interior was closed in 2019 because the ladder up to the gallery had deteriorated.[^2]

## Today

The lighthouse stands in Five Islands Lighthouse Park at the end of Broderick Lane. It is owned by the Municipality of Colchester and managed by the Five Islands Lighthouse Preservation Society.[^2][^3] The light is no longer operating, but the structure still stands.[^3]

The sources differ on whether the tower can be entered. Lighthouse Friends describes the lighthouse as open on afternoons in July and August, and the tower as open in season.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory describes the site as open and the tower closed, and says the interior has been closed since 2019.[^2] Until that is settled, do not assume the tower can be climbed.
