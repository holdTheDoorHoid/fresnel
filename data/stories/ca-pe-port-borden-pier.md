Port Borden Pier Light stands at the outer end of the old ferry pier at Borden-Carleton, Prince Edward Island. The town of Borden merged with the farming community of Carleton in 1995 to form Borden-Carleton. Ferry service to the port had lasted eighty years when the Confederation Bridge ended it.[^1]

A ferry between Borden and Cape Tormentine, New Brunswick, began in 1917. LighthouseFriends says it carried railway cars across Northumberland Strait.[^1]

The Department's annual report for 1918 lists "Port Borden" under New Aids to Navigation for Prince Edward Island. The entry is for "Erection of range lights and the installation of" reflectors, at a cost of $1,204.97.[^2] LighthouseFriends dates the range lights to 1918, set up to guide the ferry into the port.[^1]

The report for 1921 lists a change at "Fort Bordon" under changes to existing aids: "Position of range lights changed." The report prints "Fort", not "Port". The entry is evidently Port Borden, but the report does not say so.[^3]

The present tower was placed at the outer end of the ferry pier in 1976.[^1] LighthouseFriends describes it as a hexagonal tower 8.5 m (28 ft) high, showing an isophase green light with a four-second period.[^1]

The Canadian Coast Guard's List of Lights gives the light's characteristic as Iso G 4s, and its position as "On outer end of Ferry Pier."[^4] The same entry describes the tower as a "White octagonal tower with red top", 8.5 m high, and marks the light "Seasonal".[^4] The two sources disagree on the shape of the tower. The Coast Guard entry marks the light as seasonal, while LighthouseFriends says the pier light "remains active". The sources do not show which description is current.[^1][^4]

The range lights were decommissioned after the Confederation Bridge opened in 1997.[^1]

The light belongs to the Canadian Coast Guard. LighthouseFriends records the grounds and tower as closed, and says the pier is marked private, although several people were fishing from it when its writers visited.[^1] The lighthouse can be viewed from the Marine/Rail Historic Park.[^1]

No keepers are named in the sources for this light.
