Woods Harbour Lighthouse stands on Big Ledge, a rock covered at high tide, at the southern entrance to Cockerwit Passage on Nova Scotia's South Shore, in Shelburne County.[^4][^5][^1] It was put in operation in 1900 and rebuilt in the early 1960s. The white square tower with a red top that stands now is a 6.3 m (21 ft) replacement of the original.[^5][^4][^6]

## Building the light

The Department of Marine and Fisheries reported that a lighthouse on Big Ledge "was put in operation on September 1, 1900".[^1] Its fixed red light was 8.2 m (27 ft) above high water, from a seventh-order dioptric lens, and the Department said it should be visible eight miles all round the horizon.[^1] The tower was a square wooden building with sloping sides, painted white, under an octagonal iron lantern painted red, on a concrete pier built on the highest part of the ledge.[^1]

The Department says the light was built "by days' labour" under the supervision of Mr. Amos McLellan, at a cost of $1,478.10 (the report prints the decimal point as a comma).[^1][^4] The foundation had to be protected from the sea by a breakwater, which the report says "will also answer as a boat harbour".[^1] A hand fog horn at the station was sounded in answer to signals from ships.[^1]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends names James E. Goodwin as the first keeper. He was appointed on 27 August 1900 and served until April 1930, and his annual salary rose over those years from $200 to $966.[^4] The Department's keepers' salary table for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1910 lists "Woods Harbour Jas. E. Goodwin" at $265 a year.[^2] A keepers' table for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1915 has "Woods harbour" with "J. Goodwin" and an appointment date of 27 August 1900.[^3]

Lighthouse Friends gives Marsden Chetwynd as keeper from 1930 to 1953.[^4]

## After the keepers

NSLPS says the original tower was replaced between 1962 and 1965, and Lighthouse Friends says the present tower was built around 1963.[^5][^4] NSLPS says the light was automated by 1993.[^5]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the light. Lighthouse Friends says the grounds and tower are closed, and NSLPS gives the site access as by boat.[^4][^5] The Coast Guard list gives the light as a flash of two seconds with four seconds of darkness in each six-second cycle, visible 11 nautical miles.[^6] Lighthouse Friends describes the characteristic differently, as a one-second flash with five seconds of darkness.[^4] The light is easily seen from the shore, and Lighthouse Friends says it stands 800 m (2,625 ft) from the wharf at Lower Woods Harbour.[^4]
