Conche Light stands on Silver Point at the entrance to Conche Harbour, on the east coast of the Great Northern Peninsula.[^1] Its first tower, a square, pyramidal wooden tower put up in 1914, still stands.[^1] The light the Coast Guard shows now is on a newer tower that replaced the wooden one in 1992.[^1][^2]

Lighthouse Friends says James Herbert Dower arrived in 1816, and he and his crew went back to England the next year to fetch their families.[^1] By 1857 Conche was a thriving fishing settlement, with British settlers working peacefully alongside seasonal French fishermen.[^1] Permanent settlement on the French Shore was not permitted until 1904, though some men overwintered there anyway.[^1] When the French left the shore in 1904, the Newfoundland government sent Jack Fitzgerald to Conche as its first English magistrate.[^1]

In June 1860 John Dower, a son of James Herbert, saved three French seamen from drowning.[^1] The French government gave him a gold medal in 1861; it is now shown at the French Shore Interpretation Centre in Conche.[^1] The rescue came fifty-four years before the tower, and no source ties it to the light.

After its first year, the wooden tower's fixed white light was changed to an occulting white, seven seconds shown and three hidden.[^1] For many years a single red band round the white tower served as a daymark; today the tower is all white.[^1]

The light was converted to battery power in 1977.[^1] In 1992 the Canadian Coast Guard erected a new aluminium-framed tower farther out on the reef, in place of the wooden one.[^1] The Coast Guard's list now gives Conche Light as a square skeleton tower with a red and white daymark, 6.7 m high, flashing red every five seconds, with a focal height of 7.2 m and a range of four nautical miles. The list also marks the light as seasonal.[^2]

Lighthouse Friends names two keepers: Matthew McGrath, at least 1939, and Cyril Clements, with no years given.[^1] It names no keepers before 1939.[^1]

The Town of Conche owns and maintains the old wooden lighthouse.[^1] Lighthouse Friends reports the grounds open and the tower closed.[^1] Route 434, a 23 km dirt road, ends near the lighthouse.[^1]
