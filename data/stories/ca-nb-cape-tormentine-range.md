The Cape Tormentine range lights are two lights at Cape Tormentine, on the Northumberland Strait in Westmorland County, New Brunswick: a front light at the outer end of the Outer Wharf and a rear light on land nearby.[^1][^6] They were set up to guide the steamer Stanley on winter passages to Prince Edward Island.[^3]

## The place

Cape Tormentine is the easternmost point of New Brunswick. It sits where the Northumberland Strait is narrowest, between the New Brunswick shore and Cape Traverse on Prince Edward Island.[^2] In 1868 three spots were looked at as sites for a lighthouse. Cape Jourimain was chosen, and a light went into operation there in 1870.[^2]

Winter was the difficulty. When Prince Edward Island joined Canada in 1873, the Dominion agreed to cover the cost of "steam service for the conveyance of mails and passengers" between the island and the mainland, "winter and summer."[^2] The strait was often packed with ice. Iceboats carried passengers and mail between Cape Tormentine and Cape Traverse. Where the ice covered the water, the crew and some male passengers pulled them across.[^2] The New Brunswick and Prince Edward Island Railway reached Cape Tormentine in 1886.[^2]

Iceboats still crossed the ice in the 1905 season. The Department's report says, "Six boats left Cape Traverse and six left Cape Tormentine each morning, when practicable."[^7]

The Deputy Minister's report on a trip of inspection with the Minister, printed in the 1903 report, says the pier "is exposed at the outer end," and that a steamer could land or load cargo there "only under favourable ice conditions."[^4]

## Building the light

The Department of Marine's report for the year ended 30 June 1902 says that "Range lights, with a fog-bell, have been established on this pier, to guide the steamer Stanley while making winter passages between that point and Prince Edward Island." The lights were put in operation "whenever the Stanley is running," and discontinued "whenever the Stanley is not running."[^3]

The front light was fixed red and shown from a window in the eastern gable of a new freight shed on the railway pier. The report puts it 5.2 m (17 ft) above high water, visible eight miles.[^3] The back light was shown from the cupola of an iceboat house at the inner end of the pier. The report puts it 777 m (2,550 ft) from the front light, 10.4 m (34 ft) above high water, and visible nine miles.[^3] A large bell "rung by hand as a signal to the Stanley in thick weather" hung on a small building on the pier.[^3]

Rowlett's directory gives 1901 as the year the station was established.[^1] LighthouseFriends says the range lights were set up in 1902 by Artemis Lord, the Marine Department's agent at Charlottetown.[^2] The report for 1902 describes them as already established, and does not give a month.[^3]

The lights were changed several times. From 1906 they were kept lit all year, not only when the Stanley ran.[^2] In 1907 "a new tower for the rear range light was constructed atop a bank," inland from the iceboat house. In 1908 the front mast was replaced by a 9.8 m (32 ft) tower with a fourth-order lens.[^2] Both towers were raised in 1919.[^2] The present front light, a short pepperpot tower, was put in place in the 1940s, according to LighthouseFriends. Rowlett gives it as an 11 m (33 ft) tower.[^2][^1] Rowlett describes the rear tower as a "square pyramidal wood tower."[^1]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends lists four keepers. J.R. Barry served from 1906 to 1912. John Leslie Wall followed from 1912 to 1939. Belford Allen kept the light from 1939 to 1940, and Courtney Thompson Wall from 1940 to 1959.[^2] The Department's keeper list for the year ended 30 June 1907 has an entry for "Barry, J. R" at "Cape Tormentine, N.B.", with an appointment date of 26 March 1906.[^5] That date fits the 1906 start LighthouseFriends gives, but the list does not on its own show that the other three keepers served.

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

LighthouseFriends says the winter of 1903 was unusually harsh, and the Stanley was icebound from 13 January to 17 March.[^2] The Department's report for 1903 says the Stanley left Summerside on the morning of 12 January 1903 and was caught in the ice off Sea Cow.[^4]

## After the keepers

LighthouseFriends says the railcar ferry to Borden-Carleton, on Prince Edward Island, began serving the short crossing in 1917. A new set of range lights, the Cape Tormentine Entrance range lights, was set up nearby that year to help the ferries find the harbour entrance. That second set is a separate station and is not covered here.[^2]

After the bridge to Prince Edward Island opened in 1997, the ferry stopped. LighthouseFriends says the range lights stopped too: "so were the Cape Tormentine Pier Range Lights."[^2] Rowlett says the range was discontinued when the Confederation Bridge opened in 1997.[^1]

Rowlett's directory gives the front light as inactive from 1997 to 1998.[^1] LighthouseFriends says that at the request of local fishermen, the front light was reactivated in 1998.[^2]

Rowlett describes the rear light as "Abandoned with the end of the ferry service in 1997." It says the lantern later collapsed. In 2016 ownership of the rear light passed to the Cape Tormentine Community Development Corporation, and restoration began in 2017.[^1]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard list gives the front light as "Iso R 2s," with a focal height of 12.0 metres, at the outer end of the pier, and marks it "Seasonal."[^6] LighthouseFriends says the Coast Guard maintains the front light, while the rear tower has "greatly deteriorated."[^2] Rowlett says the front light has been inactive since 2022, when Hurricane Fiona heavily damaged it. Rowlett calls it out of service and gravely endangered.[^1] The sources disagree about whether it is lit today.

The Coast Guard owns the front light, and LighthouseFriends says its grounds and tower are closed.[^2] The tower is closed to visitors. The sources differ on the grounds. Rowlett says the site is open and the tower closed, and that the wharf "is in poor condition and does not appear very safe for walking."[^1] Rowlett says a company fenced the wharf in 2004, cutting off public access, and that access has since been reported restored. LighthouseFriends describes a fence across the last part of the wharf.[^1][^2] LighthouseFriends says the Cape Tormentine Community Development Corporation has been seeking to assume ownership of the range lights.[^2]
