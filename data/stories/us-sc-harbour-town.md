Harbour Town Lighthouse is a red-and-white striped tower in the marina at Harbour Town, in the Sea Pines Resort on Hilton Head Island, South Carolina. It was built in 1969 and 1970 for the resort's developer, Charles Fraser, and it is owned by the Sea Pines Resort.[^1][^2]

## The place

After the Civil War the island stayed quiet and remote through the Great Depression. Hunting, farming and lumbering kept its population low until the James F. Byrnes Bridge, a two-lane toll swing bridge, was built in 1956.[^1]

Charles Fraser then bought his father's interest in The Hilton Head Company and set out to build Sea Pines, whose plan allowed no building taller than the tallest tree.[^1] At the heart of the resort Fraser envisioned a harbour village, and his design team flew over small Mediterranean harbours "so they could capture the unique coastal charm of places like Portofino," LighthouseFriends reports.[^1]

The tower stands in the marina near the southern end of the island.[^1] The Light List, which gives more detail on navigation aids than charts can show, names four private lights, numbered 1 to 4, under the heading Harbour Town in its 2011 edition. The tower itself is not listed under its own name there.[^6]

## Building the light

Construction began in 1969 and was completed in the spring of 1970, LighthouseFriends says.[^1] The Hilton Head Sun reports that the tower was built by William and John Whalley and financed by Fraser.[^2] Those who doubted that it would draw boaters off the Intracoastal Waterway called it "Fraser's Folly," LighthouseFriends says.[^1] The Sun says some residents at first mocked it by that name.[^2]

The owner's own history describes the tower as hexagonal, 90 feet (27 m) tall, with a stucco exterior over a steel frame and 104 steps to the top.[^3] LighthouseFriends describes it as octagonal.[^1] The island's visitor website gives the height as 90 feet (27 m) and the stair count as 114 steps.[^4] Clio also gives 114 steps.[^5]

The visitor website says Fraser chose the candy-stripe design because he wanted it to be "fun and memorable," and that during construction his brother Joe, seeing the red steel girders inside, settled on red for the stripes.[^4]

LighthouseFriends says the tower was "built as more of an aid to commercialization than an aid to navigation," although it is listed on navigational charts.[^1] The Clio history guide calls it "a tourist attraction" rather than an aid to navigation.[^5] LighthouseFriends gives the light as a white flash with a period of thirty seconds, visible for fifteen miles.[^1]

## Today

LighthouseFriends says the tower is owned by the Sea Pines Resort, and that visitors pay a small fee to climb it.[^1] The stairs carry information panels on the island's history, and the top offers views over Calibogue Sound and the golf course.[^1] The two sources give different hours: LighthouseFriends says the tower opens daily at 10 a.m. and closes at sunset, and Clio lists 10 AM to 7:30 PM.[^1][^5]

Roughly $200,000 was spent on renovating the tower in 2001 and 2002, and a small admission fee has been charged since then.[^1] In March 2019 a 16-foot (5 m) replica of the USCGC Eagle, the Coast Guard Academy's tall ship, was officially dedicated, on 24 March. Its installation completed the Coast Guard exhibit in the lighthouse.[^2] In March 2022 a replica fourth-order Fresnel lens, the work of Dan Spinella of Artworks Florida, was installed atop the tower.[^1]

None of the sources names a keeper, and none ties a wreck or rescue to this tower.
