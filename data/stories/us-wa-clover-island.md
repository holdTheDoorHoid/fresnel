Clover Island Light is a 62-foot (19 m) tower on Clover Island, in the Columbia River at Kennewick, Washington.[^1][^5] The Port of Kennewick owns the light and had it built in 2010, as part of a $1.12 million project to develop the island as a park.[^4][^5] Its structural engineers called it the first lighthouse built in the United States in almost 50 years.[^3]

## The place

The island has shrunk. The Port says the island originally covered 162 acres and spans 16 today. It blames the McNary Dam and its levee, which covered most of the island and held back the original shoreline. The pond behind the levee is the old river front.[^1] Rowlett's directory notes that the river is navigable to Kennewick and Richland.[^5] None of the sources says what hazard, if any, the light was set to mark.

## Building the light

DCI Engineers provided the structural design.[^3] The base is 44 feet (13 m) tall, and the steel cupola on top, which holds the beacon, is 18 feet (5.5 m) tall.[^3] HDJ Design Group was the architect, and Mountain States was the contractor.[^4]

The sources disagree about how the base was made. The Port calls it four pre-cast concrete sections.[^1] Mountain States says its own crew built the panel forms in its yard and carried them to the jobsite, so the panels could be poured on site. It gives each panel as weighing 52,000 pounds.[^4] DCI's engineers describe on-site casting in wood forms and tilt-up construction.[^8]

In the first week of January 2010 workers hoisted the 10-foot (3 m) lantern room onto the platform.[^2] The Associated Press, citing a Port official, reported that the structure was almost complete, that crews still had to install staircases, windows and doors, and that it would start functioning the next month.[^2] The Port gives May 2010 as the construction date, and Rowlett says construction ran from September 2009 to May 2010.[^1][^5] The sources therefore disagree about when the light was ready. The AP expected it to start working in February 2010, while the Port and Rowlett put completion in May.

## Today

The Port calls it a Coast Guard-approved private aid to navigation, and the ARLHS register lists it as a private aid.[^1][^6] The Lighthouse Society's archive calls it a Minor Aid to Navigation.[^7] DCI's project page calls it a Coast Guard lighthouse.[^3] It shows a white flash every four seconds, and Rowlett gives the focal plane as about 70 feet (21 m).[^1][^5] The gallery on top, which DCI's engineers called the "Birdcage", has a stainless steel dome and frame enclosed in glass, and holds a small solar-powered LED beacon.[^8] The ARLHS register describes a 35-foot (11 m) black and tan conical tower.[^6] That does not match the 62-foot concrete tower in the Port's, Mountain States', DCI's and Rowlett's accounts.[^1][^4][^3][^5]

Today the tower stands in a plaza of about 3,700 square feet (340 m²).[^1] The Port describes the plaza as a place for sunsets, weddings and other events.[^1] A causeway reaches the island from North Washington Street in Kennewick.[^5] Rowlett's directory says the site is open and the tower closed.[^5] The U.S. Lighthouse Society's archive record marks the site "Open to Public: No".[^7]
