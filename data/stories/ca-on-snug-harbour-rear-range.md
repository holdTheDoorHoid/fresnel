The Snug Harbour Rear Range Lighthouse stands on the southern tip of Snug Island in Georgian Bay, Ontario. It marks the entrance to Snug Harbour and the channel to Parry Sound. The township's account says it was built in 1893 and was operating the next year. LighthouseFriends describes a square tower, white with a red upper section, rising from the roof of a dwelling. The federal government designated the lighthouse a heritage lighthouse in 2016.[^5][^1]

## The place

The shore here is broken up. A Royal Navy survey of 1891, as LighthouseFriends quotes it, counted "upwards of 4,000 islands and dry rocks" along this part of the bay's north-east coast.[^1] The survey said more aids were needed before vessels of large draught could safely use Parry Sound, and it recommended five new range lights. At that time the only light in the approach was on Red Rock.[^1] The Department of Marine's annual report published in 1906 still describes the Red Rock light as lying "in the approach to Parry sound."[^7]

The Department followed the survey's advice. Walton Rock and Snug Island were paired as the Snug Island range, with Walton Rock as the front light and Snug Island as the rear. Mariners would pass south of Red Rock and align on the range.[^1]

## Building the light

Charles Mickler of Collingwood, who had built other range lights in 1889, won the contract for the five lights, worth $3,165. He did the work in the 1893 season.[^1] The Department's assistant engineer, C. F. Cox, found "so many defects in the work" that the Department would not accept the buildings or pay for them until the work was put right.[^1] The Department then had its own foreman of works, W. H. Noble, finish the job. That extra work "cost $647.40" and was charged against Mickler's contract price.[^1]

The lights were "placed in operation on September 29, 1894," with Charles L. White as first keeper of the Snug Harbour range.[^1] The township dates the building to 1893 and its operation to the following year.[^5] LighthouseFriends says the rear light was erected in 1894.[^1] The association says the lighthouse "was built in 1894."[^6] The sources differ over the building year, and the records do not settle it.

In 1902, on the opening of navigation, the Department painted the Snug Harbour range buildings white, with the Jones Island pair. The annual report says this was done "to render them more conspicuous against the backgrounds," which it describes as usually dark.[^2]

## The keepers

Charles L. White was the first keeper, from 1894 to 1902.[^1] John A. Richmond followed from 1902 to 1907. The Department's list of lightkeepers, in the report published in 1907, names him against "Snug Harbour Range."[^1][^4]

LighthouseFriends lists Adam Brown as head keeper from 1908 to 1930, and calls him "the long-serving keeper of nearby Red Rock Lighthouse."[^1] The Department's statement of lightkeepers, in the report published in 1910, lists "Snug Harbour" with Adam Brown as keeper and gives his appointment as "April 11, 1900." The same statement lists Red Rock with the note "(see Snug Harbour)," so one keeper is shown at both lights.[^3] The sources do not agree on when Brown's service began. The 1900 date does not fit the White and Richmond years that LighthouseFriends and the 1907 list give for Snug Harbour, and the records do not settle it.

From 1930 the range lights were looked after by Dan Boterell, hired that year to do the work.[^1] LighthouseFriends says he rigged a mirror in the lantern room, so he could tell from the house whether the light was burning. He also rigged a snuffer that could be pulled on a string to put the light out in the morning.[^1] The same page lists E. Scott, Gus Olson and Tom Flynn as keepers, without dates.[^1]

## After the keepers

The front light changed from fixed red to fixed white in 1915. In 1959 both range lights took their present fixed green character.[^1] The township says the station had a live-in keeper "Until 1977."[^5] The association says a keeper stayed until the mid-1970s, and dates the electric lighting to "Around 1956," with a small backup generator building built nearby.[^6]

The Coast Guard later used the station as a "search and rescue station for Central Georgian Bay," staffed by cadets.[^6] A radio tower more than 40 metres tall went up around 1970 and was removed in 1996.[^6] "For several decades now the lighthouse has been unattended and largely neglected," the association says.[^6]

In 2016 the federal government designated the lighthouse a heritage site.[^5] The association says Carling Township "decided to assume ownership" that same year, and received $90,000 from the Department of Fisheries and Oceans for future repairs.[^6] The township says ownership passed to it the following year.[^5] The two accounts differ by one year.

## Today

The Township of Carling "has taken possession of the Snug Harbour Lighthouse" and is developing a management plan.[^5] LighthouseFriends says the Canadian Coast Guard owns the lighthouse, which does not match the township's account.[^1] A full restoration of the interior, to "a close facsimile of an 1894 Georgian Bay Lighthouse," is "still years away." For now, the township says, it is "concentrating on the grounds and exterior of the building."[^5]

Local volunteers have helped. The association's "Friends of the Lighthouse" group painted the shutters over the plywood-covered ground-floor windows to appear like windows again, with the township's approval. It also repainted the generator building to match the tower.[^6]

LighthouseFriends says the lighthouse is best seen by boat, and that the grounds and tower are closed.[^1]
