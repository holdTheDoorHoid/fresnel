Blue Hill Bay Light stands on Green Island, at the western entrance to Blue Hill Bay on the Maine coast, a few miles from the village of Brooklin.[^1][^2] Its white cylindrical tower first showed a light in the 1850s, and the light was discontinued in the 1930s, when an automated light on a skeletal tower took over.[^1][^2] The island and the lighthouse are now privately owned.[^2]

## The place

Green Island sits on the west side of the entrance to Blue Hill Bay, the bay for which the light station is named.[^2] At low tide rock ledges link it with Flye Island, and LighthouseFriends says those ledges were a hazard to shipping.[^2] The federal government bought the island in 1855 from Abraham Flye for $150.[^2] LighthouseFriends reports that Ellsworth, on the bay, was reputed to be the second-busiest lumber port in the world in the mid-nineteenth century, and that the light was built to help guide the ship traffic that served it.[^2]

## Building the light

LighthouseFriends says the tower and its attached dwelling were built in 1856.[^2] The USLHS record gives 1857 for the tower and for its original lens, and 1856 for the keepers' quarters.[^1] The sources differ by a year, and neither gives a reason.

The first lens was a fourth-order Fresnel, and by 1863 a fifth-order lens was in the lantern.[^2] The Lighthouse Board recorded significant work on the dwelling and boathouse in 1881, and a two-room addition followed in 1890.[^2] A brick oil house was added in 1905.[^2]

## The keepers

The USLHS roster lists twelve principal keepers, from Moses T. Graves (1856 to 1861) to Justin A. Foss (1932 to 1934).[^1] Between them were men such as Isaac Smith (1861 to 1870) and Roscoe M. Chandler (1924 to 1932).[^1]

LighthouseFriends says Chandler kept two cows on Flye Island. In spring they were walked across the exposed rock at low tide to graze through the summer.[^2] The children often took the dory to the island, where they followed the sound of the cowbells. During thunderstorms the cows often went into the bay.[^2] LighthouseFriends also says that during the 1920s a family living at a lighthouse like this one was given a yearly supply of 500 gallons of kerosene and nine tons of coal.[^2]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

The Lighthouse Board's report for fiscal 1901 records that "A bell struck by machinery September 15, 1900" was set up at the station.[^3] LighthouseFriends describes a 1,000-pound bell that struck a single and then a double blow at twenty-second intervals, and says the keeper had to wind its weight every five hours in foggy weather.[^2]

LighthouseFriends also tells of a foggy day when Chandler heard four blows of a whistle close by, which signals a vessel in trouble. He found a skipper who had turned too soon and was lost rather than in danger. He shouted directions to the ship, then found his own way back to the station by the sound of the fog bell.[^2]

On 11 October 1928 a vessel ran aground near the light, and Chandler helped free her. LighthouseFriends says that, because there was liquor aboard during Prohibition, the matter went to the sheriff.[^2] The Lighthouse Service Bulletin's 1924-1929 index lists "Chandler, Roscoe, keeper Bluehill Bay, assisted vessel" among its entries.[^4]

## After the keepers

The sources disagree about when the light was discontinued. The USLHS record gives 1933.[^1] LighthouseFriends gives 1 May 1934, the day the old light was deactivated and replaced by an automated light on a skeletal tower.[^2]

The lighthouse was bought in 1976 by a private owner, who restored it as a home and installed a long floating walkway to a deepwater mooring.[^2] The property changed hands again in 1995. It was put on the market in 2015 and sold in 2018 for $460,000.[^2] The skeletal tower from which the light was shown toppled over in 2016, and the Coast Guard promptly replaced it with a light on a post.[^2]

## Today

The island and lighthouse are privately owned, and the grounds, dwelling and tower are closed.[^2] LighthouseFriends says the best views are from the water or the air.[^2] It also says the walk out to the island at low tide, about 45 minutes, is risky and not advised.[^2] A boat operator has offered a cruise that passes the light.[^2]
