Black Bear Island Light was a square wooden tower on the east end of Black Bear Island, in the Narrows of Lake Winnipeg, built in 1898 to mark the northern entrance to the Narrows.[^1][^2] The tower has stood on the grounds of the Marine Museum of Manitoba in Selkirk since 1989.[^2][^1] By 2020 a mast on the island was being used to display a flashing white light for mariners.[^1]

## The place

Lake Winnipeg is 416 km (258 miles) long and, Lighthouse Friends says, the eleventh-largest freshwater lake in the world.[^1] Near its middle the lake narrows to about two kilometres between West Doghead Point and East Doghead Point.[^1] Black Bear Island lies just north of that narrows, known as The Narrows.[^1] The Selkirk Museum's history says that Lake Winnipeg can be dangerous.[^2]

## Building the light

In 1898 the Department of Marine announced two new lighthouses on the lake, one of them on Black Bear Island, to accommodate "the increasing steamboat traffic on Lake Winnipeg."[^1][^2] Both towers were square wooden buildings with sloping walls and square lanterns, all painted white.[^1] John W. Scott of Selkirk built them, and his contract price was $405 for the Black Bear Island tower.[^1][^2] The fixed white lights were put in operation in the autumn.[^1]

The Marine Museum of Manitoba's history gives another account. It says the 1898 tower was "built by keeper Danial Matheson on Black Island in Lake Winnipeg."[^4] The Lighthouse Friends and Selkirk accounts both name Scott as the builder, and both name Matheson as the keeper hired in 1899. Only the museum's account credits him with building the tower. This story follows the two accounts that name the contractor.[^1][^2]

## The keepers

Daniel Matheson was hired as the first keeper in 1899, at a salary of $150 a year.[^1][^2] The keeper list in the Department's 1901 report gives "Matheson, Daniel Black Bear Island, Manitoba."[^5] The Department's report for fiscal 1913-14 lists Matheson, appointed on 22 June 1899, against Black Bear island in its keepers' table for Manitoba.[^6]

The Department's 1911 report lists "Black Bear island, Man." with a hand fog horn, and the Selkirk Museum dates the horn to 1910.[^7][^2] Matheson was paid an extra sum each year to run it.[^1] Lighthouse Friends gives 1919 as his last year, when his salary was $405 a year.[^1]

A. Coullard kept the light from 1919 to 1920. J.A. McLennan was keeper from 1920, and Selkirk's account has him there until at least 1937.[^1][^2]

## After the keepers

The Department's reports list repairs at the station in 1906 and again in 1907.[^8][^9] Selkirk's history gives 1989 as the year of the move, and lists the *Selkirk Journal* of 23 May 1989 among its sources.[^2] The Lighthouse Directory says the tower was relocated from the island to the museum, without a date.[^3] The ARLHS World List of Lights classes the original structure as moved from its aid to navigation location, and lists the museum tower under its own number.[^10]

In 2020 a cylindrical mast on the east end of Black Bear Island was being used to display a flashing white light for mariners.[^1]

## Today

The tower stands at the Marine Museum of Manitoba, at 490 Eveline, Selkirk. Lighthouse Friends says the grounds are open and the tower is closed.[^4][^1] The museum was established in 1972-1973 in Selkirk. Its grounds also hold a second lighthouse, which the museum's history says was built in 1914 at the mouth of the Red River.[^4]
