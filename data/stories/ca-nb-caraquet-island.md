Caraquet Island Light stands on the west end of a small island off the Acadian Peninsula of New Brunswick, where the island protects the approach to Caraquet harbour.[^1][^2] Its first keeper drowned less than three years after the light first showed, on the crossing from the mainland.[^1][^2][^4]

## The place

The island is about 2.3 km long and roughly 400 m wide. It runs parallel to the coast near Middle Caraquet.[^1]

## Building the light

Daly and Carter of Miramichi put up the first station in 1870 for $548. It was a combined lighthouse and dwelling: a one-and-a-half-storey house with a square tower rising from one end of its pitched roof.[^1]

Thomas Kerr was appointed keeper at $200 a year and first showed the fixed white light on 26 August 1870. Its elevation was 13 m (43 ft), and LighthouseFriends says that in clear weather it could be seen for 22 km.[^1] In 1885 the illuminating apparatus was changed to a dioptric light, and a storeroom and a well were built the same year.[^1]

## The keepers

Thomas Kerr kept the light until he drowned in June 1873. His wife, Mary, took over responsibility for it for the rest of that shipping season.[^1]

The keepers who followed were Narcisse Porlier (1874 to 1876), Louis Porlier (1876 to 1882), Joseph Porlier (1882 to 1888) and Gervais Lantaigne (1888 to 1924).[^1] The Marine department's keeper list for 1903 pairs Lantaigne's name with Caraquet Island.[^5] Its table for the year ending March 1904 gives 16 June 1888 as the date of his appointment.[^6]

In 1874 Inspector Mitchell described Narcisse Porlier as "a very efficient officer".[^1] In 1875 the inspector found that no government boat had been provided. He wrote that one was "much required", because the light stood on an island and the island was "such a resort of fishermen", according to LighthouseFriends, which quotes his report.[^1]

Later keepers included Amedee Robert, from 1925 to at least 1937. André V. Gionet served to 1947, though no start date is given, and Guillaume Gionet kept the light from 1947 to at least 1953.[^1]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

On 14 October 1872 Archibald Kerr, the keeper's son, drowned near the spot where his father later drowned.[^4] A search for the bodies, reported in October 1872, found no trace of them.[^4]

On Friday 13 June 1873 Thomas Kerr crossed from the mainland to mail returns and papers connected with the light, and was returning to his duties. Between three and four in the afternoon his boat, which the Union Advocate called "a small leaky flat," upset and he drowned.[^4] The Union Advocate of Newcastle, New Brunswick, reported it on 25 June 1873. The Saint John Daily Telegraph had reported on 23 June that the body had not yet been found.[^4] While dragging for the body the next day, the searchers found the remains of Archibald, who had drowned about the same place eight months earlier.[^4] A coroner viewed Thomas Kerr's body on 27 June.[^4] Both newspaper accounts survive only as transcripts on Find a Grave.[^4]

In October 1876 a gale capsized the boat in which Narcisse Porlier and another son were fishing just off the island. Narcisse drowned, and Louis Porlier was appointed keeper in his place. His widow had been caring for the light, though Louis was the official keeper.[^1]

## Today

The Canadian Coast Guard owns the light.[^1] Its list gives the light as a white flash every six seconds, with a focal height of 22.3 m (73 ft) and a nominal range of 9 nautical miles. The tower is a skeleton tower with a red enclosed top and a white slatwork daymark, and the list shows it operating year round. The list calls the island Île de Caraquet.[^3] Rowlett's directory gives the flash as every four seconds, so the two sources disagree.[^2]

The present skeleton tower replaced the original around the 1950s.[^1][^2] Rowlett says the island is reached only by boat, and that the site is open while the tower is closed.[^2] LighthouseFriends says a distant view can be had from the mainland, but that the light is best seen by boat.[^1] It also says the island's name was officially changed to Île de Caraquet in 2009.[^1]
