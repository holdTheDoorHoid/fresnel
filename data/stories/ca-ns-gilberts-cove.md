Gilbert's Cove Lighthouse stands on the east end of Gilbert Point, on St. Mary's Bay in Digby County, Nova Scotia. It is a square white wooden house with a lantern on its roof, first lit on 18 August 1904, and members of one family kept the light in it from 1904 until 1972.[^1][^3][^2] The house is now a museum, tearoom and gift shop run by a local historical society.[^3]

## The place

The light stands on the east side of St. Mary's Bay, and the cove and point take their name from Colonel Thomas Gilbert, a Loyalist who settled there with his three sons and their families.[^1][^3][^2] From the early 1800s into the early 1900s the trade of the bay was mostly wood, carried in small schooners, called coasters, most of them built locally, that ran up and down the bay and into the cove.[^3][^2]

The *Digby Weekly Courier* of 26 August 1904, as quoted by the Nova Scotia Lighthouse Preservation Society, reported that the captains of the vessels running up the bay said the light "should have been erected at least twenty years ago."[^3]

## Building the light

The Department of Marine and Fisheries wrote its annual report for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1904 in December of that year. The report says that a light "was first shown on the 18th August, 1904" from a new lighthouse on the east end of Gilbert Point.[^1]

The building was a square wooden dwelling painted white, with a square wooden lantern on the middle of its roof, and both roofs were painted red. The report gives its height as 11 m (36 ft) to the top of the lantern's ventilator. It stood 9 m (30 ft) back from the water, on ground about 3 m (10 ft) above high water.[^1] John Roney of Granville Ferry built it under contract, for $1,193.[^1] Lighthouse Friends says the plans show a cellar and cistern under the house and a light room on the upper floor that led to the lantern.[^2]

The light was to be fixed and red, 12 m (40 ft) above high water, and the report says it should be visible seven miles from all points of approach by water. Its apparatus was dioptric, "of the seventh order."[^1]

## The keepers

The first keeper was William Joseph Melanson, known as "Willie Jane," who was born and raised in Gilbert's Cove.[^3] The Marine Department's keeper table lists "J. W. Melanson" at Gilbert Point, at a salary of $300 a year, appointed 18 August 1904, in the report for the year ended 31 March 1915.[^6] The reports for the years ended 31 March 1909 and 1910 list "Jos. W. Melanson" at Gilbert Point with an appointment date of 18 August 1894, a decade before the light was built. The sources do not explain the difference.[^7][^8]

Melanson lived in the lighthouse with his wife and children.[^2] The NSLPS history says his was the only family to live there and keep the light.[^3] In 1950, when he was approaching 75, the light keeper's job passed to his daughter Louise, who held it until her death in 1972.[^3][^2] The NSLPS keeper list gives her years as 1951 to 1972. Lighthouse Friends gives 1950 to 1965, yet its own text has her living in the lighthouse until 1972.[^3][^2]

The provincial register says that for nearly half a century Melanson "kept constant watch in all weather." It adds that during the Second World War he played "an even greater role" by "preventing German U-Boats from tracking targets." The register gives no date or incident for that second claim.[^4]

## After the keepers

The sources do not agree on when the light was automated. Lighthouse Friends says 1965.[^2] The NSLPS says it was "automated by 1975," and its history notes place the automation before 1973.[^3] Rowlett lists the light as inactive since 1975.[^5]

Lighthouse Friends says the lantern light was discontinued in 1975 and replaced by an automated beacon on a concrete base on the point.[^2] The NSLPS dates the end of that beacon to May 1984.[^3] The provincial register says the lighthouse "was officially decommissioned in 1984."[^4] After Louise Melanson's death the lighthouse was boarded up, and by the late 1970s vandals were damaging it.[^2][^3]

In 1982 Ernie Morrisey and neighbours formed the Gilbert Cove and District Historical Society.[^3][^5] The province leased the property to the Society "indefinitely." In 1984 the Society made the house a small museum, tearoom and gift shop.[^3]

The province registered the lighthouse as a Provincially Registered Property. The register gives 17 June 1991 as the date it was recognized. Lighthouse Friends and the NSLPS say it was declared a provincial heritage site in 1990.[^4][^2][^3] In 2003, grants from the NSLPS's J. M. Kaplan Fund and from government made possible more repairs to the lighthouse and armour stone to protect the shore from erosion.[^3] Lighthouse Friends reports that the Atlantic Canada Opportunities Agency announced a $29,317 investment in the work in October of that year.[^2]

## Today

The Digby area tourism association says the lighthouse is open daily from mid-June to late September, with archival displays, a quilting room, local crafts and a tearoom.[^9] Rowlett gives the site and tower as open daily from mid-June to mid-September, free, with donations welcome.[^5] The NSLPS describes the tower's inside stairs as steep, and the picnic grounds and surrounding shore as freely accessible.[^3]

Visitors reach the lighthouse by a gravel road that leaves Route 1, the Evangeline Trail, and runs 1.2 km (0.8 mi) to the parking area.[^2] The NSLPS calls the red electric light that now shines in the tower "a reminder of the original lighting mechanism."[^3] Lighthouse Friends says a decorative light in the lantern room is shown from a historic Chance Brothers Fresnel lens.[^2] Rowlett describes a sixth-order Fresnel lens, and the 1904 report describes a seventh-order dioptric apparatus, so the sources do not agree on the lens.[^5][^1]
