Victoria Beach Lighthouse stands on Battery Point, on the east side of Digby Gut, the narrow tidal channel that links the Bay of Fundy with the Annapolis Basin in Nova Scotia. The Department of Marine put the light into operation on 8 July 1901. It was deactivated in 2015, just after it was recognized as a heritage lighthouse.[^1][^2][^3]

## The place

Digby Gut is narrow, and the water in it runs fast. LighthouseFriends describes a brisk current of four to five knots through the gut around low and high tide.[^3] The register says the light warns of particularly fast tidal water just off its shores.[^2]

The register calls the light the fourth navigational aid set up on the Digby Gut and Annapolis Basin route, and the first on this site.[^2]

From February to November 1849 the beach was the end of a short-lived Pony Express in Nova Scotia. When a steamer from England reached Halifax, a rider was sent out on the 230 km journey to Victoria Beach. There a steamer carried the messages to Saint John, where they were telegraphed on to New York. The service ended when a telegraph office opened in Halifax.[^3]

## Building the light

The Department's annual report for 1901 says the new light "was put in operation on July 8, 1901," to guide vessels through the gut, and to help small boats find a landing on the beach.[^1]

The tower is a square wooden tower with sloping sides and a square wooden lantern, painted white. The report gives its height as 8.5 m (28 ft) to the top of the lantern ventilator, and the register gives 8 m (26 ft).[^1][^2] The tower stands about 30 m (100 ft) back from the water's edge, on land 9 m (30 ft) above high water.[^1]

The light was fixed white, 15.8 m (52 ft) above high water. The report expected it to be seen "12 miles from all points of approach by water." Its optics were dioptric.[^1]

The work was done under contract by John Roney, for $497. The register also names him as the builder.[^1][^2]

## The keepers

James Hinds was hired as the first keeper, at an annual salary of $100, and he served until 1912.[^3] The Department's keeper list for 1910 names James Hinds at Victoria Beach.[^4] In 1912 S. Haynes took charge of the lighthouse. LighthouseFriends gives his service as running at least until 1923.[^3]

## After the keepers

The Canadian Register records that the lighthouse was recognized as a heritage lighthouse on 12 February 2015, under the Heritage Lighthouse Protection Act.[^2] LighthouseFriends says it was deactivated in 2015, just days after that recognition.[^3]

## Today

The register lists the lighthouse's current function as a historic or interpretive site. Its address is Victoria Beach Road at Pointe Battery.[^2] LighthouseFriends says the grounds are open and the tower is closed. There is parking past the light at the government wharf, and the light can be seen from the Saint John to Digby ferry.[^3] Because the tower is closed, this story does not suggest going inside it.
