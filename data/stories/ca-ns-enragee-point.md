Enragée Point light stands on the northern tip of Chéticamp Island, beside the Acadian fishing village of Chéticamp, on Cape Breton's north-west coast. Its present tower is an octagonal concrete lighthouse, built after a fire in 1956 destroyed the wooden lighthouse that had stood on the point since 1937.[^1][^2]

## The place

Chéticamp is a traditional Acadian fishing village, lying between the Gulf of St. Lawrence and the highlands of Cape Breton.[^2] Parks Canada says many villagers depended on the fishery for their living, and that a major coastal light helped keep them safe at sea.[^1] The light's first home was not the point. It stood on the south-west end of Cheticamp Island, and LighthouseFriends dates its completion to 1872.[^2]

## Building the light

The first light was a wooden tower with an attached dwelling. LighthouseFriends says A. P. McNeil built the 7.3 m (24 ft) tower and dwelling for $1,600, and that the lantern room and apparatus cost $1,846.[^2] A Department report for the year ended 30 June 1900 records repairs at "Cheticamp": the dwelling floor and kitchen flue were mended, the buildings painted, and a new copper ventilator fitted to the lantern.[^4] In 1896 a new tower was built beside the dwelling and the iron lantern moved to it.[^2]

In 1937 the primary light moved to Enragée Point, the island's north-west point. LighthouseFriends describes the new lighthouse as a square wooden house, 12 m (40 ft) tall, with a red iron lantern room.[^2]

Fire destroyed the wooden lighthouse on 19 October 1956.[^2] Parks Canada says it was "immediately replaced after a fire in 1956 in a new reinforced concrete form."[^1] The concrete tower was built to a standard Department of Transport plan, and its lantern was made at the Dominion Lighthouse Depot in Prescott, Ontario, to withstand the sea.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory dates the concrete tower to 1957, while Parks Canada ties it to the 1956 fire.[^6]

## The keepers

LighthouseFriends gives one keeper list for the station, from 1872 to 1988, covering both the island light and Enragée Point. Edward Briard was the first keeper, from 1872 to 1881, and John LeQuesne followed.[^2] Two entries, "P. Robin & Co." (1882 to 1890) and "Charles Robin & Co." (1890 to 1896), carry the form of a firm name, and LighthouseFriends names no person behind them.[^2]

Marcelin Muise kept the light from 1896 to 1912.[^2] A keepers' list in the Department's report for the year ended 30 June 1903 places "Muise, Marcellin" against Cheticamp.[^5] D. Desveaux (1912 to 1919), P. LeBlanc (1919 to 1928) and Peter A. Cormier (1928 to 1947) followed. Cormier was on the island when the light moved in 1937.[^2] Joseph J. Aucoin (1947 to 1948) and Wilfred L. Chiasson (1948 to 1978) came next.[^2]

In 1967 the station was made a three-person station, and Gregoire Patrick LeFort joined in 1968. LighthouseFriends says he stayed until 1988, when he retired as the last keeper, on 1 April.[^2]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

The island light, the station's first home, was given stays in 1882, after it was found to vibrate heavily in winter gales. On 23 July 1883 lightning struck it and did considerable damage, though the illuminating apparatus was not injured.[^2]

Parks Canada dates the first light at this site to 1937, "a year after the steamer Kinburn wrecked near Chéticamp Island."[^1] A 1997 article in *Argonauta*, the newsletter of the Canadian Nautical Research Society, gives 1936 as the year she was thrown on the rocks of Cheticamp Island during a storm and broke up, following a local history by Father Anselme Chiasson. The article also cites Mills's history, which notes only that she was "retired" that year.[^3] The sources disagree about how the ship ended: the article says she was wrecked on the rocks, and Mills says she was retired.

## After the keepers

The station was electrified in December 1967. Its light became a 400-watt mercury-vapour lamp and its fog signal an electronic horn.[^2] The staff was cut to two in 1972 and to one by the end of the decade.[^2] The station was fully automated by 1987, and the last keeper retired in 1988.[^2]

## Today

The station is a designated heritage lighthouse. LighthouseFriends dates the announcement to 22 August 2019.[^2] Parks Canada calls the light "a significant tourism attraction" as well as an aid to navigation that the local community values highly.[^1]

LighthouseFriends says the Canadian Coast Guard owns the lighthouse, that the grounds are open and the tower is closed. Only the lighthouse and an equipment building remain at the station.[^2] To reach it, the guide gives a route from the Cabot Trail, 4.6 km south of Cheticamp, along Cheticamp Island Road and then Lighthouse Road for 6 km.[^2]
