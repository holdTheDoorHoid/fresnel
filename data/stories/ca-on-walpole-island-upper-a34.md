Walpole Island Upper A34 is a square-tapered concrete tower with no lantern, standing beside Walpole Island in the South Channel of the St. Clair River, Ontario, on the east side of the channel.[^1][^2][^3] The register gives 1929 as the year it was built, and the Government of Canada recognized it as a heritage lighthouse in 2015.[^1][^2]

## The place

The register says the Upper A34 is "strongly associated with the international through-traffic traversing the St. Clair River between Lake Huron and Lake St. Clair."[^2] It is one of a series of lights built for shipping along the South Channel, and the register also ties it to the Walpole Island First Nation community.[^1][^2]

## Building the light

The register says the Upper A34 and the Lower A32 "were established as a pair in 1920."[^2] The original lights were poles. Reinforced-concrete towers replaced them before a major dredging of the channel in the mid-1930s.[^2] The register gives 1929 as the build year for the Upper A34.[^1]

The tower stands 5.4 m (17.7 ft) tall, according to the register.[^2] The Coast Guard's list gives the same 5.4 m for the structure, and puts the light 8.1 m above the water.[^3]

The Heritage Guide page reproduces a 1932 photograph of the tower, credited to Library and Archives Canada, Canada Marine Aids Division.[^1]

In its report for the fiscal year ended 31 March 1909, the Department of Marine and Fisheries recorded two range lights set up on the northern end of Walpole Island to lead into the Chenal Ecarté, in pressed-lens lanterns hoisted on poles.[^4] The Coast Guard's list now shows a Chenal Ecarté front range light "On Walpole Island, SE. of N. extremity," with a rear range light 61 m from it.[^3] The sources do not connect those range lights with the Upper A34, and this story does not.

## Today

The Coast Guard's list gives the Upper A34 as Fl R 4s: a red light that flashes every four seconds, with a nominal range of four nautical miles.[^3] Its description reads "White square tower with red trim, marked “A34”," and its position is given as "On E. side of channel."[^3]

The register says the tower is located offshore, and that it is "closely associated to a number of key community facilities on Walpole Island."[^2] None of the sources describes public access to the tower. Walpole Island is a First Nation community.[^2] Do not land there or cross its land without permission.
