Cape Tryon is a red sandstone headland on the north coast of Prince Edward Island, and for over a century a light has flashed from it toward the Gulf of St. Lawrence. The wooden lighthouse that first stood there in 1905 has since been moved three times and is now a summer cottage; a second wooden tower carries the light today.

## The place

Cape Tryon lies about 2.4 km northwest of New London Harbour, a cliff 33.5 m high. Lighthouse Friends describes the light as guiding vessels along the north shore between Richmond Bay and New London and warning of shallow water that extends a long way off the coast.[^1] Lighthouse Friends quotes a 1909 passage by the novelist Lucy Maud Montgomery, then living at Cavendish on the eastern shore of New London Harbour, which calls the headland New London Point (the site adds that it is now called Cape Tryon) and says the new revolving light "flashes on the point in the dusk of summer nights".[^1]

## Building the light

The Department of Marine and Fisheries' chief engineer reported that a lighthouse on Cape Tryon went into operation at the opening of navigation in 1905. It stood on ground 23 m (75 ft) above high water and 15 m (50 ft) back from the edge, a rectangular white wooden building with a red octagonal iron lantern on the north end of the roof, 11 m (37 ft) high to the lantern ventilator.[^2] Lighthouse Friends gives the builder as B. D. Huntley of Vernon River Bridge, on a $1,590 contract.[^1]

The first light was temporary. The department noted that a temporary fixed white light would be replaced later by a fourth-order quick-flashing light,[^3] and its next report lists a fourth-order light installed at Cape Tryon.[^4] Lighthouse Friends adds that the lens, made in Paris by Barbier, Benard & Turenne, replaced the temporary light in 1906.[^1] In 1907 the department spent $120.20 on repairs to the building.[^5]

## The keepers

William Bell was appointed keeper on 17 March 1905 at a salary of $285.[^6] Lighthouse Friends says he came from French River and served until his death in 1915; it lists eight more keepers over the following forty-seven years, among them James Adams, Frank Pidgeon, James Garnet Graham (who served in two spells and took leave in September 1942 to join the armed forces) and Arthur Elwood Paynter, the last, from 1953 until automation.[^1] Its dates for the 1936 to 1953 changeovers overlap in places, so this story does not repeat them.

## After the keepers

Sources disagree about when the present tower replaced the first. Lighthouse Friends says 1962, when the light was automated;[^1] the heritage record says the present tower was built in 1964-65 to a Department of Transport design;[^7] Russ Rowlett's Lighthouse Directory gives 1969.[^8]

The first lighthouse was moved a short way to Cape Road and left empty, then around 1964 to Sea View by a Montreal family.[^1] In 1990 new owners bought it and moved it to Park Corner, where they restored it as a summer cottage.[^1] Lighthouse Friends notes that the new owners are related to two of the station's keepers, Graham and Bell.[^1] The heritage record suggests the 1905 building may have inspired the Four Winds Light in Montgomery's *Anne's House of Dreams*; it says the building has no direct link to her.[^7]

## Today

The current tower is a square, tapered wooden tower clad in cedar shingles,[^7] 12.4 m high and white with a red upper portion.[^9] The Canadian Coast Guard list gives its light as a white flash of 2 s every 6 s, at 33.4 m, range 11 nautical miles, seasonal.[^9] Lighthouse Friends says the L.M. Montgomery Land Trust became owner in May 2016, and the Lighthouse Directory notes the cliff is eroding.[^1][^8] The tower is closed. The access road crosses private farmland and has been closed to vehicles; the Directory says visitors may walk about 1 km to it while respecting private property, and Lighthouse Friends reported a No Trespassing sign in 2013.[^1][^8] Ownership is also reported differently: the Directory gives the Coast Guard as owner.[^8] The 1905 lighthouse at Park Corner has been fitted out as a summer cottage and is not a public site.[^7]
