McNab Point Light is a square wooden tower on the shore of Lake Huron, just south of Southampton in Bruce County, Ontario. It was built in 1877 as the rear light of a pair of range lights that led ships into Southampton harbour. It was moved to its present point in 1901 and has been out of service since about 1989.[^1][^2][^3]

## The place

Southampton is located at the mouth of the Saugeen River.[^2] Its harbour was made into a refuge by two breakwaters: one ran out from the beach at Southampton, and the other curved toward the mainland from the northern tip of Chantry Island.[^1][^3]

The approach had hazards. The Department of Marine's report on its hydrographic survey of the Great Lakes, for the fiscal year ended 30 June 1900, described Southampton as a harbour of refuge with very little room to anchor and dangerous boulders about. Its officer in charge added that the range lights then in use led vessels over dangerously shoal water a mile from the breakwater.[^4]

## Building the light

LighthouseFriends says the range was built in 1876 and that its lights were not shown until 5 May 1877. Robert Reed was paid $649 for building the towers, and E. Chanteloup of Montreal received $380.11 for the lanterns and lighting apparatus.[^1] Parks Canada gives 1877 as the year of construction.[^3] The Department described the rear light as an enclosed square tower 8.5 m (28 ft) high, carrying a fixed white light 10 m (34 ft) above the water that could be seen ten miles away in ordinary weather. LighthouseFriends quotes this from the Department's report.[^1]

The front light stood at the end of the breakwater. It was looked after by Duncan McGregor Lambert, keeper of Chantry Island Lighthouse.[^1]

The rear tower stood on the north side of Horseshoe Bay until 1901.[^1][^2] The Department's report for the year ended 30 June 1901 says the back light of the range had been "moved 1,800 feet S.W. from its old position" to "a low gravel ridge on the east or main shore of the harbour." The new range line led into the breakwater with at least 5 m (16 ft) of water, after a hydrographic survey of Lake Huron by Mr Stewart found a better channel.[^5] The report does not name McNab Point. Parks Canada says the tower was relocated from the northern tip of Horseshoe Bay to McNab Point, and LighthouseFriends gives the distance as about 550 m (1,800 ft) across Horseshoe Bay.[^3][^1]

Parks Canada describes the tower as a square, tapered wooden structure 8.5 m (28 ft) high, with an iron lantern, no gallery, and a cylindrical ventilator on top.[^3]

## The keepers

David Cascaden was paid $100 a year to care for the rear light from its first lighting. LighthouseFriends says he had seven children and tended a large flat-wick lamp in a reflector 43 cm (17 in) across, inside the lantern.[^1] The head keepers that LighthouseFriends lists after him are John Lee, 1882 to 1904; James Brown, 1904 to 1916; and John Buckley, from 1916 and at least to 1923.[^1] The Department's keeper list in its report for the year ended 30 June 1906 has Brown against Southampton Harbour, which agrees with LighthouseFriends.[^6]

A granddaughter of John Buckley, quoted by LighthouseFriends, remembered her visits to the rear light in the 1920s. A supply boat came once or twice a year and had to anchor out in the lake, so the coal oil was brought ashore in smaller amounts. Her grandfather filled a container from a big metal tank by the door, then filled the lamp, cleaned its chimney, checked the wick, and polished the large silver reflector before he lit it. She did not remember him ever missing a day.[^1]

## After the keepers

The sources do not agree on when the light went dark and came back. Parks Canada gives c.1970 for the light's deactivation, c.1980 for its reactivation and automation, and c.1989 for its decommissioning.[^3] LighthouseFriends says that when the front range light was discontinued in the late 1950s, the rear light remained active as Southampton Harbour Light until at least 1971, and that by 1974 the tower was back in operation as McNab Point Lighthouse.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory says the range was discontinued in the 1950s and the light renamed Southampton Harbour Light. It adds that the light was deactivated for a short time in the early 1970s and reactivated in 1974 as McNab Point Light.[^2]

In 1988 the Canadian Coast Guard covered the tower with white aluminium siding, which trapped moisture and let the wood under it rot.[^1][^2] Parks Canada gives the year of the siding as 1989.[^3] The Marine Heritage Society took the siding off in 2008 and put new cedar shingles on the tower.[^1][^3]

## Today

The tower was designated a heritage lighthouse on 3 August 2012, under the Heritage Lighthouse Protection Act.[^1][^3] The Town of Saugeen Shores owns it and has leased it to the Marine Heritage Society.[^1][^2]

The site is closed. The Lighthouse Directory says it is in an area of vacation homes with no public access, and that the site and tower are closed.[^2] LighthouseFriends says the tower stands in the backyard of a private residence and is best seen from the water, with a distant view from Chantry Island.[^1] The Lighthouse Directory says that by 2026 the tower was severely deteriorated, and that residents were mobilising to save it.[^2]
