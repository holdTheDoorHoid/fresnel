Pentwater North Pierhead Light is the tower at the end of the north pier, where the Pentwater channel meets Lake Michigan in Oceana County, Michigan. A National Guard helicopter set it on the pier in October 1987.[^1]

## The place

Pentwater Lake had only a shallow, swampy creek to Lake Michigan until the lumber baron Charles Mears had a channel dug between 1856 and 1858, protected by a pair of short piers.[^2] Lumber from his mill was carried out to "the end of the north pier, where the water was deep enough for larger vessels to tie up."[^2]

Terry Pepper and LighthouseFriends date Congress's first $55,000 for the Pentwater harbor to 1867.[^1][^2] The Herald-Journal column dates that appropriation to 1857, the same year it gives for the channel.[^3] The Army Corps of Engineers began a 20-year program to widen the channel and extend the piers.[^2] The Lighthouse Board judged that a light on one of the piers would guide vessels into the channel.[^2] The first beacon went up at the end of the south pier in 1873.[^2]

LighthouseFriends lists head keepers from 1873 to 1915 for the Pentwater light, whose first tower stood at the end of the south pier.[^1][^2] The Army Corps of Engineers replaced the timber piers with concrete ones in 1937.[^3][^1]

The Life-Saving Service's annual report for 1880 lists, among stations a bill would establish, one "at or near Pentwater, Lake Michigan."[^4]

## Building the light

The north tower was a community project.[^1] The prefabricated cylindrical tower weighed 12,000 pounds, was made in Tulsa, Oklahoma, and was trucked to Ludington.[^1] Its design came from Jon Kiernan of the Coast Guard's Ninth District Shore Maintenance Detachment in Cleveland, who modeled it on the common "D9 Tower" but made it taller and wider so it could house extra equipment and the necessary power sources.[^1] The Herald-Journal's column gives its height as 25 feet (7.6 m), and LighthouseFriends gives 30 feet (9.1 m).[^3][^1]

LighthouseFriends reports that high winds forced a postponement after repeated attempts to land the tower failed, and that a National Guard Chinook helicopter placed it on 8 October 1987.[^1] The tower carried a foghorn and a radiobeacon as well as the light. For the first time, the Herald-Journal says, "the piers were marked with one light on each side."[^1][^3]

Terry Pepper's history gives 1997 for the north tower.[^2] The 1987 date rests on the Ludington Daily News of 9 October 1987, as cited by LighthouseFriends, and on the Herald-Journal column. This story follows 1987.[^1][^3]

## Storms, wrecks and rescues

A year before the tower went up, "a boat went on the rocks trying to find the harbor entrance," LighthouseFriends reports.[^1] Maynard Barrus, a former fishing charter captain who pushed for the new lighthouse, said it would have been impossible to find the harbor in fog "had it not been for Coho Bob with a radio direction finder who could help guide boats into the harbor on foggy days."[^1] When Coho Bob was not available, Barrus and others "would drive to the waterfront and shine their lights and honk their horns" to help boats find the harbor.[^1]

## Today

As Terry Pepper describes it, the north tower shows a flashing green light at a focal plane of 43 feet (13 m), and its fog signal sounds "a single 3-second blast every 30 seconds."[^2] The Herald-Journal says the pierhead lights "are still operational." It quotes the website visitpentwater.com, which calls them "one of the few remaining pier range light systems located on the Great Lakes."[^3]

Carrie Mears, daughter of Charles Mears, donated the land for Charles Mears State Park to the State of Michigan in 1923.[^1] To see the light, walk the beach from Charles Mears State Park to the north pier.[^2]
