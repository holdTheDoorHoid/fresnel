Kimberly Point Park Lighthouse, often called the Neenah Light, is an eight-sided brick tower at the north end of Lake Winnebago, where the lake runs into the lower Fox River.[^1] A gift from J. C. Kimberly, of the paper family, financed it, and it was built in 1945 as a navigation aid and landmark for the boat races on the lake.[^2][^1] The city owns it.[^4] In September 2026 the city closed it for construction under a preservation project.[^3]

## The place

The lighthouse stands on Kimberly Point.[^1] The nomination says it "overlooks the headwaters of the lower Fox River," and that Lake Winnebago is "one of the largest inland lakes in the country."[^1]

The lake had been raced on for a long time. "Yacht racing on Lake Winnebago began around 1859, with the Neenah Yacht Club organizing in 1864," the nomination says. The Inland Lakes Yachting Association's regattas later "grew to become particularly significant following World War I."[^1]

The lighthouse was the last of five recreational park facilities that the community's leading paper industrialist contributed to the City of Neenah.[^2]

The point became a city park, and the nomination says its development "revealed the hostility and divisiveness that existed among Neenah's industrial elite."[^1] The city acquired "two lots" for park purposes in 1925, and Helen Kimberly Stuart donated more land to prevent a move by a Kimberly-Clark president who wanted the point for a private estate.[^1] The city's own page gives 1929 as the year the park was established.[^3]

An earlier light stood at the same entrance. LighthouseFriends says a lighthouse was selected for Lighthouse Reef "to mark the entrance to the Fox River from the lake." Known as Menasha Lighthouse, it was "placed in operation in 1855 with Dennis Crowley as its first keeper," and "the light was discontinued in August 1859."[^4] Rowlett lists "Menasha (1855-1859), entrance to the Fox River from Lake Winnebago," and says that "The abandoned lighthouse was destroyed by fire in 1880."[^5]

## Building the light

In 1939 the park commission considered a navigation light for the park. The nomination says Helen Kimberly Stuart and members of the Kimberly family planned to contribute "an estimated $15,000 towards the construction of a functioning 40-foot tall lighthouse."[^1] The outbreak of World War II and a national ban on new construction deferred the plan.[^1]

"In 1944 he enabled the plans to proceed by donating a sufficient number of shares in Kimberly-Clark to fund the structure," the nomination says of J. C. Kimberly. The city was responsible for "providing the electricity and plumbing for the public restrooms."[^1]

As "a privately owned aid to navigation," the light came under the "close scrutiny and approvals" of the Department of Commerce, the War Department and the Coast Guard.[^1] The nomination says the construction ban was circumvented "as part of the U.S. Coast Guard's navigational system," a step "most likely expedited through Kimberly's son Jimmy."[^1]

In 1945, within days of the war's official end, "construction of the lighthouse was announced and begun by Fluor brothers of Oshkosh."[^1]

The cost is given two ways. The nomination says the lighthouse was "Completed at a cost of $7,000." LighthouseFriends says it "cost $7,500."[^1][^4]

LighthouseFriends says that "When first built the tower stood forty feet tall," and Rowlett gives "Originally 40 ft (12 m) tall."[^4][^5] The nomination gives an overall height of 49 feet (15 m), after the tower was "increased in height by ten feet in 1954."[^1]

## After the keepers

The nomination names no keepers. It says the lighthouse "was operated as an official part of the Coast Guard navigational system," maintaining an official signal pattern and filing periodic reports "until the 1990s."[^1]

In 2000 floodlights were attached to the north side, as part of a federally funded enhancement of "recreational opportunities for disabled fishermen."[^1] The Neenah Landmarks Commission added the lighthouse to the Neenah Register of Historic Places in 2009.[^4] The Wisconsin Register followed on 17 February 2012, and the National Register on 5 February 2013.[^3]

In August 2025 the city announced a preservation project that would bring "interior and exterior restoration and accessibility."[^3] By January 2026 the city said it had reached its fundraising goal.[^3]

## Today

The city owns the lighthouse, and LighthouseFriends says "Grounds open, tower closed."[^4] The city's page gives a 2024 estimate, from a regional planning report, of "Estimated 500 visitors per day in the summer."[^3]

A post on the city's page, dated 3 September 2026, says that "The Kimberly Point Lighthouse is closed for construction," and it announces a "GROUNDBREAKING CEREMONY AT THE LIGHTHOUSE" on 9 September.[^3] Rowlett's directory says "Work on the project began in September 2026."[^5] The city's page does not say whether the groundbreaking took place.[^3]

Rowlett's directory lists the light as "Active (maintained by City of Neenah)."[^5] The nomination of 2012 calls it "A fully operational lighthouse."[^1] Neither source says whether the light still appears on the Coast Guard's list today.
