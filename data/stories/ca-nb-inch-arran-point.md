Inch Arran Point Range Lights stand on the northernmost point of New Brunswick, in the town of Dalhousie, where the Restigouche River meets Chaleur Bay. The front light is an 11 m (36 ft) wooden tower first lit in 1870, and for its first sixty-five years it was kept by one family, the Arseneaus.[^1][^2][^3]

## The place

According to Lighthouse Friends, immigrants from the Isle of Arran in Scotland named the point after their island home, "inch" being Gaelic for island. The same point has also been called Bonami Point and Arseneau Point, the second after the family who lived nearby in the 1800s.[^2] The light marks the entrance to Dalhousie harbour, and Parks Canada's statement of significance notes that the town, bypassed by the railway, relied on ships to export its goods.[^1]

## Building the light

The tower was built in 1870 and its fixed white light was shown for the first time on 7 November 1870, according to Lighthouse Friends. That account describes the lighting apparatus as a circular-wick lamp, two flat-wick lamps and three 15-inch reflectors, in an iron lantern 4.5 feet across; by 1875 an 18½-inch reflector had replaced the 15-inch one used with the circular-wick lamp.[^2] Parks Canada also gives 1870 as the year it was built.[^3]

Parks Canada describes it as one of many wooden square-tapered towers built by the Department of Marine and Fisheries after Confederation, and singles out its "birdcage" lantern gallery of thin curved metal spars as unique among surviving Canadian lighthouses.[^1]

## The keepers

Louis Arseneau, husband of Marie-Louise Arseneau, exhibited the first light, according to Lighthouse Friends. Marie-Louise, the same account says, set a candle and later a lamp in her window to guide her husband, sons and other fishermen home, and was known in Dalhousie as a medicine-woman.[^2] Louis died in April 1888. His grandson Joseph then took over the light, though through an oversight at the Department of Marine he was not formally appointed until 1894.[^2] The Department's list of keepers for 1904-05 shows an "Arseneau, James" at "Dalhousie Harbour", appointed 18 January 1894.[^4] The date matches Joseph's appointment, but the first name does not. Lighthouse Friends' own list of keepers also gives James Arseneau for 1894 to 1913, where its text says Joseph, and the table does not say which light Dalhousie Harbour means, so the sources leave the keeper's first name unsettled.[^2]

In 1901 the keeper wrote to the department that his pay was $100 a year, the same as his grandfather's in 1870. The deputy minister replied that this was "an unimportant harbour light" close to a village, and that the keeper should find other work.[^2]

After Joseph died in November 1913, his wife Denise was made temporary keeper, and the local Member of Parliament, W. S. Montgomery, recommended she stay on because her property was right beside the light.[^2] She was still keeper on 19 September 1929, when she slipped on the stairs with Inspector Moffet and broke her right humerus; Armand J. LeGouffe filled in for a few months.[^2] Parks Canada says the Arsenault family kept the light for sixty-five years, between 1870 and 1935.[^1]

## After the keepers

In 1972 the tower became the front light of a range when a metal skeleton tower was put up a few hundred metres to the west.[^2] The Federal Heritage Buildings Policy recognised the lighthouse in 1991, and it was designated a Heritage Lighthouse in 2017.[^2][^3]

## Today

The front range light remains in operation and is owned by the Canadian Coast Guard; Lighthouse Friends reports the grounds are open and the tower is closed. It stands at the end of Victoria Street in Dalhousie, with parking nearby.[^2]
