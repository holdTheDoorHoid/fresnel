Medway Head is the western headland at the mouth of Port Medway harbour, on Nova Scotia's South Shore in Queens County.[^3] A light has marked it since 1851.[^4] The tower there now is a wooden one, built in 1983.[^3][^5] It is owned by the Medway Head Lighthouse Society, which was formed in 2013.[^4]

## The place

Port Medway sits near the mouth of the Medway River. Loyalists from Massachusetts settled it in the mid-eighteenth century, and logging, shipbuilding and fishing became its industries. At its height it is said to have had six wharves and more than 5,000 people.[^1]

In 1849 and 1850 a petition from the inhabitants, merchants and mariners of Port Medway, asking for a light on the western headland at the harbour entrance, was read in the House of Assembly. In 1850 the legislature allocated two thousand pounds for six lighthouses, one of them a beacon light at Port Medway.[^1]

## Building the light

The first light went up in 1851. It was a square wooden dwelling, 7 m (23 ft) high, painted white with a black square daymark.[^3]

An 1874 inspection of the station, as Lighthouse Friends quotes it, describes the lantern as a three-sided window of ordinary glass, and the lamps as four with three 12-inch reflectors. The year's oil came to 160 gallons. The inspector also found the chimney resting on the chamber floor, straining the building and causing leaks.[^1]

In 1859 the keeper, Elson Perry, bought a twelve-acre lot beside the light, which had a landing place. The superintendent of lights then recommended that the Board of Works buy it from him for seven pounds, the price he had paid, and Perry was willing to sell.[^1]

In 1899 a tower built on the wharf in the town took the name Port Medway Lighthouse, and the headland light became Medway Head Lighthouse.[^1] The wharf tower is a separate station. It has been inactive since 1987 and is now a park.[^4]

A square two-storey dwelling with a lantern on its roof replaced the first light in 1927.[^3] The sources give different dates for the fibreglass tower that followed it. The Nova Scotia list says 1963, Lighthouse Friends gives 10 April 1966, and the Rowlett directory has it as 1966 to 1983.[^3][^1][^4] The wooden tower standing now was built in 1983. The Coast Guard list gives it as 8.7 m high, a white square tower with a red upper portion.[^3][^5]

## The keepers

Lighthouse Friends names Isaac K. Perry as the first keeper, from 1851 until his death in 1853 at the age of 47.[^1] Twenty-three-year-old Elson Perry replaced his father as keeper. Lighthouse Friends quotes an 1874 inspection for his appointment in November 1853, and says he served for nearly forty years, until 1892.[^1] The same list then gives six more keepers before Douglas Smiley, including Israel C. Foster from 1892 to 1909 and Mrs. William Atkins from 1912 to 1913.[^1]

Douglas R. Smiley moved into the dwelling in 1959. It then had no indoor plumbing or central heating.[^1] Smiley kept the light for 28 years. Lighthouse Friends says the station was de-staffed in 1986, after his retirement, though its keeper list runs to 1987.[^1] A plaque on the lighthouse door, given in his memory, named him in 1989.[^3]

## After the keepers

In 1980 the 1927 dwelling was sold to a buyer from Massachusetts, who had to remove it from the property.[^1] Lighthouse Friends says it was moved a short distance to a hill above the station. The Nova Scotia list and the Rowlett directory both say it was moved across the road, where it is now a private dwelling.[^1][^3][^4] The Rowlett directory records the 1927 house as inactive as a light since 1966.[^4]

The station was automated by 1987.[^3]

The Rowlett directory says a society formed in February 2013 took ownership of the 1983 tower in June 2017, and that restoration began in the fall of 2015, funded by contributions and the National Trust.[^4] Lighthouse Friends dates the transfer differently: it reports a ceremony on 11 June 2014 that celebrated the transfer of the tower to the society.[^1]

## Today

The 1983 tower stands on Long Cove Road, on the west side of the harbour entrance.[^4] The Rowlett directory says the tower is open for tours on Mondays, Wednesdays, Saturdays and Sundays from 29 June to 15 September, and daily during the late-August arts and crafts show. Check the society's current schedule before going.[^4] The last 2.4 km (1.5 miles) of the road to the site is gravel.[^2]

The 1927 house is a private dwelling and is not open to the public.[^3] On the Coast Guard list, the light flashes white every 12 seconds.[^5]
