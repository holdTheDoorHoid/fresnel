Muskegon South Breakwater Light stands at the outer end of the south arm of Muskegon's harbor breakwater, about half a mile out in Lake Michigan, where it marks the entrance to the Muskegon channel.[^4][^7] It is a red steel pyramidal tower, 63 feet (19 m) tall with its base, built around 1930 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2006.[^3][^4][^6]

## The place

Muskegon's first light stood on land, and later lights went out on the piers.[^8][^3] LighthouseFriends dates a wooden pierhead tower at the outer end of the south pier to 1872, while the Muskegon Lights history puts a beacon at the end of the pier in 1871.[^8][^3] Those older lights are a separate station. The breakwater light stands at the end of the arrowhead pier, whose construction began in 1927.[^4][^3]

## Building the light

Construction of the arrowhead harbor began in 1927, and the Muskegon Lights history says the work "lasted several years."[^3] The Lighthouse Service's summary of the 1929 works lists a light and fog-signal station at Muskegon South Breakwater among the other important aids in progress that year.[^1] The Bulletin's index for 1924 to 1929 has an entry for the same light and fog signal station, marked "constructed."[^2]

Sources differ on the year the light was first shown. The Muskegon Lights history says a temporary light was established in 1929, and that "the steel, pyramidal-designed tower and fog horn were completed in 1930."[^3] The Muskegon Lights virtual tour gives 1931 as the year the tower was built, and the National Register record gives 1931 as its significant year.[^4][^6]

The tower is pyramidal, on a rectangular base 10 feet (3 m) tall. The virtual tour says it stands at the outer end of the arrowhead pier, as the outer light of a range. Its top once held a large buoy-style light that "has long since been removed." The current optic is a red LED aid to navigation.[^4]

## The keepers

The virtual tour says the structure "was not meant to be a manned or occupied" one, and that it was built "to hold and shelter equipment, and keep the light at the correct focal plane."[^4] The Lighthouse Society's place record classes the light as a minor aid to navigation, and no keepers' names appear in it.[^7]

## After the keepers

The lights were declared excess property by the U.S. Government in 2008.[^3] In 2010 the Michigan Lighthouse Conservancy was awarded possession of both the South Pierhead and South Breakwater lights, through the National Historic Lighthouse Preservation Act.[^3][^5] The Coast Guard is still in charge of the blinking lights at the top and of the fog signal, which the Muskegon Lights site calls aids to navigation.[^5]

## Today

The Muskegon Lights website says the South Breakwater Light is "viewable from the exterior only," and calls the tower "severely deteriorated."[^5] It asks for donations and volunteers to help save the lights.[^5] The South Pierhead Light, a separate light nearby, is open for tours in the summer.[^5]
