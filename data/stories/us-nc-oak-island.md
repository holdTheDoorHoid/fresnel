Oak Island Light is a concrete tower of 1958 beside the Coast Guard station at the mouth of the Cape Fear River, in the Town of Caswell Beach, North Carolina.[^1][^2][^8] It took over the coastal light from the Cape Fear Lighthouse on Bald Head Island.[^1] The range lights that stood on the island before it were a separate station, shut down in 1894.[^4]

## The place

Bald Head Island lies on the east side of the Cape Fear River's entrance, and Oak Island on the west.[^1] Ships crossing the river's bar needed marks to steer by.[^1] In 1849 two brick range towers went up on Oak Island, 130 yards (about 120 m) apart, to guide mariners entering the river. They cost $8,985 and were lit on 10 September 1849.[^1] The channel did not stay put. The Lighthouse Board's report for the year ended 30 June 1895 lists the range light as discontinued on 31 July 1894, with "a tubular-lantern and a fourth-order light."[^4] The same report says changes in the channel at the entrance to the Cape Fear "rendered its lights useless as guides."[^5]

In 1903 the Lighthouse Board built the Cape Fear Lighthouse on Bald Head Island, a few miles east of the older Bald Head light, to warn mariners away from the Frying Pan Shoals.[^1] It served as the area's coastal light until 1958.[^1]

## Building the light

The present tower was completed in 1958, and the National Register record names W. F. Brinkley & Son Construction Co. in its architect field.[^1][^3] It stands on 24 concrete-filled pilings, driven 67 feet (20 m) into the ground and capped by an octagonal concrete base 30 feet (9 m) wide and 3 feet (0.9 m) deep.[^1]

The shaft is reinforced concrete, eight inches (20 cm) thick, with a constant diameter of 17 feet (5.2 m).[^1] LighthouseFriends says a tapering tower of aluminum-alloy panels was one of two designs put out for bids, and that the concrete tower won.[^1] The shaft was poured into a movable form that jacks lifted about 30 cm (one foot) an hour, and a temporary concrete plant ran continuously for six days.[^1] The bands were poured in gray, white and black at different heights, and LighthouseFriends says the three bands never need repainting.[^1]

The sources give different heights. LighthouseFriends gives the tower as 158 feet (48 m), and a 2026 travel guide gives 153 feet (47 m).[^1][^7] The Lighthouse Society's record gives 169 feet (52 m) above mean high water for the light, and 155 feet (47 m) from the base to the center of the lantern.[^2] The lantern room, of aluminum, weighs 1,740 pounds (790 kg) and needed a Marine Corps helicopter to set it in place.[^1]

The first light was shown on 15 May 1958.[^1] Captain Charles N. Swan, then 85, who had been keeper of the Cape Fear light from 1903 to 1933, threw the switch.[^1] According to LighthouseFriends, the light went out about twenty minutes after it was switched on. A fuse was replaced, and the beacon was back before dark.[^1]

The Lighthouse Society's record for the station lists keeper's quarters, a wood-frame farmhouse, and a radio beacon, but it names no keepers.[^2]

## After the keepers

In February 2002 a fire destroyed the Coast Guard station next to the lighthouse.[^8] A travel guide says the lighthouse itself was undamaged, and that the station was rebuilt as an almost exact replica.[^8] No other storm, wreck or rescue tied to this tower appears in the sources read.

The Coast Guard took ownership of the land in the 1930s.[^1] In 2004 the lighthouse and the ground around the tower were deeded to the Town of Caswell Beach.[^1] The Friends of Oak Island Lighthouse, a non-profit, was formed to preserve and maintain the lighthouse and grounds.[^1] In 2016 the town loaned the Friends $50,000 so that cracks in the tower could be repaired. LighthouseFriends says the repairs were expected to cost about $90,000.[^1] In December 2020 the Coast Guard installed a revolving LED beacon, the first change to the lighting apparatus since 1958.[^1] The National Register asset record for the lighthouse is dated 5 April 2007.[^3]

## Today

The Coast Guard still maintains the light, and the public climbs the tower on tours arranged through the Friends of Oak Island Lighthouse.[^1] A 2026 news report says the Friends' volunteers offered top tours on Thursdays and Sundays through Labor Day, limited climbers to two trips to the top a day, and said that from 1 October the top tours move to Wednesdays and Saturdays.[^6] The climb is 131 steps according to that report and to NC Tripping, but 134 according to LighthouseFriends, which counts a series of ship's ladders.[^6][^7][^1] The NC Tripping guide, updated 10 August 2026, says the grounds are open from sunrise to sunset. It gives tours on Tuesdays, Thursdays and Sundays from May through September, and on Wednesdays and Saturdays from October through April. The two schedules differ, so check with the Friends before you go.[^7] Just across the street, the town built a boardwalk and an observation deck, which give a view of the lighthouse.[^1]
