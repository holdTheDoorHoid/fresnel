The Miminegash Range Lights marked the entrance to Miminegash Pond, on the west coast of Prince Edward Island. Miminegash is a small west-coast port whose harbour was dredged, and its two range lights lined up the channel that runs in from the sea between two breakwaters.[^2][^3] The front light still stands on the North Breakwater, and the Canadian Coast Guard still lists it.[^1][^2] The rear light's wooden tower was sold in 1969 and moved away.[^2]

## Building the light

Two range lights were set up in 1886 to guide mariners into the pond. The front light hung from a mast and the rear light was placed on Rix's Point, about 1.6 km (one mile) away.[^3] A report of the Department of Marine for the year ended 30 June 1900 describes the change that followed. The range lights, "heretofore shown from lanterns on masts," were improved "by substituting lights shown from inclosed towers for the exposed lanterns," and the lights were moved.[^4] The front light was now shown from "a square wooden tower, with sloping sides," standing on the outer end of the south breakwater, and its red sector was set to cover "Miminegash reef."[^4] LHF gives the front light's height above high water as 5.2 m (17 ft), the rear's as 8.5 m (28 ft), and the cost of the work as $254.30.[^3]

In 1902 the front light's red sector was changed. The report says the red sector "shown from the front range lighthouse on the outer end of the south breakwater now shows," and that the change let small vessels coming from the south find the entrance and reach the anchorage behind Miminegash reef.[^5]

## The keepers

The Department's keeper tables give names and appointment dates. Patrick O'Brien was appointed to the Miminegash Range Light on 14 May 1897, and Frederick Costain to the Miminegash, Rix Point Range Light on 19 May 1897.[^6] LHF gives the rear keeper's full name as Harold Frederick Costain and says he served until 1906.[^3] The 1902 table prints only "Frederick," so the first name is one of the places where the sources differ.

The rear light passed to Elijah Costain.[^3] The 1910 keeper table lists him at "Miminegash, Inner" from 18 May 1906.[^7] The report published in 1912 still lists him, and Patrick O'Brien is in the same list.[^8] LHF gives Elijah Costain's service as 1906 to 1913 and O'Brien's as 1897 to 1917.[^3]

## After the keepers

The Lighthouse Directory says the square wooden rear tower, built in 1900, was sold in 1969 and relocated. LHF places it on the grounds of a private cottage near Kildare.[^2][^3] The directory says that the front light's square wooden tower was removed in 1972, and that the range was discontinued in 2017. The rear light has been inactive since then and was later removed.[^2] The 1900 front tower stood on the outer end of the south breakwater,[^4] but the present front light is on the North Breakwater.[^1][^2] The sources do not say whether the light moved or the breakwaters were renamed.

## Today

The Lighthouse Directory gives the Canadian Coast Guard as owner and site manager of the front light.[^2] The Coast Guard's list gives the light as "Miminegash North Breakwater Light," with the characteristic "Fl G 4s."[^1] The directory says the front light can be reached by walking the pier, that the site is open, and that the tower is closed.[^2] The rear light's tower is on private land near Kildare, and LHF says its grounds and tower are closed.[^3] Both towers are closed to visitors.[^2][^3]
