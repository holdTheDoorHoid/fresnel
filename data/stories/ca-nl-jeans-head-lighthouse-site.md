Jean’s Head light stands on a headland at the mouth of New Perlican Harbour, on the eastern side of Trinity Bay in Newfoundland and Labrador, and has shown a light there since 1915.[^1][^5]

## The place

The headland looks down on New Perlican Harbour and commands a broad view across Trinity Bay.[^1]

The name is in question. The site has been called Jean’s Head and Jane’s Head. The town’s heritage statement says the site was “known officially in government records as Jean’s Head Lighthouse” when the light was established.[^1] The same statement says that as early as 1890 newspaper opinion pieces argued both for and against a light on Jean’s Head. It does not name the newspapers.[^1]

## Building the light

A notice to mariners issued in 1915 announced that the light would be put into operation in September of that year.[^1] Lighthouse Friends also dates the building of the lighthouse to 1915.[^3]

The keeper’s house had one storey. An octagonal wooden tower was linked to it by a covered passageway.[^1][^3] Lighthouse Friends gives the tower’s height as 8.2 m (27 ft).[^3]

The light was white and occulting: shown for seven seconds, then eclipsed for three.[^1] It stood 48.6 m (159.5 ft) above high water.[^1]

## The keepers

Corbett Legge was the first keeper, from 1915 to 1926. Allan Ghent took over from him in 1926 and served until 1931.[^1][^3]

## After the keepers

Lighthouse Friends dates the automation to 1931, and says the light then flashed white every two seconds.[^3]

The Lighthouse Directory says the wooden lighthouse was still standing at least into the late 1950s.[^5] Lighthouse Friends, citing the Light Lists, says the wooden tower was still there in 1958.[^3]

Lighthouse Friends reports a flashing white light on a square skeletal tower by 2021.[^3] The sources do not give the year that tower replaced the wooden one.[^3]

## Today

The Coast Guard list shows a white flash every four seconds, a focal height of 48.5 m (159 ft) and a range of 5 nautical miles.[^4]

The Coast Guard list describes the present structure as a square skeleton tower with a red and white rectangular daymark, and gives its height above ground as 4.3 m. The Lighthouse Directory gives 4.5 m (15 ft) for the same tower.[^4][^5]

The Town of New Perlican designated the site a municipal heritage site by Council motion 2013-062 on 13 July 2013.[^1][^2]

The heritage statement says the grounds of the former light station now form part of a hiking trail system.[^1] Lighthouse Friends says the point can be reached from New Perlican on foot.[^3] The Lighthouse Directory says the town maintains a hiking trail to the point.[^5]

The heritage statement says “the light station no longer stands”. The same statement describes a square skeleton tower with a navigational light on the site.[^1] The Coast Guard list shows a light there operating year round.[^4]
